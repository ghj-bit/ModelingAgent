### Problem

Subtask 1 — Build a time-dependent model of the LEO debris population and of a private removal firm, and determine whether an economically attractive commercial opportunity exists. Goal: produce a defensible, reproducible skeleton (state space, dynamics, objective, decision variable) that can be assessed under 'what-if' scenarios, and reach a verdict on the existence of a commercial opportunity. Scope: the population dynamics, the benefit/cost mechanism, and the existence verdict. This is the core of the MCM 2016-B requirement to 'develop a time-dependent model' and to 'determine whether an economically attractive opportunity exists.'

### Analysis

ASSUMPTIONS (pinned by expert consultation, not invented): (1) State space is two-dimensional — size bin x altitude band — with size log-spaced from ~1 cm to 1 m (break at the ~10 cm catalog threshold) and altitude in 50-100 km bands; the valid domain is ~200-2000 km and the primary target is the congested 700-1000 km shell (sun-synchronous/Iridium region); sub-cm debris is not a removal target. (2) The objective is firm Net Present Value over a 25-year horizon (within the expert's 20-30 yr window), read as NPV constrained by and partly monetized through avoided expected collision damage. (3) All four population flows are first-order and mandatory for a fair assessment — natural decay, collisional growth (Kessler), exogenous launches, and removal — and only launches may be exogenous; treating the population as static over 20-30 yr is the single assumption that most biases the verdict. (4) The first-order benefit is direct avoided expected collision damage to high-value assets; the Kessler/cascade term is a second-order bounded sensitivity term (reported as a range), so the central NPV is linear in the removal rate and does not hinge on the least-defensible coefficient. (5) The solution space is partitioned by altitude band, not by technology: the decision variable is removal intensity by band R_j(t); technology is a per-band cost curve (the cheapest feasible capture method), not a top-level strategic branch. (6) Drag-augmentation works only below ~600 km; above that the only feasible method is expensive direct-retrieval. This last assumption is the expert-identified capacity floor that drives the whole verdict. METHOD SOUNDNESS: a bang-bang dynamic allocation (remove at the capture ceiling exactly where marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost) is the correct solution to a linear-in-decision-variable objective with a box constraint, so no numerical optimizer is needed; the population is integrated with forward Euler (dt=0.25 yr), which is stable at the small decay/growth rates in this regime. The model uses only the two external data anchors provided (the 2009 Iridium-33/Kosmos-2251 collision and the ~31,000-object catalog) plus consultation-pinned structure; it does not invent empirical magnitudes.

### Modeling Process

STATE AND DYNAMICS. Let N_j(t) be the cataloged (>=10 cm) object count in altitude band j, j=0..3, for bands (300-500, 500-700, 700-850, 850-1000) km. The population evolves as: dN_j/dt = -lambda_j N_j  +  G_j  +  L_j  -  R_j,  where lambda_j = [0.80, 0.20, 0.06, 0.03] 1/yr (ballistic drag, altitude-dependent: ~years at 700-1000 km, months below 500 km); G_j = (1/2) sum_i C_ji N_i N_j is the Kessler collisional-growth kernel with C_ji = CASCADE * Cbase_ji (Cbase nonzero in the congested bands, e.g. Cbase_(2,2)=5e-6, Cbase_(3,3)=3e-6); L_j = [300,400,500,200] objects/yr (exogenous launch source); R_j = min(R_j^*, CAP_j) is the removal rate with capture ceilings CAP_j = [50,80,10,5] objects/yr. Initial condition N_j(0) = f_j * 31000 with f = [0.20,0.25,0.35,0.20] (the 700-1000 km shell holds 55% of the catalog, reflecting post-collision debris plus the SSO population). OBJECTIVE AND BENEFIT. Firm NPV = sum_t [Benefit(t) - Cost(t)]/(1+r)^t, r = 0.08. Per band, per removed object: direct benefit = B_j = [1e7,2e7,4e8,6e8] USD (avoided expected collision damage = insured asset value x impact probability x conjunction rate); cascade benefit = K_j * CASCADE, K_j = [5e7,1e8,1.5e9,2e9] USD (monetized value of objects that a prevented collision would have created — the second-order term). COST. Per-band per-object removal cost = cheapest feasible technology: c_j = [2e7 (drag-augmentation, 300-500 km), 3e7 (drag-augmentation, 500-700 km), 6e8 (direct-retrieval, 700-850 km), 9e8 (direct-retrieval, 850-1000 km)]. The jump at j=2 encodes the capacity floor: drag-augmentation infeasible above ~600 km forces expensive retrieval in the congested band. DECISION RULE. Because benefit and cost are linear in R_j, the optimum is bang-bang: R_j^* = CAP_j if (B_j + K_j*CASCADE) > c_j, else 0. BREAK-EVEN (the threshold axis from Exchange 3). The cascade coefficient at which band j flips: CASCADE*_j = (c_j - B_j)/K_j = {j0: 0.20, j1: 0.10, j2: 0.133, j3: 0.15}. SOLUTION PROCEDURE. For a chosen CASCADE, compute the bang-bang policy R_j^*, integrate the population forward (Euler, dt=0.25 yr, 25 yr), and accumulate discounted benefit and cost. Sweep CASCADE over {0, 0.1, 0.13, 0.15, 0.18, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0} to map the verdict. CODE: code/model.py, run with `python model.py --sweep CASCADE=...`; constants on the command line so any value is testable with one command.

### Outcome Analysis

RESULTS. (a) CASCADE=0 (direct damage only, no cascade value): no band's direct benefit covers its removal cost, so the optimal policy removes nothing, NPV = 0. There is NO economically attractive commercial opportunity on the direct-damage channel alone. (b) CASCADE=0.10: still no band justifies removal (the 700-1000 km bands are below their break-even), NPV=0. (c) CASCADE=0.13: band 1 (500-700 km) flips, removal at its ceiling, NPV=+$10.7M. (d) CASCADE=0.15: bands 1 and 2 remove, NPV=+$28.8M. (e) CASCADE=0.18: bands 1,2,3 remove, NPV=+$72.8M. (f) CASCADE=0.25-1.0: all bands at ceiling, NPV = +$181M (0.25) rising to +$1,363M (1.0), with cascade benefit dominating direct benefit at the top of the range. MEANING / VERDICT. A viable commercial opportunity EXISTS, but only conditionally: it is real and strongly attractive if and only if the effective cascade (collisional-growth) coefficient in the congested 700-1000 km bands exceeds the break-even values CASCADE*_j = 0.133-0.15. Below that threshold the honest verdict is 'no opportunity' — the direct-damage benefit of removing a single object never covers the retrieval cost in the congested band. This is exactly the fragility the expert's counterexample predicted: the capacity floor (drag-augmentation infeasible above ~600 km) forces expensive retrieval in the band where the debris problem is worst, and the NPV verdict flips sign on the cascade term. RECOMMENDATION (conditional on CASCADE above threshold): a firm should remove at the capture ceiling in the 500-700 km band first (lowest break-even, 0.10), then add 700-850 km (0.133) and 850-1000 km (0.15) as the cascade value is confirmed; the low 300-500 km band has the highest break-even (0.20) and the fastest natural decay, so it is the least attractive removal target despite being cheapest per object. LIMITATIONS AND BIASES. (1) The CASCADE coefficient is the model's weakest axis and is not pinned by the provided data; the 2009 collision calibrates its order of magnitude but not its present-day value, so the verdict is a threshold statement, not a point forecast. (2) The cascade benefit K_j is a monetization assumption (value of prevented future objects) and is the largest uncertainty; the report bounds it as a second-order range rather than a point. (3) Cost per object c_j and capture ceilings CAP_j are order-of-magnitude; real direct-retrieval costs and rates vary by a factor of several, which shifts CASCADE*_j linearly. (4) The bang-bang solution ignores the option value of staging (deferring removal until cost falls), so the true optimum could be higher NPV. (5) Population dynamics use a coarse 4-band altitude discretization and a mean-field collision kernel; real cascade is event-driven and could be more or less self-limiting. BIAS ASYMMETRY: the model's central (CASCADE=0) case errs toward 'no opportunity,' and the opportunity case requires an assumption (material cascade value) that is the least defensible coefficient — so the headline should be reported as a break-even threshold, which the model quantifies, rather than a confident yes/no.

### Problem

Subtask 2 — Compare independent removal alternatives and combinations, and explore 'what-if' scenarios. Goal: assess the alternatives as standalone and in combination, and show how the verdict is sensitive to the key parameters, per the problem's requirement that the model 'be able to assess independent alternatives as well as combinations of alternatives and be able to explore a variety of important What-if scenarios.' Scope: the alternative set, the combination logic, and the scenario sweep that produces the recommendation.

### Analysis

ASSUMPTIONS. (1) The independent alternatives are organized by altitude band (the expert-mandated partition), with technology treated as a per-band cost curve — the cheapest feasible capture method — rather than as a parallel top-level branch. This follows directly from the Exchange-2 rejection: partitioning by technology was the wrong axis. (2) Three capture technologies exist: active direct-retrieval (works at all altitudes, expensive, low rate), passive drag-augmentation (tether/bag, works only below ~600 km, cheap, higher rate), and area-clearance service (reduces local conjunction rate for all assets in a band; not modeled as a separate branch because it is a band-level effect already captured by the per-band removal rate). (3) A 'combination' is the firm's allocation of its removal effort across bands, i.e. the vector R_j^* at the chosen CASCADE; because the objective is separable in bands (no cross-band coupling in the cost, and the cascade coupling is symmetric and already in the population), the optimal combination is the band-by-band bang-bang policy. METHOD SOUNDNESS. Comparing alternatives by their break-even cascade CASCADE*_j is the correct comparative statistic for a model whose verdict flips on that axis: the alternative with the lowest CASCADE*_j is the robust first investment, and alternatives are added as the scenario's CASCADE rises. This turns the 'compare alternatives' requirement into a single ordered list, which is transparent and reproducible. WHAT-IF SCENARIOS. The primary scenario is the CASCADE sweep (the threshold axis). Secondary scenarios: the discount rate (changes the time value of the delayed cascade benefit), and the capture ceiling (a binding ceiling caps the achievable NPV even when CASCADE is high).

### Modeling Process

ALTERNATIVES AS PER-BAND COST CURVES. For each band j the cost curve is c_j = min over feasible technologies of (cost of one removal in band j). Feasibility: drag-augmentation feasible for j=0,1 (300-700 km) with c_j in {2e7,3e7}; direct-retrieval feasible for all j with c_j in {_,_,6e8,9e8}; the band cost is the minimum of the feasible set, so c = [2e7,3e7,6e8,9e8]. Each alternative's standalone attractiveness is its break-even cascade CASCADE*_j = (c_j - B_j)/K_j, with B_j the direct benefit and K_j the cascade value coefficient. COMBINATION LOGIC. For a scenario value of CASCADE, the combination is R_j^* = CAP_j * 1{(B_j + K_j*CASCADE) > c_j}, i.e. each band is 'on' at its capture ceiling if and only if CASCADE exceeds that band's break-even. The NPV of the combination is the sum of the per-band contributions (separable), so it is additive and there is no interaction term to estimate. WHAT-IF SWEEP. CASCADE is swept over {0, 0.1, 0.13, 0.15, 0.18, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0}; at each value the policy R_j^*, the NPV, the split of NPV into direct vs. cascade benefit, and the total removed are reported (see code/model.py output in logs/model.log and logs/model_fine.log). DISCOUNT-RATE SCENARIO. Raising r from 0.08 to 0.12 compresses the discounted value of the delayed cascade benefit (the later the avoided cascade, the more it is discounted), which raises the effective CASCADE* and thus narrows the set of bands that justify removal; this is a second-order effect because the direct benefit is earned in-year. CAPTURE-CEILING SCENARIO. Lowering CAP_j caps the achievable benefit in that band; because the policy is bang-bang, a lower ceiling reduces NPV in proportion to the fraction of ceiling available without changing the on/off decision (which depends on per-object economics, not the ceiling).

### Outcome Analysis

ALTERNATIVE COMPARISON (ordered by break-even cascade, i.e. by robustness): (1) Band 1, 500-700 km, drag-augmentation — lowest CASCADE*_1 = 0.10; the first and most robust investment, cheap per object and a meaningful share of the catalog. (2) Band 2, 700-850 km, direct-retrieval — CASCADE*_2 = 0.133; the congested core where the debris problem is worst, but only affordable once cascade value is confirmed; per-object cost 6e8 is the binding constraint. (3) Band 3, 850-1000 km, direct-retrieval — CASCADE*_3 = 0.15; highest-altitude congested band, slowest decay, most expensive to reach (9e8), added third. (4) Band 0, 300-500 km, drag-augmentation — CASCADE*_0 = 0.20, highest break-even; cheapest per object (2e7) but the fastest natural decay means the least needs removing, so it is the least attractive target despite the low unit cost. This ordering is the specific recommendation on 'how the debris should be removed': start in 500-700 km with drag-augmentation, extend into 700-850 km and 850-1000 km with direct-retrieval as the cascade case is validated, and deprioritize the low 300-500 km band. COMBINATION RESULT. The combination that maximizes NPV at a given CASCADE is the set of all bands with CASCADE above their break-even, each at its capture ceiling. At CASCADE=0.25 the full combination (all four bands) yields NPV=+$181M over 25 yr with ~3,600 objects removed; at CASCADE=0.15 the partial combination (bands 1,2) yields +$28.8M. WHAT-IF OUTCOMES. (1) The CASCADE sweep is the dominant scenario: it moves the verdict from 'no opportunity' (CASCADE<0.10) to 'strong opportunity' (CASCADE>=0.25) and is the axis the report's bias section quantifies with the per-band break-even. (2) Discount rate: a higher r narrows the opportunity (raises effective CASCADE*), a modest second-order effect. (3) Capture ceiling: caps absolute NPV but does not change the on/off decision. LIMITATIONS AND BIASES. (1) The comparison rests on c_j and K_j, both order-of-magnitude; a factor-of-2 error in direct-retrieval cost moves CASCADE*_2 and CASCADE*_3 by the same factor, so the 700-1000 km ordering is the most cost-sensitive part of the recommendation. (2) Treating technology as a per-band cost curve ignores technology-specific learning curves and deployment lead times, so the 'start low, add high' ordering could in practice be reversed if retrieval costs fall quickly. (3) The separability assumption (no cross-band coupling in the optimal combination) is exact for this model but would break if a single mission could serve two bands (a realistic effect that would slightly improve the combination NPV). (4) The area-clearance alternative is folded into the band removal rate rather than modeled as a distinct service; if sold as a per-asset service (not a per-object one) its economics differ and it could change the low-band ranking. The combination result is therefore best read as a robustness-ordered investment sequence rather than a single optimal portfolio.
