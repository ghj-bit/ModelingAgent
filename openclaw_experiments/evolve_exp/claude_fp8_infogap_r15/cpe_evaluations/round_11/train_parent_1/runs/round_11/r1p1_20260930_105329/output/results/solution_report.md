## Subtask 1: Determine the shape, size, and merging pattern of the area following a barrier toll plaza in which vehicles fan in from 

### Problem

Determine the shape, size, and merging pattern of the area following a barrier toll plaza in which vehicles fan in from B tollbooth egress lanes (B > L) down to L regular travel lanes on a divided limited-access highway with L lanes per direction. The deliverable is a design (not a performance analysis of an existing plaza): the geometric form of the fan-in, its length and width as a function of L and B, the merging pattern (how the B egress lanes reduce to L lanes), and the objective and constraints governing the design. Scope: a general design rule for any L and B, parameterized so it can be applied to a specific plaza (e.g., L=3, B=8).

### Analysis

Assumptions. (1) Per Exchange 1, throughput at the plaza exit (the B-to-L join) is the objective; accident prevention (safety) and cost (land + pavement) are constraints, not objectives. (2) Light traffic = free-flow operation at 0.4 x basic-segment capacity; heavy traffic = near-capacity operation at 0.9 x basic-segment capacity; both judged at the point where the plaza meets the L outgoing lanes. (3) The fan-in geometry and merging pattern are free design variables, not a mandated standard form. (4) Per-lane basic-segment capacity is ~2200 pc/h/ln at 65-75 mph free-flow speed (HCM, external_data.md item 2); lane width 12 ft; merge-influence length ~1500 ft (450 m). (5) Per-booth service rates: manual ~350 veh/h, exact-change ~500 veh/h, ETC (traditional plaza) ~1200 veh/h, ETC (all-electronic) ~1800 veh/h (TTI, external_data.md item 1). (6) Unit costs: ~$1M per lane-mile of new pavement, ~$2M per lane-mile of right-of-way (declared order-of-magnitude assumptions, not from the provided data). (7) Gap-acceptance: a merging vehicle accepts a minimum gap of 0.6 s; AVs relax this to 0.3 s via platooning (declared assumptions). (8) The design is for one direction of a divided highway; the other direction is a mirror image.

Approach. The fan-in is modeled as a staggered-merge uniform taper. The B egress lanes are divided into M merge groups (M between 2 and 4). Within each group, the egress lanes converge to one lane on a short internal taper (~450 ft). The M surviving lanes then fan in to L lanes over a main taper via staggered merges, each merge separated by at least one merge-influence length (1500 ft) so that the disturbance zones do not overlap and at most one merge is occurring at any cross-section. This bounds the number of simultaneous conflict points, which is the dominant accident mechanism in a fan-in. The decision variables are taper length T, number of merge groups M, and merge spacing. The objective is to maximize exit throughput; the constraints are (i) exit throughput <= L x 2200 (basic-segment capacity), (ii) per-merge-point conflict-point rate <= threshold (safety), and (iii) total paved-area cost <= ceiling (cost). The method is sound because it separates the three competing considerations into one objective and two constraints, which matches the expert's Exchange 1 reading, and because the staggered-merge geometry is the standard form that bounds conflict points while keeping the taper length short (shorter taper = less pavement = lower cost).

### Modeling Process

Geometry. Let B egress lanes be divided into M merge groups, each group removing (B-L)/M lanes on average. The internal taper per group has length T_intra = max(100 ft, 0.3 x 1500 ft) = 450 ft. After the M internal tapers, M lanes survive. The main taper removes (M - L) lanes (if M > L) via staggered merges at spacing >= 1500 ft, so T_main = max(1500 ft, (M-L) x 1500 ft). Total taper length T = M x T_intra + T_main. Cross-sections are (position, n_lanes): (0, B), (T_intra, M), then (T_intra + i x 1500, M - 1 - i) for i = 1..(M-L), ending at (T, L).

Throughput. Exit throughput = min(supply, taper_cap). Supply = B x mu_mix, where mu_mix = (1 - av_share) x mu_booth + av_share x 1800, and mu_booth = f_manual x 350 + f_coin x 500 + f_etp x 1200 + f_aet x 1800 (mix-weighted per-booth service rate, with f_manual + f_coin + f_etp + f_aet = 1). Taper_cap is computed by a segment-by-segment cumulative-capacity check: at each cross-section, capacity = n_lanes x 2200 x f_penalty, where f_penalty is a staggered-merge penalty that reduces the main-taper sections' capacity based on the number of staggered merges per unit length: f_penalty = 1 / (1 + 0.5 x n_staggered / (T_main / 1500)). Upstream sections (before the main taper) carry full 2200 per lane. taper_cap = min over sections. For small L (L <= 2) with n_merge >= 3, a zipper-merge term bounds taper_cap further: C_zipper = L_final x 3600 / (t_gap_eff + 1.0), where t_gap_eff = (1 - av_share) x 0.6 + av_share x 0.3 (s); this term captures the Exchange 3 finding that at L=1 the final merge is a forced single-file zipper governed by gap-acceptance, not summed lane capacity.

Safety. Per-merge-point conflict-point rate. At each merge, CP rate scales with the ratio of merging flow to through flow and decreases with merge spacing: CP_segment = 0.05 x (q_minor / q_major) x (1500 / spacing), where q_minor = demand_level x 2200 (one lane of demand) and q_major = n_surviving x demand_level x 2200. Total CP per vehicle-mile = sum(CP_segment) / (T in miles). AVs reduce CPs by 50% x av_share (predictable behavior). Safe if CP_rate <= 0.15 per veh-mi (declared threshold).

Cost. Paved area = integral of width over taper length, width at a section = n_lanes x 12 ft, trapezoidal rule over segments. Right-of-way = paved width + 20 ft (10 ft shoulder each side). Cost = (paved_area / (12 x 5280)) x $1M + (ROW_area / (12 x 5280)) x $2M.

Solution procedure. For given L, B, M: compute geometry, then throughput, safety, and cost at light (0.4) and heavy (0.9) demand. The design is feasible if safe and within the cost ceiling. Vary M and merge spacing to maximize throughput subject to the constraints.

### Outcome Analysis

Base case (L=3, B=8, M=3, base booth mix 50% manual / 30% coin / 10% ETC / 10% AET, no AVs). Geometry: T = 1350 ft, 5 merges, 3 merge groups. Throughput = 5000 veh/h (booth-bound: supply 5000 < taper capacity 6600). Cost = $1.7M. CP rate = 0.217 (exceeds the 0.15 threshold, so the design is safety-infeasible at the base mix — the staggered merges are too close for the demand). The binding constraint is booth supply, not taper capacity: the 8 booths at the base mix pass 5000 veh/h, well below the 3-lane capacity of 6600. This means the fan-in is oversized for the booth supply; the design could be shortened (fewer merge groups, shorter taper) to reduce cost while remaining booth-bound.

L boundary (B=8, M=3). L=1: T = 4350 ft, throughput = 1467 veh/h (taper-bound, the zipper term is not the binding constraint here because the staggered-merge penalty reduces capacity below the zipper cap; the design is safety-infeasible at CP=0.158, cost $7.0M). L=2: T = 2850 ft, throughput = 2933 veh/h (taper-bound, safe at CP=0.149, cost $4.8M). L>=3: T = 1350 ft, throughput = 5000 veh/h (booth-bound, cost $1.7M). The L=1 result confirms the Exchange 3 boundary: when B egress lanes must converge to a single lane, throughput collapses (1467 vs. 5000 at L=3) because the fan-in is long (4350 ft) and the single-lane capacity is low. The design rule is: for B=8, use L>=3 to stay booth-bound; L=1 is impractical for B=8.

AV share (L=3, B=8, base mix). av=0%: throughput 5000 (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe). av=25%: throughput 6600 (taper-bound, supply 7350), CP 0.190 (unsafe). av=50%: throughput 6600 (taper-bound, supply 9700), CP 0.163 (unsafe). av=75%: throughput 6600 (taper-bound, supply 12050), CP 0.136 (safe). av=100%: throughput 6600 (taper-bound, supply 14400), CP 0.109 (safe). The design transitions from booth-bound to taper-bound at ~25% AV share, because AVs use ETC-class non-stop service (1800 veh/h) and raise the booth supply above the taper capacity. AVs also reduce CPs by 50% x av_share, so the design becomes safety-feasible at >=75% AV. The geometry is unchanged (fixed-geometry re-parameterization per Exchange 1); only the service rates and gap-acceptance change.

Booth mix (L=3, B=8, no AV). All manual: throughput 4267 (booth-bound). All coin: 4588 (booth-bound). All ETC traditional: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 7179). All AET: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 9453). 50/50 manual+coin: 4833 (booth-bound). 50/50 ETC+AET: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 8111). The booth mix determines whether the plaza is booth-bound or taper-bound: any mix dominated by manual or coin booths is booth-bound (throughput < 6600); any mix dominated by ETC or AET is taper-bound (throughput = 6600). The geometry is unchanged; only the supply changes. The safety CP rate is the same across all mixes at 0.217 (it depends on demand and geometry, not on booth type, in this order-of-magnitude model), so all mixes are safety-infeasible at the base geometry.

Limitations and biases. (1) The zipper-merge term (Exchange 3) is an order-of-magnitude headway-based approximation; it does not capture the full gap-acceptance distribution or the interaction between the zipper and the upstream staggered merges, so the L=1 throughput (1467) is a lower-bound estimate, not a precise prediction. (2) The safety model uses a single order-of-magnitude CP threshold (0.15 per veh-mi) and a linear ratio model; it does not distinguish conflict-point severity or account for the fact that closely spaced merges (within one impact length) compound their CPs non-linearly. The base design (CP=0.217) is flagged unsafe, but the model cannot say by how much. (3) The cost model uses declared unit costs ($1M/lane-mile pavement, $2M/lane-mile ROW) that are not from the provided data; the absolute cost figures are order-of-magnitude only. (4) The model assumes a single homogeneous demand stream per booth type and does not capture the spatial non-uniformity of the arrival stream when booth types are interleaved (the Exchange 2 flaw the expert rejected); the segment-by-segment cumulative-capacity check is a bottleneck-propagation approximation, not a full queuing model. (5) The model does not optimize M or merge spacing — it evaluates a fixed M=3 configuration; a full design would search over M and spacing to maximize throughput subject to the safety and cost constraints. (6) The geometry-variation-with-AV-share question (Exchange 1, left open) is not modeled: the geometry is held fixed as AV share changes, which is a declared assumption.

## Subtask 2: Determine the performance of the fan-in design in light and heavy traffic, judged at the point where the end of the plaz

### Problem

Determine the performance of the fan-in design in light and heavy traffic, judged at the point where the end of the plaza joins the L outgoing traffic lanes. Light traffic = free-flow operation (density well below capacity density); heavy traffic = near-capacity operation. The deliverable is the exit throughput, the limiting mechanism (booth supply vs. taper capacity vs. zipper), the safety state, and the cost at both demand levels, for the base design and across the L, AV, and booth-mix sweeps.

### Analysis

Assumptions (from Exchange 1 and the core model): light = 0.4 x basic-segment capacity (880 pc/h/ln), heavy = 0.9 x basic-segment capacity (1980 pc/h/ln), both judged at the B-to-L join. The throughput model is the same as in task 1: min(supply, taper_cap) with the zipper term. The demand level affects the safety CP rate (which scales with the demand ratio) but not the throughput (which is supply- or capacity-bound, not demand-bound, in this model — the plaza is designed so that at the design demand the exit is not demand-saturated). Method: evaluate the throughput, safety, and cost at light (0.4) and heavy (0.9) demand for the base design (L=3, B=8, M=3, base mix, no AVs) and for each sweep case.

Why this is sound: the HCM defines light as free-flow (density < ~20 pc/mi/ln, well below the 45 pc/mi/ln capacity density) and heavy as near-capacity; the 0.4 and 0.9 demand levels are standard design levels (40% and 90% of capacity). Judging at the B-to-L join is correct because that is where the fan-in ends and the regular L-lane highway begins; the throughput there is the plaza's effective capacity.

### Modeling Process

For each demand level d in {0.4, 0.9}: exit_throughput = min(B x mu_mix, taper_cap), where mu_mix and taper_cap are as in task 1 (independent of d in this model). Safety CP_rate(d) = [sum over merges of 0.05 x (q_minor(d) / q_major(d)) x (1500 / spacing)] / (T / 5280) x (1 - 0.5 x av_share), where q_minor(d) = d x 2200 and q_major(d) = n_surviving x d x 2200. Cost is independent of d. The design is feasible at demand d if CP_rate(d) <= 0.15 and cost <= ceiling.

The key insight: in this model the throughput is independent of the demand level (it is supply- or capacity-bound), so light and heavy traffic give the same exit throughput. The demand level affects only the safety CP rate, which is higher at heavy traffic (0.9) because the merging flow is a larger fraction of the through flow. Therefore a design that is safe at light traffic may be unsafe at heavy traffic.

### Outcome Analysis

Base design (L=3, B=8, M=3, base mix, no AVs). Light (0.4): throughput 5000 veh/h (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe), cost $1.7M. Heavy (0.9): throughput 5000 veh/h (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe), cost $1.7M. The throughput is identical at both demand levels because the plaza is booth-bound (supply 5000 < taper capacity 6600, independent of demand). The CP rate is also identical (0.217) because the CP model uses the demand ratio q_minor/q_major, which is independent of the absolute demand level (both q_minor and q_major scale with d, so the ratio is constant). This is a limitation: the CP model should depend on the absolute density (higher density = closer headways = more conflicts), but in this order-of-magnitude form the d cancels. In a more refined model, the heavy-traffic CP rate would be higher than the light-traffic CP rate.

L sweep (B=8, M=3, base mix, no AVs). L=1: light/heavy throughput 1467 (taper-bound), CP 0.158 (unsafe), cost $7.0M. L=2: 2933 (taper-bound), CP 0.149 (safe), cost $4.8M. L=3-5: 5000 (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe), cost $1.7M. The L=2 design is the only one that is safety-feasible; it trades 2067 veh/h of throughput (2933 vs. 5000) for safety. The L=1 design is infeasible on both safety and cost.

AV sweep (L=3, B=8, base mix). av=0%: 5000 (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe). av=25%: 6600 (taper-bound), CP 0.190 (unsafe). av=50%: 6600, CP 0.163 (unsafe). av=75%: 6600, CP 0.136 (safe). av=100%: 6600, CP 0.109 (safe). Same pattern as task 1: the design becomes safety-feasible at >=75% AV.

Booth-mix sweep (L=3, B=8, no AV). All mixes give the same CP rate (0.217, unsafe) because the CP model does not depend on booth type. The throughput varies: all manual 4267, all coin 4588, all ETC 6600, all AET 6600. The mix determines the throughput but not the safety in this model — a limitation, because in reality manual booths produce more variable headways and more conflicts at the merge than ETC booths.

Summary. The base design is booth-bound and safety-infeasible at both light and heavy traffic. To make it safety-feasible, either (a) reduce the merge density (fewer, more widely spaced merges — longer taper, higher cost), (b) increase L (more lanes to spread the merges over), or (c) increase the AV share (AVs reduce CPs by 50% x av_share). The throughput is demand-independent in this model, which is a limitation: a full queuing model would show the exit throughput decreasing as demand approaches capacity.

### Problem

Determine how the fan-in design changes as more autonomous (self-driving) vehicles are added to the traffic mix. The deliverable is the change in exit throughput, the limiting mechanism, the safety state, and the geometry (if any) as a function of the AV share (fraction of vehicles that are autonomous), for the base design (L=3, B=8, M=3, base booth mix).

### Analysis

Assumptions (from Exchange 1): the geometry is held fixed as AV share changes (fixed-geometry re-parameterization); whether the geometry may itself vary with AV share is left open by the problem statement and is a declared assumption, not modeled. AVs use ETC-class non-stop service (1800 veh/h, the AET rate) regardless of booth type, because AVs are equipped for electronic toll collection and do not require a staffed or coin booth. AVs also platooning, which relaxes the gap-acceptance constraint (t_gap reduces from 0.6 s to 0.3 s) and reduces the conflict-point rate by 50% x av_share (predictable behavior, no gap-acceptance variance). Method: re-parameterize mu_mix and t_gap_eff as a function of av_share, keeping the geometry and booth mix fixed, and re-evaluate throughput, safety, and cost.

Why this is sound: AVs affect the fan-in through two mechanisms — (1) they raise the booth supply by using the fastest service rate (1800 veh/h), which can shift the limiting mechanism from booth-bound to taper-bound, and (2) they reduce the conflict-point rate by making the merge behavior more predictable. Both mechanisms are captured by re-parameterizing the service rate and the gap-acceptance, which is the minimal change consistent with the fixed-geometry assumption.

### Modeling Process

mu_mix(av) = (1 - av) x mu_booth + av x 1800, where mu_booth = f_manual x 350 + f_coin x 500 + f_etp x 1200 + f_aet x 1800 (fixed booth mix). t_gap_eff(av) = (1 - av) x 0.6 + av x 0.3 (s). f_penalty is increased by the factor (0.6 / t_gap_eff(av)) to reflect the relaxed gap acceptance (AVs can accept smaller gaps, so the staggered-merge penalty is less severe). CP_rate(av) = CP_rate_base x (1 - 0.5 x av). Throughput(av) = min(B x mu_mix(av), taper_cap(av)). Cost is unchanged (geometry fixed).

The transition from booth-bound to taper-bound occurs when B x mu_mix(av) > taper_cap, i.e., when the AV share is high enough that the booth supply exceeds the taper capacity. For the base case (taper_cap = 6600, B = 8, mu_booth = 625 at the base mix), the transition is at mu_mix > 825, i.e., (1 - av) x 625 + av x 1800 > 825, i.e., av > 0.172. So the design becomes taper-bound at ~17% AV share (the model shows 25% because of the discrete sweep and the f_penalty adjustment).

### Outcome Analysis

Base design (L=3, B=8, M=3, base mix). av=0%: throughput 5000 (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe), cost $1.7M. av=25%: throughput 6600 (taper-bound, supply 7350), CP 0.190 (unsafe), cost $1.7M. av=50%: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 9700), CP 0.163 (unsafe). av=75%: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 12050), CP 0.136 (safe). av=100%: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 14400), CP 0.109 (safe). The design changes in two ways as AV share increases: (1) the limiting mechanism shifts from booth-bound to taper-bound at ~25% AV, raising the throughput from 5000 to 6600 veh/h (a 32% increase); (2) the safety state improves, becoming feasible at >=75% AV. The geometry and cost are unchanged. The maximum throughput is capped at 6600 (the taper capacity of 3 x 2200), so further increases in AV share raise the supply but not the throughput — the design is then capacity-bound, and the only way to raise throughput further is to increase L or reduce the merge density (longer taper).

Implication. The fan-in design is insensitive to AV share in terms of geometry (fixed by the fixed-geometry assumption) but sensitive in terms of throughput and safety. At low AV shares (<25%), the design is booth-bound and the AVs have little effect on throughput (they raise the supply but not the exit throughput, which is capped by the booths). At high AV shares (>=75%), the design is safety-feasible and the AVs' platooning is the dominant benefit. The transition region (25-75%) is where the design is throughput-optimal (taper-bound at 6600) but safety-infeasible — a conflict that can only be resolved by changing the geometry (more lanes or more widely spaced merges), which is outside the fixed-geometry scope.

### Problem

Determine how the fan-in design is affected by the proportions of conventional (human-staffed) tollbooths, exact-change (automated) tollbooths, and electronic toll collection booths (such as ETC via a transponder). The deliverable is the change in exit throughput, the limiting mechanism, the safety state, and the geometry (if any) as a function of the booth-type proportions (f_manual, f_coin, f_etp, f_aet, summing to 1), for the base design (L=3, B=8, M=3, no AVs).

### Analysis

Assumptions (from Exchange 1): the geometry is held fixed as the booth mix changes (fixed-geometry re-parameterization). The three booth types (plus AET as a fourth, faster ETC configuration) have different per-booth service rates: manual ~350, coin ~500, ETC traditional ~1200, AET ~1800 veh/h (TTI, external_data.md item 1). The mix-weighted per-booth rate is mu_booth = f_manual x 350 + f_coin x 500 + f_etp x 1200 + f_aet x 1800. The mix also affects the headway variability at the merge: manual booths produce the most variable headways (driver reaction time, payment processing), ETC booths produce the least (fixed transponder read time). In this order-of-magnitude model, the headway-variability effect on safety is not separately modeled (the CP rate is the same across mixes), which is a limitation. Method: re-parameterize mu_booth as a linear combination of the three (four) service rates by their fractions, keeping the geometry fixed, and re-evaluate throughput, safety, and cost.

Why this is sound: the booth mix is the primary determinant of the booth supply (B x mu_booth), which is one of the two terms in the throughput min(). The mix does not affect the taper capacity (which depends on geometry), so it can only shift the limiting mechanism between booth-bound and taper-bound. This is the minimal, correct re-parameterization.

### Modeling Process

mu_booth = f_manual x 350 + f_coin x 500 + f_etp x 1200 + f_aet x 1800, with f_manual + f_coin + f_etp + f_aet = 1. Supply = B x mu_booth. Taper_cap is unchanged (geometry fixed). Throughput = min(supply, taper_cap). Safety CP rate is unchanged (limitation: the model does not capture the mix-dependent headway variability). Cost is unchanged.

The transition from booth-bound to taper-bound occurs when B x mu_booth > taper_cap, i.e., mu_booth > taper_cap / B. For the base case (taper_cap = 6600, B = 8), the transition is at mu_booth > 825. This requires a mix dominated by ETC or AET: e.g., all ETC gives mu_booth = 1200 > 825 (taper-bound), all AET gives 1800 > 825 (taper-bound), but all manual gives 350 < 825 (booth-bound), all coin gives 500 < 825 (booth-bound). A 50/50 ETC/AET mix gives 1500 > 825 (taper-bound); a 50/50 manual/coin mix gives 425 < 825 (booth-bound).

### Outcome Analysis

Base design (L=3, B=8, M=3, no AVs). All manual: throughput 4267 (booth-bound), CP 0.217 (unsafe), cost $1.7M. All coin: 4588 (booth-bound). All ETC traditional: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 7179). All AET: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 9453). 50/50 manual+coin: 4833 (booth-bound). 50/50 ETC+AET: 6600 (taper-bound, supply 8111). The booth mix determines the limiting mechanism: mixes dominated by manual or coin booths are booth-bound (throughput < 6600), mixes dominated by ETC or AET are taper-bound (throughput = 6600). The safety CP rate is the same across all mixes (0.217, unsafe) in this model, because the CP model does not depend on booth type — a limitation, since in reality manual booths produce more variable headways and more conflicts. The geometry and cost are unchanged across mixes.

Implication. The booth mix is a lever for throughput but not for safety (in this model). To raise the throughput, use more ETC or AET booths; to make the design safety-feasible, the mix alone is insufficient (the geometry must also change, or the AV share must be increased, which reduces CPs). The practical recommendation is to use a mix that matches the expected demand: if the design demand is < 4267 veh/h (all manual), a manual-heavy mix is sufficient; if the design demand is > 6600 veh/h, the plaza is capacity-bound regardless of mix and the geometry must be changed (more lanes or a longer taper). The cost is independent of the mix, so the mix choice is a throughput/safety trade-off, not a cost trade-off.
