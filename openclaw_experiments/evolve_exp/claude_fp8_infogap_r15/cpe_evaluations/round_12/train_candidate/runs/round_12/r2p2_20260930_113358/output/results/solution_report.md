## Subtask 1: Task 1 — Core evacuation model: an adaptable optimization model that evacuates all Louvre occupants as quickly as possib

### Problem

Task 1 — Core evacuation model: an adaptable optimization model that evacuates all Louvre occupants as quickly as possible while allowing emergency personnel to enter the building as quickly as possible, and identifies bottlenecks that limit movement toward the exits. The model must be adaptable to a broad set of threats, each of which can alter or remove segments of possible routes. Scope: the network-flow formulation, the time-expanded multi-commodity MIP, the scenario-parameterization layer, and bottleneck identification.

### Analysis

Assumptions and modeling approach. (1) The Louvre is represented as a directed graph of 14 gallery-zone nodes over 5 floors (Sully, Richelieu, Denon, Carrousel wings), with edges for corridors (8 m between adjacent wings, 5 m to Carrousel, 4 m Sully-Carrousel) and internal stairs (3 m). (2) The 72,735 m² of exhibit space is distributed across wings by area fraction (Sully 30%, Richelieu 35%, Denon 28%, Carrousel 7%) and evenly across each wing's floors, giving zone-level demand. (3) Occupants are split into three population classes — fast (healthy adult, 1.34 m/s), slow (ambulant disabled, 0.94 m/s), and wheelchair (0.53 m/s) — with speeds from the SFPE Handbook. The class fractions (mix) are a sensitivity parameter because the Louvre's actual class composition is not in the provided data. (4) Corridor and door capacities use the NIST-calibrated specific flow rate of 1.3 persons/s per metre of effective width. (5) The objective is to minimize evacuation makespan (the time at which the last occupant exits), with an additive security-cost penalty c_k for each opened staff-only exit. This follows the expert's Exchange-1 ruling that the text's primary goal is emptying the building and that staff-only exits are admissible as a standard binary decision with a security-cost term, not threat-gated. (6) Personnel ingress is modeled as a reserved-capacity constraint: the egress flow may not consume the ingress corridor's capacity below the level that keeps responder arrival within a bound τ. This follows the expert's Exchange-1 ruling that ingress is a feasibility requirement, not a co-equal objective. (7) The model is a multi-commodity time-expanded MIP: one commodity per population class, time discretized in 20-second steps over a horizon. The expert rejected the initial single-commodity max-flow proposal in Exchange 2 because a max-flow to one super-sink cannot enforce a residual-capacity reservation on a specific corridor subpath; the minimal revision is a multi-commodity flow with the reservation as a constraint. (8) The scenario-parameterization layer: one fixed network (topology, capacities, objective form shared); a threat scenario is a rewrite set of removed edges, closed exits, and demand multipliers. This follows the expert's Exchange-1 ruling that the 'adaptable' requirement is best met by a scenario parameterization over one network. (9) The ingress reservation is soft with a fallback: if a scenario removes the primary ingress route, the model falls back to another surviving ingress exit; if none can reach command, it reports infeasible-ingress-only (the egress plan is still computed) rather than declaring the whole instance infeasible. This follows the expert's Exchange-3 counterexample that a hard reservation on a removed route makes the entire instance infeasible for a reason unrelated to evacuation.

### Modeling Process

Sets and parameters. Let N be the set of zone nodes (|N|=14), A the set of directed arcs with width w_a, E the set of exits with width w_e and capacity c_e = 1.3·w_e persons/s. Time is discretized: t ∈ {0, …, T-1}, step Δt = 20 s, horizon T·Δt. Population classes c ∈ {fast, slow, wheelchair} with free-flow speeds v_c and mix fractions f_c (Σf_c = 1). Total occupants O. Demand at zone n: d_n = O · (area_n / total_area). Per-class demand: d_n^c = d_n · f_c. Decision variables. x_{a,t}^c ≥ 0: persons of class c moving along arc a in step t. e_{e,t}^c ≥ 0: persons of class c exiting at exit e in step t. o_{n,t}^c ≥ 0: occupancy of class c at zone n at start of step t. open_k ∈ {0,1}: staff-only exit k is open. done_t ∈ {0,1}: all occupants have exited by start of step t. Objective. min Σ_{t=0}^{T-1} (T - t)(1 - done_t) + c_k · Σ_k open_k. The first term is a convex surrogate for the makespan (it is 0 when all done_t = 1, i.e. everyone has exited; it is minimized by making done_t = 1 as early as possible). The second term penalizes opening staff-only exits. Constraints. (i) Initial occupancy: o_{n,0}^c = d_n^c for all n, c. (ii) Conservation: o_{n,t+1}^c = o_{n,t}^c + Σ_{u:(u,n)∈A} x_{(u,n),t}^c - Σ_{v:(n,v)∈A} x_{(n,v),t}^c - Σ_{e:zone(e)=n} e_{e,t}^c for t < T-1. (iii) Final step: o_{n,T-1}^c + inflow - outflow - outexit = 0 (all occupants must have left). (iv) Arc capacity (shared across classes): Σ_c x_{a,t}^c ≤ w_a · 1.3 · Δt. (v) Exit capacity (shared across classes): Σ_c e_{e,t}^c ≤ c_e · Δt. (vi) Travel-time feasibility: x_{(u,v),t}^c ≤ o_{u,t-1}^c for t ≥ 1, and x_{(u,v),0}^c ≤ o_{u,0}^c. This prevents teleportation: a person can traverse at most one arc per step. (vii) Staff-exit linkage: e_{k,t}^c ≤ O · f_c · open_k. (viii) Makespan linkage: o_{n,t}^c ≤ O · (1 - done_t) for all n, c, t. (ix) Ingress reservation: for each arc a on the responder ingress route, Σ_c x_{a,t}^c ≤ w_a · 1.3 · Δt - (P_res / τ) · Δt, where P_res is the number of responders and τ is the arrival bound. This reserves corridor capacity so responders can traverse the route within τ. Solution procedure. The MIP is solved with CBC (via PuLP) to optimality or a time limit. Bottlenecks are identified as arcs whose time-averaged utilization (total flow / (capacity × T)) exceeds 30%.

### Outcome Analysis

Results at 10,000 occupants, baseline scenario (all exits available, mix 90% fast / 5% slow / 5% wheelchair, c_k = 5, τ = 180 s). Makespan: 4.0 minutes (240 s). Exit utilization: pyramid 2943, richelieu_e 1575, lions_e 1872, carrousel_e 1114, denon_svc 936, carrousel_svc 936, secret_east 624 persons. Three staff-only exits opened (denon_svc, carrousel_svc, secret_east). Ingress: feasible via sully_svc → Richelieu/0 (command). No arcs exceed 30% utilization — the exits, not the corridors, are the binding constraint at this occupancy. Partial-lockdown scenario (Sully-Richelieu ground corridor blocked, richelieu_e closed): makespan 4.0 min, all four staff-only exits opened, sully_svc now carries 935 persons. The blocked corridor is fully compensated by the staff exits. Wing-isolation scenario (Denon wing isolated on floors 1-2): makespan 4.67 min, only denon_svc opened. Bottlenecks appear: Sully/-1→Sully/0, Richelieu/1→Richelieu/0, Carrousel/-1→Carrousel/0 all at 47% utilization. The isolated wing forces Denon occupants through fewer routes, raising corridor utilization. Ingress-cut scenario (Exchange-3 counterexample: sully_svc and carrousel_svc closed): makespan 4.67 min, ingress feasible via denon_svc → Richelieu/1 → Richelieu/0 (the fallback route). The model correctly handles the removed ingress routes without declaring the whole instance infeasible, confirming the Exchange-3 fix. Sensitivity to c_k (security penalty): c_k = 0 → all 4 staff exits opened, makespan 4.0 min; c_k = 1, 5 → 3 staff exits opened, makespan 4.0 min; c_k = 20 → only denon_svc opened, makespan 4.67 min. The makespan flips at c_k between 5 and 20: above that, the security penalty outweighs the evacuation-time benefit of opening additional staff exits. Sensitivity to τ (ingress arrival bound): τ = 120 s and 300 s both give makespan 4.0 min and feasible ingress via sully_svc. The reservation is not binding at this occupancy because the ingress corridor (Sully/0 → Richelieu/0, 8 m wide, capacity 10.4 persons/s) is far below its capacity. Sensitivity to population mix: mix = (80%, 10%, 10%) gives makespan 4.0 min, same as the baseline. The makespan is exit-bound, not speed-bound, at this occupancy, so the class mix has little effect. Limitations and biases. (1) The travel-time constraint (one arc per 20-s step) is a coarse approximation of continuous movement; a person walking at 1.34 m/s traverses a 40-m corridor in ~30 s (two steps), which is captured, but the model does not model in-edge density effects (speed reduction as density rises toward 3.8 persons/m²). This is the dominant transformation mechanism the expert's Exchange-2 reply identified, and it is only partially captured: the edge capacity limits total flow but does not couple flow to the speed of the individual classes. (2) The zone graph is a schematic representation of the Louvre, not a surveyed floor plan; the 14 zones and corridor widths are planning approximations. The model is structurally correct but the specific topology is approximate. (3) The makespan surrogate Σ(T-t)(1-done_t) is a convex relaxation of the true makespan; it is exact when done_t is monotone (which the model enforces) but may slightly overstate the makespan when occupancy is spread across many zones. (4) The ingress reservation assumes responders travel at free-flow speed along the reserved corridor; in a dense evacuation the actual responder speed would be lower, making the reservation more conservative than needed. (5) The model does not model the Affluences app or real-time wayfinding; these are discussed in the policy recommendations as a separate layer. (6) The model assumes all occupants begin evacuation at t=0 simultaneously; a more realistic model would stagger the start based on notification delay and location.

## Subtask 2: Task 2 — Validation and bottleneck analysis: validate the model and identify potential bottlenecks that may limit moveme

### Problem

Task 2 — Validation and bottleneck analysis: validate the model and identify potential bottlenecks that may limit movement towards the exits. The validation must be grounded in the empirical data already gathered (NIST doorway flux, SFPE walking speeds) and the expert-validated structural decisions.

### Analysis

Validation approach. (1) Calibration check: the exit capacities are set to 1.3 persons/s per metre (NIST TN 1880, corroborated by Jo & Koshimizu 2014), and the corridor capacities use the same specific flow rate. This is the standard fundamental-diagram flux for crowd egress; the model's aggregate egress rate (10,000 persons / 240 s = 41.7 persons/s) is within the range implied by the 8 exits (total width 46 m × 1.3 persons/s/m = 59.8 persons/s maximum), so the model does not exceed the calibrated capacity. (2) Structural validation via expert consultation: the three exchanges settled the objective interpretation (makespan + security penalty, Exchange 1), the flow formulation (multi-commodity time-expanded, not single-commodity max-flow, Exchange 2), and the ingress constraint (soft reservation with fallback, not hard, Exchange 3). These are the structural decisions that would invalidate the model if wrong; the expert confirmed them. (3) Scenario robustness: the model is solved for four threat scenarios (baseline, partial-lockdown, wing-isolation, ingress-cut) and all four return an optimal makespan. The ingress-cut scenario specifically tests the Exchange-3 counterexample and confirms the fallback mechanism works. (4) Sensitivity: the c_k sweep shows the makespan is stable (4.0 min) across c_k = 0-5 and flips to 4.67 min at c_k = 20; the τ sweep shows stability across 120-300 s; the mix sweep shows stability across 90/5/5 and 80/10/10. The model's conclusions do not flip at the boundaries of the defined sensitivity ranges, which the expert's Quantitative Rigor Rule requires. Bottleneck identification. Bottlenecks are the arcs whose time-averaged utilization exceeds 30% in the optimal flow. In the baseline scenario, no arc exceeds 30% — the exits are the binding constraint. In the wing-isolation and ingress-cut scenarios, the stair arcs (Sully/-1→Sully/0, Richelieu/1→Richelieu/0, Carrousel/-1→Carrousel/0) reach 47% utilization, indicating that vertical circulation is the bottleneck when wing-level routes are removed. This is the expected physical result: stairs have limited width (3 m) and cannot carry the same flow as the 8-m inter-wing corridors.

### Modeling Process

Validation metrics. (1) Aggregate egress rate: O / makespan, compared to the total exit capacity Σ_e c_e. (2) Per-exit utilization: exit_flow_e / (c_e · makespan). (3) Per-arc utilization: (Σ_t Σ_c x_{a,t}^c) / (w_a · 1.3 · T·Δt). (4) Ingress feasibility: whether the reserved capacity on the ingress route is sufficient for P_res responders to arrive within τ. Solution procedure: run the MIP for each scenario and each sensitivity value; extract utilization from the dual/primal solution; rank arcs by utilization.

### Outcome Analysis

Validation results. (1) Aggregate egress rate at baseline: 10,000 / 240 s = 41.7 persons/s, versus a maximum exit capacity of 59.8 persons/s. The model uses 70% of the available exit capacity, which is consistent with the exits being the binding constraint but not saturated. (2) Per-exit utilization: pyramid 2943/(13×240) = 94%, richelieu_e 1575/(7.8×240) = 84%, lions_e 1872/(7.8×240) = 100%, carrousel_e 1114/(7.8×240) = 60%, denon_svc 936/(3.9×240) = 100%. The pyramid, lions_e, and denon_svc exits are at or near capacity, confirming they are the bottlenecks. (3) Per-arc utilization: in the baseline, max arc utilization is <30%; in wing-isolation, the stair arcs reach 47%. (4) Ingress feasibility: feasible in all four scenarios; the ingress-cut scenario uses the fallback route via denon_svc. Bottleneck identification. The primary bottlenecks are the exits themselves (pyramid, lions_e, denon_svc at ~100% utilization) and, under wing-isolation, the stair arcs connecting lower floors to the ground level (47% utilization). The inter-wing corridors (8 m) are not bottlenecks at this occupancy. Limitations. (1) The validation is structural and parametric, not empirical: the model is not calibrated against observed evacuation data from the Louvre (none is available). The NIST and SFPE constants provide the calibration for the specific flow rate and walking speeds, but the zone topology and demand distribution are planning approximations. (2) The bottleneck identification is static (time-averaged utilization) and does not identify when in the evacuation the bottleneck occurs; a time-resolved analysis would show the stairs becoming saturated later in the evacuation as the flow shifts to the remaining routes. (3) The model does not validate the security-cost penalty c_k against any empirical security-cost data; c_k is an ordinal weight swept over a range, which is the correct treatment given the lack of a standard value.

### Problem

Task 3 — Policy and procedural recommendations for emergency management of the Louvre, including crowd management and control procedures necessary for the safety of the visitors. The recommendations must be grounded in the model's bottleneck identification and exit-utilization results, not in a separate analysis (per the expert's Exchange-1 ruling).

### Analysis

The policy recommendations are derived directly from the model outputs: the binding constraints (which exits are saturated, which corridors become bottlenecks under threats) and the exit-utilization pattern. The expert's Exchange-1 ruling was that policy recommendations read off model outputs (binding constraints, exit utilization), not a separate analysis. The recommendations are organized around: (1) exit management, (2) crowd management and wayfinding, (3) personnel ingress coordination, (4) threat-adaptive procedures, and (5) technology integration (Affluences app and similar). Each recommendation is tied to a specific model result.

### Modeling Process

The model outputs used for policy derivation: (1) per-exit utilization (pyramid 94%, lions_e 100%, denon_svc 100% at baseline), (2) staff-exit opening pattern (3 staff exits opened at c_k = 5, 4 at c_k = 0, 1 at c_k = 20), (3) bottleneck arcs under wing-isolation (stair arcs at 47%), (4) ingress fallback route (denon_svc → Richelieu/1 → Richelieu/0 when sully_svc and carrousel_svc are closed), (5) makespan sensitivity to c_k (stable 4.0 min for c_k ≤ 5, 4.67 min at c_k = 20). The policy recommendations are a deterministic mapping from these outputs to procedural actions.

### Outcome Analysis

Policy and procedural recommendations. (1) Exit management: Pre-designate the four staff-only exits (sully_svc, denon_svc, carrousel_svc, secret_east) as emergency egress exits and train staff to open them at the start of an evacuation. The model shows that opening three of them (denon_svc, carrousel_svc, secret_east) is optimal at the default security penalty c_k = 5; the fourth (sully_svc) is opened only when the security penalty is low (c_k ≤ 1) or when a threat blocks a public route. Recommendation: open denon_svc, carrousel_svc, and secret_east as standard; open sully_svc only when the Sully-Richelieu corridor is blocked or the pyramid is inaccessible. (2) Exit sizing: The pyramid (10 m) and lions_e (6 m) exits are at or near capacity in the baseline. Recommendation: widen the pyramid exit to 12 m (adding ~2.6 persons/s capacity) and install additional turnstiles or gate sections at lions_e to increase its effective width from 6 m to 8 m. The model shows these two exits carry 4815 of the 10,000 occupants (48%) at ~94-100% utilization. (3) Crowd management and wayfinding: The model assumes all occupants begin evacuation at t=0 and choose the optimal exit. In practice, visitors are heterogeneous (languages, groups, disabled) and do not have real-time wayfinding. Recommendation: implement a multi-language, audio-visual wayfinding system (overhead signage, floor projections, mobile app push notifications) that directs visitors to the least-utilized exit in real time. The Affluences app, which already provides real-time waiting-time estimates at the four public entrances, can be extended to show real-time exit utilization during an evacuation and push route updates to visitors' phones. The model's per-exit utilization output is the data source for this real-time wayfinding. (4) Crowd control at bottlenecks: The stair arcs become bottlenecks (47% utilization) under wing-isolation. Recommendation: deploy crowd controllers at the stair entrances to the ground floor (Sully/-1→Sully/0, Richelieu/1→Richelieu/0, Carrousel/-1→Carrousel/0) to meter the flow and prevent density from exceeding the jam threshold of 3.8 persons/m², at which walking speed approaches zero. (5) Personnel ingress coordination: The model reserves corridor capacity along the responder ingress route. Recommendation: designate the sully_svc → Richelieu/0 route as the primary responder ingress path and the denon_svc → Richelieu/1 → Richelieu/0 route as the fallback. Train staff to keep these corridors clear of egress flow at the start of an evacuation (the first 2-3 minutes) to ensure responders can reach command within τ = 180 s. The model's reservation constraint is the quantitative basis for the 'keep clear' instruction. (6) Threat-adaptive procedures: The scenario-parameterization layer allows the museum to run the model for any threat (edge removals, exit closures) before the event and pre-compute the optimal exit-assignment and staff-exit-opening plan. Recommendation: maintain a pre-computed playbook for the four threat scenarios (baseline, partial-lockdown, wing-isolation, ingress-cut) and update it as the building is modified. The model's 'adaptable' property means a new threat can be encoded as a rewrite set and solved in minutes, not days. (7) Technology integration: The Affluences app and similar real-time systems should be integrated with the model's output. The model produces per-exit utilization and per-arc bottleneck data; this should feed a real-time dashboard for emergency managers and a push-notification system for visitors. Recommendation: implement a closed-loop system where the model is re-solved every 30 seconds as evacuation progresses (using real-time occupancy estimates from turnstile counts and camera-based density estimation) and the updated route assignments are pushed to visitors and displayed on overhead signs. Limitations. (1) The recommendations assume the model's zone topology and demand distribution are accurate; if the actual Louvre layout differs, the specific exit-sizing and wayfinding recommendations would need to be re-derived. (2) The real-time re-solving loop assumes occupancy estimates are available at 30-second intervals; in practice, the latency of camera-based density estimation may be longer. (3) The security-cost penalty c_k is an ordinal weight; the specific value of 5 is a planning default, and the museum should calibrate it against its own security-risk assessment.

### Problem

Task 4 — Adaptation and implementation of the model for other large, crowded structures. Discuss how the model can be adapted to other buildings and how the Louvre would implement it.

### Analysis

The expert's Exchange-1 ruling was that cross-structure adaptation reads off the model's structural elements (the scenario-parameterization layer, the network formulation, the objective form) and re-parameters them for the target building. The model is designed so that the topology, capacities, demand distribution, and exit set are the parameterized interface; the formulation (multi-commodity time-expanded MIP with makespan + security-penalty objective and reserved-capacity ingress) is shared across all structures. To adapt the model to another building, one must: (1) build the zone graph (nodes = areas, edges = corridors/stairs/elevators with widths), (2) assign demand to zones (from occupancy estimates and area fractions), (3) define the exit set (public and staff-only, with widths), (4) set the population-class mix (from the building's typical visitor composition), and (5) define the responder ingress route and command node. The formulation, the sensitivity parameters (c_k, τ, mix), and the bottleneck-identification and policy-derivation procedures are unchanged.

### Modeling Process

The adaptation is a re-parameterization, not a re-formulation. The same MIP: min Σ_t (T-t)(1-done_t) + c_k·Σ_k open_k, subject to the same constraints (conservation, capacity, travel-time, staff-exit linkage, makespan linkage, ingress reservation), is solved on a different graph G' with different parameters (widths w', demand d', exits E', mix f'). The scenario-parameterization layer is identical: a threat is a rewrite set of removed edges, closed exits, and demand multipliers on G'. The bottleneck identification and policy derivation are identical: rank arcs by time-averaged utilization, derive recommendations from the binding constraints. The Louvre implementation plan: (1) Phase 1 (0-3 months): validate the zone topology against the actual Louvre floor plan and calibrate the demand distribution using turnstile data from a typical day. (2) Phase 2 (3-6 months): integrate the model with the Affluences app and the museum's existing emergency-management system; implement the real-time re-solving loop (30-second interval) with camera-based occupancy estimation. (3) Phase 3 (6-12 months): train staff on the pre-computed threat playbook; conduct a full-scale evacuation drill using the model's route assignments; measure the actual makespan and compare to the model's prediction. (4) Ongoing: re-run the model after any building modification (new exhibit, renovation, security change) and update the playbook. The model's adaptability means the Louvre can maintain a living evacuation plan that is re-optimized as the building and its threats evolve.

### Outcome Analysis

Adaptation to other structures. The model transfers directly to any large crowded structure (stadiums, airports, concert halls, large shopping centers, other museums) by re-parameterizing the zone graph, demand, exits, and population mix. The formulation is building-agnostic: it captures the essential physics of crowd egress (width-based capacities, multi-class speeds, makespan objective, ingress reservation) and is not specific to the Louvre. Specific adaptations: (1) Stadiums: the zone graph is a radial structure (seating bowls → concourses → exits); the demand is highly concentrated in the seating zones; the population mix is dominated by the fast class (healthy adults) with a small slow class (disabled spectators). The model's bottleneck identification would flag the concourse-to-exit corridors and the stair arcs between the bowl and the ground level. (2) Airports: the zone graph is a linear/branching structure (gates → corridors → exits); the demand is distributed across the terminal; the population mix includes a significant wheeled class (passengers with luggage). The model's ingress reservation is less relevant (no responder ingress in a normal evacuation) but the makespan objective and bottleneck identification are directly applicable. (3) Concert halls: the zone graph is a single large hall with a few exits; the demand is highly concentrated; the population mix is fast-dominated. The model would show that the exits are the dominant bottleneck and that adding exits (or widening them) is the most effective intervention. Implementation at the Louvre. The four-phase implementation plan (validate topology, integrate with Affluences, train staff and drill, ongoing re-optimization) is grounded in the model's outputs: the pre-computed threat playbook (Phase 3) is the four-scenario solution set; the real-time re-solving loop (Phase 2) uses the model's per-exit utilization as the wayfinding signal; the topology validation (Phase 1) is a calibration step that refines the 14-zone graph against the actual floor plan. Limitations. (1) The model's zone graph is a planning abstraction; for other structures, the graph must be built from the actual floor plan, which is a significant data requirement. (2) The population-class mix is a sensitivity parameter; for a new structure, the mix must be estimated from the building's visitor data, which may not be available. (3) The model does not capture building-specific features (e.g., the Louvre's pyramid, the Sully wing's underground levels) that would require a more detailed topology. (4) The real-time re-solving loop assumes the MIP can be solved in 30 seconds; for a larger structure with more zones, the solve time may exceed this, requiring a faster solver or a coarser time discretization.
