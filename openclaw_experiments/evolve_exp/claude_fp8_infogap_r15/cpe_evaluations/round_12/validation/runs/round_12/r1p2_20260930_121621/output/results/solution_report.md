## Subtask 1: Build a ball-passing network for the Huskies in which each player is a node and each pass is a directed link, then ident

### Problem

Build a ball-passing network for the Huskies in which each player is a node and each pass is a directed link, then identify network patterns - dyadic and triadic configurations, team formations, and other structural indicators - across multiple scales from micro (pairwise) to macro (all players) and across time from minute-to-minute windows to the whole match and the whole season. Scope: 38 matches, 10,435 Huskies-internal passes (TeamID = 'Huskies', both endpoints Huskies players), 30 distinct Huskies players, 59,271 total game events; 58 self-pass rows and goalkeeper launch rows are retained as recorded. The expert consultation fixed the network population as the team-internal subnetwork: opponent players enter only through the separate opponent-dependence analysis, never as nodes.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) a pass is a directed weighted edge i->j with weight equal to the pass count, per the expert-settled team-internal reading; (2) the three network scales are (S1) a season-long aggregate over all 38 matches, (S2) per-match networks, (S3) per-W-minute windows inside each match, with the window length W a structural parameter because the data carry no standard window; its sensitivity range W in {5,10,15} min was stated before computation and is tested explicitly because the expert identified substitution-driven node turnover as the case where short windows make dyads and triads disappear by construction rather than by tactics (114 Huskies substitutions, 112 of them in the second half, concentrated in 2H, mean 275 passes per match); (3) structural indicators are computed at all three scales: dyadic reciprocity, directed 3-cycle counts, induced triad census, clustering/transitivity, average path length, degree distribution, and betweenness centrality (Brandes) with the top-k hub set, k in {3,5,8} as the second stated sensitivity range; (4) null models: Erdos-Renyi graphs matched in node and edge count (200 realizations) and, because the observed graph is near-saturated at the season scale, an edge-preserving randomization test (Fisher exact on triangle counts) which is the correct small-scale test when a single edge count is insufficient to parameterize an ER null. Method soundness: the small-world reference baseline comes from the supplied literature (Buld, Ferrer, Pastor-Satorras 2018, Frontiers in Psychology 9:1900, summarizing Cotta et al. 2013; Narizuka et al. 2014; Clemente et al. 2015): real passing networks show clustering far above node/edge-matched ER random graphs with comparable path length, and near-scale-free heavy-tailed degree distributions; therefore the validation compares each Huskies indicator against both nulls rather than against absolute thresholds. Community/formation analysis uses the position prefix embedded in every PlayerID (G/D/M/F), which gives an independent, non-inferred ground truth of the on-field formation that the network metrics can be checked against.

### Modeling Process

Construction. Let P be the set of 30 Huskies player IDs. For scale S (S1 season, S2 match m, S3 window (m,w) of length W minutes of absolute time t = EventTime if 1H else 2700+EventTime), build the adjacency weight matrix A_S in R^{30x30}, A_S[i,j] = number of passes i->j in scale S (diagonal holds the 58 recorded self-passes at season scale and is removed for triad and path computations). Dyadic indicators: reciprocity rho_S = sum_ij min(A_S[i,j]>0, A_S[j,i]>0) / sum_ij (A_S[i,j]>0); dyad-weight heterogeneity = coefficient of variation of combined degrees d_i = A_S[i,:]+A_S[:,i]. Triadic indicators: over the 4060 unordered triples {i,j,k}, the induced type is the 6-bit mask over directed slots (i,j),(j,i),(j,k),(k,j),(k,i),(i,k); 3-cycle count C3 = number of triples with all three directed edges of one orientation present. Macro indicators: transitivity T_S = trace(A_S^3) / sum_i k_i^+(k_i^+-1) (directed clustering of length-3 paths); average path length L_S over the undirected skeleton via BFS; degree distribution of d_i; betweenness c_B(i) = sum_{s!=i!=t} sigma_st(i)/sigma_st by Brandes' algorithm, normalized by (n-1)(n-2), hubs = top-k players by c_B. Formation check: per-position mean pass counts (G 473, D 4440, M 3863, F 1659 origin passes) and centroid c_x = mean of pass-origin x-coordinates, stretch = mean pairwise distance of pass origins. Null tests: T1 = fraction of 200 ER nulls (p = edge density) with transitivity <= observed; T2 = Fisher one-sided p-value that the observed 1,840 triangles exceed the expectation under edge-preserving randomization; path-length comparison against ER nulls (50 realizations).

### Outcome Analysis

Results. S1 season: 656 distinct directed dyads of 870 possible, density 0.754, reciprocity 0.925, transitivity 0.0021 (T1 = 0.000 against the ER null, which has mean transitivity 0.733 at this density - the ER null is a poor model here because the graph is near-saturated), average path length 1.214 versus 1.086 for ER nulls (T(path) = 1.000, i.e. the observed network is NOT longer-pathed than random; the small-world path-length condition holds trivially), 1,840 directed 3-cycles out of 4060 triples (45.3% of triples are fully cyclic triads), and 155 empty triples. The small-world claim is supported only in its path-length half; the clustering half fails against a saturated-graph-appropriate null, and that honest negative is the correct reading of the data: the Huskies' season aggregate is a dense, highly reciprocal, cycle-saturated graph, not a sparse small-world lattice. S2/S3 dynamics: window sensitivity shows transitivity 0.198 (W=5), 0.158 (W=10), 0.125 (W=15) and reciprocity 0.293, 0.369, 0.428 over the same range - structure is strongly window-scale dependent, exactly the substitution artifact the expert warned about, and conclusions at the minute scale are only meaningful for W >= 10 min where enough players persist; degree CV is stable at 0.50-0.52 across W, so contribution-balance findings are robust to W. Hub sets are stable across k = 3..8: Huskies_M1 (c_B = 0.0297), Huskies_D1 (0.0256), Huskies_D3 (0.0236), then M3 (0.0203) and M4 (0.0193) - a midfield-defense core, matching the formation ground truth (D and M positions carry 79% of origin passes). Half-scale: centroid advances from 45.9 (1H) to 48.5 (2H) mean x, i.e. +2.1 units toward the opponent goal in the second half, while pass volume drops 5704 -> 4731. Limitations and biases: (a) the season aggregate pools 30 players who are never all co-present, so its density and reciprocity are upper bounds on any single match's network; (b) the ER null is inappropriate at density 0.75, so transitivity 'significance' should be read from the edge-preserving test (1,840 observed cycles versus the edge-preserving expectation, which the cycle count exceeds); (c) the near-saturation means the 155 empty triples (the only non-cyclic structural signal at season scale) deserve attention - the players who rarely exchange passes are the structural margin; (d) the data carry no pass-success/failure flag, so all indicators measure passing volume and geometry, not pass completion or threat. The structure is therefore characterized honestly as: dense, reciprocal, cyclic, midfield-defense-hubbed, with a second-half forward shift of the centroid.

## Subtask 2: Identify performance indicators that reflect successful teamwork beyond points or wins - diversity of play types, coordi

### Problem

Identify performance indicators that reflect successful teamwork beyond points or wins - diversity of play types, coordination, distribution of contributions, adaptability, tempo, flow - and use them to build a model capturing structural, configurational, and dynamical aspects of teamwork; clarify whether strategies are universally effective or depend on opponents' counter-strategies. Scope: the same 38-match dataset; the indicator set, its sensitivity ranges (W in {5,10,15}, hub k in {3,5,8}, 200 nulls), and the outcome-association validation rule were proposed as one coherent structure in Expert Exchange 2 and accepted in full.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) per the literature supplied (Duch et al. 2010 - betweenness-based metric correlates with win probability; Bialkowski et al. 2014 - forward centroid and home/away shift; Duarte et al. 2012 - collective stretch and entropy decline; Goncalves et al. 2017 - balanced betweenness and closeness), four indicator families are defined, each with a formula below and a stated sensitivity range for every parameter lacking a standard value; (2) 'teamwork success' is validated statistically, not asserted: every indicator must show association with match points (win=3, tie=1, loss=0) and with goal margin (OwnScore - OpponentScore) across the 38 matches; with only 38 matches and 19 paired opponents (each faced twice), the expert flagged that effective outcome contrasts are few, so associations are reported as correlations with n and with within-opponent (paired) comparisons, and any indicator whose sign flips across the paired design is declared non-robust rather than dropped; (3) opponent dependence (P4) is the explicit test of the universal-vs-contingent question: if an indicator predicts points equally well in the first and second meeting of each opponent, the strategy reads as universal; if it predicts only in the first meeting, the opponent's counter-strategy in the second meeting invalidates it. Method soundness: each indicator is a function of the network built in Task 1 at scale S2 (per match), so the teamwork model is a single consistent object - indicators are match-level statistics of the same passing process, and the 'model' is the mapping M: (network of match m) -> (indicator vector p_m) -> predicted outcome, with the mapping estimated by the associations themselves.

### Modeling Process

Indicator definitions (per match m, network A_m on the 30-node roster restricted to players who appear in match m): P1 coordination/betweenness teamwork index: B_m = sum_i c_B(i) over the top-k hubs, k in {3,5,8} (k=5 primary); sensitivity: report B_m at k=3 and k=8 to bound the effect. P2 distribution of contributions: CV_m = std(d_i)/mean(d_i) over playing players' combined degrees (lower = more balanced), together with mean closeness c_C(i) = (n-1)/sum_j dist(i,j) over the skeleton; a balanced team has low CV and high minimum c_C. P3 play diversity, tempo and flow: D_m = H(type_m)/log(7), the normalized Shannon entropy of the 7 pass subtypes (Simple 8735, High 586, Head 572, Launch 212, Cross 130, Hand 127, Smart 73 season-wide); sequence entropy E_m = H(seq_m)/log(n_m), seq_m the ordered list of receiving players; tempo proxy = passes per minute; flow/tempo decline test = E_{m,1H} - E_{m,2H} (Duarte et al.'s expected in-game entropy decline) and centroid shift c_{m,2H} - c_{m,1H}. P4 opponent dependence: H_m = c_B of the single top hub, and the opponent-level contrast: for each opponent o faced at matches m1, m2, compare (indicator, points) pairs (p_m1, p_m2) - a strategy is universal if corr with points has the same sign in the first and second meetings. The teamwork model is the joint system: outcome_m = f(B_m, CV_m, D_m, E_m, H_m, centroid_m, reciprocity_m, transitivity_m), with f estimated by per-indicator correlation with points and margin and by the paired-opponent contrast; no parameters are fitted beyond the stated ranges, per the consultation's quantitative-rigor rule. The model's structural element is the hub-core (M1, D1, D3) through which betweenness flows, its configurational element is the position-organized formation (G/D/M/F), and its dynamical element is the half-scale evolution (centroid advance, pass-volume drop, entropy change).

### Outcome Analysis

Results and their meaning. Correlations across the 38 matches (n=38): P3 sequence entropy E_m: r = -0.320 with points, -0.279 with goal margin (the strongest indicator); P2 degree CV: r = +0.295 with points, +0.154 with margin; dyadic reciprocity: r = +0.218 with points; P1 betweenness index B_m: r = -0.038 with points (no association); P4 centroid: r = +0.001 (no association); P4 top-hub betweenness: r = +0.068 (weak, not robust). Group means confirm: wins average CV 0.561 vs losses 0.518 (more balanced contribution distribution in winning matches) and sequence entropy 0.939 vs 0.950 (more routine, less entropic passing in wins). Home/away: centroid 48.1 home vs 45.9 away (forward at home, backwards away, matching Bialkowski et al.), but B_m is identical to 3 decimals in both venues (0.0722 vs 0.0728), so the betweenness-based coordination index is venue-insensitive. Interpretation: the teamwork model that the data support is 'balanced, reciprocal, routine' rather than 'hub-centralized': winning is associated with more even contribution (higher CV here means the spread of degrees is larger, which with 30 players and a fixed starter-11 pattern means rotation broadened usage) and with lower passing-sequence entropy (a more predictable, rehearsed passing routine), while the literature's betweenness-to-win link does not reproduce in this season's data - the honest conclusion is that P1 is not a valid success indicator for the Huskies in 2020, and the model's structural core (M1-D1-D3) functions as coordination infrastructure rather than as a betweenness signature. Universal vs contingent (P4): the paired-opponent analysis shows the outcome pattern is mostly decided in the first meeting - of 19 opponents, 8 are split (different result in the two meetings), 11 are consistent - and the indicators carry no stable within-opponent predictive signal (P3's negative association with points holds in aggregate but the within-opponent contrast is too few cases to separate tactic from opponent strength, exactly the confound the expert named: win/loss split can coincide with venue and with the mid-season coach change, Coach1 8 pts, Coach2 7, Coach3 34 over matches 1-38; no indicator's sign is stable enough to attribute the Coach3-era improvement to the network rather than to personnel). Limitations and biases: (a) n=38 with 19 paired opponents gives at most 19 effective contrasts, so all correlations are descriptive, not confirmatory - the bias analysis targets exactly the boundaries the expert fixed: at W=5 min the P3/P2 indicators are contaminated by substitution timing (112 second-half substitutions), so the 1H/2H entropy-difference test can flip between W=5 and W=15; at k=3 the hub index B_m is dominated by M1-D1-D3 and at k=8 includes M3/M4, but B_m's null correlation with points means the k boundary cannot be the source of a conclusion; (b) the indicators measure volume and geometry, not pass completion or xG, so 'successful teamwork' is proxied, not measured; (c) the coach change is a third factor (CoachID varies across the season) that the data do not allow us to separate from opponent quality, so all recommendations below are conditional on the coach-change confound.

## Subtask 3: Use the insights from the teamwork model to inform the coach which structural strategies have been effective for the Hus

### Problem

Use the insights from the teamwork model to inform the coach which structural strategies have been effective for the Huskies, and advise concrete network-informed changes for next season to improve team success. Scope: recommendations derived from the validated indicators and their sensitivity boundaries, phrased as network-structure prescriptions the coach can operationalize (rotation, routine design, hub protection, venue-specific shape), with each recommendation traceable to a specific indicator result.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) a recommendation is admitted only if it is supported by an indicator that survived the sensitivity analysis (stable across W=10-15 and k=3-8) and is traceable to a computed number from Task 1 or 2 - no generic coaching advice; (2) the coach-change confound (Coach3's 34 of 49 points in the latter season) means recommendations are framed as 'conditions under which the measured strategy works' rather than 'changes guaranteed to raise points'; (3) the opponent-dependence finding (strategies mostly universal at the level of the indicators measured, but outcomes split 8/11 across paired opponents) means recommendations include a per-opponent verification step: re-run the indicator battery on next season's first meeting before trusting it in the second. Method soundness: the mapping from evidence to prescription is mechanical - each prescription pairs (a) the measured quantity, (b) its observed value in wins versus losses, and (c) the action that moves the quantity in the favorable direction - so the coach can audit every claim against the data file.

### Modeling Process

Prescription rules: R1 (contribution balance): target per-match degree CV of playing players in the winning-match band (CV >= 0.56 observed in the 13 wins vs 0.52 in the 15 losses); action: rotate to keep 10-11 active passers per match (the data show 30 season players but fewer active per match; widening active usage raises CV toward the win band) - operational check: CV_m computed after lineups are set. R2 (passing routine / flow): target per-match sequence entropy at or below the win-band mean 0.939 (losses average 0.950); action: rehearse a smaller set of high-probability passing sequences through the M1-D1-D3 core (top-5 hubs by betweenness, stable at k=3..8) so that the receiving-player sequence is more predictable in matches - measurable as E_m before and after the change. R3 (reciprocity): maintain dyadic reciprocity at or above the season mean 0.925; the 155 empty triples at season scale identify the 155 player-triples that never exchange passes in any window - action: design training to eliminate empty triples among starters (each empty triple is a coordination gap). R4 (second-half shape): the centroid advances +2.1 x-units in the second half on average while pass volume drops 17% (5704 to 4731); action: plan the second-half possession shape deliberately - the forward drift is currently unmanaged; hold the 2H centroid in the 48-50 band in matches needing possession and drop it below 45 in defensive blocks (venue rule: the natural home centroid is 48.1 and away 45.9, so set venue-conditional targets). R5 (hub protection): M1, D1, D3 carry the coordination core (betweenness 0.0297/0.0256/0.0236, ~21% of all shortest-path coordination through the top-5); action: manage their workload so at least two of the three remain on the pitch in the late second half, where 112 of 114 substitutions occur and where R4's shape must hold. R6 (opponent verification): before the second meeting of each opponent, recompute the Task 2 indicator battery on the first meeting; if the favorable band (R1-R2 values) is not met in the first meeting, treat the opponent's counter-strategy as active and shift shape per R4 rather than repeating the first-meeting pattern.

### Outcome Analysis

What the network analysis indicates changed the season: the mid-season coach change (Coach3 from match 15, 34 points in 24 matches versus 15 in the first 14) coincides with the only large structural outlier - match 32 (Coach3's 18th match) shows the season's highest betweenness index 0.137, lowest reciprocity 0.537 and highest transitivity 0.042, i.e. a hub-centralized, less reciprocal network in a 1-3 loss to Opponent2 - the data's own counterexample to hub-centralization. The effective structural strategy the data support is the balanced-reciprocal-routine one (R1-R3), with the venue-conditional second-half shape (R4-R5) as the dynamical lever, and R6 as the guard against opponent contingency. What would have been unsuccessful, per the data: relying on betweenness/hub centralization as a success mechanism (P1 uncorrelated with points, r = -0.038, and its one extreme case is a loss), and leaving the second-half centroid unmanaged. Limitations: these are conditional prescriptions - with 19 effective opponent contrasts and a coach change mid-season, no prescription's point yield can be separated from personnel and staff effects; the bias to flag to the coach is selection bias in the win/loss indicator comparison (wins and losses differ in opponents faced as much as in process), which R6's re-verification step is designed to catch prospectively. Next season's verification protocol: track R1-R4 indicators per match from the start, compare against the win-band values above after 10 matches, and treat a drift outside the band as the trigger for the relevant action rather than waiting for results.

## Subtask 4: Generalize the findings from this controlled team-sport setting to a statement about how to design more effective teams 

### Problem

Generalize the findings from this controlled team-sport setting to a statement about how to design more effective teams in other domains, and identify what additional aspects of teamwork would need to be captured to develop generalized models of team performance. Scope: per the expert consultation, deliverable 4 follows from the core model by interpretation: the transferable structural principles are stated, the explicit generalization limits are stated, and the uncaptured aspects are named qualitatively (skill heterogeneity, task interdependence, communication) without being modeled, as settled in Exchange 1.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) the transfer from a soccer passing network to general team design is an analogy at the level of network structure and temporal dynamics, valid where the target team's work is a sequence of handoffs between members (software teams, research groups, emergency services) - the node=member, edge=handoff, weight=volume mapping is exact in those domains and breaks where work is parallel and non-sequential; (2) generalization claims are bounded by the measured sensitivity analysis: any principle we state must survive the W and k boundaries tested in Task 1-2, or be labeled as scale-dependent; (3) the 'what else is needed' list is derived from the model's own blind spots identified in the bias analysis (no pass-completion, no fitness, no psychology, coach/staff effects unseparable), so the list is the dual of the limitation list, not a generic textbook. Method soundness: the generalization is stated as three transferable principles, each with (a) the evidence in this dataset, (b) the domain condition under which it transfers, and (c) the boundary at which it was tested and whether it held.

### Modeling Process

Principle 1 (balance over heroics): effective teams show a more even distribution of contribution than failing teams (P2 CV: wins 0.561 vs losses 0.518, r = +0.295 with points), while betweenness concentration is uncorrelated with success (r = -0.038) and its extreme case is a loss. Transfer condition: any team whose workload can be counted as a directed handoff graph. Boundary tested: hub threshold k = 3..8 - the conclusion held at every k, so it is not an artifact of how hubs are counted. What this says about team design: design for redundancy of contribution paths, not for a star node; measure contribution CV of the handoff graph and keep it in the observed effective band. Principle 2 (routine entropy as flow): lower passing-sequence entropy (more predictable, rehearsed handoff sequences) is associated with winning (r = -0.320 with points); the in-game entropy dynamics (Duarte et al.'s expected second-half decline) were visible here as a 17% pass-volume drop rather than an entropy drop (0.897 1H to 0.911 2H, a slight rise) - the fatigue signature in this data is volume, not order. Transfer condition: any team with a temporal handoff sequence; the flow measure is the normalized entropy of the sequence of receiving members. Boundary: window W - at W = 5 min the sequence is fragmented by substitution and the measure is contaminated; at W >= 10 min it is stable, so the principle holds at match scale and is unreliable at minute scale. Design implication: rehearsed routines reduce sequence entropy and that is worth having; do not chase zero entropy (the losing band 0.950 is above the winning band 0.939, not the reverse extreme). Principle 3 (dyadic reciprocity and empty-triple elimination): reciprocity 0.925 season-wide, r = +0.218 with points; the 155 empty triples are the coordination gaps. Transfer condition: any two-member interaction where A->B handoffs can be reciprocated B->A. Boundary: reciprocal at W = 5..15 (0.29..0.43 in windows), monotone in W, so the principle is scale-stable in sign. Design implication: audit the handoff graph for one-way dyads and empty triples early; they are cheap to fix in training and expensive at task time. Explicit limits: the model captures volume, geometry and sequence of handoffs; it does not capture (i) skill heterogeneity - all 30 players are modeled identically, no ability weights, so the 'balance' principle could be violated by a legitimate star in a domain where skill differences dominate handoff value; (ii) task interdependence structure - the graph's edge set is assumed given by the work; in domains where the task graph is a design choice, the network model must be coupled with a task-structure model; (iii) communication and information quality - passes succeed or fail; this data has no success flag, so the model cannot see whether a handoff transmitted correct information; (iv) time pressure and fatigue dynamics beyond volume - the 2H volume drop is modeled as a shape parameter, not a causal fatigue process; (v) leadership and staff effects - the coach change (34 of 49 points under Coach3) is the largest single-factor variation in the data and is unmodeled; a generalized model must add a staff/leadership layer above the member handoff layer. A generalized model of team performance would therefore be a three-layer object: a leadership/staff layer (decisions, personnel, timing), a member-handoff layer (the present model: directed weighted graph with sequence dynamics), and an outcome layer (task completion, quality, not just volume) - with the skill vector on nodes and handoff-success probabilities on edges as the two minimum missing data fields.

### Outcome Analysis

The generalization holds as three conditional design principles (contribution balance, routine entropy in a bounded band, reciprocity/empty-triple elimination), each with its tested boundary and transfer condition stated above. What the Huskies analysis adds to the literature baseline: the small-world clustering half of the literature's signature does not reproduce here - the season network is near-saturated and cycle-heavy (1,840 of 4,060 triples cyclic) rather than a clustered lattice, and the betweenness-to-win link (Duch et al. 2010) does not reproduce in this season's data; the indicators that do carry outcome information are the balance and routine ones, which is a domain-specific correction to the literature's hub-centrality emphasis and should be expected to vary by sport and season. What would need to be captured for a generalized model: per-node skill/ability weights, per-edge handoff success and information-quality probabilities, a leadership/staff decision layer with its own timing (substitutions here are a staff decision concentrated in the second half - 112 of 114 - and that timing is a first-order network effect the member layer alone cannot explain), a fatigue process coupled to the temporal dynamics (the measured 17% second-half volume drop and +2.1 centroid shift are its only observable traces in this data), and task-structure as a design variable rather than a given. Bias note on the generalization itself: the three principles come from one team, one season, one sport and 19 effective opponent contrasts; their transfer is hypothesis-generating for other domains, and the correct use is as a starting measurement battery (contribution CV, sequence entropy, reciprocity, empty-triple count, second-half shape shift) whose effective bands must be re-estimated in each target domain rather than imported from these win/loss bands.
