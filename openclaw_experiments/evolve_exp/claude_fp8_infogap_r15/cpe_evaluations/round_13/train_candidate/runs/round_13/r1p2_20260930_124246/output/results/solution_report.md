## Subtask 1: Determine whether a universal, decentralized digital currency with internal security (blockchain-style) is feasible, i.e

### Problem

Determine whether a universal, decentralized digital currency with internal security (blockchain-style) is feasible, i.e. whether a currency mechanism exists that is (i) decentralized in the institutional sense (no single legal entity can freeze, censor, or reverse a confirmed transaction), (ii) secure (double-spend prevention via consensus, internal security without reliance on banks or governments as intermediaries), and (iii) universal (usable for instantaneous, borderless transactions and capable of onboarding the ~1.4 billion unbanked adults). The scope, per the expert consultation, is a CLASS of such mechanisms: first establish the necessary conditions any member must satisfy, then provide one concrete instantiation as a worked example. 'Possible' is interpreted as EXISTENCE (at least one parameterization of the class meeting all necessary conditions) plus SELF-SUSTAINING stability (an adoption and validator equilibrium that does not collapse).

### Analysis

The problem statement leaves three structural/definitional questions ambiguous that must be settled before parameterization. The expert consultation (Exchange 1, Operator 1) resolved them: (a) the object of evaluation is a mechanism CLASS with necessary conditions plus one instantiation; double-spend prevention via consensus, transferability, and internal security are FIXED by definition, while the consensus rule, key/address structure, issuance and fee scheme, and the degree of anonymity are FREE parameters; (b) 'decentralized' is a two-tier predicate — institutional (primary: no legal entity can freeze/reverse) enabled by technical (a minimum honest coalition must control consensus against any feasible colluding coalition); the logical boundary is the honest-coalition threshold, below which no legal-entity guarantee holds and the mechanism exits the decentralized domain; (c) 'possible' = existence as the primary criterion, with Pareto/stability as the test that the existence is self-sustaining. The mechanism is therefore decomposed into two layers: a settlement/consensus/issuance layer (the 'secure' layer) and an identity/transaction layer carrying a continuous anonymity parameter alpha in [0,1], with anonymity orthogonal to consensus. Phase 1 (existence) verifies that a parameterization exists meeting the honest-coalition boundary, the derived institutional guarantee, and a resource (energy) envelope calibrated to the Cambridge CBECI PoW baseline (~100 TWh/yr) as the upper reference and stake-based consensus as the lower reference.

### Modeling Process

Layer A (settlement/consensus/issuance). Consensus: permissionless, stake-weighted BFT over a fluid, frequently-rotating validator committee drawn by a verifiable random function from all stake-holders, with a max-stake-cap c_max so no single legal entity can exceed the honest-coalition boundary. The institutional guarantee is DERIVED from the cap plus rotation: because no entity holds more than c_max < threshold and the committee rotates, no entity is a freeze/reverse veto point. Variables: c_max (max stake share per entity), T (honest-coalition threshold), S (total stake), n = 1/c_max (max distinct entities at the cap). Necessary conditions: (a) technical: honest share h = 1 - s_collude > T, where s_collude is the worst-case colluding share of sub-cap entities (design value T - margin); (b) institutional: c_max < T and (a) holds; (c) resource: E_stake <= E_PoW. Worked instantiation: c_max = 0.05, T = 0.5, so n = 20 distinct entities at the cap, design colluding share 0.45, honest share 0.55 > 0.5, c_max = 0.05 < 0.5. Energy: E_stake ~ 0.1 TWh/yr (stake-based BFT, order-of-magnitude lower reference) vs E_PoW ~ 100 TWh/yr (Cambridge CBECI upper reference), ratio 0.001. All three necessary conditions hold, so EXISTENCE = YES. Issuance: dual-leg — a deterministic base schedule (base_issuance fraction of supply per yr) plus a bounded, stake-voted fine-tuning band (band fraction of supply per yr) providing a monetary-policy leg without a central entity. Fee-sink: fee_fraction of supply per yr, paid in-currency. Phase 1 output: existence = (a) & (b) & (c) = YES for the worked instantiation.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: a universal decentralized digital currency mechanism EXISTS. The worked instantiation (stake-weighted BFT, c_max = 0.05, T = 0.5, stake-based energy) satisfies the technical honest-coalition boundary (honest 0.55 > threshold 0.5), derives the institutional guarantee from the cap + rotation rather than assuming it, and operates at ~0.1% of the PoW energy baseline, comfortably inside the resource envelope. Limitations and biases: (1) the honest-coalition boundary is a static threshold; it does not capture correlated or endogenous collusion that forms in response to issuance incentives (this is exactly the failure mode surfaced in the security/anonymity task). (2) The 0.1 TWh/yr stake-based energy figure is an order-of-magnitude lower reference, not a measurement; the feasibility conclusion is robust to it being up to ~2 orders of magnitude higher and still below the PoW baseline. (3) The cap value 0.05 and threshold 0.5 are design choices within the class; the existence result is insensitive to their exact values as long as c_max < T, which is the structurally load-bearing inequality.

### Problem

Analyze the implications of the core currency mechanism for current banking systems and nation-based currencies. This deliverable inherits the consensus, issuance, and settlement structure of Layer A (per the expert's deliverable mapping) and asks: how does a universal decentralized currency affect national monetary policy, inflation control, and the ~1.4 billion unbanked adults (World Bank Findex 2021), and under what conditions is it regulatorily acceptable to existing banking and currency institutions?

### Analysis

Two sub-analyses. (1) Financial inclusion / demand side: parameterize the addressable population from Findex 2021 (N0 = 1.4e9 unbanked adults; total adult population ~5.4e9 implied by 76% account ownership; developing-economy account ownership rose ~17 percentage points over 4 years, 2017-2021). Model adoption with a Bass diffusion over a 10-year horizon, anchored to the observed developing-economy trajectory. (2) Monetary policy / regulatory acceptability: the banking deliverable's inflation-control leg depends on issuance being DISCIPLINED. The expert (Exchange 3) identified the systemic coupling that governs this: as the anonymity parameter alpha rises, fee revenue becomes unattributable to any jurisdiction, so the only legible monetary signal left is the stake-voted fine-tuning band; stake-holders then have a dominant strategy to vote expansion to defend staking yield, and effective monetary policy becomes endogenous to stake concentration. The banking deliverable's inflation-control leg flips to 'non-acceptable' at the flip point alpha* where marginal fee-sink revenue falls below marginal staking-yield loss from non-expansion. This is modeled in the same script as the core mechanism (Layer A issuance + Layer B anonymity) so the two deliverables are evaluated on the SAME parameterization.

### Modeling Process

Adoption (Bass): F(t) = (1 - e^{-G t})/(1 + e^{-G t}), G = p + q, with innovation p = 0.05/yr and imitation q = 0.35/yr, chosen so the mid-adoption local slope is consistent with the observed ~17pp/4yr developing-economy gain. Unbanked adopters at horizon = F(T_h) * N0 with T_h = 10 yr, N0 = 1.4e9. Monetary-policy flip (the systemic coupling): let fee = fee_fraction (fee-sink, fraction of supply/yr), band = fine-tuning_band (stake-voted adjustment, fraction of supply/yr). The legible fee-sink that disciplines issuance is fee*(1 - alpha) (as alpha -> 1 fees become unattributable). The staking-yield loss from non-expansion is band*alpha. The flip occurs where fee*(1 - alpha) < band*alpha, giving the closed form alpha* = fee/(band + fee). Above alpha*, issuance becomes endogenous to stake concentration and the inflation-control leg flips to non-acceptable. The max-stake-cap does NOT appear in alpha*: it caps WHO can stake, not HOW MUCH issuance responds to alpha (the expert's exact point). Regulatory acceptability is a decreasing function of alpha (illicit-transaction susceptibility), so the banking deliverable requires alpha below both the regulatory-acceptability threshold and alpha*.

### Outcome Analysis

Adoption result: with p = 0.05, q = 0.35 over 10 years, F(10) = 0.964, i.e. ~1.35e9 of the 1.4e9 unbanked adults would adopt — the currency is a genuine financial-inclusion vehicle at the demand scale the Findex data support. Monetary-policy result: for the worked values fee = 0.002, band = 0.005, the flip point is alpha* = 0.002/(0.005 + 0.002) = 0.2857. The banking deliverable is 'acceptable' only for alpha < 0.2857; above that, issuance is endogenous to stake concentration and inflation control fails. Sensitivity (from the sweep): alpha* = fee/(band + fee) rises with the fee fraction (0.167 at fee = 0.001; 0.286 at 0.002; 0.444 at 0.004; 0.615 at 0.008) and falls with the band (0.5 at band = 0.002; 0.286 at 0.005; 0.167 at 0.01; 0.091 at 0.02). Limitations and biases: (1) the Bass parameters are anchored to the developing-economy average, not to a specific currency, so the 0.964 adoption fraction is a plausible-scale estimate, not a prediction; (2) the flip point assumes the fee-sink and band are the only two issuance legs and that stake-holders optimize yield; if a third leg (e.g. a hard cap on total expansion) exists, the flip shifts; (3) the banking deliverable and the security/anonymity deliverable are evaluated on the same alpha, which is what makes their conflict visible (next task). The key structural finding is that the banking deliverable's acceptability is BOUNDED ABOVE in alpha by a point that the core mechanism's fee and band parameters control — a higher fee or a tighter band widens the acceptable window.

### Problem

Analyze the security-versus-anonymity trade-off that governments cite (tax sheltering, illicit purchase) versus the user-side benefits (privacy, instant borderless transfer) for the unbanked population. Per the expert's deliverable mapping, this deliverable inherits ONLY the transaction/identity layer (Layer B) — anonymity is orthogonal to consensus — and must show, on the SAME parameterization as the other two deliverables, at what anonymity level the currency becomes regulatorily non-acceptable and whether the 'universal' claim is then only conditionally possible.

### Analysis

Anonymity is modeled as a continuous first-class parameter alpha in [0,1]: alpha = 0 is a fully transparent ledger (government-forensic-friendly, minimal illicit susceptibility) and alpha = 1 is full unlinkability (maximal privacy, maximal illicit susceptibility). Two opposing curves are evaluated against alpha: (i) unbanked-adoption benefit, which RISES with alpha because privacy is a driver for the unbanked (who distrust banks and governments) — normalized to alpha itself in [0,1]; (ii) regulatory acceptability, which FALLS with alpha because illicit-transaction susceptibility rises — normalized to (1 - alpha). The systemic failure mode from the expert (Exchange 3) couples Layer B's alpha to Layer A's issuance: the fee-sink <-> fine-tuning <-> alpha coupling fires at the flip point alpha* = fee/(band + fee), which is BELOW the alpha that maximizes unbanked adoption (near alpha = 1). This is the cross-layer fragility the final report must center: each component's local constraints hold (alpha in [0,1], keys valid, cap respected, honest coalition intact) but the coupling flips the banking deliverable's verdict while the adoption deliverable's verdict improves.

### Modeling Process

For each alpha in [0,1]: adoption_benefit(alpha) = alpha; regulatory_acceptability(alpha) = 1 - alpha; fee_legible(alpha) = fee_fraction * (1 - alpha); staking_yield_loss(alpha) = fine_tuning_band * alpha; issuance_endogenous_flip(alpha) = [fee_legible < staking_yield_loss]; flip point alpha* = fee_fraction/(fine_tuning_band + fee_fraction) (closed form). The feasible window for a currency that is BOTH regulatorily acceptable (banking deliverable) AND adoption-competitive (this deliverable) is alpha in [0, min(alpha_regulatory_threshold, alpha*)]. For the worked values (fee = 0.002, band = 0.005): alpha* = 0.2857. The adoption optimum is near alpha = 1, which is ABOVE alpha*; hence the two deliverables reach OPPOSITE verdicts on the same parameterization. The currency is 'universal' only CONDITIONALLY: it is feasible for alpha <= alpha* = 0.2857 (regulatory and inflation-control legs hold), but the privacy level the unbanked most want (alpha near 1) lies outside the feasible window.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the security/anonymity trade-off is a genuine tension, not a solvable one within the mechanism as specified. The flip point alpha* = 0.2857 (worked values) is the boundary: below it, fees discipline issuance and the currency is both regulatorily acceptable and inflation-stable; above it, fees stop disciplining, issuance becomes endogenous to stake concentration, and the banking deliverable flips to non-acceptable even though unbanked adoption (the user-side benefit) is at its best. Sensitivity: alpha* is controlled by the fee fraction (higher fee -> higher alpha*, wider window) and the fine-tuning band (tighter band -> higher alpha*, wider window); the max-stake-cap does NOT control it. This is the systemic fragility the expert named: the mechanism's two deliverables are in structural conflict over alpha, and 'universal' is only conditionally possible — feasible in the low-anonymity regime, not in the high-anonymity regime the unbanked prefer. Limitations and biases: (1) the linear adoption_benefit = alpha and regulatory_acceptability = 1 - alpha are monotone normalizations, not calibrated curves; the qualitative conclusion (opposite verdicts) is robust to any monotone choice where adoption rises and acceptability falls with alpha, but the exact feasible window width depends on their shape; (2) alpha* assumes the fee-sink and band are the only issuance legs and that stake-holders optimize yield; (3) the conflict is a property of the coupling, not of any single parameter — this is why a max-stake-cap (which fixes WHO can stake) cannot resolve it, and only the fee/band ratio (which fixes HOW MUCH issuance responds) can.
