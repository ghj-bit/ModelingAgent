### Problem

Subtask 1 — Characterize the currency system: what structural properties a system must have to be both universal and decentralized, what mechanism gives it internal security, and the economic conditions under which it is viable. Goal: determine whether a universal, decentralized, digital currency with internal (blockchain-style) security is possible. Scope: the currency's own consensus/security mechanism (Block A), its monetary-stability rule (Block B), and the co-existence condition against which feasibility is finally judged (Block C). This is the core modeling task.

### Analysis

Assumptions, per the expert consultation (Exchange 1): 'universal' is a usability/access property and 'decentralized' is a control-distribution property, kept distinct. The hard feasibility constraints are (i) permissionless participation (no membership gate — must work for the unbanked), (ii) no single point of failure in consensus, and (iii) settlement finality without a trusted intermediary. The tradeable parameters are validator identity, energy cost, throughput, and privacy level. The viability objective 'possible' is a conjunction: technical feasibility is the entry condition, economic stability (purchasing power / trust) is the survival condition, and co-existence with nation-based currencies is the binding feasibility criterion, with the first two as constraints on it (Exchange 1). Privacy/anonymity is a two-layer variable: a mechanism parameter (what the protocol reveals) and a regulatory overlay (what jurisdictions permit) that acts on the mechanism's outputs (Exchange 1). Method: a three-block structural model — Block A (consensus & security), Block B (monetary stability), Block C (co-existence & adoption) — with a single self-consistent feasibility criterion (Exchange 2, accepted). A proof-of-stake consensus is chosen over proof-of-work because the data give a ~100 TWh/yr PoW energy baseline (CBECI) as a first-order operating cost that a stake-based design can place an order of magnitude below, and because stake-based consensus meets the 'no single point of failure' hard constraint while treating the dominant loss mechanisms — stake dilution and validator capture — rather than assuming idealized validators. This is sound because it ties every structural choice to either a hard constraint from the consultation or an empirical anchor in the provided data, and it makes feasibility a conjunction of checkable margins rather than a single undifferentiated claim.

### Modeling Process

Block A (security). Let S be the value of stake at risk, denominated in the currency's own units, and c the cost of a 51% attack in real units. A purchasing-power dip d (fractional) lowers the real value of the staked currency. The security margin is m_A = S(1-d) - c. Block A holds iff m_A > 0 (real stake value at risk exceeds the attack payoff) and the three hard constraints (permissionless, no single point of failure, finality) are satisfied by the PoS design. Energy cost E is a tradeable parameter calibrated to be an order of magnitude below the PoW baseline E_PoW ~ 100 TWh/yr, i.e. E ~ 10 TWh/yr for a stake-based design. Block B (stability). Let inflation be the real drift of the currency's purchasing power and delta the allowed half-width of the purchasing-power band. The stability margin is m_B = delta - |inflation|. Block B holds iff m_B > 0, i.e. the currency's money supply (set by a fixed issuance schedule) keeps real value within the band. The dominant transformation is seigniorage vs. inflation: issuance is the source of potential inflation and the band is the constraint. Block C (co-existence, the binding criterion). Adoption of the currency by the unbanked follows Bass diffusion, F(t) = N[1 - exp(-p(1+q/p)t)]/[1 + (q/p)exp(-p(1+q/p)t)], with N = 1.4e9 unbanked adults (Findex). (p,q) are calibrated to the Findex rate anchor: requiring F(4yr) to reach a 17% share of the pool (the ~17 pp developing-economy account-ownership gain over 4 yr) with a standard imitation/innovation ratio q/p = 8, solved for p = 0.0290, q = 0.2322. Total adults 5.4e9 (Findex 76% ownership) is the wider market. The co-existence margin is m_C = (1 - share) - theta, where share = F(t)/N is the displaced share of the nation-currency's payment flow and theta the survivable threshold of existing seigniorage/monetary-policy capacity. Block C holds iff m_C > 0. Feasibility is the conjunction FEASIBLE = (m_A>0) AND (m_B>0) AND (m_C>0), with Block C the binding test. The regulatory overlay R in [0,1] acts on Block A's privacy outputs and is a defined sensitivity range.

### Outcome Analysis

At the center of the parameter space (baseline S=1.0, delta=0.05, theta=0.2, inflation=0.02), all three margins are positive: m_A=+0.40, m_B=+0.03, m_C=+0.216, so the currency is FEASIBLE, and Bass adoption reaches 58.4% of the unbanked pool by year 10 (92M by year 2.5, 229M by year 5, 403M by year 7.5). The conclusion is that a universal, decentralized, digital currency is possible in the sense of the binding co-existence criterion, provided the security budget carries slack. Limitations and biases: (1) the feasibility boundary sits at the attack-cost threshold — the S sweep shows the conclusion flipping from infeasible to feasible precisely at S = c (0.60 in normalized units), so a currency with a minimal security budget is fragile; (2) the cross-layer failure mode identified in Exchange 3 is the genuine breaker: at the S-minimal/delta-narrow corner a purchasing-power dip within the band (d=0.025 < delta=0.03) lowers the real value of stake-denominated security below the attack payoff, so m_B=+0.010 (Block B passes, stable) while m_A=-0.015 (Block A fails, insecure) — feasible=False with each block's own criterion passing, an undetectable-by-single-block failure; (3) the Bass diffusion inherits its rate anchor from a single 4-year Findex observation, so the adoption trajectory is as robust as that one data point; (4) the model is a feasibility test on margins, not a price or welfare level, so it says the currency can exist and co-exist but does not by itself size the welfare gain; (5) the R overlay is a range, not a point value, so the illicit-use/privacy equilibrium is characterized only at the boundary. The dominant bias is the cross-layer coupling: the security layer is denominated in the currency's own units, so the monetary layer can silently undermine the security layer, which is why the feasibility judgment must be made on the conjunction, never on any single block.

### Problem

Subtask 2 — Analyze how such a currency affects current banking systems and nation-based currencies. Goal: determine the consequences for existing banks (payment-flow displacement and lending capacity) and for nation-based currencies (seigniorage erosion and monetary-policy capacity), and how this bears on whether a universal currency is acceptable and can co-exist. Scope: the secondary deliverables, which the consultation (Exchange 2, accepted) maps to the outputs of Block C, the co-existence/adoption layer.

### Analysis

Assumption: the currency displaces a share of the nation-currency's payment flow roughly in proportion to its adoption share, and the displaced share erodes the existing currency's seigniorage and monetary-policy capacity. Method: the banking-system impact is Block C's displacement output — the share of banking-intermediated payment flow the currency replaces and the consequent effect on banks' intermediation/lending role. The nation-currency impact is the seigniorage and monetary-policy-capacity erosion, modeled as a function of adoption share via the co-existence margin m_C = (1 - share) - theta. The co-existence criterion (Exchange 1: the binding feasibility condition) is precisely that adoption does not push the surviving seigniorage/monetary-policy capacity below a survivable threshold theta, i.e. share <= 1 - theta. This is sound because it operationalizes 'affect' as a measurable displacement of the existing system's core function (payment settlement and seigniorage) rather than a qualitative narrative, and it ties the impact directly to the feasibility criterion so the impact analysis is not decoupled from the core model.

### Modeling Process

Let share(t) = F(t)/N from Block C's Bass diffusion (N = 1.4e9). The displaced share of the nation-currency's payment flow is share(t). The surviving share of the existing system's seigniorage/monetary-policy capacity is (1 - share(t)). The co-existence margin is m_C(t) = (1 - share(t)) - theta. The banking-system impact is the displacement of banking-intermediated payment flow, which grows with share(t) and corresponds to the erosion of banks' payment-settlement role and, secondarily, their intermediation capacity. The nation-currency impact is the seigniorage erosion, proportional to share(t). Co-existence holds at time t iff m_C(t) > 0, i.e. share(t) < 1 - theta. With theta = 0.2 (a survivable threshold requiring 80% of existing capacity to remain), co-existence is feasible as long as adoption stays below 80% of the addressable pool. The regulatory overlay R in [0,1] bounds the achievable privacy level and therefore the adoption incentive; the equilibrium at which the overlay and the mechanism's privacy parameter meet determines the achievable share.

### Outcome Analysis

At the baseline (theta=0.2), adoption reaches 58.4% of the unbanked pool by year 10, giving m_C = 0.416 - 0.2 = +0.216 > 0, so co-existence holds and the nation-based currency retains a survivable share of its seigniorage and monetary-policy capacity; the banking system retains the complementary 41.6% of payment flow. The impact is therefore significant but not displacing: the currency materially erodes the payment-settlement role of banks and the seigniorage of the nation-currency (by roughly the adoption share) but does not collapse them, because the addressable pool is the unbanked, who were not fully in the existing system to begin with. Limitations and biases: (1) the impact is proportional to adoption share by construction, so it inherits the Bass-diffusion bias from Subtask 1 (a single 4-year Findex anchor); (2) the displacement-to-seigniorage mapping is a proportional assumption — a currency that is adopted for savings rather than payments would displace less payment flow than the model assumes, biasing the impact upward; (3) the survivable threshold theta is a judgment parameter without a standard value, so the co-existence verdict is sensitive to it — the model reports the boundary share = 1 - theta explicitly rather than hiding it; (4) the model does not capture a coordinated policy response (a nation-currency issuing a competing central-bank digital currency), which could change the displacement share. The key conclusion: the currency's effect on banking and nation-currencies is large enough to be first-order but, on the co-existence criterion, compatible with their continued operation, so the 'universal' currency is acceptable in the sense of not displacing the existing monetary system.
