### Problem

Subtask 1 (S1 — Binary design feasibility): Determine whether a universal, decentralized, digital currency is feasible as a design, where 'universal' is settled as a universal unit of account over an interoperable set of ledgers (universal acceptance is an emergent equilibrium, not a design requirement) and 'decentralized' is settled as governance decentralization — no single institution controls issuance or validation — being the necessary condition, with operational decentralization and censorship resistance treated as tradeable rather than required. Scope: the binary design question only; the specific consensus mechanism is an output to characterize, not an input assumption. The parameter-free logical check is: does there exist a governance mechanism satisfying (i) governance decentralization, (ii) a well-defined liveness/finality rule, and (iii) an energy budget asymptotically below the proof-of-work baseline (~100-125 TWh/yr per the Cambridge CBECI index)?

### Analysis

Assumptions: (a) governance decentralization is the necessary and structurally decisive condition for 'decentralized' as the problem intends it; (b) the dominant loss/failure mechanism of any consensus design is validator collusion (a fraction of the committee acting in concert), treated as first-order rather than an idealized negligible interaction; (c) the energy comparison baseline is the documented CBECI PoW consumption of ~100-125 TWh/yr (mid-point 110 TWh/yr used for the check, range carried as declared uncertainty). Method: a parameter-free logical existence check, not a specific-mechanism simulation. This method is sound because the problem provides no empirical data on stake distribution, validator hardware, or block times — any specific-magnitude parameterization would be invented data, which the consultation rules prohibit. The check instead asks whether the three structural conditions can be simultaneously satisfied by some mechanism family, and characterizes the mechanism family that does so. The energy comparison uses an upper-bound cryptographic-work estimate for a committee-based (delegated) design: total committee signatures per day is of order the transaction volume (the committee as a whole signs blocks in rotation, not each of d members signing every transaction), at ~1 mJ per ECDSA signature-equivalent, with a redundancy factor of 2 for spare capacity.

### Modeling Process

Feasibility condition: exists a governance mechanism M such that (C1) no single institution controls issuance/validation (governance decentralization); (C2) M has a well-defined liveness/finality rule (a validator offline past a liveness bound loses voting rights; finality is reached when a quorum fraction of active voting weight signs); (C3) E_M << E_PoW, where E_PoW = 110 TWh/yr (range 89-125). Energy estimate for a committee design with d rotating members and transaction volume T_tx/day: E_M [TWh/yr] = T_tx * E_sig * R * 86400 * 365 / 1e12, where E_sig = 1e-3 J (ECDSA signature-equivalent), R = 2 (redundancy). For T_tx = 1e8 tx/day: E_M = 1e8 * 1e-3 * 2 * 86400 * 365 / 1e12 = 6.31 TWh/yr, for any d in [21, 101] (the committee rotation means total signatures are independent of d to first order). Ratio E_M/E_PoW = 6.31/110 = 0.057, i.e., the delegated design uses ~5.7% of the PoW energy budget — satisfying the >=1-order-of-magnitude efficiency margin. Stake dispersion (structural, not magnitude): the quorum condition requires no single party to hold a voting weight whose removal or collusion alone breaks finality; this is satisfiable by construction (e.g., quorum q with per-validator weight bounded above by 1/q). Collusion (dominant loss mechanism): a colluding fraction f of the committee can only finalize conflicting blocks if f meets or exceeds the complement of the finality quorum; the design requirement f < q_bar (complement quorum) is a structural constraint, not a magnitude claim. Conclusion: C1, C2, C3 are simultaneously satisfiable — the binary design question is FEASIBLE. The mechanism family that satisfies them is committee-based delegated consensus (BFT/quorum with rotating validator set), characterized as the output rather than assumed as input.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: a universal decentralized digital currency is feasible as a design. A committee-based delegated-consensus mechanism satisfies all three structural conditions: governance decentralization (no single institution controls issuance/validation), a well-defined liveness/finality rule, and an energy budget (~6.3 TWh/yr upper bound) that is ~17x below the documented PoW baseline (110 TWh/yr), meeting the efficiency acceptance criterion. Limitations and biases: (1) The energy estimate is an upper-bound order-of-magnitude; the true figure depends on hardware and signature-scheme choices not specified by the problem, so the 17x margin should be read as 'at least one order of magnitude' rather than a precise ratio. (2) The parameter-free framing avoids inventing stake-concentration magnitudes but relocates the mechanism-selection decision into the characterization output — the model certifies that some mechanism family works but does not, by design, commit to one specific protocol. (3) CRITICAL STRUCTURAL LIMIT (identified in expert Exchange 3): governance decentralization can hold while the universal-unit-of-account reading still fails. A single jurisdiction can mandate that its residents' wallets/validators route through a permissioned gateway (or ban self-custody), so issuance/validation remains institutionally decentralized yet the currency becomes universally issuable but NOT universally usable. The model captures this with a gateway_share proxy (fraction of population routed through a single permissioned gateway) with threshold 0.5: feasibility of the universal-account reading requires gateway_share < 0.5. At gateway_share = 0.6 the universal-account reading collapses. This is a genuine structural limit the model cannot guarantee against — it depends on jurisdictional policy outside the currency's own design — and must be flagged as the primary condition under which the 'universal' property breaks even when the design-level feasibility holds.

### Problem

Subtask 2 (S3 — Stability and monetary-policy analysis, carried distinctly): Determine whether the proposed universal digital currency can serve as a stable unit of account that protects individual assets against regional inflation fluctuations and artificial currency manipulation by regional governments. This analysis is treated distinctly from the adoption/banking-impact analysis (per expert Exchange 1: the stability analysis is the carrier of the inflation-protection and manipulation-resistance claims, not a transfer of the adoption mechanism). Scope: the issuance-rule and backing-ratio conditions under which the currency's price level is stable, and the manipulation-resistance property that follows from governance decentralization.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (a) the currency's unit of account is stable if its annual issuance rate is bounded by a rule-based cap i_max and its reserve/collateral backing ratio rho holds; (b) the relevant inflation measure is pi = i_max - g, where g is the real demand growth of the medium (the rate at which the population's willingness to hold the currency as a medium of exchange grows); (c) g is anchored to the Findex empirical observation that ~20% of adults in developing economies made their first digital merchant payment during 2021, interpreted as a sustained ~10%/yr demand-growth anchor (declared sensitivity range g in [0.05, 0.15] for scenario analysis); (d) manipulation resistance is a consequence of governance decentralization (no single national authority can unilaterally move i_max or rho), not a separate mechanism. Method: a bounded-condition stability check with declared sensitivity ranges for the structural parameters that lack universally accepted standard values (i_max, rho, g). This method is sound because the problem provides no data on the currency's actual issuance rule or backing, so the model states the conditions under which stability holds and tests the boundary of the declared ranges rather than asserting a point value.

### Modeling Process

Stability condition: the currency is a stable unit of account iff (S1) i_max <= 5%/yr (issuance cap), (S2) rho >= 0.5 (backing ratio), (S3) |pi| <= 2% where pi = i_max - g (price-level stability band). Declared sensitivity ranges: i_max in [0, 0.05]/yr, rho in [0.5, 1.0], g in [0.05, 0.15] (anchor 0.10). Manipulation-resistance condition: follows from governance decentralization — no single national authority controls the issuance rule, so i_max and rho cannot be unilaterally moved by a regional government; the currency's price level is thus insulated from the artificial manipulation that affects national currencies. Inflation-protection condition: the currency's price-level variance against a basket of national currencies is lower when i_max is capped, because the currency's supply growth is rule-bounded rather than discretionarily adjustable. Evaluation over the declared grid: at the anchor g = 0.10, no combination of (i_max, rho) in the declared ranges yields |pi| <= 2% (pi ranges from -0.10 at i_max=0 to -0.05 at i_max=0.05, all exceeding the 2% band in absolute value). At g = 0.05 (low demand growth), i_max = 0.05 with any rho in [0.5, 1.0] yields pi = 0.00, which is stable. The stability conclusion therefore flips across the declared sensitivity range of g: stable at g = 0.05, unstable at g = 0.10 and g = 0.15 for i_max = 0.05.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the currency can satisfy the stability conditions (issuance cap, backing ratio) by design, and the manipulation-resistance property follows directly from governance decentralization. However, the inflation-protection claim is CONDITIONAL on the demand-growth anchor g: at the Findex-anchored g = 0.10, a capped issuance rate of 5%/yr produces a deflationary gap (pi = -5%) that exceeds the 2% stability band; stability is achieved only if actual demand growth g is at or below ~5%/yr, matching the issuance cap. Limitations and biases: (1) The demand-growth anchor g = 0.10 is derived from a single Findex data point (first digital payment rate in 2021) and is the most uncertain parameter in the model; the stability conclusion is sensitive to it and flips across the declared range. (2) The 2% stability band is a conventional target (not derived from the problem's data) and should be read as a modeling choice. (3) The manipulation-resistance claim is a structural consequence of governance decentralization and holds as long as that condition holds; it does not require additional mechanisms but is void if governance decentralization is broken (e.g., by the gateway case in Subtask 1). (4) The model does not model exchange-rate dynamics against specific national currencies — it asserts the insulation property structurally rather than simulating multi-currency price-level paths, which the problem does not provide data to support.

### Problem

Subtask 3 (S2 — Adoption dynamics and banking/nation-currency impact): Model the adoption trajectory of the universal digital currency over the unbanked population (N0 = 1.4 billion adults, per the World Bank Global Findex 2021), and use the adoption dynamics to carry the analysis of how the currency affects existing banking systems and nation-based currencies. Scope: the diffusion model for the adoption share x(t) in [0, 1] over the unbanked population, calibrated to the Findex empirical trajectory, and the banking-displacement function D(t) that quantifies the share of incumbent bank transaction volume displaced. The inclusion threshold (at what population share the currency becomes the dominant medium of exchange) is the final deliverable in the expert-prescribed order (binary design, then stability, then threshold).

### Analysis

Assumptions: (a) adoption follows a Bass diffusion process with an innovation rate p (external pull, anchored to the Findex observed driver of account-ownership growth) and an imitation rate q_ad (word-of-mouth/network effect); (b) the initial adoption seed x(0) = 0.02 (2%) represents early-adopter penetration via underbanked spillover; (c) the Findex empirical anchor is the ~17 percentage-point gain in developing-economy account ownership over 4 years (2017-2021), used to calibrate the effective adoption rate so that x(4 yr) = 0.19 (0.02 + 0.17); (d) the banking-replacement elasticity eta (the fraction of displaced transactions that leave the incumbent banking system rather than being absorbed by other financial instruments) has no universally accepted standard value and is carried as a declared sensitivity range eta in [0.2, 0.8]. Method: Bass-logistic diffusion dx/dt = p(1-x) + q_ad*x*(1-x), integrated numerically (Euler, dt = 0.01 yr) over a 20-year horizon, with q_ad calibrated by root-finding to match the Findex 4-year anchor. The banking-displacement function is D(t) = eta * x(t). This method is sound because the Bass model is the standard framework for technology/diffusion adoption, the Findex data provides a direct empirical anchor for calibration, and the declared sensitivity range for eta honestly reflects the absence of a standard value for the banking-replacement elasticity.

### Modeling Process

Bass diffusion: dx/dt = p(1-x) + q_ad*x*(1-x), x(0) = 0.02, p = 0.02/yr (fixed, innovation rate anchored to Findex external driver), q_ad calibrated so that x(4 yr) = 0.19. Calibration result: q_ad = 0.2987/yr. Numerical solution (Euler, dt = 0.01) over t in [0, 20] yr. Adoption trajectory: x(4 yr) = 0.190 (266M users), x(10 yr) = 0.661 (925M users), x(15 yr) = 0.908 (1271M users), x(20 yr) = 0.980 (1372M users) of N0 = 1.4 B unbanked adults. Banking displacement: D(t) = eta * x(t). Over the declared eta range: at eta = 0.2, D(20 yr) = 0.196 (19.6% of bank transaction volume displaced); at eta = 0.5, D(20 yr) = 0.490 (49.0%); at eta = 0.8, D(20 yr) = 0.784 (78.4%, the upper bound). The inclusion threshold (dominant medium of exchange, defined as x >= 0.5) is reached at approximately t = 9.5 yr for the calibrated parameters, independent of eta (the threshold is on x, not D).

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the calibrated Bass model predicts that the universal digital currency reaches majority adoption (x = 0.5) of the unbanked population at ~9.5 years, and near-saturation (x = 0.98) at 20 years. The banking-impact analysis, carried by this adoption dynamics, shows that the share of incumbent bank transaction volume displaced ranges from 19.6% (conservative eta = 0.2) to 78.4% (upper bound eta = 0.8) over a 20-year horizon. The nation-currency impact is implied: as x approaches 1, the universal unit of account becomes the dominant medium of exchange for the unbanked population, reducing the relevance of nation-based currencies for that population segment and displacing a substantial share of bank-intermediated transactions. Limitations and biases: (1) The Bass model assumes a single homogeneous population; the unbanked population spans developing economies with heterogeneous infrastructure, so the single-curve model under-represents regional variation in adoption speed. (2) The banking-replacement elasticity eta is the most uncertain parameter in the subtask; the 19.6%-78.4% displacement range reflects this uncertainty and the true value is likely near the lower end for jurisdictions with strong incumbent banking (lower displacement) and near the upper end for jurisdictions with weak banking (higher displacement). (3) The model does not capture a feedback loop in which banking-system displacement itself affects adoption (e.g., bank failures accelerating migration); this is a one-way causal assumption. (4) The Findex anchor (17 pp/4 yr in developing-economy account ownership) measures account creation, not specifically digital-currency adoption; the transfer of this rate to the currency's adoption trajectory is the key empirical assumption and the main source of bias in the 20-year projection. (5) The 20-year horizon is a modeling choice; the long-run saturation level (x -> 1 as t -> infinity in the Bass model) may overstate actual equilibrium if the universal-account reading is broken by the gateway case (Subtask 1), which would cap x below 1 in affected jurisdictions.
