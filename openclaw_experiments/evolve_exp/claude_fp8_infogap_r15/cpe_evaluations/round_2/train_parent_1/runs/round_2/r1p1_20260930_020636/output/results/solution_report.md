### Problem

Subtask 1 — School selection. Identify the set of candidate schools for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100M/yr (5-yr, $500M total) investment that demonstrate the greatest potential for effective use of private funding to improve undergraduate student performance, without duplicating the focus of other large grantors. Scope: a defensible subset of the IPEDS candidate list (2,977 institutions) and the College Scorecard most-recent-cohort data (7,804 rows), merged on UNITID.

### Analysis

Assumptions. (a) The scorecard's UGDS (undergraduate headcount) and PCTPELL (Pell share) identify the target population: n_i = UGDS_i * PCTPELL_i, the Pell-eligible students whose outcomes a completion/earnings intervention addresses. (b) The outcome fields md_earn_wne_p10 (median earnings 10 yrs after entry) and gt_25k_p6 (share earning >$25k 6 yrs after entry) are cross-sectional cohort outcomes, not panels; no causal effect size is identifiable from them, so the outcome-shift magnitude is a declared adoption rate eta, not an estimated one (per the expert's Exchange-1 direction to declare and bound such gaps rather than borrow numbers). (c) 'Demonstrated potential for effective use of funding' is decomposed into three measurable components: need (n_i), headroom (the gap between the school's median earnings and the national 75th percentile, the room a grant has to lift outcomes), and demonstrated effectiveness (RET_FT4 * gt_25k_p6, the school's own record of converting enrollment into degrees and into earnings above the $25k threshold). (d) 'Not duplicating other grantors' is operationalized as a headroom requirement: a school already at or above the earnings frontier has no room for a marginal grant to add value, so it is excluded regardless of need. Method: min-max/median normalization of the three components, a hard gate requiring positive headroom AND effectiveness >= 0.5 * median effectiveness, then ranking by intrinsic ROI. This is sound because it grounds every input in a data field, isolates the one genuinely assumed parameter (eta) for sensitivity analysis, and uses the headroom gate to implement the no-duplication constraint in a falsifiable way.

### Modeling Process

Universe: IPEDS list merged with scorecard on UNITID -> 2,936 rows. Cleaning: coerce 'PrivacySuppressed' strings to NaN; drop schools with UGDS < 200 or missing md_earn_wne_p10 / gt_25k_p6 -> 2,635. Per school i: n_i = UGDS_i * PCTPELL_i; E75 = 75th percentile of md_earn_wne_p10 across the cleaned universe (= $42,750); headroom_i = max(0, E75 - md_earn_wne_p10_i); effectiveness_i = RET_FT4_i * gt_25k_p6_i (RET_FT4 imputed by C150_4_POOLED_SUPP where missing). Gate: headroom_i > 0 AND effectiveness_i >= 0.5 * median(effectiveness) -> 970 eligible. Intrinsic ROI uses a uniform reference grant of $2.5M/yr (the $100M/40-schools average) so the ranking is independent of the final allocation: ROI_i = [eta * headroom_i * n_i * 5 * (1 + 0.6)] / ($2.5M * 5). Selection: the top 40 gated schools by ROI, funded all five years.

### Outcome Analysis

970 of 2,635 schools pass the gate; the top 40 are selected. The list is dominated by mid-size public and HBCU/flagship-serving community colleges and regional universities with high Pell shares and large earnings headroom: (1) Valencia College FL, (2) Liberty University VA, (3) Palm Beach State College FL, (4) Keiser University FL, (5) South Texas College TX, (6) Florida State College at Jacksonville FL, (7) Seminole State College of Florida FL, (8) UT Brownsville TX, (9) Indian River State College FL, (10) Alabama State University AL, (11) Eastern Florida State College FL, (12) University of Akron OH, plus 28 more (ranks 13-40 include Middle Tennessee State, Jackson State, Western Governors, UNM, New Mexico State, Texas Southern, CUNY City Tech, Kent State, NC A&T, Boise State, Grambling State, Arkansas State, among others). Mean intrinsic ROI 10.4 (range 6.7-26.5); the 40 schools collectively serve ~280,000 Pell-eligible students. Limitations/bias: the gate excludes large, well-funded frontier institutions (e.g., research universities already above the earnings 75th pctile) by construction — this is the no-duplication constraint, but it also means the list skews to lower-tier institutions whose absolute outcome levels are modest; the 0.5*median effectiveness cutoff is arbitrary and the top-40 cutoff on N is a budget decision, not a statistical one. The counterexample identified in consultation (a high-Pell, zero-headroom frontier school) is excluded by the headroom>0 gate, which is verified: no selected school has headroom <= 0.

### Problem

Subtask 2 — Investment amount per school and funding duration. Allocate the $100,000,000 annual budget across the 40 selected schools and specify the time duration over which each school's money is provided, to maximize the likelihood of a strong positive effect on student performance.

### Analysis

Assumptions. (a) Per the expert's Exchange-1 finding, the only defensible ROI denominator the data support is the grant amount itself, so the allocation is expressed in dollars per school with no external cost baseline. (b) A multi-year (5-yr) commitment is required to change cohort outcomes that materialize 6-10 years after entry; a one-year pulse cannot do so, so the full 5-year window is used, with the entire budget committed in each of the 5 years (no front-loading, because the outcome lag is long and flat and there is no data to favor an early tranche). (c) Bounds: a $5M/yr per-school cap (a grant larger than this at a single mid-size school is an institutional transformation, not a targeted intervention, and would re-concentrate the foundation's risk) and a $2M/yr floor (below this the grant is too small to fund a meaningful advising/completion program). Method: proportional-to-benefit allocation with iterative cap/floor enforcement and residual redistribution. This is sound because it is a constrained water-filling that respects the budget exactly, is monotone in the evidence (benefit), and the cap/floor keep every award in the plausible intervention band.

### Modeling Process

Let b_i = benefit_5yr_i = eta * headroom_i * n_i * 5 * (1+0.6) be the 5-yr benefit and G the annual budget $100M. Initialize annual grant g_i = b_i / sum(b) * G. Iterate: set g_i = CAP for g_i > $5M, g_i = FLOOR for g_i < $2M, and redistribute the residual (G - sum g) among the unconstrained schools in proportion to their b_i, until the cap/floor set stabilizes (converges in <= 20 iterations). Five-year grant to school i = 5 * g_i. Duration = 5 consecutive years, full amount each year, no rotation.

### Outcome Analysis

The allocation converges to exactly $100M/yr: three schools at the $5M/yr cap (Valencia College, Liberty University, Palm Beach State College — the three largest-benefit schools), twenty-two schools at the $2M/yr floor, and fifteen schools graduated between $2.0M and $4.6M/yr in proportion to their benefit (the residual after the three caps is redistributed to the next-highest-benefit uncapped schools). Five-year grants: $25M for each capped school, $10M for each floor school, $10.8M-$22.8M for the graduated schools; total $500M over five years. Duration: 5 consecutive years, full amount each year, no rotation. Robustness (sweep): varying N over 20/40/60 changes mean intrinsic ROI from 13.4 to 8.9 (fewer schools concentrate funds and raise per-dollar impact but reduce total Pell students reached from 170k to 370k); varying eta over 0.10/0.25/0.40 scales ROI linearly (4.1/10.4/16.6) without changing the ranking or the allocation, confirming the selection is insensitive to the declared adoption rate. Limitation: the cap/floor make the allocation piecewise — small changes in a school's benefit near a bound flip its grant discretely, so the marginal dollar is not smoothly allocated; a true continuous optimum would spread more evenly, but the bounds are a deliberate risk-control choice.

### Problem

Subtask 3 — Return on Investment (ROI) for a charitable educational funder. Define and compute an ROI appropriate for the Goodgrant Foundation, and report the estimated ROI per funded school.

### Analysis

Assumptions. (a) For a charity, 'return' is the improvement in student outcomes attributable to the grant, not financial profit; the natural outcome is the earnings trajectory of the Pell-eligible students the grant serves. (b) The $25k 6-yr threshold (gt_25k_p6) marks 'did the school add any earnings value at all' (the DOE's chosen benchmark ~ the high-school-graduate earnings level), and md_earn_wne_p10 is the primary outcome 10 yrs after entry (per the external data, whose ~$46k national 10-yr mean is a sanity reference). (c) Per the expert, the denominator is the grant amount itself (the data carry no per-student intervention cost). (d) The adoption rate eta = 0.25 is declared: a multi-year grant closes 25% of the school's earnings headroom over the funding window. (e) A 4-yr tail (0.6x the 5-yr benefit) is added because the cohort outcomes are measured 6-10 yrs after entry, so part of the benefit accrues after the grant ends. Method: a benefit-per-dollar-of-grant ratio. This is sound because it uses only data-identifiable quantities for the numerator's scale (headroom, Pell headcount) and isolates the single assumed conversion parameter (eta) and the tail, both swept.

### Modeling Process

ROI_i = Benefit_i / Investment_i. Benefit_i = eta * headroom_i * n_i * T * (1 + tau), where headroom_i = max(0, E75 - md_earn_wne_p10_i) in dollars (E75 = $42,750, the 75th percentile of md_earn_wne_p10), n_i = UGDS_i * PCTPELL_i, T = 5 funding years, tau = 0.6 (4-yr tail as a fraction of the 5-yr benefit), eta = 0.25. Investment_i = the 5-yr grant to school i (denominator per expert). For the ranking statistic (so it does not depend on the final allocation), Investment_i is a uniform $2.5M/yr * 5 = $12.5M reference grant; ROI_i(rank) = eta * headroom_i * n_i * 5 * 1.6 / $12.5M. The reported per-school ROI in the list uses this reference; the allocation-level ROI (benefit / actual 5-yr grant) is reported in the allocation subtask.

### Outcome Analysis

Base-case (eta=0.25, tau=0.6) per-school ROIs range 6.7 to 26.5, mean 10.4, across the 40 funded schools — i.e., each $1M of 5-yr grant is associated with an estimated $6.7M-$26.5M of 5-yr (plus tail) earnings benefit to the served Pell-eligible students, under the declared 25% adoption of headroom. Highest: Valencia College 26.5, Liberty 24.4, Palm Beach State 22.0. Sanity check: the implied per-Pell-student benefit at the mean school is eta * headroom * 5 * 1.6 ~ 0.25 * $10,278 * 8 ~ $20.6k over 9 years ~ $2.3k/yr, ~ 4% of the 2015 median household income ($56,516) — a modest, defensible 'strong positive effect' consistent with the external reference levels. Limitations/bias: ROI is entirely driven by the declared eta (sweep 0.10-0.40 scales it 4.1-16.6 with no rank change) and by treating cross-sectional headroom as if it were a causal, grant-inducible gain — the scorecard has no pre/post panel, so ROI is a potential-return index, not a measured causal effect; the 0.6 tail and the E75 (75th pctile) headroom anchor are assumptions that a more conservative anchor (e.g., 50th pctile) would shrink. These are the model's principal biases and are the ones a future panel study should target.
