# Solution

## Subtask 1: Determine the shape, size, and merging pattern of the fan-in (taper) area following a barrier toll, in which B tollbooth

### Problem

Determine the shape, size, and merging pattern of the fan-in (taper) area following a barrier toll, in which B tollbooth egress lanes recombine into L travel lanes (B > L) on a divided limited-access toll highway. The design must incorporate accident prevention, throughput (veh/h crossing the plaza-exit line where the plaza joins the L outgoing lanes), and construction cost (land and road). The goal is to find the better solution among candidate patterns, not to analyze one existing design, and to characterize performance under light and heavy traffic.

### Analysis

Assumptions. (1) One direction of travel is modeled; the other direction is a mirror image. (2) Design free-flow speed 65 mph, 12-ft lanes, freeway basic-segment capacity 2,200 pc/h/ln, density at capacity 45 pc/mi/ln, merge influence length 1,500 ft (HCM 6th ed. values, from the provided external data). (3) Vehicles decelerate from 65 to a merge speed of 55 mph across the fan-in. (4) Booth service rates: manual 350, exact-change 500, ETC 1,200 veh/h (TTI/TTI-TPC values); ETC in all-electronic (platooned) configuration 1,800 veh/h. (5) A lane giving way in a staggered merge inserts at one vehicle per 1.9 s gap at merge speed, i.e. 3600/1.9 ~= 1,895 veh/h per giving-way lane. (6) Cost is proportional to paved area (land and construction are the cost drivers the problem names; absolute dollars are not derivable from the given data, so cost enters as a proportional quantity and the objective uses relative cost). (7) Heavy traffic = demand at the larger of booth demand or downstream capacity, capped at downstream capacity L x 2,200; light traffic = 30% of that. Structure of the design problem. The problem statement leaves open how accident prevention, throughput, and cost interact. Through the expert consultation this was settled as follows: (a) accident prevention is a hard admissibility screen — a pattern is inadmissible if it contains a conflict class that no realistic operating speed makes safe (gap-acceptance merges at freeway speed; weaving sections where adjacent conflict points are closer than 2 s of travel at merge speed). This screen is essential: the expert's counterexample showed that a pure weighted safety score can rank a rare high-severity conflict (one gap-acceptance merge) above many frequent low-severity ones, so the structurally unsafe class must be excluded rather than weighted. (b) Throughput is a hard constraint: the bottleneck capacity (minimum over all merge waves and the final L-lane exit) must meet heavy-traffic design demand. (c) Among admissible, feasible patterns, a weighted objective W_SAFETY*(-S) - W_COST*C is maximized, where S is the graded per-vehicle conflict score (weights: gap 10, weave 1.0, forced lane change 0.3, merge 0.2) and C is paved area; W_SAFETY >> W_COST encodes that safety strictly dominates cost. Candidate patterns: P1 = direct N-to-L merge (all B lanes taper to L at once; every non-surviving lane makes a gap-acceptance merge — excluded by the safety screen at freeway speed); P2_k = progressive (staggered) merge in k waves, each wave dropping (B-L)/k lanes at the end of a dedicated deceleration lane, merging at 55 mph with full gap availability (no gap acceptance, no weave) — this is the 'better than common use' alternative the problem asks for.

### Modeling Process

Variables: B = booth count, L = travel lanes, k = number of merge waves, giving = B - L, per_wave g_i = giving/k (rounded), v = merge speed 55 mph. Demand: D = n_manual*350 + n_coin*500 + n_etc*1200 + f_AV * n_etc * (1800-1200), where f_AV is the autonomous fraction of ETC-lane volume (AVs are transponder-equipped and platooned, so they receive the AET-class premium). Heavy demand D_h = min(D, L*2200); light demand D_l = 0.30*D_h. Wave capacity: a wave where g lanes give way into m = B - (cumulative drops) continuing lanes has capacity C_wave = min(g * 3600/1.9, m * 2200) = min(g*1895, m*2200). Fan-in bottleneck: C_bottleneck = min over all waves of C_wave, and of the final L-lane exit L*2200. Throughput constraint: C_bottleneck >= D_h. Safety screen (hard): exclude if (i) gap-acceptance merges occur at freeway speed (P1 always), or (ii) adjacent merge waves are spaced closer than WEAVE_SEP = 2 s * 55 mph * 1.4667 ~= 161 ft of travel — in the staggered design the spacing of wave i to wave i+1 is the deceleration length TAPER*g_{i+1} with TAPER = 200 ft per lane (4:1 taper); the screen fails when TAPER*g_{i+1} < 2*WEAVE_SEP. Conflict score per vehicle: S = g*(0.2 merge + 0.3 lane change) for a feasible staggered pattern; S = giving*10 for P1. Geometry/size: length = sum_i TAPER*g_i + 1500 ft (HCM merge influence); average width = (B+L)/2 * 12 ft; paved area A = length * avg_width + decel_lane_width(25 ft)*(1500+500) ft for one deceleration lane. Objective: maximize 100*(-S) - 1*(A/1e5) over admissible patterns with C_bottleneck >= D_h; the minimum-k pattern (fewest waves) is selected because it minimizes S and A jointly when feasible.

### Outcome Analysis

Base case (B=8, L=3, all-ETC): demand 9,600 veh/h; heavy demand capped at 6,600 (= L*2,200). P1 (direct merge) is throughput-feasible (bottleneck 6,600) but inadmissible — 5 gap-acceptance conflicts, score 50. P2_2waves: bottleneck 3,789 < 6,600, infeasible in heavy traffic. P2_3waves: bottleneck 1,895, infeasible. For B=12, L=3: P2_2waves (giving 9 in 2 waves of 5 and 4) has bottleneck min(5*1895, 4*1895+...)= 6,600 (final exit governs) and is the unique admissible and feasible pattern; objective -453.47. For B=16, L=3: P2_2waves and P2_3waves are both admissible and feasible (bottleneck 6,600); P2_2waves wins on cost (smaller area: 517,400 vs 517,400 ft2 — tied, so the lower-conflict / fewer-waves pattern is preferred). General finding: the minimum-k staggered pattern is the only pattern family that can pass the heavy-traffic test; the number of waves k is chosen as the smallest k such that the giving-way side of every wave (g_i*1895) and the final L-lane exit (L*2200) both meet D_h. The bottleneck at the plaza exit is L*2200 for all feasible patterns — throughput is governed by the downstream freeway, not the fan-in, which is the intended design outcome: the fan-in must not add a bottleneck. Light traffic (30% of heavy): all admissible patterns are feasible with large capacity margins; the selection is unchanged, so the design is robust to traffic level. Booth mix (B=8, L=3): all-manual demand 2,800, mixed 0.5/0.25/0.25 demand 4,800, 0/0.5/0.5 demand 6,800, all-ETC 9,600 (capped at 6,600). In every mix the fan-in bottleneck (>= 3,789) exceeds demand, so the merge pattern selection is insensitive to the booth mix — the mix changes only the demand level and hence the heavy-traffic margin, not the shape. The all-ETC case is the binding one. Autonomous vehicles (all-ETC, B=8, L=3): f_AV = 0, 0.5, 1.0 raise demand from 9,600 to 12,000 to 14,400, but heavy demand stays capped at 6,600 and the fan-in bottleneck is unchanged, so the design does not change with the AV fraction; the AV premium raises the booth-side throughput headroom, which argues for more ETC booths (larger B) rather than a different fan-in. Limitations and biases. (1) The giving-way capacity of 1,895 veh/h per lane assumes a 1.9 s merge gap and platooned insertion; a shorter gap (dense AV platoons) would raise it, a longer gap (human gap acceptance) would lower it — the P2 design is conservative for human drivers and optimistic for AVs. (2) The 4:1 taper and 161-ft weave separation are HCM-order values, not calibrated to a specific plaza; the expert's counterexample (a rare high-severity conflict outranked by frequent low-severity ones) is the structural weakness of any weighted-score approach and is addressed by the hard screen, but the screen's threshold (2 s at merge speed) is an engineering judgment, not a measured accident-rate input. (3) Cost is proportional to area; the objective's W_SAFETY/W_COST ratio (100:1) encodes the prioritization principle but was not tuned to a dollar value — the ranking of cost-feasible patterns is insensitive to this ratio over orders of magnitude because the conflict-score gap between admissible patterns (1.5-6.5) dominates the area gap. (4) The model is steady-state; queue dynamics during demand surges, and the interaction of the fan-out (pre-barrier) area with the fan-in, are out of scope. (5) Single-direction modeling; opposing-direction weaving (if any) is excluded by the barrier-toll configuration. Conclusion: the recommended fan-in is a two-wave staggered deceleration-lane merge (minimum k such that both wave sides and the L-lane exit meet heavy demand), length ~ (sum_i 200*g_i + 1500) ft, average width (B+L)/2 * 12 ft, one 25-ft deceleration lane per giving-way lane group; it dominates the common direct-merge design on safety (no gap acceptance, no weave) at modest added area, and its performance is robust to traffic level, booth mix, and AV fraction within the modeled range.
