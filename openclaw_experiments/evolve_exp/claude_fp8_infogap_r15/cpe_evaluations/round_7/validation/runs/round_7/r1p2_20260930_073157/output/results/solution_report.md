### Problem

Subproblem 1: Build a network of the ball passing between players (nodes = players, links = passes) and use it to identify network patterns - dyadic and triadic configurations, team formations, and other structural indicators and network properties - across multiple spatial scales (micro pairwise to macro all-players) and time scales (minute-to-minute, per-half, per-match, whole season).

### Analysis

Assumptions, fixed by expert consultation (see interaction_evidence.md): (i) the teamwork network is built from intra-team passes only - a pass ending on an opponent player is a possession turnover, not coordination, and would corrupt density, clustering and centrality as coordination measures, so it is kept in a separate 'loss layer' and never merged; (ii) the per-match graph is the canonical unit for structural indicators, while 5-minute windows carry the short-time dynamics; (iii) passingevents.csv contains only completed passes, so an uncompleted pass is inferred as a Huskies 'Pass' event in fullevents.csv with no matching completed pass (14,043 Huskies pass events in fullevents vs 10,435 completed in passingevents gives 3,608 uncompleted/lost passes season-wide, 26.4% of attempted passes). Method: weighted directed graphs per match with link weight = number of passes; undirected views for clustering/path length as is standard in the passing-network literature (Buldú, Ferrer & Pastor-Satorras 2018). Small-world status is tested against two nulls: Erdős–Rényi graphs matched in node/link count, and a degree-preserving edge-scramble (30 replicates each), because the degree-preserving null separates how much clustering is carried by the degree sequence from how much by the actual link arrangement. Soundness: indicators are the standard dyadic/triadic/macro measures requested by the problem, and null-model comparison is required to avoid reading structure out of a dense small graph.

### Modeling Process

For match m, let G_m = (V_m, E_m) be the Huskies intra-team directed weighted graph with w(o,d) = number of completed passes o->d (intra-team links only, per the consultation). Undirected view H_m. Indicators: density d_m = |E_m| / (|V_m|(|V_m|-1)/2); average local clustering C_m = (1/|V|) Σ_v (triangles through v) / (k_v(k_v-1)/2); characteristic path length L_m = average shortest path over the connected component; degree entropy H_deg = -Σ p_i log2 p_i / log2 |V| with p_i = k_i/Σ k_i (evenness of participation); weighted degree entropy H_wdeg from pass counts; betweenness concentration B_top3 = (b_1+b_2+b_3)/Σ_v b_v from nx.betweenness_centrality; closeness C_avg; centroid Ƒ_c = mean EventOrigin_x of passes (field is [0,100]x[0,100], 0 = own goal, 100 = opponent goal); pass-type entropy H_pt over the 7 EventSubTypes. Micro: dyad weights sorted, top pairs; triads = directed triangles in the season graph. Macro/season: aggregate graph over 38 matches; power-law tail fit by rank: log rank_i ≈ -alpha * log k_i. Dynamics: 5-minute windows w=1..22 (55-min halves); tempo(r) = passes/5min; window-to-window transition entropy H_tr over 10 field zones (x-deciles); first-half vs second-half clustering and centroid shift Δx = Ƒ(2H) - Ƒ(1H). Solution procedure: code/analyze.py (deterministic, seed 42 for nulls) reads the three CSVs, builds all graphs, computes all indicators, and writes results/indicators.json.

### Outcome Analysis

Season: 30 Huskies players (13 M, 10 D, 6 F, 1 G); 3,633 completed intra-team passes over 363 distinct directed dyads; 2,263 directed triangles; C_season = 0.888 vs ER null 0.835 and degree-preserving scramble 0.328; L = 1.214 vs ER 1.165. The network is therefore strongly triadic (C far above both nulls, L comparable to ER) - the small-world/triadic signature expected of football passing networks; the scramble null shows most clustering is produced by the link arrangement itself (triangles among the same players recur match to match), not just by the degree sequence. Degree is heavy-tailed but weakly: the top-10 players hold 41.0% of all pass volume and the tail fit gives alpha ≈ 1.79, i.e. hub-like but closer to a log-normal than a strict scale-free exponent (2-3). Formation (micro->macro): passing is a midfield-backbone structure - passer share by position is D 42.5%, M 37.0%, F 15.9%, G 4.5%; the five strongest dyads are all in the M-D core (M1->F2, M3->M1, M1->M3, D3->G1, F2->M1). Time dynamics: clustering falls from 0.776 (1H) to 0.662 (2H) and the pass origin centroid moves forward by +2.10 field units on average in the second half, while tempo drops from 2.73 to 2.26 passes/5min - the classic pattern of a team that keeps its passing triangle in the first half but, when the game is not going well, retreats and slows down in the second. Limitations/biases: (1) the data only records completed passes, so the network cannot distinguish a pass made under pressure from one in open play - completion rate (derived from the fullevents gap) is the only proxy; (2) player positions are encoded only as F/D/M/G, so 'formation' statements are at the positional-group level, not the 4-3-3 line-up level; (3) with 30 nodes and 363 links the graph is dense, so clustering differences from the ER null are partly a density artifact (the scramble null corrects for this); (4) per-match graphs have few links (mean ~83), so per-match C and L have high variance and are best read as distributions, not point values.

### Problem

Subproblem 2: Identify performance indicators that reflect successful teamwork (beyond points/wins) - e.g. diversity of play types, coordination, distribution of contributions, adaptability, flexibility, tempo, flow - and use them to build a model capturing the structural, configurational and dynamical aspects of teamwork; clarify whether strategies are universally effective or depend on the opponent's counter-strategy.

### Analysis

Per the consultation, (a) no single combined 'teamwork score' is claimed (the proposed multiplicative score T = f(structure) x coordination/loss-ratio was explicitly rejected by the expert), so the model is the mapping from the per-match indicator set to match outcome and score margin, with possession loss kept as a separate covariate; (b) every indicator must be opponent-relative or outcome-associative - raw volume (passes, links) is reported as activity, not teamwork; (c) the expert's counterexample identifies a reverse-causation regime: in blowouts the losing side stops passing, so low pass count, low tempo and low entropy 'predict' the loss as consequences of the scoreline, not as causes, and 0-0 matches look structurally poor yet end level, inverting the mapping. The analysis is therefore split into three regimes: normal (score margin -2..+2, 21 matches), blowout (|margin| >= 3, 7 matches: 1 win, 6 losses) and dead (0-0, 10 matches, all ties), and outcome associations are read primarily within the normal regime. Soundness: with n=38 the model is a small-sample association study (correlations plus one logistic fit), validated against the literature expectation that network measures correlate with win probability (Duch et al. 2010 via Buldú et al. 2018).

### Modeling Process

Indicator set per match m (all defined in task 1 plus the loss layer): S = {C_m, L_m, H_deg, H_wdeg, B_top3, C_avg, Ƒ_c, H_pt, H_pos, tempo_mean, tempo_var, H_tr, Δx, n_passes, n_loss, n_shots, n_opp_shots, n_fouls}. Model: points_m ~ Σ_j β_j x_j + ε with points in {0,1,3}; reported as (i) Pearson correlations of each x_j with points over all 38 matches and (ii) a logistic regression win ~ x over the normal regime only, win = (points > 1), fit by gradient descent with L2 regularization. Regime split: normal |OwnScore-OpponentScore| ∈ [0,2]; blowout |margin| >= 3; dead margin = 0 with 0-0. Opponent-dependence: all indicators also computed per opponent (both matches vs each of the 19 opponents) and by side (home/away) to test whether an indicator's outcome association holds across opponents or only against specific ones.

### Outcome Analysis

Outcome associations (Pearson r with 3-point score, n=38): strongest are B_top3 +0.429 (winners concentrate betweenness on 3 hubs), H_wdeg -0.407 (winners' pass volume is more concentrated - evenness of weighted contribution is lower in wins), n_opp_shots -0.558 (opponent shooting volume, the loss layer, is the single strongest outcome signal), tempo_var +0.244, tempo_mean +0.234, H_tr +0.213, H_pos +0.212, H_deg -0.205, n_intra_passes +0.174 (activity), C +0.159, n_loss +0.096, n_shots +0.048, centroid_x +0.001. Win vs loss contrast: wins 302 passes/match vs 279 in losses; H_wdeg 0.94 (W) vs 0.96 (L); B_top3 0.61 (W) vs 0.51 (L); tempo 3.04 (W) vs 2.67 (L); opponent shots 8.7 (W) vs 15.7 (L). Logistic fit on the normal regime (n=21): weights H_wdeg -1.74, B_top3 +5.50, H_pt +0.02, tempo_var +0.47, intercept -2.90; accuracy 0.62, AUC 0.75. Interpretation: successful Huskies teamwork is a hub-driven, higher-tempo, more volatile (bursts of activity) style in which 3 connecting players (M1, M3, F2) carry the routes, while the defensive layer that actually separates win from loss is how many shots the opponent gets. H_deg is flat at 0.98 in every regime - participation is nearly uniform season-long, so it has no within-sample spread to be an indicator, though it is the expert-identified transferable principle (task 4). Opponent-dependence: the association is universal in direction - B_top3 and tempo are above their season means in the Huskies' 3 two-win opponents (Opp1, Opp15, Opp17) and in the 4 four-point opponents, while the three 0-point opponents (Opp4, Opp5, Opp7) coincide with below-average B_top3 and tempo; but n=2 per opponent means this is a pattern, not a test. Home/away: 10W/5T/5L at home vs 3W/6T/10L away - the away deficit is a game-state effect (centroid, not quality, moves backwards away), consistent with the literature and the expert's caveat. Reverse-causation check (blowout regime, n=7): correlations flip or explode in the direction the expert predicted - H_wdeg -0.889, B_top3 +0.799, n_opp_shots -0.695 in blowouts vs -0.558 overall - confirming these must not be read as causes; the logistic is therefore fit on the normal regime. Limitations: n=38 (power is low, several |r| ≈ 0.2 signals are not individually significant at n=38); the logistic has 4 parameters on 21 points (AUC 0.75 is suggestive, not conclusive); no player tracking data, so 'coordination' is proxied by triadic structure and transition entropy rather than synchronized movement; completion is binary (completed pass present/absent), no pass quality or pressure context.

### Problem

Subproblem 3: Use the insights from the teamwork model to advise the coach on which structural strategies have been effective for the Huskies and what changes the network analysis indicates should be made next season to improve success.

### Analysis

Per the consultation, the advisory is restricted to leverable (drill-able) indicators only. The expert explicitly excluded three indicators from any recommendation: the network centroid (set by game state and opponent, not by training), average shortest path L (a near-constant of the 11-on-11 formation), and clustering C (constrained by positional geometry - it cannot be raised by drills without changing the formation). Recommendations are therefore built only on: contribution distribution (H_wdeg, H_deg), betweenness concentration (B_top3, the hub role), pass-type diversity (H_pt), tempo and tempo variance, window-transition flexibility (H_tr), and the defensive loss layer (opponent shots). Season record: 13W/10T/15L, goal difference 44:58, home 10W/4T/5L vs away 3W/6T/10L.

### Modeling Process

Advisory procedure: for each leverable indicator, compare (win vs loss means), check sign stability in the normal regime only (blowout regime excluded as reverse-causation), and ask whether the indicator is drill-able. Effective-structure identification: the structural configuration present in the 13 wins (hub trio M1-M3-F2, D-M backbone, forward tempo, high second-half pass rate) is the 'what works'; the structural signature of the 15 losses (flatter betweenness, lower tempo, second-half centroid retreat, opponent shots 15.7 vs 8.7 conceded) is the 'what to change'. Each recommendation below is tied to a measured win/loss gap.

### Outcome Analysis

What has been effective (keep): (1) the M1-M3-F2 connecting triangle - the three highest-betweenness players are exactly these (top dyads M1->F2 182, M3->M1 168, M1->M3 143 season passes); B_top3 = 0.61 in wins vs 0.51 in losses (r=+0.429): hub-led routes correlate with winning. (2) The D-M backbone: 79.5% of passes originate in defense/midfield; wins keep a higher first-half pass rate (3.04 vs 2.67 passes/5min in losses). (3) First-half structure: 1H clustering 0.776 vs 2H 0.662 - the passing triangle holds early; the 21 normal-regime matches show the gap is largest when the team stays in its first-half shape. What to change next season: (1) Second-half tempo - the biggest within-match decline: pass rate falls 2.73 -> 2.26 (/5min) and the centroid retreats -2.10 in matches where the second-half structure breaks; drill for maintaining the 1H triangle into the 2H (tempo_mean +0.234 and tempo_var +0.244 with points). (2) The away-game defensive layer: opponents average 15.7 shots per match against the Huskies in losses vs 8.7 in wins (n_opp_shots r = -0.558, the strongest signal in the model) and 10 of 15 losses come away; this is a structure-preserving problem (hold the D-M line's position under pressure so the opponent's shot volume does not rise), not a quality problem - Huskies shots are flat across outcomes (9.2 W, 9.1 L, 6.5 T), so attack volume is not the lever, defense-in-possession is. (3) Contribution concentration - H_wdeg is lower in wins (0.94) than losses (0.96): winning passes concentrate volume on the hub trio; ensure M1/M3/F2 remain the primary routes in training (do not flatten the distribution) while keeping H_deg ≥ 0.98 so participation stays broad (the near-flat 0.98 means the bench already participates - protect that). (4) Pass-type diversity is low (H_pt ≈ 0.36, dominated by 'Simple pass') and slightly lower in wins (0.35) than losses (0.37) - this is NOT a problem to fix: the Huskies' winning style is a simple-pass, high-volume, hub-driven game, and forcing more 'Smart pass'/'High pass' variety would fight the measured pattern. Do NOT advise (excluded by the expert): chasing a more forward centroid, raising clustering, or lengthening/shortening route length - all three are game-state or geometry constants. Bias note: with 2 matches per opponent, 'effective against X' cannot be established; the advisory is stated for the season aggregate, and the hub recommendation carries the risk that a single-hub dependency (M1 alone carries a large share of the top dyads) is a vulnerability if that player is out - a next-season structural change worth testing is a secondary connecting pair (e.g. D3-M1, the D3->G1 and D3->M1 links) to reduce single-point-of-failure without flattening B_top3.

### Problem

Subproblem 4: Generalize the Huskies findings to say something about how to design more effective teams in general (non-sport settings), and state what other aspects of teamwork would need to be captured to develop generalized models of team performance.

### Analysis

The transferable content is read strictly through the expert's filter: of all measured indicators, the one identified as a domain-independent group-dynamics principle is the evenness of contribution (degree entropy H_deg) - 'even distribution of interaction across members' - because it does not depend on field geometry, 11-player rules, or possession mechanics. Indicators that are soccer-specific and must not be transferred: centroid, path length L, clustering C (geometry/game-state constants, per the expert), and shot-based defensive metrics (a soccer-specific 'loss' mechanic). The reverse-causation warning transfers as a methodological principle: in any longitudinal team study, indicators measured after the outcome is determined (fatigue, disengagement after going behind) are consequences, not causes, and any model that reads them as causes is biased; a generalized model must condition on the state of the task, not just on team activity.

### Modeling Process

Generalization schema. Let a team be a directed interaction graph G_t over members, with edge weight = interaction volume and the same three layers as the soccer model: (i) structure - the interaction topology (who talks/passes to whom), summarized by participation entropy H_deg, route concentration B_top3, and triadic closure; (ii) dynamics - the time evolution of G_t (burstiness = tempo_var, flexibility = transition entropy H_tr, persistence of structure over time = within-task clustering stability); (iii) loss layer - the task's dominant failure mode (in soccer: possession turnover/opponent shots; in a project team: dropped decisions, miscommunication, scope loss). Teamwork quality = f(structure, dynamics) subject to the loss layer, with the two validity conditions found here: (a) an indicator is a teamwork indicator only if it is opponent-relative/task-state-relative or outcome-associative, and (b) the outcome mapping is valid only in the normal-regime (task not yet decided) window. Design principles for effective teams, each traceable to a measured finding: P1 keep participation even (H_deg high - the Huskies hold 0.98 in wins, ties and losses; the literature-expected small-world structure survives only with broad participation); P2 deliberately maintain a small set of connector/hub roles (B_top3 0.61 in wins) - structure with a core, not flat structure; P3 measure the loss layer, not the activity layer - the strongest outcome signal in this data was external (opponent shots, r=-0.558), not internal volume; P4 monitor the second-half equivalent - the within-task decay of structure (2H clustering drop, tempo drop, centroid retreat) is the measurable early-warning signal of a team that will underperform; P5 condition all indicators on task state to avoid reverse causation.

### Outcome Analysis

What the generalization can claim: (1) even participation is a necessary, domain-independent marker of healthy team interaction (it is the expert-named transferable principle and is present in all Huskies outcomes); (2) effective teams are not flat - a small hub/connector core that carries a disproportionate share of routes (B_top3) coexists with broad participation (high H_deg) and both co-occur with better outcomes; (3) the team's dominant failure channel is usually external (what the environment takes from the team) rather than internal activity volume - shot volume is flat across Huskies outcomes while opponent shot volume separates wins from losses; (4) structure decays within a long task (second-half pattern: clustering -14%, tempo -17%, positional retreat), and the decay rate is a team-level trait; (5) reverse causation is the central bias of team-performance modeling and must be handled by state-conditioning. What a generalized model would additionally need to capture, beyond what this data or this model has: (i) directed influence and decision flow, not just interaction volume - the data records passes, not who decides; (ii) the content/quality of interactions (a 'smart pass' flag exists but completion quality, pressure, and decision correctness are unobserved); (iii) temporal coupling and synchronization (simultaneous coordinated action - e.g. a steal-poised-for-offense - is not observable from pass logs alone); (iv) individual skill distributions and how hubs leverage them (star-player effects); (v) the opponent/counter-strategy as an adaptive system (this study treats the opponent's shot volume as an outcome covariate, not as a strategy); (vi) longitudinal team memory - how a team's structure adapts across the season to repeated opponents (38 matches is one season; cross-season adaptability is out of scope); (vii) in non-sport teams, the loss layer and the 'task state' that defines the normal regime must be re-specified per domain (project milestones, defect counts, review latency), which is the real cost of transfer. Limitations of the generalization itself: it is inferred from one team, one season, one sport, with 38 observations; every principle above is a hypothesis supported by one dataset, and the small n means none of the associations clears conventional significance thresholds - the value of the generalization is the structural template (three layers + two validity conditions + reverse-causation rule), not the specific coefficients.
