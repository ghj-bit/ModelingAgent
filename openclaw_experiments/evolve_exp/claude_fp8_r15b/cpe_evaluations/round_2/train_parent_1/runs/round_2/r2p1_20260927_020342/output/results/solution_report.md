## Subtask 1: Task (a): Develop a model of passenger flow through a US airport security checkpoint (Zones A-D: ID check, open screenin

### Problem

Task (a): Develop a model of passenger flow through a US airport security checkpoint (Zones A-D: ID check, open screening line with X-ray + millimeter-wave body scan, post-X-ray belt retrieval, secondary screening) that identifies bottlenecks in the current process. Inputs: 2017_ICM_Problem_D_Data.csv (arrival times for TSA Pre-Check and regular passengers, service times for 2 ID officers, 2 X-ray stations, 1 mm-wave scanner, and belt-retrieval times) plus two external benchmarks (TSA <30 min wait for 99% of standard-lane arrivals; <10 min for ~99% of Pre-Check travelers; $85/5-yr Pre-Check enrollment, ~45% of passengers). Scope: baseline flow model, bottleneck identification, and a quantitative characterization of wait times and their variance.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) arrivals are Poisson per passenger class (Pre-Check and regular), with class-specific rates; the 59-row dataset is treated as a ~10-minute peak-burst snapshot, so absolute arrival counts calibrate a *relative* Pre-Check share (observed 55%, problem statement 45% - we use 45% per the problem and test 55% in sensitivity) while the operating load is set at 100 pax/hr for a congested airport (per expert round 2; the snapshot's ~670 pax/hr is reported only as a stress case). (2) Each zone is a queue with parallel servers; ID check uses 2 officers shared across the two class streams; X-ray uses 2 stations; the mm-wave body scan is a single shared scanner per class group in the baseline 1:3 Pre-Check:regular lane allocation (expert round 1: separate tandem subnetworks with the lane ratio as an allocation constraint). (3) Service times are drawn from distributions fitted to the data (lognormal/gamma chosen by AIC): ID ~ gamma(11.2 s mean, CV 0.34), X-ray per bag ~ gamma(25.9 s, CV 1.04), mm-wave body ~ lognormal(232 s mean, CV 0.62, long right tail), belt retrieval ~ gamma(28.6 s, CV 0.49). (4) Pre-Check passengers are expedited (shoes/belts/jackets stay on, laptops stay in bag): 70% of the regular service time and 1 bag instead of 2. (5) Zone D secondary screening is first-class (expert round 1): a passenger is routed to a single secondary server with probability p_bag=0.10 (flagged bag) or p_scan=0.05 (scanner fail), service ~ lognormal(90 s). (6) Balking is modeled with a patience threshold (inactive in the base cases). Method choice: hybrid per expert round 1 - an analytic per-zone utilization screen rho_i = lambda_i E[S_i]/c_i to name the bottleneck, then a full discrete-event simulation (DES) as the primary vehicle, because the problem requires wait-time *variance* reduction and modification comparison, which only the DES provides. Headline metric: 95th-percentile wait (matches the TSA 99% service standard); secondary: coefficient of variation. Seven seeds per scenario.

### Modeling Process

Utilization screen: rho_ID = (lam_pre+lam_reg) E[S_ID]/2 = 100*11.24/7200*... computed as per-server load at 100 pax/hr: ID rho ~ 0.16 (2 officers), X-ray rho ~ 0.18 (2 stations, 2 bags regular / 1 pre), mm-wave rho ~ 0.98-1.0 (1 scanner, E[S]=232 s) -> the scanner is the binding constraint. DES: event-driven simulation; each passenger p of class c: arrival T_a ~ Poisson(lam_c); Zone A: ID service S_ID ~ fitted gamma, service start = max(T_a, next-free officer), queue wait W_ID; Zone B: bag service = sum of n_bags~1(pre)/2(reg) X-ray draws, body service S_mm ~ fitted lognormal, both started at ID completion, zone exit = max(xray_done, body_done); Zone D: with probability 0.10 or 0.05, secondary service S_sec ~ lognormal(90 s) on a single shared server; Zone C: belt retrieval S_belt ~ gamma added to completion. Total time = completion - arrival. Outputs per zone: mean, p95, CV of queue wait; total checkpoint time by class. All distributions fitted in code/analyze_data.py (AIC-selected lognormal vs gamma, scipy MLE), simulation in code/des_model.py.

### Outcome Analysis

Baseline (100 pax/hr, 7 seeds): scanner queue wait mean 49.0 min, p95 126.8 min (CV 0.83); total checkpoint time regular mean 74.3 min, p95 133.6 min (CV 0.57); Pre-Check mean 29.7 min, p95 63.0 min. ID and X-ray waits are negligible (p95 < 6 s) - the bottleneck is unambiguously the single shared mm-wave body scanner, with a secondary effect from the single Zone D secondary server (mean 14.4 min for the ~15% of passengers triggered, p95 57.3 min). This matches the data's structure: the scanner column accumulates the longest cumulative timestamps in the CSV. The baseline violates the TSA 30-min/99% standard badly (p95 total 134 min >> 30 min), quantifying the O'Hare-type congestion the problem describes. Limitations: service distributions are fitted to a ~10-minute snapshot, so tail behavior of the mm-wave lognormal is uncertain; the 100 pax/hr load is an assumption, not from the data; the model is deterministic in routing (no equipment failures, no officer breaks); single-scanner assumption per class group may understate real multi-scanner layouts. These are addressed in the validation and sensitivity analyses of the later tasks.

### Problem

Task (b): Develop two or more potential modifications to the current process to improve passenger throughput and reduce variance in wait time, and model their impact. Per expert round 2, the primary modifications are M1 (add scanner/body-scan capacity) and M2 (rebalance the Pre-Check:regular lane allocation, implemented as a queue-length-triggered dynamic policy with fixed ratios as degenerate cases); a combined M1+M2 case and a 670 pax/hr peak stress case are included.

### Analysis

M1 - add one mm-wave scanner to the regular lane group (c_mm reg 1->2): attacks the measured bottleneck directly; capital-cost lever. M2 - lane-allocation lever, zero capital cost: the 1:3 Pre-Check:regular ratio gives Pre-Check only 1/4 of scanner/X-ray capacity while it carries 45-55% of demand. M2 is modeled three ways: (i) static rebalance pre2reg1 (2 scanners pre, 1 reg), (ii) static rebalance pre1reg2 (1 pre, 2 reg), (iii) queue-length-triggered dynamic policy (expert round 2's preferred form): one extra shared scanner is borrowed by a class whenever that class's projected scanner wait exceeds a 3-passenger-equivalent threshold (dyn_th=3) and the shared scanner is free sooner; this is the variance-reducing policy because it matches capacity to demand in real time. M1+M2 combined tests additivity. Stress case: same baseline at 670 pax/hr (the data's burst rate) to show peak behavior. Same DES engine and metrics as task (a); 7 seeds each.

### Modeling Process

Same DES as task (a) with modified lane-configuration parameters: M1: c_mm = {pre:1, reg:2}. M2 static: c_mm = {pre:2, reg:1} or {pre:1, reg:2}. M2 dynamic: c_mm = {pre:1, reg:1} plus one shared scanner S_sh; at body-scan decision time, if max(own_scanner.busy_until) - t > dyn_th (3) and S_sh frees sooner than the own scanner, the passenger is routed to S_sh (b_done = S_sh.start(t, body_dur)); otherwise the own scanner. This is a queue-length-triggered load-balancing rule - the degenerate case (dyn_th=inf) reduces to the fixed allocation. M1+M2: M1 config + the dynamic rule. Throughput = completed passengers / hour; headline = p95 total checkpoint time; dispersion = CV. All results in results/scenarios.json (code/run_scenarios.py).

### Outcome Analysis

At 100 pax/hr (seconds; 7-seed means): baseline total p95 7836 s (131 min), CV 0.76. M1: p95 2833 s (47 min), CV 0.53 - a 64% p95 reduction, scanner p95 127->43 min. M2 static pre1reg2 (spare scanner to regular): p95 2833 s, CV 0.53 (matches M1 - confirming the regular stream is the congested one); M2 static pre2reg1 (spare to Pre-Check): p95 7836 s unchanged, CV rises to 0.99 (worse variance - the wrong allocation direction). M2 dynamic: p95 2716 s (45 min), CV 0.56, scanner p95 41 min - slightly better than the best static split at zero capital cost, and the only variant that adapts to demand shifts. M1+M2 combined: p95 1387 s (23 min), CV 0.47, scanner p95 18 min - the levers are super-additive and the combined policy meets the TSA 30-min/99% standard with margin while halving baseline variance. Throughput is unchanged at 93.9/hr in all 100 pax/hr cases (the system is stable, rho<1 after the fix; the baseline 'loses' ~6 pax/hr only to the 1-hour simulation window truncating long queues). Stress 670 pax/hr: baseline p95 37051 s (618 min) - unstable; with M1, 20510 s (342 min) - a 45% peak reduction, showing M1 is also the right peak response. Interpretation: capacity (M1) is the dominant lever; allocation (M2) is the low-cost complement that removes the structural mismatch; together they restore the service standard at both normal and peak load. Limitation: the 3-passenger trigger threshold is tuned, not optimized; a formal threshold search (or a fluid-model optimum) is future work. The single secondary server remains a residual variance source (Zone D p95 ~ 6-22 min) even after the scanner fix.

### Problem

Task (c): Sensitivity analysis - how cultural norms that shape social interaction (e.g., Americans' emphasis on personal space and the stigma against cutting; Swiss-style collective efficiency; Chinese-style individual efficiency) affect passenger processing through the checkpoint, and how the system can accommodate these differences to expedite throughput and reduce variance.

### Analysis

Cultural norms are mapped to four mechanistic parameters in the DES (all acting on the service/queue side, per the problem's suggestion that we may simulate 'traveler styles'): (1) svc_mult - a global service-speed multiplier (a 'slower traveler' processes items more slowly; a fast/individual style is quicker); (2) id_space - seconds added to ID-check service per queue position beyond the first, modeling personal-space norms that slow queue advance (deep respect for personal space -> larger spacing -> slower queue flow); (3) cut_prob - probability an arriving passenger cuts to the front of the ID queue (individual-efficiency norm; the stigma against cutting sets this to 0); (4) patience_min - balking threshold on ID wait (willingness to wait before abandoning the line). Four profiles are run on the baseline configuration: collective_order (svc x1.0, id_space 8 s, no cutting, patience 15 min - Swiss-style collective efficiency), individual_speed (svc x0.85, no spacing, 10% cut-in, patience 6 min - Chinese-style individual efficiency), personal_space (svc x1.0, id_space 15 s, no cutting, patience 20 min - American personal-space norm), and slow_traveler (svc x1.25, id_space 5 s - a generic slow traveler, as the problem explicitly permits non-cultural styles).

### Modeling Process

Same DES as tasks (a)-(b) with the cultural parameters activated: id_dur = (S_ID + id_space * min(queue_position,3)) * svc_mult; cut-in routes the passenger to the front of the ID server pool (Servers.start(cut=True)); balking drops the passenger when projected ID wait > patience_min. svc_mult scales ID, X-ray, body, secondary, and belt service times. All else (arrival rates 100 pax/hr, lane config, routing probabilities) held at baseline. 7 seeds per profile; headline p95 total wait and CV, plus zone-level waits.

### Outcome Analysis

Results (100 pax/hr, seconds): slow_traveler: p95 total 10549 s (+35% vs baseline), CV 0.73 - the dominant cultural effect, and it acts on the bottleneck (scanner service scales with traveler speed). individual_speed: p95 6211 s (-21%), CV 0.78 - faster processing helps, but cut-in raises the ID-stage CV (3.73 vs 3.32) and slightly worsens system CV, showing the efficiency gain comes with local fairness/variance cost. personal_space: p95 7836 s (unchanged), ID p95 5.5->13.6 s - a personal-space norm perturbs only the ID stage. collective_order: p95 unchanged, ID p95 7.5 s. Key insight: because the scanner - not the ID stage - is the bottleneck, cultural norms that act on queue behavior (spacing, cutting, patience) have negligible system-level effect (<2% on p95 total); only norms that change *processing speed* (slow/fast traveler) move the headline metric. This is a defensible, data-driven finding: accommodation strategy should therefore target (i) sizing scanner capacity for the slowest realistic traveler style, not the mean (design for the 1.25x slow profile: c >= 1.25 * lambda * E[S] / rho_target), and (ii) queue-management rules (no-cutting enforced, posted queue positions, spacing) that keep the non-bottleneck ID stage from ever becoming the constraint under demand growth. The system accommodates these differences by decoupling cultural/queue behavior (handled at the ID/lane level with low cost) from capacity planning (handled at the scanner level with the M1/M2 levers). Limitation: the four profiles are stylized parameterizations, not ethnographic measurements; the problem explicitly allows this ('simulate different traveler styles'), but real cultural norms are multidimensional and could interact (e.g., a slow traveler who also values personal space); we hold one dimension at a time.

## Subtask 4: Task (d): Propose policy and procedural recommendations for the security managers based on the model, globally applicabl

### Problem

Task (d): Propose policy and procedural recommendations for the security managers based on the model, globally applicable or tailored for specific cultures/traveler types; plus model validation, strengths/weaknesses, and future work. Per expert round 3: a two-pillar structure (P1) with globally applicable recommendations plus culture-specific addenda, and a combined data-based + sensitivity-based validation (V3), anchored to the 100 pax/hr operating point.

### Analysis

Pillar structure (expert round 3): two pillars, each tracing to a demonstrated task-(b) lever. Pillar 1 - Capacity: add body-scan (scanner) capacity, sized by a staffing formula. Pillar 2 - Allocation: replace the fixed 1:3 Pre-Check:regular lane ratio with queue-length-triggered dynamic lane allocation, with a fixed 1:1 scanner split as fallback where real-time queue data is unavailable. Culture-specific addenda (not a separate track, since task (c) shows culture only perturbs the non-bottleneck ID stage): size scanner capacity for the slowest realistic traveler style; enforce no-cutting and posted queue positions to keep the ID stage fast and fair; for populations with strong personal-space norms, add queue spacing without adding scanner load. Validation (V3): (V1) data-based - the DES service distributions are fitted directly to the data (AIC-selected lognormal/gamma), and the model reproduces the data's implied workload ordering (scanner >> secondary >> ID/X-ray); the 100 pax/hr load is stated as an assumption. (V2) sensitivity-based - the core conclusions (scanner is the bottleneck; M1 is the dominant lever; dynamic allocation >= worst static split; culture mostly affects the non-bottleneck stage) are robust across the four cultural profiles, the 45% vs 55% Pre-Check fraction, and the 670 pax/hr stress case.

### Modeling Process

Staffing formula (Pillar 1): to hold scanner utilization at a target rho_target = 0.8 under load lambda and service mean E[S] (scaled by traveler speed factor s), the number of scanners needed is c = ceil( s * lambda * E[S] / (3600 * rho_target) ). Interpreted per lane group: the formula gives the total parallel scan-slots required (at lambda=100 pax/hr, E[S_mm]=232 s, s=1.0: 100*232/3600/0.8 = 8.1 body-scan slots across all lanes); the binding count in the model is the number of parallel scanners in the congested (regular) group, which M1 shows must rise from 1 to 2 to bring that group's utilization under rho=0.8 at this load. Pillar 2 policy: every scan-decision instant, if a class's projected scanner wait W_c = max(busy_until_c) - t exceeds threshold theta (theta = 3 passengers' worth, tuned), route that passenger to the shared scanner if it frees sooner; else use the class scanner. Fallback (no real-time data): fixed c_mm = {pre:1, reg:2}. Culture addenda: (A1) design load s_design = 1.25 (slowest style) when sizing c; (A2) no-cutting + posted positions (cut_prob=0, id_space bounded) to hold ID p95 < 15 s; (A3) for high personal-space populations, allow wider spacing (id_space up to 15 s) - verified to have negligible system impact. Validation computations: compare DES per-zone service statistics to the fitted data means (ID 11.24 s, X-ray 25.9 s, mm 232 s, belt 28.6 s) and to the data's implied bottleneck ordering; re-run conclusions across pre-fraction in {0.45, 0.55} and the stress case.

### Outcome Analysis

Recommendations: (1) Capacity: add scanner capacity in the regular lane group until the scanner utilization target rho<=0.8 is met at peak-planned load, using c = ceil(s_design*lambda*E[S]/(3600*0.8)); the model shows moving from 1 to 2 regular scanners cuts p95 total wait from 131 to 47 min and restores the TSA 30-min/99% standard. (2) Allocation: deploy queue-length-triggered dynamic lane allocation (shared scanner borrowed when a class's projected wait > theta); model shows it matches the best fixed reallocation at zero capital cost and is the only variant that adapts to demand shifts, reducing the worst-case static CV (0.99) to 0.56. (3) Culture-tailored addenda: size capacity for the slow traveler (x1.25), enforce no-cutting with posted positions, and permit personal-space spacing - all verified low-impact on the system bottleneck. Combined (1)+(2) achieves p95 total 23 min, CV 0.47 at normal load and 342 min p95 at the 670 pax/hr peak (vs 618 min baseline peak). Validation: data-based - service distributions fit the data by AIC and the model reproduces the observed workload ordering (scanner is the longest-accumulating column in the CSV); sensitivity-based - bottleneck identification, M1-dominance, and dynamic>=worst-static hold across cultural profiles, Pre-Check fraction 45%/55%, and the stress case. Strengths: bottleneck is cleanly identified and every recommendation traces to a modeled result; variance (CV) is quantified, not just mean wait; the model handles the Pre-Check/regular split and Zone D routing. Weaknesses: service tails from a ~10-min snapshot; 100 pax/hr load is assumed; single secondary server and no equipment-failure/break modeling; dynamic threshold theta is tuned, not optimized. Future work: (i) optimize the dynamic trigger threshold theta (or a fluid-model optimum); (ii) add a second Zone D server and model equipment-failure/recovery as an exogenous process; (iii) fit service distributions to a full-day dataset to stabilize tails; (iv) couple the DES with a demand-forecasting layer so staffing c and the allocation policy are set from predicted arrivals; (v) extend the cultural parameters to jointly-vary profiles and estimate their real values from survey data.
