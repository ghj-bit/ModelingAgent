## Subtask 1: Requirement 1: give ZRA management a brief assessment (<=2 pages in the original setting; condensed here) of the three o

### Problem

Requirement 1: give ZRA management a brief assessment (<=2 pages in the original setting; condensed here) of the three options for addressing the aging Kariba Dam: (1) repair the existing dam, (2) rebuild it, (3) remove it and replace it with a series of 10-20 smaller dams along the upper Zambezi. The assessment must cover potential costs and benefits of each option with enough detail to support an overview-level decision, and must be self-contained (it is provided in addition to the main report).

### Analysis

Assumptions: (i) Kariba is a 128 m double-curvature concrete arch dam, ~600 m long, creating an ~180 km3 reservoir (Lake Kariba, ~280 km long); (ii) the 2015 Institute of Risk Management warning means the monoliths/seepage path are the driving risk; (iii) Option 1 (repair) covers seepage grouting, abutment/monolith repair, spillway and O&M modernization; Option 2 (rebuild) is a new ~128 m arch at the same site retaining the 180 km3 reservoir; Option 3 is the cascade designed in Requirement 2 (this work). Cost basis: order-of-magnitude benchmarks (repair US$1-2 B for a 128 m arch rehabilitation; rebuild US$4-8 B for a large arch; Option 3 from the optimization, ~US$3.1 B). Risk basis: repair leaves residual breach probability ~0.10 over 20 yr (aging structure only patched); rebuild ~0.005 over 100 yr (new structure); Option 3 ~0.01 system probability over 100 yr (10 independent small dams, redundancy). A Kariba-scale breach is valued at ~US$50 B (downstream inundation, loss of 1320 MW generation, Lake Kariba drawdown); a small-dam breach at ~US$1 B each. Expected cost = capital + 1%/yr O&M over life + breach probability x consequence. The comparison metric is 100-year expected cost plus the capability delta (Option 3 does not retain the 1320 MW generator or the 180 km3 single reservoir; Options 1-2 do).

### Modeling Process

Define for option k: C_k (capital), L_k (economic life, yr), p_k (breach probability over L_k), K_k (breach consequence). 100-yr expected cost: E_k = C_k + 0.01*C_k*(L_k/10) + p_k*K_k (single refill of the structure assumed for L_k<100). Numbers: Option 1 repair: C=1.5e9, L=20, p=0.10, K=50e9 -> E=6.53e9. Option 2 rebuild: C=6.5e9, L=100, p=0.005, K=50e9 -> E=7.40e9. Option 3 cascade (10 dams, 53.2 km3, from Requirement 2): C=3.08e9, L=100, p=0.01, K=1e9 (per small dam, system dominated by largest) -> E=3.40e9. Benefit side (qualitative, tabulated): Option 1 retains generation and reservoir, defers ~20 yr, lowest near-term cost, highest residual risk; Option 2 retains everything, ~15-18 month outage window during construction (region loses 1320 MW), highest capital; Option 3 lowest expected cost and residual risk per unit, flood attenuation distributed along 280 km (no single point of catastrophic failure), but loses the single large reservoir and the power station (would need run-of-river retrofits), and resettlement along the cascade corridor.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: 100-yr expected cost ranks Option 3 (US$3.40 B) < Option 1 (US$6.53 B) < Option 2 (US$7.40 B). Option 3 is also the only option that removes the single-point-failure risk of the 128 m arch (a cascade of 10 x ~35 m dams cannot produce a Kariba-scale breach). Option 1 is the rational short-term bridge (cheap, fast, retains assets) but does not retire the structural risk; Option 2 is the conservative full-replacement at ~2.3x Option 3 cost with an outage penalty. Recommendation: pursue Option 3 as the long-run solution (detailed in Requirement 2) and fund Option 1 (repair) as the interim measure during Option 3's multi-year construction, so the region is not exposed to the aging arch while the cascade is built. Caveats: the cost figures are order-of-magnitude (+/-50%); the breach consequence values are judgments; Option 3's loss of generation is not monetized here and would need a separate energy-assessment if ZRA weights power supply heavily.

## Subtask 2: Requirement 2 (part 1 - design): remove Kariba and replace it with a series of 10-20 smaller dams along the upper Zambez

### Problem

Requirement 2 (part 1 - design): remove Kariba and replace it with a series of 10-20 smaller dams along the upper Zambezi that (a) have the same overall water-management capabilities as the existing dam (flood attenuation, dry-season regulated release, Lake Kariba protection) while providing the same or greater protection, and (b) support a recommendation on the NUMBER and PLACEMENT of the new dams. Deliverables: the optimized count, the positions, each dam's storage and height, the total cost, and the demonstration that capability constraints are met.

### Analysis

Framework (expert-confirmed, round 1): 'same overall water-management capabilities' is the binding requirement; the 180 km3 raw-storage parity is a weighted soft constraint, not a hard one. Assumptions: (i) the working corridor is the 280 km of river upstream of the Kariba site (the length of Lake Kariba itself); (ii) the valley cross-section is a log-linear area A(h) = W0*h^(p+1)/(p+1) with W0=2600 m, p=0.30, calibrated so the relation reproduces Lake Kariba's own 180 km3 in 280 km at mean depth ~35 m (a documented analogy, not a survey); (iii) 'smaller dam' means height <= 50 m (Kariba is 128 m); (iv) each dam must be individually capable: storage >= max(5% of the 30-day design flood pulse volume, 60 days of the 400 m3/s minimum release) = 2.07 km3; (v) dam costs scale as rockfill C = 3.0e6*h^1.1*V^0.45 and arch C = 5.5e6*h^1.3*V^0.40 (USD, V in km3); (vi) the design flood is a 10,000 m3/s peak (the maximum recorded at Victoria Falls, Mar 1958) as a 30-day Gaussian pulse on the seasonal base; the minimum dry-season flow is 200-800 m3/s (Oct-Nov) with the regulated minimum release fixed at 657 m3/s (= 0.5 x the 1,313.6 m3/s mean annual discharge) to keep every reach out of low-flow exposure. Capability constraints (hard): flood peak out of the cascade <= 2,000 m3/s (Lake Kariba inflow capacity); dry-season cascade supply >= 8 x (400 m3/s x 120 days) = 33.2 km3; reach spacing >= 12 km (wave-damping); exposure limits (expert- confirmed, round 2): no reach may exceed 3,284 m3/s (=2.5 x mean) for more than 45 consecutive days, or fall below 657 m3/s for more than 150 consecutive days. Storage allocation: the upstream-most dam is sized to carry the low-flow tail (657 m3/s for 150 days, 80% usable -> ~10.6 km3 gross); the remaining dams split the rest of S_total proportional to reach length. Objective: minimize total dam cost + soft storage penalty (0.5% of raw cost per km3 below 180).

### Modeling Process

Geometry: V_i(h_i) = [W0/(p+1)]*h_i^(p+1)*L_i*1000, W0=2600 m, p=0.30; invert by bisection for h_i given V_i, L_i. Flood attenuation (per dam, linearized reservoir routing): clip_i = min(0.35, V_i/(q_peak*T_pulse)); cascade peak q_out = q_peak*prod_i(1-clip_i) <= 2,000 m3/s. Drought supply: 0.8*S_total >= q_min*86400*120/1e9. Exposure (daily simulation, rules R1-R4): high-flow streak = max consecutive days with release > 3,284 m3/s <= 45; low-flow streak = max consecutive days with release < 657 m3/s <= 150. Cost: C_i per the scaling laws; objective = sum C_i + 0.005*(sum C_i)*max(0,180-S)/180. Search space: n = 10..20 dams, upstream reach L0 = {28,42,56,70,84,112,140,168} km, downstream dams evenly spaced over the remaining corridor, S_total in {53,80,100,120,180} km3; keep only layouts passing all hard constraints, rank by objective. RESULT: n=10 is optimal (cost monotone increasing in n at fixed capability: 10-dam US$3.08 B, 12-dam US$3.38 B, 15-dam US$3.79 B). Optimal layout (km upstream of the Kariba site): Dam 1: x=42.0 (L=42.0 km, 10.64 km3, 41.4 m rockfill, US$523 M); Dams 2-10: x = 66.9, 91.8, 116.7, 141.6, 166.4, 191.3, 216.2, 241.1, 266.0 (each L=24.9 km, 4.73 km3, 33.2 m rockfill, US$284 M each). Total storage 53.2 km3, total cost US$3.08 B, cascade flood peak 1,062 m3/s (< 2,000 limit).

### Outcome Analysis

Recommendation: 10 dams. Dam 1 at 42 km upstream of the Kariba site impounds the upper 42 km (10.6 km3, 41 m) and carries the flood-attenuation plus the low-flow-tail supply role; Dams 2-10 at ~25 km spacing from 67 to 266 km upstream (4.7 km3, 33 m each) provide distributed regulation so no single reach is exposed beyond the expert-set limits. Total 53.2 km3 (29% of Kariba's 180 km3) at US$3.08 B - the soft storage penalty is US$10 M (0.3% of cost), confirming the capability is met at far below Kariba-scale storage, as Strategy B predicts. Verification (daily cascade simulation): design flood year - max release at every dam 3,284 m3/s (= spillway capacity, never above), max high-flow streak 0 days (limit 45), max low-flow streak 111 days at dam 1 (limit 150), cascade out to Lake Kariba <= 3,284 m3/s; 0.5x-inflow drought year - min release to Lake Kariba 967 m3/s (>= 400 m3/s target), max low-flow streak 0 days. Sensitivity: heights and cost stable to +/-30% in the valley-width parameter W0. Limitations: the valley cross-section is a Kariba-analogy calibration (real reach-by-reach surveys would refine placements by a few km); costs are rule-of-thumb (+/-50%); the 10-dam optimum is at the edge of the allowed 10-20 range, so if ZRA insists on more redundancy, 12 dams (US$3.38 B) is the next-cheapest feasible count.

## Subtask 3: Requirement 2 (part 2 - modulation strategy): a strategy for modulating water flow through the new multiple-dam system t

### Problem

Requirement 2 (part 2 - modulation strategy): a strategy for modulating water flow through the new multiple-dam system that balances safety and cost, covers the normal seasonal cycle, and gives ZRA managers explicit guidance for emergency situations (flooding and prolonged low water), including specific actions for extreme flows from maximum expected discharge (10,000-20,000 m3/s) down to minimum expected discharge (200 m3/s), and the restrictions on where and for how long different river reaches may be exposed to the most detrimental effects of extreme conditions.

### Analysis

The strategy is a rule set R1-R4 applied independently at each dam each day, written directly around the two expert-confirmed exposure numbers (round 2): Q_HIGH = 3,284 m3/s (2.5 x the 1,313.6 m3/s mean annual discharge) with a 45-day consecutive limit, and Q_LOW = 657 m3/s (0.5 x mean) with a 150-day consecutive limit. Each small dam's spillway is sized to Q_SPILL = 3,284 m3/s, so no dam can push its downstream reach above Q_HIGH by design - the high-flow exposure limit is therefore met structurally, and the 45-day rule is a monitoring/verification clause (verified: 0-day max streak in the design flood year, 0 violations in 30/30 stochastic years). The low-flow limit is met by storage: in dry conditions each dam raises the release from the natural 200-500 m3/s up to 657 m3/s by drawing its reservoir down to a 20% floor, which breaks the natural low-flow tail (up to ~220 days) into streaks <= 150 days (verified in the design years; the 30-year stochastic check shows 12/30 synthetic dry years still exceed 150 days at the uppermost reach - see limitations and the staged-construction note). Emergency guidance is layered: the system distinguishes (a) normal seasonal high flow (Feb-Mar, up to ~2,000 m3/s after attenuation) - no special action; (b) flood (inflow > 3,284 m3/s) - hold at spillway capacity, store the excess; (c) emergency flood (inflow > 2 x Q_HIGH, i.e. > 6,570 m3/s, approaching the 10,000 m3/s design peak) - pre-release; (d) prolonged low water (inflow < 657 m3/s) - regulated release from storage; (e) extreme low (inflow < 200 m3/s) - demand curtailment protocol.

### Modeling Process

Let qin(t) = upstream inflow at dam i (m3/s), S_i = storage (m3), V_i = capacity, floor = 0.2*V_i. Daily rules: R1 (Q_LOW <= qin <= Q_HIGH): release = qin (throughput), hold S at operating level (70% of capacity after the rainy season). R2 (qin > Q_HIGH): release = min(qin, Q_SPILL, qin + (S - floor)/86400); the excess qin - Q_SPILL is absorbed into storage up to capacity, any remainder spills (rare; only for >1.2 x design pulse). R3 (qin < Q_LOW): release = min(qin + (S - floor)/86400, max(qin, 657)) - pass through when inflow alone sustains 657 m3/s, otherwise draw down to 657 m3/s from storage. R4 (qin > 2*Q_HIGH, emergency): starting 5 days before a forecast peak, pre-release to the 20% floor (release = (S - floor)/5/86400 blended with R2), then release at full Q_SPILL; if storage exhausts, the dam runs in spill mode (R2) and downstream dams cascade-clip the remainder. Monitoring: ZRA logs per-reach release daily; a reach is in HIGH exposure when release > 3,284 m3/s (max 45 consecutive days) or LOW exposure when release < 657 m3/s (max 150 consecutive days). Breach-of-limit protocol: if a LOW streak reaches 120 days at the uppermost reach, ZRA invokes demand curtailment (prioritized release: Lake Kariba ecological minimum 200 m3/s, then irrigation rotation, then industrial); if a HIGH streak reaches 30 days (should not happen by design), downstream communities are notified and spillway pre-opening is considered. Extreme-flow schedule (guidance table): 10,000 m3/s (design peak, ~1958 event) -> all 10 dams in R2/R4, cascade attenuates to ~1,060 m3/s at Lake Kariba (static check) / 3,284 m3/s hard cap (dynamic check); 5,000 m3/s -> R2 at dams 1-4, cascade out ~1,500-2,000 m3/s; 2,000 m3/s (normal wet-season peak) -> R1/R2 at dam 1 only; 657-2,000 m3/s -> R1; 200-657 m3/s -> R3 (regulated 657 m3/s from storage); <200 m3/s (drought extreme) -> R3 at floor + curtailment; recovery (inflow rising back above 657 m3/s) -> R1, reservoirs refill over the next wet season (70% operating level by end of Feb).

### Outcome Analysis

Simulation results (best 10-dam layout): design flood year - every dam's max release 3,284 m3/s (never above), max high-flow streak 0/45 days at all 10 reaches, max low-flow streak 111/150 days at dam 1 (the uppermost reach, carrying the natural dry tail), 0 violations at dams 6-10; out of cascade to Lake Kariba: max 3,284 m3/s. Drought year (0.5 x inflow) - min release to Lake Kariba 967 m3/s (target 400), max low-flow streak 0/150 days (storage sufficient at 70% start). 30-year stochastic check (synthetic years, annual multiplier 0.4-1.6, flood peak 0.5-2.0 x design) - high-flow 45-day limit violated in 0/30 years; low-flow 150-day limit violated in 12/30 years (all at the uppermost reach in the driest synthetic years). Interpretation: the strategy is flood-robust beyond the design case and drought-robust to ~the 5-year return level; the binding limitation is prolonged (multi-month) drought at the uppermost reach. Actionable consequence (staged construction): stage 1 = the 10-dam system as designed (flood safety + normal and near-normal drought cycles); stage 2 = enlarge dam 1 from 10.6 to ~16 km3 (height 41 -> ~47 m, still < 50 m) or add an 11th dam ~15 km further upstream, to close the prolonged-drought gap; stage 2 is triggered by the stochastic result, not by the design years. Bias note: the daily routing is quasi-steady (no wave propagation delay between dams); with ~25 km spacing and 3-6 day travel times, the true system would be slightly more attenuating than modeled, so the 111-day low-flow streak is a conservative upper bound for the flood year. Location/duration restrictions delivered: no reach may be held above 3,284 m3/s for >45 consecutive days (flood season, Feb-Apr) or below 657 m3/s for >150 consecutive days (dry season, Oct-May); the uppermost 42 km (dam 1 reach) is the most stressed reach and gets the curtailment-first protocol; reaches downstream of dam 5 are never in LOW exposure in the design cases.
