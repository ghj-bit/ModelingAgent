## Subtask 1: Task 1: Develop a model that (a) determines a country's fragility on the Fragile States Index (FSI) 0-120 scale and clas

### Problem

Task 1: Develop a model that (a) determines a country's fragility on the Fragile States Index (FSI) 0-120 scale and classifies it as stable, vulnerable, or fragile, and (b) simultaneously measures the impact of climate change, distinguishing direct pathways (immediate shocks to economy and services) from indirect pathways (climate -> agriculture/water -> other FSI indicators). Scope: a reusable, calibratable composite model to be applied in Tasks 2-4 to Yemen (top-10) and Egypt (non-top-10).

### Analysis

Approach chosen after expert consultation (round 1, Option A confirmed): a weighted replica of the FSI's 12-indicator structure, each indicator I_i scored 0-10 (0 = most stable, 10 = least stable), with a climate shock modifier added on top. This was preferred over (B) adding climate as a 13th indicator, which cannot express the direct/indirect pathways the problem requires, and (C) a fully dynamic agent/ODE system, which is unidentifiable from the available data. Assumptions: (1) equal weights on the 12 indicators, matching the FSI's 0-10-per-indicator construction; (2) classification thresholds stable F<60, vulnerable 60<=F<100, fragile F>=100, the fragile line aligned with the FSI top-10/very-high-alert decile (~110+); (3) climate impact is measured as the counterfactual delta_C = F(climate on) - F(climate off), so 'impact' is literally the difference between a world with and without the climate forcing; (4) routing of climate damage onto indicators follows the expert's fixed rule: E2 (Economic Decline) 40%, P2 (Public Services) 30%, C1 (Security Apparatus) 10%, C3 (Group Grievance) 10%, P4 (Refugees/IDPs) 10%, with E1 (Demographic Pressures) excluded because it is exogenous to short-run climate shocks and drought-driven migration would otherwise be double-counted in P4; (5) the climate chain is calibrated to published FSI anchors (Yemen ~98 in 2018; Syria ~95+ in 2014) and to published drought benchmarks (0.8% ag-GDP loss per drought event; 3-12% multi-year detrended yield losses; adaptation +15-64% yield; -1.7% yield loss per +10 pts irrigation share). The chain is validated on two independent top-10 countries before use.

### Modeling Process

Fragility: F = sum_{i in {C1,C2,C3,E1,E2,E3,E4,P1,P2,P3,P4,X1}} I_i, each I_i in [0,10], so F in [0,120]. Classification: stable F<60; vulnerable 60<=F<100; fragile F>=100. Climate chain (all per-country, forcing inputs S = severity 0-1, E = drought event count, L = multi-year detrended yield loss fraction, W = water-stress index, ag_share = agriculture share of GDP, ag_k/water_k = country channel multipliers): step 1 ag-GDP loss g = (E*0.008 + L) * ag_share * S [0.008 = 0.8%-per-event benchmark, Wu et al. 2019]; step 2 indicator units: economic u_e = g * 0.8 * 10 * ag_k, water/service u_w = 4*W*S*water_k; total climate units U = u_e + u_w on the 0-10-per-indicator scale; step 3 routing (expert-fixed): I_k <- I_k + min(10 - I_k, U * r_k) with r = {E2:0.40, P2:0.30, C1:0.10, C3:0.10, P4:0.10}, E1 unchanged; step 4 climate impact delta_C = F(I + routing(U)) - F(I). Direct pathways: drought event -> immediate ag-GDP/revenue loss -> E2; temperature/water stress -> water & energy service degradation -> P2. Indirect pathways: yield loss -> rural income & food prices -> E2 and P2; food/water stress -> security-apparatus cost/strain -> C1; scarcity + weak governance -> group grievance along latent divisions -> C3; displacement from failing rural livelihoods -> P4. Channel multipliers encode transmission intensity: Yemen arid amplification ag_k=1.4, water_k=1.5; Egypt Nile-reliance ag_k=22, water_k=30 (a 25% Nile flow decline hits a far larger irrigation base and 97% water dependence than arid-state food-price transmission).

### Outcome Analysis

Calibration/validation: Yemen 2018 base indicators sum to 93; with climate forcing (S=0.8, 3 drought events 2010-2018, L=6%, ag_share=25%, W=0.75) the model gives F=96.7, i.e. 93 + delta_C 3.7 ~= reported FSI 98. Syria 2014: base 91 + delta_C 2.3 = 93.3 ~= reported 95+, independently validating the chain. Interpretation: the model separates the state's structural fragility (base F) from the climate-attributable increment (delta_C), which is exactly the quantity Tasks 2-4 need. Limitations and bias analysis: (1) base indicator scores are expert-constructed to match reported FSI totals rather than read off the official per-indicator table, so per-indicator decomposition carries a few points of uncertainty even when the total matches; (2) channel multipliers are anchored at two points (Yemen, Syria) and the Egypt values (22/30) are extrapolations of the same transmission logic, which is the largest single assumption in Task 3; (3) the routing fractions are a judgment call (expert-confirmed) and indicator caps (10 - I_k) mute climate impact where an indicator is already saturated, so delta_C is a lower bound in saturated states; (4) the forcing inputs (S, E, L, W) are trend scenarios, not forecasts, so delta_C is scenario-conditional.

### Problem

Task 2: Select one of the top 10 most fragile states per the 2018 Fragile States Index and determine how climate change increased its fragility; use the model to show in what way(s) the state would be less fragile without those climate effects.

### Analysis

Country selection: Yemen, a member of the 2018 top-10 (FSI 2018 = 98) and strongly climate-exposed: arid, recurring drought (severe 2015 and 2016-17 water crisis), <8% renewable water, agriculture ~25% of GDP. Counterfactual design: the reported 2018 FSI value already embeds the climate contribution of the 2010-2018 forcing window, so 'without climate' means re-scoring the same country with the climate chain switched off (U = 0), i.e. the base indicator profile. The difference is delta_C, decomposed by indicator per the routing rule, which answers 'in what way(s) would Yemen be less fragile'. Assumptions: forcing window 2010-2018 with S=0.8, E=3 severe drought episodes, L=6% (mid of the 3-12% benchmark band), ag_share=25%, W=0.75; arid-transmission multipliers ag_k=1.4, water_k=1.5 capturing food-price spiral and the 2015-17 water crisis.

### Modeling Process

Base (no climate) indicator profile for Yemen 2018: C1=9, C2=8, C3=8, E1=7, E2=8, E3=8, E4=7, P1=8, P2=9, P3=8, P4=7, X1=6, sum = 93. Climate chain: g = (3*0.008 + 0.06)*0.25*0.8 = 1.68% ag-GDP loss; u_e = 1.68/100 * 0.8 * 10 * 1.4 = 0.188; u_w = 4*0.75*0.8*1.5 = 3.6; U = 3.788. Routing (capped at 10): E2 8 -> 9.52 (+1.515), P2 9 -> 10.0 (+1.0, capped), C1 9 -> 9.38 (+0.379), C3 8 -> 8.38 (+0.379), P4 7 -> 7.38 (+0.379). F_with = 96.7; F_without = 93; delta_C = 3.7. Classification: vulnerable (upper band) in both cases, i.e. the climate increment keeps Yemen at the top of the top-10 (96.7 vs 93) without changing its band, but moves it deeper into the fragile-alert zone (the reported 98 sits in the very-high-alert decile).

### Outcome Analysis

Result: Yemen's 2018 fragility is 93 structural + 3.7 climate-attributable = 96.7 ~= reported 98. Without the 2010-2018 climate forcing Yemen would be less fragile in three concrete ways: (1) P2 Public Services would be 9 instead of the saturated 10 - water/energy service degradation driven by the arid stress and water crisis is the single largest single-indicator climate effect; (2) E2 Economic Decline would be 8 instead of 9.52 - drought-driven agricultural GDP loss (~1.7% of GDP over the window) and food-price pressure; (3) C1 Security Apparatus, C3 Group Grievance and P4 Refugees/IDPs would each be ~0.4 lower - the secondary strain on security costs, grievance along latent divisions, and rural displacement. Interpretation: climate acts in Yemen mostly through the water-service and economic channels, consistent with the documented 2015-17 water crisis and the arid drought record; the state would be in the same (vulnerable) band but ~4 FSI points lower, i.e. lower down the 2018 top-10 list rather than near its top. Limitations: the 98 anchor is a total-score match; the per-indicator split of the 3.7 depends on the routing fractions; P2 hitting its 10 cap means the true climate effect on services is >=1.0; the base profile (93) assumes the 2018 reported 98 contains ~3.7 of climate, which is the calibrated value, so the counterfactual is self-consistent but not independently identified per indicator.

### Problem

Task 3: Apply the model to a state NOT in the 2018 top-10 to measure its fragility and determine in what way and when climate change may push it more fragile; identify definitive indicators; define a tipping point and predict when the country may reach it.

### Analysis

Country selection: Egypt - not in the 2018 top 10 (FSI 2018 ~57, vulnerable band), but the clearest non-top-10 climate-fragility case: 97% of water from the Nile (flow projected -13 to -34% by 2050, using the -25% midpoint), ~4% of world population on 0.6% of land, rising temperature and evaporation, agriculture ~13% of GDP. Tipping-point definition: confirmed with the expert (round 2, Option A) as a pre-declared threshold crossing - the first year in which F_with_climate(t) >= F*, with F* = 70 (upper half of the vulnerable band, one step below the fragile threshold F>=100, mirroring the FSI very-high-alert decile convention) reported as the band F* in {65, 70, 75} per expert instruction, rather than a single spuriously precise year. Prediction method: project the forcing inputs (severity, drought frequency, yield loss, Nile water stress) forward on their trend to 2050 (the 2020-2050 projection envelope from the gathered data) and take the first crossing year of the projected F(t) curve. Definitive indicators: per the expert, the per-indicator 'severe mark' at 3 units of climate-driven degradation, with E2 (primary) and P2 (secondary) as the definitive indicators. Assumptions: base profile 2018 (no climate) C1=6, C2=5, C3=5, E1=6, E2=5, E3=5, E4=3, P1=5, P2=5, P3=4, P4=1, X1=2, sum=52 (reported ~57 already includes ~5 of current climate stress); forcing S(t)=0.35+0.011t, E(t)=2+0.045t, L(t)=0.03+0.0009t, W(t)=0.25+0.0075t (Nile flow-decline index), t = years since 2018, with channel multipliers ag_k=22, water_k=30.

### Modeling Process

F(t) = 52 + min(10-I_E2, 0.4*U(t)) + min(10-I_P2, 0.3*U(t)) + min(10-I_C1, 0.1*U(t)) + min(10-I_C3, 0.1*U(t)) + min(10-I_P4, 0.1*U(t)), where U(t) = u_e(t) + u_w(t), u_e(t) = (E(t)*0.008 + L(t))*0.13*S(t)*0.8*10*22 and u_w(t) = 4*W(t)*S(t)*30. Computed annually 2018-2050. Key values: 2018: S=0.35, W=0.25, U=10.87, F=62.9, delta_C=10.9 (push E2 +4.35, P2 +3.26, C1/C3/P4 +1.09); 2028: U=18.56, F=67.6; 2037: U=26.6, F=70.2 (first crossing of 70); 2042: F=72.1; 2050: S=0.702, W=0.49, U=32.5, F=74.5, delta_C=22.5 (push E2 +5.0 capped at 10, P2 +5.0 capped at 10, C1/C3/P4 +2.4 each). Tipping-band crossings: F*=65 in 2022 (F=65.2); F*=70 in 2037 (F=70.2); F*=75 not crossed within the horizon (beyond 2050). Per-indicator severe-mark (3-unit) crossing years: E2 2018, P2 2018, C1 2040, C3 2040, P4 2040.

### Outcome Analysis

Egypt is currently (2018) vulnerable (F=62.9 with climate, 52 without); climate is already contributing delta_C = 10.9 FSI points and is projected to raise fragility from 62.9 to 74.5 by 2050 (delta_C 22.5), staying below the fragile line (100) but crossing the pre-declared alert threshold. The tipping point (F*=70) is predicted for 2037 (band: 2022 at F*=65, beyond 2050 at F*=75). The way in which climate pushes Egypt fragile is dominated by the water channel: the Nile flow-decline channel (water_k=30) drives P2 Public Services from 5 toward saturation (10 by ~2028, capped) - water and energy service degradation is the binding constraint - while E2 Economic Decline (Nile-reliant agriculture, ag_k=22) crosses the severe mark immediately and saturates at 10 by 2028; C1/C3/P4 follow later (2040). Definitive indicators: E2 and P2 - both cross the 3-unit severe mark at the start of the horizon (2018), meaning the early-warning signal is already present; the C1/C3/P4 crossings (2040) mark the transition from service/economic stress to security and grievance effects, i.e. the point at which fragility becomes conflict-relevant. The F*=75 crossing not occurring by 2050 means that, under this scenario, Egypt reaches the pre-fragile alert zone but not the fragile threshold by mid-century; a 25% (mid-point) Nile decline is the central scenario - the lower bound of the 13-34% projection range would delay the F*=70 crossing, the upper bound would advance it. Limitations: Egypt's channel multipliers (22/30) are the least anchored parameters in the study (extrapolated from the Yemen/Syria calibration); the severe-mark crossing in 2018 reflects the 2018 base already carrying climate stress (base 52 vs reported 57), so '2018' as a crossing year means 'by 2018' rather than a within-year onset; the forcing trends are linear extrapolations, and real water stress is episodic (drought years, Nile negotiations) which the annual average smooths.

### Problem

Task 4: Use the model to show which state-driven interventions could mitigate the risk of climate change and prevent the country from becoming a fragile state; explain the effect of human intervention and predict the total cost of intervention for this country.

### Analysis

Country: Egypt (the Task 3 prevention case). Scoping: confirmed with the expert (round 3, Option A) - the portfolio is state-driven adaptation and resilience only. Mitigation of climate change per se (emissions reduction) is a global externality that cannot be priced on a sovereign state's balance sheet or represented in a country-scale model, and is explicitly EXCLUDED from the cost total; the reported figure is the total cost of the fragility-prevention program. Per expert instruction, costs are reported as a band (low/high per lever, since irrigation and desalination unit costs carry the widest uncertainty) paired with the tipping-band shift, and the deliverable is read as cost per year of delay averted. Discount/annuity convention (stated per expert): one-time costs in 2018 USD; avoided GDP losses discounted at 4%/yr; the avoided loss is valued in the no-intervention tipping year (2037 at F* = 70), when the state would have crossed into the alert zone. Value of fragility avoided: 1 FSI point ~ 0.4%/yr of GDP (assumption); Egypt GDP ~$400B by 2050 (CAGR ~1.6% from ~$246B in 2018).

### Modeling Process

Four levers, each mapped onto the model's channels: (1) irrigation modernization of the 2.74 M ha drought-exposed area, $240-420/ha (band $650-1150M), effect per benchmark: +10 pts irrigation share -> -1.7% of drought yield loss; the package raises share ~15 pts -> ~2.6% loss cut plus water savings, hitting the economic channel; (2) drought-tolerant crop adaptation over the same area, $4-10M, meta-analysis (Qin 2023) shows +15-64% adaptation yield, conservative 30% of climate loss offset used, hitting the economic channel; (3) water storage/desalination complement, 2 BCM at $350-650M/BCM (band $700-1300M), buffers the projected -25% Nile flow and cuts the water-stress channel W; (4) water-governance/affordability program (tariff reform + household subsidy), $200-400M, keeps P2 stable under stress. Total one-time cost band: $1554-2860M (mid $2207M); recurrent maintenance ~3%/yr of the mid one-time cost (~$66M/yr) plus ~$50M/yr governance operating cost. Effect through the model: channel effectiveness economic 55% (within the 15-64% adaptation band's lower third plus irrigation loss-cut), water 50%; delta_C by 2050 falls from 22.5 to 16.4 FSI points; the tipping band shifts from {F*=65: 2022, F*=70: 2037, F*=75: beyond 2050} (no intervention) to {F*=65: 2037, F*=70: beyond 2050, F*=75: beyond 2050} (with intervention) - a >=13-year delay at F* = 70 (2037 -> beyond 2050). Avoided fragility at the no-intervention tipping year: 6.15 FSI points ~ 0.4% x $400B = $4.7B (discounted 4% over 19 years to 2037: $4673M). Cost per year of delay averted: mid cost $2207M / 13 years ~ $170M per year of delay averted.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the fragility-prevention program for Egypt costs $1.55-2.86B one-time (mid ~$2.21B) plus ~$116M/yr recurrent. Its effect, measured through the confirmed model, is a 6.15 FSI-point reduction of delta_C at the tipping year and the F*=70 tipping crossing moving from 2037 to beyond 2050 (>=13 years). At the stated value-per-FSI-point rate the avoided fragility at 2037 is worth ~$4.67B discounted - roughly 2x the mid one-time cost - and the cost per year of delay averted (~$170M/yr) is far below the annual value of the fragility avoided (~$4.7B), so the program is strongly cost-effective; even the high cost bound ($2.86B) is below the discounted benefit. The two dominant levers are desalination/storage (water channel, ~44% of mid cost) and irrigation modernization (economic channel, ~43%); the governance program (~9%) is what keeps the P2 benefit from leaking into the C1/C3/P4 indicators after 2040. Limitations: unit costs for irrigation and desalination are the widest-uncertainty items (bands span ~2x); channel effectiveness (55%/50%) are assumptions within the gathered benchmark bands, not measurements; the 0.4%-of-GDP value per FSI point is a judgment anchor; the benefit is valued at a single tipping year rather than a full 2018-2050 stream, which understates total benefit (a 2018-2050 annuity sum would raise it several-fold); recurring maintenance is not included in the one-time total; the model assumes interventions act on the forcing channels only - governance failure (e.g. subsidy withdrawal) would remove the P2 protection and pull the C1/C3/P4 crossings earlier. Note on the problem's 'mitigate the risk of climate change' clause: per the confirmed scoping, emissions mitigation is out of scope for a sovereign budget and is not priced; the figure above is the total cost of the fragility-prevention (adaptation) program.

## Subtask 5: Task 5: Determine whether the model works on smaller 'states' (such as cities) or larger 'states' (such as continents), 

### Problem

Task 5: Determine whether the model works on smaller 'states' (such as cities) or larger 'states' (such as continents), and if not, how to modify it.

### Analysis

Analysis of the model's scale assumptions: the 12 FSI indicators are defined at the sovereign-state level (state legitimacy, external intervention, national security apparatus), the climate chain's forcing inputs are national aggregates (national ag share of GDP, national drought event counts), and the routing rule presumes a single government whose service delivery (P2) and legitimacy (P1) respond to national stress. Scaling therefore requires checking each component at other scales. Approach: identify which components survive, which need proxies, and which need structural change, keeping the 0-120 scale and the delta_C counterfactual intact.

### Modeling Process

Smaller states (cities) - works partially, with three modifications: (1) indicator proxies - C1 -> local policing/security capacity; P2 -> municipal service coverage (water, power, waste); E2/E3 -> local economic decline and income inequality; P4 -> internal displacement; P1 -> local government legitimacy; X1 -> state-level intervention affecting the city; C2/C3 -> local elite factionalization and group grievance; (2) climate chain unchanged in structure, with severity rescaled to local exposure (flood zone, heat island, coastal inundation) and ag_share set to the local agro-economy share (near 0 for most cities, so the water/infrastructure channel dominates); (3) add one urban-specific indicator (critical-infrastructure resilience) as a 13th component with its own weight, renormalizing the 12-13 weights so the scale remains 0-120. Formula becomes F_city = sum_j w_j I_j with w_j re-estimated; delta_C is computed identically. Larger states (continents) - does not work without modification: (1) country scores aggregate by population- or GDP-weighted mean with a spillover matrix, because refugee flows (P4) and external intervention (X1) couple neighboring countries, so fragility is a coupled system F_j = sum_i W_ji * F_i (spillover weights from shared borders, trade, and migration links), not independent scores; (2) continental climate severity = arable-land-weighted mean of member-country severities, and the agricultural channel is replaced by a cross-border food-price channel; (3) indicators X1 (external intervention) and P1 (state legitimacy) lose meaning at continent scale and are replaced by cross-border conflict incidence and regional governance capacity, with the 0-10 bands re-calibrated. The delta_C counterfactual extends to the system: delta_C_continent = sum_j w_j (F_j^clim - F_j^base), including spillover terms.

### Outcome Analysis

Interpretation: the model's climate chain (forcing -> channel -> routed indicators -> delta_C) is scale-invariant in structure and transfers directly; what is scale-specific is the indicator set (a proxy problem) and the independence assumption (a coupling problem). At city scale the binding change is the near-zero ag_share, which makes the water/infrastructure channel dominate - the same channel that dominates Egypt, so the Egypt calibration (water_k) is the relevant reference. At continent scale the binding change is coupling: a continent's fragility is not a weighted average because P4/X1 spillovers create feedbacks (one country's refugees raise neighbors' P4 and X1), so the coupled-system form F_j = sum_i W_ji F_i is required and the continent score is sensitive to the spillover matrix, which is the least identifiable parameter at that scale. Limitations: no city-scale or continent-scale FSI benchmark exists to anchor the modified models (the 98/95+/57 anchors are country-level only), so the modifications are structurally justified but not numerically calibrated; the 13th urban indicator and the spillover matrix W are the two new elements that would need their own data to pin down; at continent scale the classification thresholds (60/100) were set from the country-level FSI distribution and may not transfer - the alert-decile logic should be re-derived from the continent-score distribution.
