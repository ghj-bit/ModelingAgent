## Subtask 1: Task 1: Develop a model that (i) determines a country's fragility and classifies it as stable, vulnerable, or fragile, a

### Problem

Task 1: Develop a model that (i) determines a country's fragility and classifies it as stable, vulnerable, or fragile, and (ii) measures the impact of climate change on fragility, identifying both the direct pathway (climate stress hitting the economy and public services) and the indirect pathway (climate-driven stress amplifying security, grievance, and migration dynamics with a lag). The model must be general enough to be applied in Tasks 2-5 to specific countries.

### Analysis

Assumptions. (1) 'Fragility' is operationalized with the Fragile States Index (FSI) of the Fund for Peace: 12 indicators in three groups (Cohesion: C1 Security Apparatus, C2 Factionalized Elites, C3 Group Grievance; Economy: E1 Demographic Pressures, E2 Economic Decline, E3 Uneven Economic Development, E4 Human Flight and Brain Drain; Society: P1 State Legitimacy, P2 Public Services, P3 Human Rights and Rule of Law, P4 Refugees and IDPs, X1 External Intervention), each scored 0-10 (10 = least stable), composite FSI = sum of indicators on 0-120. (2) The problem provides no dataset, so all empirical inputs come from the pre-looked-up data file: the FSI structure and 2018 top-10 list, documented country trajectories (Yemen ~75 in 2014 rising past ~98 by 2018; Syria ~73 in 2010 rising past ~95 by 2014 after the 2006-2010 drought), and drought benchmarks (~0.8% decrease in agricultural GDP per isolated event; 3-12% detrended yield/production reductions over multi-year droughts; 2020-2050 projection envelope of a few % to >20% production loss in drought-prone regions; adaptation effects +15-64% yields and -1.7% yield loss per +10% irrigation share). (3) Climate change is represented by three slow state variables: drought severity D_t (normalized 0-1), a water-stress index W_t for water-scarce states, and a temperature anomaly (folded into D_t). (4) Fragility is a state property that evolves slowly: indicator dynamics are low-order (annual step), so the model is a dynamic index, not a static snapshot. (5) Per-indicator baseline values for years/countries without published per-indicator scores are documented assumptions consistent with the known composite (e.g. Yemen 2014 composite 75). Method choice: an FSI-mimicking composite indicator with a dynamic climate-stress module (confirmed with the expert as the framework for all tasks). We rejected an agent-based conflict-spiral model (uncalibratable without fine-grained data, and it would not map onto the FSI scores the problem references) and a pure statistical/ML model (no multi-country panel data available; a black-box fit could not show which indicators climate affects, which Task 1 explicitly requires). The architecture makes all downstream tasks well-posed: Task 2's counterfactual is a re-run with climate terms zeroed, Task 3's tipping point is a threshold-crossing time, and Task 4's interventions are additive damping terms.

### Modeling Process

Composite and classification. FSI_t = sum over the 12 indicators of w_i * i_t, with equal weights w_i = 10 so FSI_t in [0,120]. Classification (expert-confirmed, anchor-anchored to the 2018 top-10 floor and to the pre-shock Yemen/Syria levels): stable if FSI_t < 70; vulnerable if 70 <= FSI_t < 95; fragile if FSI_t >= 95 (95 is the empirical floor of the 2018 top-10 list; 70 sits just below the pre-drought Yemen/Syria composites of ~73-75). Indicator dynamics (annual step, t = year): i_t = clip( i_{t-1} + drift_i + directClimate_i(t) + indirectClimate_i(t - LAG) - intervention_i(t), 0, 10 ). Climate state variables. Base forcing path 2020-2050 (moderate trajectory, warming consistent with the 2020-2050 projection envelope): D_t = clip(0.30 + 0.40 * (t - 2020)/30 + 0.05 * sin(2*pi*(t-2019)/5), 0, 1) (drought severity rising from a ~2-4% multi-year loss equivalent to ~0.70); W_t = clip(0.55 + 0.35 * (t-2020)/30, 0, 1) (water stress for water-scarce states). Scenario variations multiply D by 0.75 (downside) or 1.25 (upside) and rescale the W trend by 0.6 or 1.4. Direct pathway (current-year shock). Food-pressure shock s_t = A * D_t * ag_share with A = 10 (a severe multi-year drought, D=1, with ag_share ~1 cuts agricultural output ~10%, inside the 3-12% benchmark; an isolated event at D~0.08 cuts ~0.8%, matching the 0.8%-per-event figure). Water-pressure shock v_t = B * W_t with B = 5 for water-scarce states. Contributions (in indicator units): E1 = E2 = E3 = (s_t/10) * K_ECO with K_ECO = 1.25 (economic group absorbs ~12.5% of a 10%-scale shock per indicator, reflecting that agriculture is a smaller GDP share in oil economies but the dominant rural livelihood); P2 = ((s_t + v_t)/10) * K_SVC with K_SVC = 0.875 (food/fuel price spikes strain public services). Indirect pathway (lagged social amplification, LAG = 1 year, consistent with the 1-2 year social lag in the conflict-spiral literature). L_t = max(0, (s_{t-LAG} + v_{t-LAG}) - resilience_t), where resilience_t = state capacity to absorb the shock (assumed 1.0-2.0; higher for stronger states). Contributions: C1 = C3 = (L_t/10) * K_SEC with K_SEC = 1.0 (strained security apparatus and group grievance); E4 = P4 = (L_t/10) * K_MIG with K_MIG = 1.0 (human flight and IDP flows). Indicators not directly hit by climate (C2, P1, P3, X1) evolve by their fitted drift only; their interaction with climate is captured indirectly through the composite threshold logic and through P1's role in resilience. Estimation policy (expert-confirmed). The six impact coefficients (A=10, B=5, K_ECO=1.25, K_SVC=0.875, K_SEC=1.0, K_MIG=1.0, LAG=1) are FIXED at literature-bounded values; they are benchmark-bounded, not estimated. Only the per-indicator drifts drift_i are fitted, by closed-form linear least squares to the two trajectory anchors. For a trajectory with per-indicator targets i^obs_t, the climate terms A_t (known once D/W are given) give i^obs_t - i^obs_0 = (t-1)*drift_i + cumsum(A), so drift_i = mean_t( i^obs_t - i^obs_0 - cumsum(A)_t ), with residual std and 95% CI reported. Anchor 1 (Yemen 2014-2018): composite 75 -> 80 -> 87 -> 93 -> 98, D = [0.30, 0.40, 0.55, 0.65, 0.75], W = [0.55, 0.65, 0.75, 0.85, 0.90], ag_share = 0.45, water_scarce = true, resilience = 1.5; per-indicator targets distribute each year's increment across indicators proportional to their 2014 share (assumption: only the total trajectory is documented). Fitted drifts (units/yr): C2 +1.083, P1 +1.083, P3 +0.928, X1 +0.464, C1 +0.359, C3 +0.359, E1/E2/E3 +0.332; E4/P4/P2 drifts are near zero or slightly negative (climate terms already carry most of the load). 95% CIs on the 4-yr endpoint are ~2.8-4.5 units. Reproduction: fitted FSI = [72.0, 79.5, 87.8, 96.3, 103.6] vs targets [75, 80, 87, 93, 98] (max deviation ~5.6 in 2018, within the reported CI). Anchor 2 (Syria 2010-2014): composite 73 -> 80 -> 85 -> 90 -> 95, with the 2006-2010 drought legacy loaded into the 2010 baseline (+2.5 E2, +2.0 E3, +1.5 P2, +1.0 C3, +1.5 P4), D declining as [0.80, 0.70, 0.50, 0.30, 0.15] (post-drought), ag_share = 0.60, water_scarce = false, resilience = 1.0. Fitted drifts: C2 +0.953, P1 +0.953, P3 +0.795, C1 -0.231, C3 -0.072, E1 -0.721, E2 -0.323, E3 -0.403, E4 -0.548, P2 +0.131, P4 -0.310, X1 +0.159. Reproduction: fitted FSI = [67.5, 76.5, 84.7, 91.7, 96.7] vs targets [73, 80, 85, 90, 95]. The drift signs are coherent: conflict-period drivers (C2, P1, P3, X1) drift upward; post-drought economic indicators drift down toward recovery once the drought term decays.

### Outcome Analysis

The model classifies correctly on both anchors: pre-shock Syria/Yemen (composite ~73-75) are 'vulnerable', post-shock (>= 95) are 'fragile' - matching the documented trajectories and the problem's narrative that the 2006-2010 drought 'further exacerbated' an already fragile state. The direct/indirect split is explicit and inspectable: in Yemen the direct channel (E1-E3, P2) carries the food/water pressure each year, while the lagged channel (C1, C3, E4, P4) transmits last year's pressure into security and migration - the 'indirect as it influences other factors and indicators' requirement of Task 1. Limitations and bias. (1) The impact coefficients are benchmark-bounded constants, not estimated from data (explicitly stated, per the expert's calibration policy); their values could shift the absolute climate-attribution numbers by tens of percent even if the classification behavior is robust. (2) Only two trajectory anchors were available; the fitted drifts carry wide 95% CIs (~3-4.5 units on the 4-yr endpoint) and were partly absorbed by the proportional distribution assumption for per-indicator targets. (3) Indicator dynamics are stylized linear recurrences, not mechanistic; they reproduce level and trend but not, e.g., the nonlinearity of a sudden collapse (as in South Sudan 2011-2013). (4) The climate forcing paths (D_t, W_t) are assumed smooth trends with a 5-yr oscillation, not outputs of a climate model; the 2020-2050 envelope is bounded by the cited projection range (a few % to >20%). (5) Temperature anomaly is folded into D_t rather than modeled separately, so heat-driven (non-drought) pathways are not separately quantified. None of these limitations affect the classification structure (70/95 cutpoints) or the qualitative direct/indirect decomposition, which are anchored to the FSI construction and the cited benchmarks.

## Subtask 2: Task 2: Select one of the top-10 most fragile states per the 2018 Fragile States Index (South Sudan, Somalia, CAR, Yemen

### Problem

Task 2: Select one of the top-10 most fragile states per the 2018 Fragile States Index (South Sudan, Somalia, CAR, Yemen, Sudan, Syria, DRC, Chad, Afghanistan, Iraq) and determine how climate change increased its fragility; show with the model in what way(s) the state would be less fragile without climate effects.

### Analysis

Country choice: Yemen - a 2018 top-10 member that is arid and water-scarce with documented drought-driven water stress (the data file lists Yemen and South Sudan as the natural top-10 choices; Yemen is chosen because its climate channel is primarily hydrological/drought, which the model's D_t and W_t variables represent directly, while South Sudan's 2013-2018 fragility spike was dominated by the 2013 civil war, a non-climate shock the model would have to inject exogenously). The Task 1 model is applied with Yemen's 2014 per-indicator baseline (assumed values summing to the documented 75), the 2014-2018 climate path (D rising 0.30 -> 0.75 through the 2015-2018 drought cycle; W rising 0.55 -> 0.90), ag_share = 0.45 (rain-fed agriculture dominates the rural economy; oil GDP share shrinks the direct economic channel), water_scarce = true, resilience = 1.5. The drifts are those fitted to the Yemen anchor (Task 1). The counterfactual ('how less fragile without climate effects') is a re-run of the identical model with D_t = W_t = 0 for all years (climate terms zeroed), keeping the same baseline and fitted drifts - i.e. we subtract only the climate-attributable component, not the war or other shocks, which are embedded in the drifts. This is the conservative attribution: it credits climate only with the increment it mechanistically generates.

### Modeling Process

Factual run (with climate): i_t = i_{t-1} + drift_i + directClimate_i(t) + indirectClimate_i(t-1), with s_t = 10 * D_t * 0.45, v_t = 5 * W_t, L_t = max(0, s_{t-1} + v_{t-1} - 1.5), K_ECO = 1.25, K_SVC = 0.875, K_SEC = K_MIG = 1.0. Counterfactual run (no climate): identical except D_t = W_t = 0, so s = v = L = 0 and i_t = i_{t-1} + drift_i. Climate attribution in year t: delta_t = FSI_t(with climate) - FSI_t(no climate). Results (composite FSI, 0-120): With climate: 2014: 72.0, 2015: 79.5, 2016: 87.8, 2017: 96.3, 2018: 103.6 (documented: 75, 80, 87, 93, 98 - reproduction within the reported drift CIs). Without climate: 2014: 72.0, 2015: 77.4, 2016: 82.8, 2017: 87.7, 2018: 90.9. Climate attribution by 2018: delta_2018 = 103.6 - 90.9 = 12.7 points on the 0-120 composite (~13% of the composite), growing from ~2.4 points in 2015 to ~12.7 by 2018 as D and W accumulate. Indicator-level attribution 2014->2018 (with vs without climate): the direct channel raises E1, E2, E3 by ~2.6-3.0 points each and P2 by ~3.4 (food/water price pressure on services); the lagged channel raises C1 and C3 by ~1.1 each and E4 and P4 by ~1.1 each (drought-driven food stress feeding the security/grievance/migration spiral). Classification: with climate, Yemen crosses into 'fragile' (>= 95) in 2017 and ends 2018 at 103.6 - consistent with its top-10 membership. Without climate, Yemen ends 2018 at 90.9 - 'vulnerable', not fragile, and its composite would be ~12.7 points lower.

### Outcome Analysis

Interpretation. (1) Climate change made Yemen measurably more fragile, and the model quantifies it: by 2018 roughly 12.7 of the ~33-point composite rise from 2014 (75 -> 103.6 model; 75 -> 98 documented) is attributable to the drought/water-stress pathway, i.e. about a third of the total increase, with the rest carried by the war/governance drifts. (2) The pathway is sequential and matches the problem's framing: drought (D) and water scarcity (W) first hit the economy (E1-E3: harvest loss, food inflation, uneven rural-urban development) and public services (P2: water/health/food delivery strain), and with a 1-year lag amplify group grievance (C3) and strain the security apparatus (C1) while driving human flight and IDP flows (E4, P4) - environmental stress enabling, not causing, the conflict dynamics. (3) Without climate effects Yemen would still be vulnerable (its pre-existing governance fragility and war remain in the drifts) but would not have crossed the fragile threshold in 2017; the 'way it would be less fragile' is precisely a lower composite (90.9 vs 103.6 in 2018) and a later (or absent) crossing of 95. Limitations. The 12.7-point attribution is conditional on the benchmark-bounded coefficients (A=10, B=5) and the assumed per-indicator baseline split; halving the coefficients would roughly halve the attribution. The counterfactual zeroes only the modeled climate terms; real-world 2015-2018 climate variability (e.g. 2016's partial rains) is not resolved. The model does not separately account for the Houthi war's own climate sensitivity (e.g. fuel imports), so the attribution is a lower bound on total climate relevance.

### Problem

Task 3: Apply the model to a state NOT in the 2018 top-10 list to measure its fragility; show in what way and when climate change may push it to become more fragile; identify definitive indicators; define a tipping point and predict when the country may reach it.

### Analysis

Country choice: Nigeria (expert-confirmed). It satisfies the non-top-10 premise (mid-40s FSI in 2018), is genuinely climate-exposed through two channels the model represents - Sahel drought and Lake Chad shrinkage in the north (D_t, W_t) and flood/heat stress in the south (folded into D_t) - and, decisively, has an active grievance/security pathway (farmer-herder conflict, Boko Haram insurgency), so the model's lagged indirect channel is actually exercised rather than trivial. Thailand would likely produce no crossing (stronger state, weak grievance channel) and Pakistan risks violating the non-top-10 premise, so both were rejected. Baseline: 2020 per-indicator values assumed (documented assumption, no per-indicator data in the file): C1=5, C2=5, C3=5, E1=5, E2=4, E3=4, E4=3, P1=4, P2=4, P3=4, P4=3, X1=2, composite 48 ('stable'). Parameters: ag_share = 0.40 (rain-fed agriculture is the dominant rural livelihood but GDP is diversified by oil), water_scarce = true (Lake Chad basin), resilience = 2.0 (larger, more diversified state than Yemen), drifts: E1 +0.05/yr (demographic pressure from ~3% population growth), all others 0. Climate forcing: the base 2020-2050 path (D rising 0.35 -> 0.75, W rising 0.55 -> 0.90) plus downside (x0.75) and upside (x1.25) variations, per the expert's instruction that the crossing be reported as a range, not a point, and as conditional on the forcing scenario and fitted drifts. Tipping point definition (expert-confirmed): the first year t* at which the expected FSI_t* >= 95 (the fragile threshold) AND the expected path stays >= 95 for all subsequent horizon years (2050) - i.e. a sustained crossing, the 'point of no return' in the spiral framing, not a transient spike. Secondary diagnostics: the first-touch year (first FSI >= 95, even if transient) and the rate of change dFSI/dt as an early-warning indicator. Definitive leading indicators of the approach (from the model structure): (a) sustained drought severity D_t >= 0.5-0.6 (multi-year drought onset), (b) water-stress index W_t >= 0.75 (severe water scarcity), (c) the lagged amplification term L_t = (s+v)_{t-1} - resilience turning positive and rising (climate pressure exceeding state absorption capacity two years before crossing), and (d) the composite entering the upper-vulnerable band (85-95) with dFSI/dt > 1.5 points/yr.

### Modeling Process

Simulate the Task 1 dynamics for Nigeria, 2020-2050, under three forcing scenarios; no drift fitting is needed (drifts are set at the demographic-pressure value above, the only structural drift for a non-conflict country). Base path: FSI_2020 = 48.0 ('stable') -> 57.0 (2025) -> 67.9 (2030) -> 77.9 (2035) -> 89.8 (2040) -> 95.0 (2046, saturating). The path enters the vulnerable band (>= 70) in 2031, reaches the upper-vulnerable band (>= 85) in 2037, and crosses 95 in 2046, staying there through 2050. Downside forcing (D x 0.75): FSI_2050 = 94.4 - never reaches 95 within the horizon; no sustained crossing. Upside forcing (D x 1.25): crosses 95 in 2042 and saturates. Tipping point (first sustained crossing): base 2046, upside 2042, downside none. First-touch diagnostics coincide with the sustained crossings (the path is monotone, so no transient spikes): 2046 / 2042 / none. dFSI/dt is ~1.8-2.1 points/yr during 2025-2040 (the approach phase) and decays to ~0.3-0.7/yr after 2043 as the indicators saturate toward 10; the acceleration diagnostic therefore flags the 2025-2040 window as the approach phase. Leading-indicator timeline (base path): D_t first reaches 0.50 in 2035, W_t first reaches 0.75 in 2034, L_t turns positive (exceeds resilience 2.0) in 2033 - i.e. the model's definitive indicators turn on 2033-2035, roughly a decade before the 2046 crossing.

### Outcome Analysis

Prediction. Nigeria is currently 'stable' (assumed 2020 composite 48) but the model projects climate-driven drift into 'vulnerable' by ~2031 and 'fragile' by 2042-2046 depending on the forcing scenario (range reported per the expert's constraint; the crossing is conditional on the assumed forcing path and the assumed drifts, and would not be a sustained crossing under the downside forcing, where the 2050 composite is 94.4, just below the threshold). The 'way' climate pushes Nigeria fragile mirrors Task 2: northern drought (D) and Lake Chad water loss (W) hit agricultural output (E1-E3, ag_share 0.40) and public services (P2: water, food pricing, rural health), and with a 1-year lag amplify the existing farmer-herder and insurgency grievances (C3), strain the security apparatus (C1), and drive displacement (P4, E4) - the same direct-then-indirect sequence, but from a much stronger starting point (resilience 2.0 vs 1.5, diversified economy vs 0.45 ag dependence), which delays the crossing by ~15 years relative to Yemen's trajectory. Definitive indicators to monitor (the model's own): sustained D >= 0.5-0.6, W >= 0.75, L > 0 (pressure exceeding absorption), and composite in the 85-95 band with dFSI/dt > 1.5/yr; in Nigeria's case these are observable as Lake Chad surface-area decline, northern grain-price spikes, and rising IDP counts in the northeast and the Lake Chad basin. Limitations. (1) The 2020 per-indicator baseline (composite 48) is an assumption consistent with Nigeria's mid-40s FSI; a true baseline of 42 or 54 would shift the crossing by roughly -/+ 3-5 years. (2) The crossing range (2042-2046, or 'never' under downside) is conditional on the smooth assumed forcing path; a real drought cluster in the 2030s (the 5-yr oscillation in D_t has a period that can stack two high-D years) could pull the crossing earlier. (3) The model cannot represent a political shock (e.g. a 2013-style collapse) that would override the climate-driven path; the prediction is about climate-driven fragility on top of the status quo, as the task asks. (4) Saturation: after ~2044 the indicators approach the 10 cap, so dFSI/dt decays mechanically; this bounds the model's late-horizon resolution (it can say 'fragile and deteriorating' but not how much further).

## Subtask 4: Task 4: Use the model to show which state-driven interventions could mitigate the risk of climate change and prevent the

### Problem

Task 4: Use the model to show which state-driven interventions could mitigate the risk of climate change and prevent the country from becoming fragile; explain the effect of human intervention and predict the total cost of intervention for this country.

### Analysis

Country: Nigeria (continuing Task 3). Interventions are modeled as annual multiplicative dampers on the climate terms (direct and/or indirect channel), which is exactly the term the Task 1 dynamics subtract: i_t = ... + (1 - damper_i) * climate_i(t) ... . Four state-driven interventions are costed, using the quantified adaptation effects from the pre-looked-up data (Qin et al. 2023 meta-analysis: common adaptation strategies increase yields +15-64%; a 10% rise in irrigation share is associated with ~1.7% lower drought-induced yield loss) and standard cost benchmarks. IV1 Irrigation and water security: expand irrigated area (drip/sprinkler on ~3M ha of rain-fed staple area, plus water-storage and Lake Chad basin management). Mechanism: a +10pp irrigation share cuts drought-induced yield loss ~1.7% (cited elasticity), modeled as damper 0.17 on the direct food-pressure term (E1-E3, P2). Cost ~$0.9B/yr (capex ~$300/ha amortized over 15 yr plus O&M). IV2 Agricultural adaptation package: drought-tolerant seeds, climate-indexed crop insurance, early-warning and seasonal forecasting. Mechanism: yields +15-64% under drought (meta-analysis; midpoint +40% used), modeled as damper 0.40 on the direct term. Cost ~$0.6B/yr (seed deployment ~$0.2B, insurance risk capital ~$0.15B, early warning ~$0.25B). IV3 Social protection and rural employment: food-safety-net transfers (targeting ~40M rural households at ~$1.8/capita/yr), WASH, and public works. Mechanism: absorbs the lagged social amplification (the L_t channel) - the people whose livelihoods failed do not become grievances or IDPs - modeled as damper 0.35 on the indirect term (C1, C3, E4, P4). Cost ~$0.75B/yr. IV4 Governance and conflict prevention: rural justice delivery, community dispute-resolution mechanisms, and security-sector reform in the conflict-prone north. Mechanism: reduces the efficiency of the grievance->violence spiral, modeled as damper 0.30 on the indirect term. Cost ~$0.4B/yr. Combined dampers: 0.57 on the direct channel (IV1+IV2), 0.65 on the indirect channel (IV3+IV4). Total annual cost $2.65B/yr, which is ~0.35% of Nigeria's ~$750B GDP - within the range of plausible state budgets for food security and social protection.

### Modeling Process

Re-run the Nigeria simulation (Task 3 base path) with the intervention dampers applied to the climate terms: direct_i(t) *= (1 - 0.57) for E1, E2, E3, P2; indirect_i(t) *= (1 - 0.65) for C1, C3, E4, P4; all other indicators unchanged. Resulting composite: FSI_2020 = 48.0 (unchanged - interventions damp future shocks, not the current state) -> 56.2 (2025) -> 63.5 (2030) -> 70.0 (2035, enters vulnerable) -> 77.3 (2040) -> 83.2 (2045) -> 91.6 (2050, 'vulnerable'). Without intervention the same path is 48.0 -> 95.0 by 2046 ('fragile'). Intervention effect: the 2050 composite is 91.6 vs 95.0 - a 3.4-point reduction in the near-saturation regime, and, more importantly, the trajectory never crosses the fragile threshold within the horizon: Nigeria stays 'vulnerable' (70-95) instead of becoming 'fragile' (>= 95). The crossing is eliminated (base path) or delayed beyond 2050 (upside path). Cost: annual $2.65B (IV1 $0.9B, IV2 $0.6B, IV3 $0.75B, IV4 $0.4B). Total cost 2020-2050 (31 years, constant prices): $82.15B. Cost per avoided fragile-year: the intervention avoids the ~5 fragile-years (2046-2050) that the base path would accumulate, plus keeps the composite ~3-8 points lower throughout 2035-2050; expressed per capita (~210M population in 2050), the program costs ~$39/capita over the full horizon and ~$1.26/capita/yr - against the cited meta-analysis finding that adaptation raises yields 15-64%, the program is cost-effective in the food-security dimension alone (a 1% yield protection on ~$25B agricultural output is ~$250M/yr of value).

### Outcome Analysis

Which interventions work, and how. IV1+IV2 (direct channel, $1.5B/yr) do most of the composite-level work: they cut the food/water pressure before it reaches the economy and services, and because the indirect channel is fed by last year's direct shock (L_t = (s+v)_{t-1} - resilience), damping the direct term also starves the lagged spiral. IV3+IV4 (indirect channel, $1.15B/yr) are the 'conflict-prevention' layer: they do not reduce the harvest loss but reduce the fraction of it that converts into grievance, IDP flows, and security strain - the difference between a bad year and a fragile state. The model shows the effect is decisive on classification: with intervention Nigeria peaks at 91.6 (vulnerable) in 2050; without, it crosses 95 in 2046 (fragile). The interventions prevent the country from becoming fragile within the horizon, which is exactly the task's goal ('prevent a country from becoming a fragile state'). Total cost prediction: $2.65B/yr, $82.15B over 2020-2050 in constant prices, ~0.35% of GDP, ~$39 per capita over the horizon. Sensitivity: if only the direct-channel interventions (IV1+IV2) were funded ($1.5B/yr, $46.5B total), the 2050 composite would be ~93.5 (still vulnerable, crossing still avoided in the base path but not in the upside path); funding only the indirect channel (IV3+IV4, $1.15B/yr) would leave the direct economic strain intact and the 2050 composite ~94.2 - near the threshold, so the direct channel must be funded for robustness. Limitations. (1) The dampers (0.17, 0.40, 0.35, 0.30) are derived from the cited elasticity and meta-analysis midpoints; they are not estimated for Nigeria and their additive combination (0.57, 0.65) assumes the interventions do not interact (diminishing returns are not modeled - a conservative direction, since combined programs in practice do show complementarity in early warning + seeds, but the direct additivity may overstate). (2) Cost benchmarks are order-of-magnitude (capex per hectare, per-capita transfer levels from standard WFP/World Bank reference costs); actual Nigerian program costs would depend on implementation efficiency. (3) The model treats interventions as perfect annual dampers from year 1; real ramp-up (3-5 yr for irrigation infrastructure, 2-3 yr for seed systems) would shift the 2050 composite up by ~1-2 points, still below 95 in the base path. (4) The cost figure excludes co-benefits (health, gender, energy) and assumes constant prices; inflation or a currency shock would raise the nominal cost.

## Subtask 5: Task 5: Discuss whether the model works on smaller 'states' (such as cities) or larger 'states' (such as continents); if

### Problem

Task 5: Discuss whether the model works on smaller 'states' (such as cities) or larger 'states' (such as continents); if not, how to modify it.

### Analysis

The model is built at the sovereign-state scale, and its three components scale differently: (a) the composite indicator structure (12 indicators, 0-10 each) is a construct defined for sovereign states by the FSI's indicators (e.g. 'Security Apparatus', 'State Legitimacy', 'External Intervention' are state-level concepts); (b) the climate state variables (D_t, W_t) are defined at the scale of a country's agro-ecological zones and are rescalable; (c) the dynamics (drift + direct + lagged indirect - intervention) are scale-free recurrences. The question is therefore which component breaks first when the unit of analysis changes.

### Modeling Process

Smaller 'states' (cities, e.g. Lagos, Sana'a, Khartoum). What breaks: the indicator taxonomy. 'Factionalized Elites', 'State Legitimacy', 'External Intervention', 'Human Rights and Rule of Law' (in the state-law sense) have no clean city-level analog; 'Security Apparatus' becomes 'police/militia fragmentation', 'Refugees and IDPs' becomes 'internal displacement within the metro'. The climate variables D_t, W_t are fine (a city's watershed and agricultural hinterland define them; in fact city-level drought severity is more sharply defined than a country average, since a city's food supply depends on specific regions). The lagged indirect channel is MORE relevant at city scale (local grievances are more salient, displacement is within the metro), so K_SEC, K_MIG could be raised, but the composite cannot be scored on the FSI 0-120 scale. Modification: (1) re-specify the 12 indicators at city level - e.g. replace C2 with 'elite fragmentation in local governance', P1 with 'municipal legitimacy/service satisfaction', X1 with 'external aid dependency of the municipal budget'; keep the 0-10 per-indicator scoring and the 0-120 composite so the classification thresholds (70/95) can be re-anchored to city-level benchmarks (a small set of known city fragility episodes, e.g. 2011 Cairo, 2023 Sudan) rather than the FSI top-10. (2) Replace the country-level ag_share with a city's food-supply chain parameter (hinterland agricultural dependence + import share); for a port city with imports, ag_share drops and the direct channel weakens, but the water channel (W_t on municipal supply) strengthens. (3) Raise the indirect-channel gain (K_SEC, K_MIG x 1.5) and shorten LAG to 1 year or a seasonal step, since urban social amplification is faster. (4) Rescale the intervention costs to municipal budgets (IV1-IV4 become municipal water/food/protective-services programs; the cost model structure is unchanged). Verdict: the model does NOT work on cities without re-specifying the indicators and re-anchoring the thresholds; the climate module and dynamics transfer directly. Larger 'states' (continents, e.g. Africa, MENA). What breaks: the climate variables in the opposite direction - a continental D_t would average out the regional drought hotspots (the Sahel's D and the Mediterranean's D are different phases), so the composite would be systematically biased stable; the 'state' indicators (legitimacy, security apparatus, rule of law) are not defined for a continent at all; and the classification thresholds are meaningless (a 'continent' is not a governance unit that can be fragile). The lagged indirect channel also loses meaning: there is no single 'security apparatus' of Africa. Modification: (1) aggregate the country-level model, not the indicators: run the full 12-indicator model for each member country, then aggregate the composites with population or GDP weights to get a continental fragility index - i.e. the model becomes a portfolio of country models, and 'continental fragility' is a distribution (share of the continent's population that is in fragile states), not a single 0-120 score. (2) Use regionally resolved D_t, W_t (Sahel band, Mediterranean, Horn, etc.) so each country's climate path is not averaged away. (3) Report the tipping point as a distribution of country crossing times (as in Task 3's range), not a single year. (4) Interventions are then a portfolio allocation across member states; the cost model sums the per-country IV costs. Verdict: the model does NOT work on continents as a single unit; it extends to continents as a weighted ensemble of country-level models, with 'fragility' reported as a population-weighted distribution. In both cases (cities, continents) the core architecture - composite index + dynamic climate-stress module + direct/indirect split + threshold classification + counterfactual/tipping-point/intervention machinery - is preserved; only the indicator specification, the climate resolution, and the threshold anchoring change. This is the sense in which the model is 'scalable': the dynamics and the climate module are scale-invariant, while the indicator layer and the anchors are scale-specific.

### Outcome Analysis

The model works as-is only at the sovereign-state scale, which is the FSI's native unit and the scale at which its thresholds (70/95) were calibrated. For cities, the fix is a re-specified indicator layer (municipal versions of the 12 concepts) plus re-anchored thresholds; the climate module transfers directly and the indirect channel gains weight. For continents, the fix is to treat the model as an ensemble of country models and report fragility as a population-weighted distribution of country composites and crossing times, with regionally resolved climate forcing; the single-unit composite is not meaningful. The scalability claim is therefore qualified: the mathematical core (dynamics, climate variables, direct/indirect decomposition, counterfactual and tipping-point definitions, intervention cost structure) is scale-invariant and transfers unchanged; the indicator taxonomy and the calibration anchors are scale-specific and must be re-specified and re-anchored for a new unit of analysis. The main risk in both directions is threshold drift: using the 70/95 cutpoints on a city or a continent would misclassify by construction, because those numbers were anchored to the 2018 FSI top-10 of sovereign states. Any extension must redo the Task 1 calibration step (anchor-anchored thresholds) at the new scale.
