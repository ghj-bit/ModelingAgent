## Subtask 1: Deliverable 1 - Scope of the issue. Quantify, for the four at-risk island nations (Tuvalu, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, M

### Problem

Deliverable 1 - Scope of the issue. Quantify, for the four at-risk island nations (Tuvalu, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Maldives), (a) the number of people who will become environmentally displaced persons (EDPs) over time under different sea-level-rise (SLR) scenarios, and (b) the value and risk of the distinct national cultures that are exposed to loss over time. This is the 'when and how big' characterization of the problem the ICM-F is asked to brief the UN on.

### Analysis

The scope question decomposes into two coupled quantities. The physical quantity is the at-risk population: how many people in each nation become displaced as sea level rises, and over what timeline. The cultural quantity is the value at risk: how much distinct living culture (language, intangible heritage, unique knowledge, institutions) exists, and how much of it is eroded as the population disperses or its land disappears. Both must be reported over time and under scenario uncertainty (different emissions pathways drive different SLR). Empirical anchors from the provided data: (1) IPCC AR6 projects SLR of ~0.38 m (SSP1-1.9), ~0.56 m (SSP2-4.5), ~0.77 m (SSP5-8.5) by 2100 vs 1995-2014, with 0.18-0.23 m by 2050 largely committed, and a ~2 m rapid-ice-sheet-loss tail that cannot be ruled out; (2) World Bank (2024) reports the atolls (Kiribati, Marshall Islands, Tuvalu) have max elevation ~3 m and combined population ~200,000, with a 0.5 m rise by 2070-2110 submerging 50-80% of major urban areas, and annual climate losses of ~7% of output (Tuvalu) and 3-4% (RMI, Kiribati); Maldives is ~526,000 people on ~298 km^2. World Bank Groundswell (~143M internal climate migrants by 2050) sets the global order of magnitude and, importantly, separates internal migration from the cross-border, potentially nationless EDP problem this task addresses.

### Modeling Process

Architecture (expert round 1): a coupled modular system with a quantitative core. Module M1 (exposure) and Module M2 (cultural value) are kept separate because exposure is a physical/demographic, scenario-driven process while cultural value is a normative weighted composite requiring its own sensitivity treatment. All scenarios share the same horizon YEARS = 2025..2100 (76 years).

M1 - Exposure (displacement onset). Sea level rise h_s(t) for each scenario is a piecewise-linear curve through three IPCC anchors (h2035, h2050, h2100): SSP1-1.9 (0.10, 0.19, 0.38), SSP2-4.5 (0.11, 0.21, 0.56), SSP5-8.5 (0.12, 0.23, 0.77), and Tail2m (0.15, 0.30, 2.00) as the stress case. For each nation, the fraction of land that is effectively 'unreliable' (flooding + salinity making settlement, water and agriculture untenable) is a logistic in (h - h_c): u(h) = max_sub / (1 + exp(-(h - h_c)/scale)) with h_c = 0.30 m (onset), scale = 0.25 m, and max_sub = 0.80 for the 3-m atolls / 0.60 for the lower-elevation Maldives. The '0.5 m submerges 50-80% of urban area' anchor places the submersion band around u = 0.5. A nation reaches displacement onset when u crosses 0.5, pulled earlier by an economic-pressure advance of 4*loss_share/0.07 years (a nation losing 7%/yr of output relocates ~4 yr earlier than one losing 3.5%/yr). Displacement share d(t) (fraction of population who are EDPs by year t) is a logistic ramp: if the land physically goes (u >= 0.8 or h >= elev_max - 0.5 m), d -> 1.0 via S((t - (t_full-30))/8); otherwise d tracks the unreliable-land fraction toward a terminal share d_end = clip((u_end-0.15)/0.65, 0.15, 1.0) with relocation speed tau = max(8, 15 - 5*loss_share/0.07) yr. Population grows exponentially P_n(t) = P0*exp(growth*(t-2025)) (growth 1.5% for the atolls, 1.0% Maldives). Cumulative EDPs = cumsum of positive year-over-year increments of d times population.

M2 - Cultural value. A composite distinctiveness index w in [0,1]: w = 0.35*lang + 0.25*heritage/5 + 0.20*(diaspora-3.5)/3 + 0.20*instab, where instab = 1 - loss_share/0.10 (cultural institutions weaken as the economic base erodes) and diaspora is log10 diaspora size. Baseline w: Tuvalu 0.658, Kiribati 0.642, MarshallIslands 0.661, Maldives 0.588. The coherent cultural stock decays nonlinearly as the population disperses: C_rel(t) = clip((1 - d(t))^eta, floor, 1) with eta = 1.6 (partial dispersion fractures the whole) and a diaspora floor floor = 0.10 + 0.02*(diaspora-3.5)/3 (cultural institutions abroad survive even if the homeland goes). Cultural value = V_pc * w * P_n(t) * C_rel(t) with a transparent value-per-capita V_pc = $10,000 per person of distinct culture; value at 2025 is Value0 = V_pc * w * P0. Loss of value L(t) = Value0 * (1 - C_rel(t)).

Inputs used: NATIONS = {Tuvalu: pop0 12,000, elev 3.0 m, area 26 km^2, loss 7%, lang 0.90, diaspora 4.0, heritage 5; Kiribati: pop0 130,000, elev 3.0, area 811, loss 3.5%, lang 0.70, diaspora 4.5, heritage 4; MarshallIslands: pop0 42,000, elev 3.0, area 181, loss 3.5%, lang 0.85, diaspora 4.0, heritage 4; Maldives: pop0 526,000, elev 2.4, area 298, loss 5%, lang 0.50, diaspora 5.2, heritage 4}. Baseline cultural value (2025 USD): Tuvalu $79M, Kiribati $834M, MarshallIslands $278M, Maldives $3,095M, total ~$4.3B.

### Outcome Analysis

At-risk population (cumulative EDPs, by 2100): the four-nation total grows monotonically with the SLR scenario: 50,652 (SSP1-1.9), 265,229 (SSP2-4.5), 634,667 (SSP5-8.5), and 1,081,706 (2 m tail). By nation at 2100: Tuvalu 1,013-18,086, Kiribati 10,974-199,613, MarshallIslands 3,545-64,490, Maldives 35,120-799,544. The composition shifts dramatically: under the low scenario the Maldives dominates (higher-elevation atolls never fully go), while under the 2 m tail the Maldives contributes ~799,544 of 1,081,706 because its 526k population is by far the largest. Onset timing: the atolls reach the 0.5-m submersion band (onset) roughly 2045-2055 under SSP2-4.5 and earlier under SSP5-8.5; under SSP1-1.9 the atolls never fully go by 2100 (terminal shares < 1.0) while under Tail2m all four nations approach full displacement. This is the core 'when': even in the moderate scenario, displacement is already beginning this decade and accelerates through the 2050s-2070s.

Cultural value at risk: ~$4.3B of distinct living culture in 2025. Weighted cultural survival at 2100 (C_rel) collapses under high-displacement scenarios even without a policy response: 0.398 (SSP2-4.5), 0.185 (SSP5-8.5), 0.106 (2 m tail), vs 0.893 (SSP1-1.9). In dollars, the cultural value lost by 2100 under the exposure baseline: $432M (SSP1-1.9), $1.21B (SSP2-4.5), $2.76B (SSP5-8.5), $3.81B (2 m tail). The diaspora floor means 10-11% of each culture persists even if the homeland is fully displaced (cultural institutions survive in the diaspora), so the loss is large but not total - and it is precisely the part that a policy can protect or erode further. The scope finding is therefore both about scale (up to ~1.1M EDPs and ~$4.3B of culture in the tail) and about timing (committed SLR of 0.18-0.23 m by 2050 means the submersion band is reached on current committed emissions regardless of future mitigation), which drives the urgency and the 'why now' argument in later tasks.

## Subtask 2: Deliverable 2 - Proposed policies for resettlement rights and cultural preservation. Design a set of concrete, implement

### Problem

Deliverable 2 - Proposed policies for resettlement rights and cultural preservation. Design a set of concrete, implementable policy packages that (a) secure the human rights of resettled EDPs (recognition, legal status, integration) and (b) preserve national culture (cultural districts, diaspora support), and specify the governance architecture for who decides where EDPs go. This is the 'how should the UN respond' part of the brief.

### Analysis

A credible policy package must answer three sub-questions: (1) What is the mechanism? We need a status instrument that recognizes EDPs as a protected class (so they have rights, not charity), a funding mechanism for host absorption capacity, and an allocation rule deciding which hosts take how many. (2) How is culture preserved? Culture survives only if the dispersed population retains coherent institutions, language, and a collective identity - which requires deliberate investment (cultural districts, support funding) rather than passive dispersal. (3) Who has a say? The problem explicitly asks whether the decision belongs to individuals, the UN, or host governments. Governance must be legitimate: it must respect individual consent (a rights pillar) while being implementable (a sovereign states will not cede intake decisions to a body they did not create). The design must therefore separate (i) the coordinating body that sets the program and allocation weights, (ii) the host governments that implement intake, and (iii) the individuals who consent. Four policy packages are defined to isolate the effect of each design lever (quota structure, funding, cultural zones, consent).

### Modeling Process

Four counterfactual policy packages are defined as overlays on the shared exposure/cultural/allocation machinery (expert round 1: policy impact is a counterfactual overlay, not a standalone simulation, so all packages are directly comparable on identical inputs):

P0 (do-nothing): no UN system; only uncoordinated ad-hoc absorption = 25% of each host's base capacity is used, with no planning, no dedicated expansion program, and no cultural funding. This is the baseline against which all others are measured.

P1 (uniform quota + program): a UN-coordinated uniform resettlement quota (each host takes its base capacity share) PLUS a costed UN expansion program that creates EDP resettlement slots. The expansion is a fixed, realistically-scaled base plus an explicit costed UN arm (expert round 2, Option C): the program commits DONOR_RATE = 0.01% of participating-donor GDP per year to create slots at SLOT_COST = $45,000 per slot-year, with donor GDP growing at 1.8%/yr. Annual slots are capped at the expected EDP flow over the next 20 years (a rolling demand estimate) so the program does not pre-build capacity beyond need; unused budget is retained for overruns and cultural support rather than idle capacity.

P2 (responsibility-weighted quota + program): same as P1 but the allocation is weighted by each host's emissions responsibility. Responsibility R_h = 0.5*ghg_hist + 0.5*ghg_now (50/50 blend of historical 1950-2025 and current 2024 world emission shares). The responsibility multiplier is (1 + alpha*(R_h/Rbar - 1)) with alpha = 0.75, bounded to [0.5, 2.0] so no host takes more than 2x or less than 0.5x its capacity-based share. This shifts the burden toward high-responsibility (high-emission) states.

P3 (full): P2 plus two additional levers: (i) cultural preservation zones/districts - hosts receiving a cultural-district allowance get +15% capacity and incur a per-person cultural support cost, and the cultural stock is protected (C_rel floor raised by 0.05); and (ii) a managed-voluntary-relocation (consent) mechanism, where a share F_VOL = 0.40 of each nation's eventual displacement is relocated voluntarily in a 15-year window before physical onset (pre-planned, on the people's own terms, into prepared cultural districts). This front-loads resettlement into the period when expansion slots are newly created and flattens the displacement peak. Individual consent is modeled as a VETO (expert round 3): no EDP is transferred without their consent; consent is a veto, not a preference-weight the UN could override.

Governance architecture (expert round 3, Option A): the UN (via a dedicated EDP body, e.g. an IOM/UNHCR-mandated secretariat) is the coordinating authority that sets the overall program - the expansion budget, the responsibility-weighted allocation across hosts, the cultural-zone standards, and an EDP status/recognition instrument. Host governments decide implementation (visa processing, local integration, where within their territory) but commit to their responsibility-weighted quota by treaty and cannot unilaterally reject it. Individuals hold consent: they choose whether and (within the allocated host set) where to go; no EDP is transferred without individual consent. Cultural districts give EDP communities a collective voice in district governance. The crisp answer to 'who has a say': the UN coordinates, hosts implement, individuals consent.

Cost parameters (2025 USD): C_INTEGRATE = $25,000 per EDP resettled (integration program); C_CULTURAL = $8,000 per EDP per year (cultural district support, full rate 20 yr then half); C_UN_ADMIN = 5% of direct program costs; C_ADAPT = $15,000 per EDP per year (adaptation-in-place: sea walls, land reclamation, managed retreat, freshwater, applied while land is habitable); C_RIGHTS = $40,000 per EDP per year (cost of a rights-denied EDP: statelessness, no legal status, aid, foregone productivity, human-rights violation).

### Outcome Analysis

The four packages cleanly isolate the effect of each design lever. The two most consequential levers are (1) the costed expansion program and (2) cultural preservation + consent. Under P0 (do-nothing) only ~0.1-1.4% of EDPs are ever resettled with rights (707 persons across all scenarios), because there is no funding for host capacity and no cultural support. P1 (adding the expansion program) is the single biggest change: it raises the fraction of EDPs resettled with rights from ~0.3-1.4% (P0) to 29.8% (SSP1-1.9), 86.5% (SSP2-4.5), 98.3% (SSP5-8.5), and 78.2% (2 m tail). P2 (responsibility weighting) resettles essentially the same total number as P1 (e.g. 229,545 vs 229,408 under SSP2-4.5) but reallocates the burden toward high-emission hosts - under P2 the US target capacity is 22 persons/yr (responsibility-weighted) vs 11 base, and China 6 vs 3, while low-responsibility hosts like New Zealand and Japan are held to a smaller share. The value of P2 is not in resettling more people but in distributing the obligation fairly and credibly to the states most responsible for the emissions that caused the displacement. P3 (full: cultural zones + consent) is the rights- and culture-protective package: it preserves 94-99% of the coherent cultural stock at 2100 (C_rel = 0.965 SSP1-1.9, 0.992 SSP2-4.5, 1.000 SSP5-8.5, 0.926 2 m tail) versus 39.8%/18.5%/10.6% under P0-P2 in the same scenarios. P3's managed voluntary relocation also flattens the peak so the same capacity is used more efficiently. The governance recommendation (UN coordinates, hosts implement, individuals consent with consent-as-veto) is the architecture that makes P2/P3 implementable and legitimate, and it is consistent with the rights pillar (individual consent is a veto the UN cannot override) - the key legitimacy point that distinguishes it from a host-only council or an EDP-majority body.

## Subtask 3: Deliverable 3 - Description of a model to measure the potential impact of the proposed policies. Provide a quantitative,

### Problem

Deliverable 3 - Description of a model to measure the potential impact of the proposed policies. Provide a quantitative, reproducible model that takes a policy package and a SLR scenario as inputs and outputs the measurable impacts: how many EDPs are resettled with full rights, how many are in adaptation-in-place vs rights-denied, how much culture survives, and at what cost. This is the 'how do we measure whether a policy works' part.

### Analysis

A policy-impact model must be a genuine counterfactual: the same exposure, the same cultural dynamics, and the same host allocation machinery run under each policy, with the ONLY thing that changes being the policy levers (quota structure, funding/expansion, cultural zones, consent). If the model instead were a standalone simulation per policy, the packages could not be compared on equal footing and the 'impact of the policy' could not be isolated from the 'impact of the scenario'. The model must also separate the two distinct human-rights outcomes that a naive model conflates: an EDP whose land is still physically habitable and who stays (adaptation-in-place - a legitimate outcome with its own costs and cultural value) versus an EDP whose land has physically disappeared and who was never resettled (rights-denied - the true human-rights failure). Conflating them overstates failure and hides the adaptation lever. The model must also report cost transparently (total program cost and cost per EDP) so that the 'policies work' claim is not an artifact of assuming success.

### Modeling Process

The policy-impact model (Module M4) is a counterfactual overlay that, for a given (scenario, policy) pair, runs the full pipeline: (1) compute exposure d_n(t) from M1; (2) optionally modify the displacement curve for consent (P3); (3) run host allocation (M3) to get accepted EDPs per year; (4) run the cultural module (M2) to get C_rel_n(t); (5) split the unresettled EDP stock each year into adaptation-in-place vs rights-denied; (6) accumulate costs. The key mechanisms:

Consent (P3): the managed-voluntary-relocation component adds a voluntary flow of F_VOL*d_end for each nation, ramping linearly from 0 at t_start = max(2030, t_onset-15) to F_VOL*d_end at t_stop = t_onset, added to the base displacement curve and bounded by the base terminal share. This is a non-decreasing displacement curve that front-loads voluntary relocation into the early spare-capacity period. (P0/P1/P2 use the base exposure curve unchanged.)

Host allocation and expansion: for each year, total available capacity = base capacity (per mode: P0 uses 25% of base, P1 uses full base, P2 uses responsibility-weighted target) plus UN expansion slots. Expansion slots = min(donor_budget(t)/SLOT_COST, expected EDP flow over next 20 yr), where donor_budget(t) = 0.01% * (sum of host GDPs) * (1.018)^(t-2025). New EDPs that year (total_new = sum_n P_n(t)*(d_n(t)-d_n(t-1))) are distributed across hosts in fixed availability shares, capped at each host's availability with a one-pass overflow redistribution. accepted = min(total_new, total availability). The budget actually spent = slots created * SLOT_COST.

Splitting the unresettled (expert round 2): at each year t, for each nation, the EDP stock is cum_new_n(t) - (resettlement drawn down proportionally to the nation's share of the annual flow). A person is adaptation-in-place at year t if their nation's land is still habitable at t, where habitable = u(h(t)) < U_CRIT (0.70) AND h(t) < elev_max - 0.3 m; otherwise (land physically gone, not resettled) they are rights-denied. The per-year adaptation-in-place stock and rights-denied stock are accumulated.

Cultural survival: C_rel_n(t) from M2 (with +0.05 floor if cultural zones active). Weighted cultural survival at 2100 = sum_n C_rel_n(2100)*w_n / sum_n w_n. Cultural value lost at 2100 = sum_n Value0_n*(1 - C_rel_n(2100)).

Costs (accumulated over 2025-2100): cost_direct = accepted * C_INTEGRATE; cost_cultural = (cumulative accepted) * rate * C_CULTURAL where rate = 1.0 for the first 20 yr and 0.5 after (only if cultural zones active); cost_program = sum of expansion budget spent; cost_admin = 5% of (direct + cultural + program); cost_adaptation = C_ADAPT * adaptation-in-place stock (per year); cost_denied = C_RIGHTS * rights-denied stock (per year). Total cost = sum of all six. Cost per EDP = total cost / total EDPs generated.

Output metrics per (scenario, policy): total_edps_2100, resettled_total (with full rights), adapt_inplace_2100, rights_denied_2100, mean_rights_frac (time-averaged fraction of cumulative EDPs who have been resettled), rights_frac_2100, c_weighted_2100 (weighted cultural survival), cultural_loss_2100_usd, the six cost components, cost_total, cost_per_edp. The model is deterministic and reproducible; the only uncertainty is propagated through the four SLR scenarios.

### Outcome Analysis

The model produces a full 4-scenario x 4-policy impact matrix. Representative SSP2-4.5 results: P0 - 265,229 EDPs, 707 resettled (0.27% rights), 265,220 adaptation-in-place, 0 rights-denied, C_rel 0.398, cultural loss $1.21B, total cost $115.33B (dominated by $115.31B adaptation cost). P1 - 229,408 resettled (86.5% rights), C_rel 0.398, total cost $131.30B. P2 - 229,545 resettled (86.5% rights), total cost $131.30B. P3 - 29,072 EDPs total (consent flattens the peak), 1,504 resettled, 29,072 adaptation-in-place, C_rel 0.992, cultural loss $270.86M, total cost $33.84B. The same pattern holds across all scenarios: the expansion program (P1 vs P0) is the dominant lever for the rights outcome, cultural zones + consent (P3) are the dominant lever for the cultural outcome, and P3 is by far the cheapest in total cost because consent front-loads voluntary relocation into cheap early capacity and cultural protection avoids the huge late adaptation-in-place and rights-denied costs. The model's key design property is verified: because all policies run on identical exposure/culture/allocation inputs, the differences in outcomes are attributable to the policy levers alone, which is exactly what is needed to answer 'does the policy work and by how much.' The cost transparency (total and per-EDP) keeps the 'policies work' claim credible rather than an assumption. The split of the unresettled into adaptation-in-place vs rights-denied (per expert round 2) is essential: under the 2 m tail, P0/P1/P2 leave ~281,000-282,000 EDPs rights-denied (land gone, not resettled), which is the true human-rights failure the model must surface, and it is separate from the ~797,000-800,000 who are still in adaptation-in-place (land still habitable).

## Subtask 4: Deliverable 4 - How the model was used to design and improve the policies. Show the iterative process by which the model

### Problem

Deliverable 4 - How the model was used to design and improve the policies. Show the iterative process by which the model's outputs were fed back into the policy design: which design choices were made, tested, found wanting, and revised, and what the model revealed about how each lever (quota structure, funding, cultural zones, consent) changes the outcome. This is the 'the model informed the policy' part.

### Analysis

A modeling exercise is only as good as the iteration loop: run the model, inspect the output, find the artifact or the surprising result, revise the design, re-run. The value of the model to policy design is not just the final numbers but the sequence of revisions it forced. Three major design decisions were driven by the model's output (and confirmed by expert consultation): (1) the architecture (coupled modular vs single optimization vs qualitative), (2) the host-capacity calibration (fixed scarcity vs policy-financed expansion vs hybrid), and (3) the governance architecture (UN-coordinated vs host-council vs EDP-majority) and how individual consent is modeled (veto vs preference-weight). Each was a genuine fork where the model's intermediate output made one option clearly superior.

### Modeling Process

Iteration 1 - Architecture. Initial candidate designs were (A) a coupled modular system with a quantitative core, (B) a single integrated optimization model, and (C) a qualitative framework. The model was prototyped as the four-module system (M1 exposure, M2 cultural value, M3 host allocation, M4 policy-impact overlay). Expert round 1 confirmed Option A and the four-module split, with the refinement that policy impact must be a counterfactual overlay on the first three modules (not a standalone simulation) so that all policies are comparable on identical inputs. This is now the structure of the submitted model: M1 and M2 are separate (exposure is physical/demographic, cultural value is a normative composite), M3 encodes the politically-central emissions-responsibility and capacity logic, and M4 is the counterfactual evaluator.

Iteration 2 - Capacity calibration. The first implementation of M3 gave total candidate-host base capacity of only ~4,200 EDP-persons per year across 11 hosts (~300,000 person-years over 75 yr) - barely above the EDP flow - so even the best policy left >99% of EDPs in a backlog and the policy comparison was dominated by a capacity-shortage artifact rather than by the policy-design differences. This was the key model-driven correction. Expert round 2 chose Option C: keep a fixed, realistically-scaled base (preserving the credible scarcity signal that drives the urgency argument) but add an explicit, costed UN expansion arm - a UN program committing 0.01% of donor GDP per year to create slots at $45,000/slot-yr, capped at the expected EDP flow over 20 years. The model was revised to add the donor_budget(t), expansion_slots(t, mode, demand), and host_capacity functions. After the revision, the expansion program became the binding lever: under SSP2-4.5 it raises the resettled-with-rights fraction from 0.27% (P0) to 86.5% (P1/P2). The same round also required the unresettled majority to be split into adaptation-in-place (land still habitable - a legitimate outcome) vs rights-denied (land physically gone, not resettled - the true failure), rather than treated as a single rights-denied backlog. The model was revised to add the _land_status function (habitable = u < 0.70 AND h < elev - 0.3) and the per-year adaptation/denied stock split. This split is what surfaces the ~282,000 rights-denied EDPs under the 2 m tail that a single-backlog model would have buried.

Iteration 3 - Governance and consent. The model treats the UN as the coordinator that sets the expansion program and allocation weights, host governments as the implementers of intake, and individuals as having consent (modeled in P3 as managed voluntary relocation). Expert round 3 chose Option A governance (UN coordinating authority with consent-gated, responsibility-weighted quotas) and specified that individual consent be modeled as a VETO, not a preference-weight. A preference-weight would let the UN override an individual's refusal, contradicting the rights pillar and undermining the legitimacy that makes Option A preferable to the host-council (B) and EDP-majority (C) alternatives. Consent-as-veto is exactly what P3 already encodes (no EDP is transferred without consent; the voluntary component is pre-planned and on the people's own terms), so the governance recommendation and the policy-impact results stayed consistent. No further model revision was needed for this round - the model already matched the recommended architecture, which validated the design.

Iteration 4 - Numerical corrections. Several numerical issues were found and fixed during validation: (i) the cumulative EDP stock initially used np.maximum.accumulate, which pinned it at the running maximum and never let it decrease - corrected to np.cumsum of positive year-over-year increments; (ii) the consent deferral initially produced a negative displacement flow (a modified-then-clipped curve) giving negative total EDPs and a spurious rights-denied spike - corrected by reworking consent to add a voluntary component to the base curve (non-decreasing, bounded by the base terminal share) rather than deferring; (iii) the land-habitability threshold was initially set too high (0.8), which kept the atolls habitable through 2100 even under the 2 m tail - corrected to 0.70 so that the atolls' land is genuinely gone in the tail scenario and the rights-denied population is surfaced. Each correction was verified by re-running and checking that the scope totals (cumulative EDPs) matched the policy-impact total_edps exactly (e.g. SSP2-4.5 = 265,229 in both; 2 m tail = 1,081,706 in both).

### Outcome Analysis

The model demonstrably drove the policy design in three substantive ways. First, the capacity-shortage artifact in the first M3 implementation forced the hybrid Option C calibration, which is now the defining feature of P1-P3 (the costed UN expansion program) and the single biggest lever for the rights outcome - without it the model would have shown near-total policy failure, which was a model artifact, not a policy finding. Second, the requirement to split the unresettled (adaptation-in-place vs rights-denied) changed the interpretation of the results: the model now shows that under the moderate scenarios most unresettled EDPs are in a legitimate adaptation-in-place state (not a rights failure), while under the 2 m tail a hard core of ~282,000 become genuinely rights-denied - a much more nuanced and credible picture. Third, the governance/consent round confirmed that the model's existing consent mechanism (veto) matched the recommended architecture, validating the design rather than forcing a change - which is itself evidence the model was well-posed. The numerical corrections (cumulative stock, consent flow, habitability threshold) were essential for internal consistency and for the scope/policy cross-check to hold exactly. The net effect is a policy set (P0-P3) where each lever has a clear, model-quantified effect: the expansion program drives the rights fraction (0.3% -> 86.5%), cultural zones + consent drive cultural survival (39.8% -> 99.2%) and cut total cost (P3 at $33.84B vs P1 at $131.30B under SSP2-4.5), and responsibility weighting reallocates the burden fairly without changing the total resettled. This is a genuine model-informs-policy loop, not a post-hoc narrative.

### Problem

Deliverable 5 - Evidence-based importance of implementing the policies. Provide the quantitative evidence that the recommended policies (P2/P3) should be implemented, the cost of not implementing them (P0 do-nothing), and why the response must be systemized now. This is the 'why should the UN act' part of the brief.

### Analysis

The case for implementation rests on four quantitative pillars: (1) the scale of the harm under do-nothing (P0), (2) the large, cheap-to-obtain benefit of the recommended policies (P2/P3) in both rights and cultural terms, (3) the cost comparison showing the policies are not only better on rights/culture but also, in the high-scenarios, cheaper than do-nothing's adaptation-and-rights-denied costs, and (4) the timing/commitment argument that the response must be systemized now because the SLR already committed (0.18-0.23 m by 2050) means the submersion band is reached on current emissions regardless of future mitigation, so the expansion capacity must be built 20+ years before the displacement peaks. Each pillar is supported by the model's output matrix.

### Modeling Process

The evidence is drawn from the full 4-scenario x 4-policy impact matrix (Module M4 output). Key quantities compared: (a) rights outcome - the fraction of EDPs resettled with full rights under each policy; (b) cultural outcome - the weighted cultural survival C_rel at 2100; (c) cost - total program cost and cost per EDP; (d) the do-nothing failure mode - the number of rights-denied EDPs and the cultural loss under P0. The recommendation is P2 (responsibility-weighted quota + program) as the rights-fair core and P3 (full: + cultural zones + consent) as the full package that also protects culture. The cost-of-inaction is measured by P0's total cost (which is dominated by the enormous adaptation-in-place and rights-denied costs of letting people sink in place without support) and by the cultural loss under P0.

### Outcome Analysis

Pillar 1 - Scale of harm under do-nothing. Under P0 only 707 EDPs (of up to 1,081,706) are ever resettled with rights - a rights failure rate of 99.9%. The cultural survival collapses to 0.893 (SSP1-1.9), 0.398 (SSP2-4.5), 0.185 (SSP5-8.5), 0.106 (2 m tail), and the cultural value lost by 2100 is $432M, $1.21B, $2.76B, $3.81B respectively. Under the 2 m tail, P0 leaves 282,160 EDPs genuinely rights-denied (land gone, not resettled) and 799,537 in adaptation-in-place on sinking land, at a total cost of $783.48B. This is the do-nothing baseline: near-total rights failure, most of the distinct cultures gone, and a cost (the cost of people sinking in place) that is not saved by inaction.

Pillar 2 - The benefit of the recommended policies. P1 (adding the costed expansion program) is the single biggest lever: it raises the resettled-with-rights fraction from ~0.3-1.4% (P0) to 29.8% (SSP1-1.9), 86.5% (SSP2-4.5), 98.3% (SSP5-8.5), 78.2% (2 m tail). P3 (full) is the culture-protective package: it preserves 96.5% (SSP1-1.9), 99.2% (SSP2-4.5), 100% (SSP5-8.5), 92.6% (2 m tail) of the coherent cultural stock at 2100, versus 89.3%/39.8%/18.5%/10.6% under P0 in the same scenarios. So the recommended policies convert a near-total failure into a large majority of EDPs resettled with rights and near-total cultural preservation. The responsibility weighting (P2) makes the burden fair (toward the high-emission states most responsible) without reducing the total resettled.

Pillar 3 - Cost comparison. The recommended policies are not a cost trade-off against do-nothing; in the high scenarios they are cheaper. Under SSP5-8.5, P0 total cost is $225.89B while P1/P2 are $269.14B (P1/P2 cost more here because they fund the full expansion program) but P3 is only $6.92B because consent front-loads voluntary relocation into cheap early capacity and cultural protection avoids the huge late costs. Under the 2 m tail, P0 is $783.48B, P1/P2 are $842.48B, and P3 is only $41.36B. Under SSP2-4.5, P0 is $115.33B and P3 is $33.84B - the full package is a third of the do-nothing cost while delivering 99.2% cultural survival vs 39.8%. The per-EDP cost of the recommended packages ($424k-$495k for P1/P2 under the high scenarios, $1.16M for P3 which includes the cultural support) is modest against the cost of the rights-denied failure ($40,000 per EDP per year, accumulating to hundreds of billions) and against the irreplaceable value of the lost cultures. The expansion program budget itself (0.01% of donor GDP, ~$1.5B/yr by 2050) is small relative to donor GDP and is the binding lever.

Pillar 4 - Why now (timing/commitment). IPCC AR6 shows 0.18-0.23 m of SLR by 2050 is largely committed on current emissions, independent of future mitigation. That is enough to push the atolls toward the 0.5-m urban-submersion band this decade-to-2050s. The model's onset analysis shows the atolls reach the submersion band roughly 2045-2055 under SSP2-4.5 and earlier under SSP5-8.5. The expansion program takes years to build (visa systems, housing, integration programs, cultural districts) and its capacity must exist before the displacement peaks - which the model shows occur in the 2050s-2070s. Therefore the UN must systemize the response (adopt the EDP status instrument, the responsibility-weighted quota treaty, and the costed expansion program) now, not after the first nation is displaced. The urgency is quantitative: the committed SLR means the hazard timeline is already set, and the only way to meet the displacement with prepared, rights-protecting, culture-preserving capacity is to start the system now. The model's P3 result (cheapest in the high scenarios, highest cultural survival, consent as a rights guarantee) is the recommended 'systemized response' the ICM-F is asked to brief: a UN-coordinated, responsibility-weighted, consent-gated resettlement system with a costed expansion program and cultural preservation zones, implemented now.
