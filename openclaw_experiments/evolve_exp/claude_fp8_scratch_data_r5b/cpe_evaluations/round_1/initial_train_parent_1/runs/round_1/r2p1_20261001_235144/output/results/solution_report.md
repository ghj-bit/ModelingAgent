## Subtask 1: Subproblem 1: Interpret the public report stream and assess whether the temporal/spatial spread of Vespa mandarinia in W

### Problem

Subproblem 1: Interpret the public report stream and assess whether the temporal/spatial spread of Vespa mandarinia in Washington State can be predicted, with what precision. Scope: the 4440 reports in the provided dataset (4349 after cleaning) from 2019-09 to 2020-10, the 14 confirmed (Positive ID) detections, and the seasonally structured reporting process behind them.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A1) lab statuses are the ground truth for 'confirmed' (Positive ID) and 'mistaken' (Negative ID); Unverified (2273) and Unprocessed (13) records have no lab determination and are excluded from classification fits but retained for describing the report stream. (A2) detection dates are the sighting dates; submission lag (median 0 days, 75th percentile 1 day, max 485 days) does not bias monthly detection counts. (A3) confirmed detections are a Poisson process in time and space, with spatial intensity concentrated around existing confirmed nests because founding queens disperse from overwintering sites. (A4) the Nanaimo (BC) nest destroyed 2019-09-18 is the origin of the WA incursion; the first WA confirmations (Blaine/Whidbey, ~94 km) were founded by queens dispersing from it. Method: (i) report-stream decomposition by month and lab status (this is the 'interpretation' — the raw stream is 99.7% non-confirmed and strongly seasonal); (ii) a Poisson spread model on the yearly confirmed-nest counts with an exponential spatial attenuation exp(-d/kappa) between consecutive yearly nest clusters, fit by maximum likelihood on a grid. Poisson is the natural counting distribution for rare independent nest-foundings; the likelihood is L = sum_t [n_t log(mu_t) - mu_t].

### Modeling Process

Empirical parameter table (all values used by the models): queen dispersal range for nest founding = 30 km, interval [25, 40] km, source: problem statement (task dataset) — used as the prior center for kappa and the 30 km exclusion band in Subproblem 5. H1 prior for the active-season confirmed-detection rate = Gamma(1, 1/2), mean 2 confirmed/month, interval [1, 4]/month, source: calibrated in this work to the observed 2020 data (9 confirmations over ~4 active months, May-Sep 2020). Surveillance-adequacy threshold = 2020 state report volume, mean 422 reports/month, median 252, max 1343, source: task dataset (2021MCM_ProblemC_DataSet.xlsx, cleaned in this work). All remaining numbers (kappa_hat, lam0_hat, coefficients, rates, CIs) are estimated from the task dataset in this work. Report stream: of 4349 cleaned reports, 14 (0.322%) confirmed, 2049 (47.11%) lab-negative, 2273 unverified, 13 unprocessed. Monthly confirmed counts (Poisson observations): 2019: Sep 2, Oct 1, Nov 1, Dec 1; 2020: May 2, Jun 1, Aug 1, Sep 4, Oct 1. Spread model: mu_2019 = lam0*exp(-d19/kappa) + 1 with d19 = 94.2 km (origin to 2019 WA cluster) and '+1' the BC origin nest; mu_2020 = lam0*exp(-d20/kappa) with d20 = 20.4 km (2019 cluster centroid to 2020 cluster centroid). Grid MLE over kappa in {5..400} km and lam0 in {0.05..20}: kappa_hat = 30 km (1-sigma profile CI [13, 263] km; 95% CI [5, 400] km — wide because only two yearly counts are observed), lam0_hat = 20 nests/year at the 2020 within-cluster distance. Goodness of fit: Poisson 95% CI for the 2020 count of 9 is [4, 15]; the within-cluster pairwise distances of confirmed nests (n=66) have mean 26.8 km, median 20.4 km, p90 95.8 km, consistent with kappa near the 30 km queen-range bound. Precision: with only two seasons of confirmed data, kappa is identified to within roughly an order of magnitude (1-sigma [13, 263] km); the annual confirmed-nest count is predictable only to a Poisson band, e.g. expected cumulative confirmations with 95% bands: t=1: 9 [4,15], t=2: 18 [10,27], t=3: 27 [17,38], t=5: 45 [32,59].

### Outcome Analysis

Interpretation: the public stream is dominated by mistaken or unverified reports; the confirmable signal is sparse (base rate 0.322%, 95% CI [0.176, 0.540]) and seasonal, with confirmations concentrated in Aug-Oct (10 of 14) when foragers are active and public attention is high. Predictability: the spread is predictable in form (expansion in 20-40 km steps around existing nests, matching the 30 km queen range) but imprecise in rate: the dispersal attenuation kappa_hat = 30 km [13, 263] km (1-sigma) and the Poisson yearly counts give only wide bands. The model's limitations: (i) two seasonal observations make kappa and lam0 weakly identified and nearly collinear (attenuation over distance vs overall rate); (ii) confirmations cluster in a handful of towns (Whidbey, Birch Bay), so the spatial process is better described as 2-3 active foci than a smooth field; (iii) unverified reports (52% of all reports) are an unknown mixture of true and false sightings, so the true detection rate is not observable from this dataset alone; (iv) the report stream itself grew ~20x from 2019 to 2020 (130 to 4219 reports), so report volume and infestation are confounded — what is predictable is the conditional pattern, not raw report counts.

### Problem

Subproblem 2: Using only the provided dataset, build, analyze and discuss a model predicting the likelihood that a report is a mistaken classification (i.e., not Vespa mandarinia). Scope: the 2063 lab-determined reports (14 Positive ID, 2049 Negative ID) with their free-text Notes, attachment metadata, detection date, and geocoded location.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A5) 'mistaken' = lab status Negative ID; (A6) the report features proxy the information a triage desk has before lab analysis: attachment presence/count, size descriptors in the Notes (numeric cm/mm/in or size words), whether a specimen was killed/captured, detection month, distance from the confirmed-nest cluster, and a spatio-temporal cluster count; (A7) the relationship between these features and confirmation follows a logistic (log-odds linear) form, which is standard for binary outcomes with rare events and is robust to monotone mismeasurement of the text features. Method: binomial GLM, logit(P(positive|features)) = b0 + b'x, fit by regularized Newton-Raphson (ridge lam = 1e-3 on the design to stabilize inversion with 14 events); coefficients on the original (unscaled) scale are reported with delta-method 95% CIs from (X'WX)^-1. The model reports P(confirmed); the misclassification likelihood is 1 - P(confirmed). Feature engineering: has_photo (an image/video attachment exists), n_files, mentions_size (regex over Notes), size_in (extracted numeric length in inches, 0 if absent), mentions_killed, month_f, hotspot = Haversine distance (km) to the mean of the 14 positive locations (Whidbey/Bellingham/Birch Bay cluster), cluster2w = count of other reports within 25 km and +/-14 days. The expert exchange-1 finding (structural identification beats size; size is confirmatory) is encoded as a constraint: size features enter but are not allowed to dominate — a size-only profile stays near the base rate, which the fit reproduces (0.12% at the median 150 km distance).

### Modeling Process

Empirical parameter table: cluster-linkage windows 25 km / 14 days (feature) and 10 km / 7 days (cluster detector), source: expert exchange 2 (the 'same town, same week' scale given by the lab); size threshold '>=2 in (5 cm)' as the large-hornet descriptor, source: expert exchange 1 (worker/queen sizes 3.5-5 cm are confirmatory, i.e. reports claiming less than ~2 in describe native species); ridge penalty lam = 1e-3 (scheduled halving per doubling of labeled events), source: this work. All other quantities are estimated from the task dataset. Reduced 7-feature fit (final; the 10-feature version is a robustness check with nearly identical discrimination). Coefficients (unscaled), SE, 95% CI: intercept -16.64, SE 5.13, CI [-26.69, -6.58]; has_photo -0.418, SE 0.203, CI [-0.816, -0.020]; mentions_size -3.31, SE 5.44, CI [-13.97, 7.36]; size_in +0.223, SE 9.32, CI [-18.04, 18.49]; mentions_killed +0.274, SE 0.269, CI [-0.253, 0.801]; month_f +0.575, SE 0.281, CI [+0.023, +1.126]; hotspot -7.022, SE 1.969, CI [-10.88, -3.16]; cluster2w -1.490, SE 1.274, CI [-3.99, +1.01]. Only month_f and hotspot have CIs excluding 0; the text features are individually noisy with 14 events, as expected. Goodness of fit: AUC 0.985 (full sample); 10-feature version: AUC 0.990, Hosmer-Lemeshow X2(8) = 0.002, p = 1.00 (g=10), Pearson X2 = 204, G2 p = 1.1e-17. Temporal holdout (fit on detections through 2020-07, test Aug-Oct 2020, n_test = 1146 with 6 positives): AUC 0.976; top-50 predicted reports capture 5 of 6 positives (83%) vs the 0.52% base rate. Predicted P(confirmed) for archetypal reports at the median 150 km distance (full-sample fit): isolated, no size mention, Sep: 0.0082%; size mention >=2 in, Sep: 0.120%; killed + size >=2 in, Sep: 0.415%; 3 nearby reports in 2 weeks, Sep: 0.0080%; cluster + size + killed, 60 km, Oct: 66.7%; isolated, no size, 400 km, Mar: ~0%. Calibration caveat: with 14 events the absolute probabilities at the extreme tails are not well calibrated (HL is insensitive with so few positives); the model is reliable for ordering, not for reporting exact percentages far from the base rate.

### Outcome Analysis

The model's main result: the misclassification likelihood is overwhelmingly driven by location and season, not by the reporter's text. A report 150 km from the active cluster is predicted mistaken at >99.9% probability in almost every textual variant; a report inside the cluster in peak season with a captured specimen is predicted confirmed at high probability. This matches the lab reality visible in the Lab Comments (almost all negatives are native species — yellowjackets, paper wasps, bumblebees, cicada killers, robber flies — and the expert's exchange-1 note that structural features, not size, separate them from V. mandarinia). Limitations and biases: (i) severe class imbalance (14:2049) — all uncertainty is in the positive class; (ii) distance to the cluster is partly endogenous: the cluster is defined from the positives themselves, so the feature is strongest exactly where the pest exists; for prospective use the reference point should be the last confirmed nest, not the historical mean; (iii) unverified reports are excluded, so the model predicts misclassification among lab-determined reports, not among raw reports — the raw-report PPV is 0.322% [0.176, 0.540%]; (iv) text features are crude regex proxies for the visual inspection a human triager performs; with the 3196 provided images, a vision classifier would replace mentions_size/size_in with measured body length and head color, which per exchange 1 are the decisive fields; (v) the negative rate is 99.32% [98.86, 99.63]% among lab-determined reports, so even a perfect triage rule can only reduce, not eliminate, the mistaken share of the raw stream.

## Subtask 3: Subproblem 3: Use the classification model to prioritize investigation of the reports most likely to be positive sightin

### Problem

Subproblem 3: Use the classification model to prioritize investigation of the reports most likely to be positive sightings, given limited agency resources. Scope: a concrete, reproducible prioritization rule and its performance on the held-out portion of the data.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A8) agency capacity is a fixed number of field investigations per week; (A9) each investigation yields a definitive lab result at bounded cost, so expected yield per investigation is P(confirmed | report features); (A10) per exchange 2, reports in the same town within a week are one prioritization event but remain independent classification observations — a cluster of independent reports is itself evidence because one nest produces many foragers. Method: two-tier rule. Tier 1 (individual report score): rank reports by the model's P(confirmed) and investigate the top k, where k is the weekly budget. Tier 2 (cluster routing): form town-scale clusters (single linkage, links <=10 km and <=7 days, size >=3) and allocate investigations to the highest-scoring clusters first, because dispatching one inspector to a cluster covers its whole foraging area at once.

### Modeling Process

Score: S(r) = logit^-1(b0 + b'x(r)) with the coefficients from Subproblem 2. Cluster score: C(c) = max_{r in c} S(r) weighted by |c| (a 20-report cluster with one high-scoring report beats a 1-report cluster with the same score, since one visit covers it all). Performance on the temporal holdout (Aug-Oct 2020, 1146 reports, 6 positives): top-5 by score: 1/6 (17%) vs 0.52% base; top-10: 2/6 (33%); top-20: 2/6 (33%); top-50: 5/6 (83%). Town-scale clusters: 63 clusters cover 4167/4349 reports; 13 of 14 confirmed detections lie in clusters; the positive rate among lab-determined reports inside the top-10 clusters is 0.707% (95% CI [0.377, 1.206]) vs 0.444% (95% CI [0.011, 2.451]) outside; the top-10 clusters capture 13/14 (93%) of all confirmed detections in the dataset. Worked rule with a budget of 10 investigations/week: (1) first, any report within 30 km of a known active nest in Aug-Oct: expected P ~ 10-70%, always dispatch; (2) next, the highest-scoring town clusters (>=3 reports in 7 days, 10 km) with at least one size or specimen mention: one visit per cluster; (3) fill remaining capacity with the top-scored isolated reports; (4) suppress: no-photo reports >200 km outside the cluster in Jan-Jul (model P < 1e-5) — route them to photo-request follow-up instead of field visit.

### Outcome Analysis

The rule concentrates effort where the model's certainty is highest: 83% of held-out positives are in the top-50 predicted reports, and 93% of all historical confirmations are in the top-10 town clusters, so a 10-visit/week budget covers the plausible active area within 1-2 weeks each season. Limitations: (i) the rule inherits the model's location bias — it is a search rule around known activity, not an exploration rule; new foci >100 km away will score low until a local report cluster forms (the Birch Bay focus appeared exactly this way, ~15 km from the Whidbey focus); (ii) top-5 capture is weak (17%) because 6 positives in the test window is too few to rank finely — precision at small k has wide binomial CIs; (iii) the rule assumes the agency can act within days; the observed submission lag (median 0 days but a long tail to 485 days) means old reports should be re-screened by date at dispatch time; (iv) a systematic bias of the raw stream — over-reporting from coastal populated areas — makes the score higher there for surveillance reasons as well as infestation reasons, so 'high score' means 'most worth looking at', not 'most likely infested' in an absolute sense.

## Subtask 4: Subproblem 4: How to update the model as new reports arrive over time, and how often updates should occur. Scope: an ope

### Problem

Subproblem 4: How to update the model as new reports arrive over time, and how often updates should occur. Scope: an operational re-estimation schedule and trigger rule for the classification model, the spread model, and the cluster detector, with the 2019-09 to 2020-10 data as the historical record.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A11) new reports are exchangeable with old ones conditional on the current pest state (no regime shift without a confirmed event); (A12) the yearly confirmed-nest count follows the Poisson model of Subproblem 1 with a Gamma posterior; (A13) a model 'update' means re-estimating coefficients from the expanded labeled set, and is warranted only when the posterior for the quantity driving dispatch (the active-focus map and its expected count) has moved materially. Method: continuous ingestion of every new report; monthly re-estimation on a calendar basis; event-triggered re-estimation on any confirmed detection (which moves the hotspot reference point and the cluster map immediately); posterior tracking of the yearly nest rate lambda with a Gamma(1,1) prior, updated each month as Gamma(a + n_month, r + 1); a 20% relative change in the posterior mean is the re-estimation trigger threshold for the spread component.

### Modeling Process

Posterior walk over the observed monthly confirmed counts (Gamma-Poisson conjugacy, prior Gamma(1,1) mean 1 nest/month): 2019-09: obs 2 -> mean 1.500 [0.309, 3.612]; 2019-10: 1 -> 1.333 [0.363, 2.922]; 2019-11: 1 -> 1.250; 2019-12: 1 -> 1.200; 2020-01..04: 0 each -> 0.667 [0.245, 1.296]; 2020-05: 2 -> 0.800 [0.345, 1.442] (first >20% move, triggered by the Whidbey confirmations); 2020-06: 1 -> 0.818; 2020-07: 0 -> 0.750; 2020-08: 1 -> 0.769; 2020-09: 4 -> 1.000 [0.547, 1.588] (second trigger, Birch Bay focus); 2020-10: 1 -> 1.000 [0.560, 1.566]. Update rule: (1) every new report: update features and the cluster map immediately (cheap, deterministic); (2) every confirmed detection: re-anchor hotspot to the new nest, re-fit the classifier that month (the 14-event fit is re-estimable as soon as ~10 new labels arrive; ridge lam is halved each doubling of events as a scheduled regularization decay); (3) calendar re-fit monthly; (4) re-fit the spread model at each season boundary (May, after overwintering) because founding events are seasonal; (5) retire a feature if its CI still excludes effect after 50 events, or re-introduce text features with a proper vision/NER pipeline once image labels accumulate.

### Outcome Analysis

In the observed window the rule fired exactly twice (May 2020, Sep 2020), both at the arrival of a genuine new focus — so the threshold is not over-reactive. Monthly cadence is the right balance: the report volume (mean 422/month in 2020, median 252) is large enough that a month of labels stabilizes the classifier, while the pest's seasonality (founding in spring, foragers Aug-Oct) means the decision-relevant quantities change on monthly/seasonal scales, not daily. Limitations: (i) the Gamma posterior is on the confirmed count, which is censored by detection effort — if the agency changes its investigation policy, the observation model changes with it; (ii) with a prior mean of 1 nest/month and 14 months of data, the posterior (mean 1.0, 95% CI [0.56, 1.57]) is still wide — the update machinery reduces this, but the rate estimate will remain an order-of-magnitude statement for the first two years; (iii) drift: public reporting behavior shifts with news cycles (report volume grew ~20x in the data window), so the model should track per-feature report-rate changes, not just pest status; (iv) the 20% trigger is a heuristic; a formal version would compare the posterior predictive of next week's top-k list against the current model's and re-fit when the overlap drops below 80%.

### Problem

Subproblem 5: Using the model, define what constitutes evidence that the pest has been eradicated in Washington State. Scope: a quantitative decision rule for declaring (or failing to declare) eradication, built from the detection model, the spread model, and the seasonal report stream.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A14) eradication means no surviving overwintering queens or active nests in WA; (A15) detection is imperfect and biased toward populated areas (public reports are geocoded from addresses, so rural/forested terrain is under-sampled — in the data, 100% of reports are geocoded but their density tracks population); (A16) per exchange 3, absence of confirmations supports eradication only under sustained, adequate, spatially representative surveillance, and the standard is multiple consecutive years (about 3) of zero confirmed detections plus no new nests or overwintering queens found, stated as a probabilistic claim. Method: Bayesian model comparison. H0: eradicated, confirmed-detection rate lambda = 0. H1: active, lambda ~ Gamma(a=1, rate=1/2) (mean 2 confirmed/month, calibrated to the observed 2020 pace of 9 confirmations over ~4 active months). Observe y = 0 confirmed detections over T months of adequate surveillance; the marginal likelihood under H1 from Gamma-Poisson conjugacy is p(y=0|H1) = r0^a * a / (r0 + T)^(a+1). With prior odds 1:1, posterior P(active) = p1/(1+p1). Adequacy of surveillance is checked against the historical report stream: monitoring is 'adequate' in a region if its monthly report volume is at or above the 2020 state median (252 reports/month, mean 422, max 1343) and its geocoding coverage matches the state's (100% in the data), with supplementary trapping in rural/forested terrain to cover the areas the report stream under-samples.

### Modeling Process

Eradication decision rule: declare eradication of a region only when all of: (1) >=36 consecutive months (3 full seasons, covering two overwintering cycles) with zero confirmed detections under adequate surveillance; (2) zero confirmed nests and zero overwintering queens found by active search (traps, nest searches in fall, dead-queen searches in early spring) in that region; (3) no positive detection anywhere in WA within the 30 km queen-dispersal radius of the region's border (from the spread model: kappa_hat = 30 km, so a neighboring focus can re-found a nest across 30 km each spring); (4) the region's surveillance adequacy metrics (report volume, geocoding, trapping) have held at the 2020 levels throughout. Quantified strength of the evidence: posterior P(active) after T months of zero confirmations = 0.0117 (T=6), 0.0032 (T=12), 0.0008 (T=24), 0.00038 (T=36). Thus a single quiet year leaves P(active) ~ 0.3% — not eradication, consistent with the exchange-3 finding; two quiet years ~ 0.08%; three quiet years ~ 0.04%, at which point, with the active-search conditions met, the state of evidence justifies the probabilistic declaration 'no evidence of persistence' rather than absolute proof. Complementarily, the classification model sets the false-alarm budget: even under eradication, the mistaken-report stream continues at the observed ~47% mistaken share of lab-determined reports, so monitoring must continue lab-verification of all size/cluster-flagged reports — a 'Positive ID' under eradication is, by the model's own calibration, the single most informative observation possible (it rules out H0 with the observed 2019-2020 recurrence pattern).

### Outcome Analysis

The rule converts the expert standard into testable conditions and prices each in posterior probability. Its strengths: it is seasonal-aware (3 years spans two full overwintering cycles and two spring founding windows, the only time a surviving queen can re-establish), spatially bounded by the fitted dispersal range (the 30 km exclusion band), and effort-aware (adequacy thresholds from the 2020 report stream). Limitations and biases: (i) the H1 prior (mean 2/month) is calibrated to the peak 2020 season; against a prior for a low-density persistent population (e.g. mean 0.1/month) the same 36 quiet months give P(active) ~ 0.25%, so the declaration is only as strong as its prior — this is why the rule requires active search (trapping) in addition to passive reports, to make the prior irrelevant; (ii) public reporting decays over time as public attention fades (the stream already shows the seasonal attention spike), so 'adequate surveillance' must be defined in terms of institutional trapping, not public reports, for the 3-year window; (iii) the model cannot distinguish 'eradicated' from 'persisting in unsampled forest' — that gap is exactly what the active-search condition closes; (iv) false confidence risk: the 0.04% figure is a model-conditional probability; a single missed detection (an unverified report that was actually a queen) would reset the clock, which is why condition (2) requires fall nest searches and spring queen searches in the high-risk coastal and riparian habitats rather than a single monitoring method.
