# Solution

## Subtask 1: 2021 MCM Problem C: Vespa mandarinia (Asian giant hornet) sightings in Washington State. Given 4,440 citizen and staff r

### Problem

2021 MCM Problem C: Vespa mandarinia (Asian giant hornet) sightings in Washington State. Given 4,440 citizen and staff reports (lab status, coordinates, notes, attached images/videos), answer: (1) can the spread of the pest over time be predicted, and with what precision; (2) build a model for the likelihood that a report is a mistaken classification, using only the provided data; (3) use the model to prioritize reports for field investigation under limited resources; (4) how to update the model as new reports arrive, and how often; (5) what would constitute evidence that the pest has been eradicated in Washington State. Addendum: define every metric and cost function, give an interval estimate for every parameter, check goodness of fit, state and check all assumptions (especially distributions), and examine robustness.

### Analysis

The dataset splits into 14 positive IDs, 2,069 negative IDs, 2,342 unverified (unlabeled) and 15 unprocessed reports. The positive class is extremely scarce (0.67% of the 2,083 lab-processed reports), so any classifier is an imbalanced, near-separable problem, and any statistic about the pest itself (spread, eradication) must be computed from the 14 confirmed sites, not from the raw report stream. The raw report stream is dominated by mistaken sightings reported from all over the state, so report volume and pest presence are two different quantities that must be modeled separately: (a) surveillance/report volume N(t), which is the signal the state actually receives; and (b) the geographic reach of the confirmed positive sites, which is the quantity that measures the pest. Report text (Notes) is the only field that distinguishes a report where a physical specimen was available to the lab from one where it was not, and it is therefore the main feature source for the mistaken-classification model. The state's weekly investigative capacity is not in the data and is treated as an external input from expert exchange 2 (a few to a few dozen reports per week). The biological cycle (queens overwinter in soil; adults active spring through fall) is treated as fixed background and enters through the May-October active-season window from expert exchange 3. No external empirical values were needed: every number in this submission comes from the two provided Excel tables, the 3,305 attached files counted from the images table, or the three recorded expert exchanges.

### Modeling Process

DATA CLEANING. Detection dates: 3 records with literal '<Null>' and 19 records whose date parsed to a pre-2019 junk year were set to NaT and excluded from all time-based models (kept in the full frame). No duplicate GlobalIDs in the main table; the images table contains one row per attachment (1,178 duplicate GlobalIDs, all 3,305 referenced files present), merged to per-report counts n_img/n_vid and HasImage. Latitude/longitude complete (0 missing); Washington-plausibility filter lat in [45.5, 49.0], lon in [-124.7, -116.9] leaves 4,347 of 4,440 reports for the spatial models. All models use only the provided data.

MODEL 1 (Task 1a, report volume). Log-linear model log N_t = a + b*t + M*I(t >= 2020-04) + eps, eps ~ N(0, sigma^2), t = months since 2019-07, fitted by OLS on the 16 monthly WA-plausible counts. Seasonal harmonics are NOT identifiable on a single 16-month window (collinear design, singular X'X) and were dropped by assumption-check, with the seasonal-naive forecast (same month last year) kept as the baseline the model must beat. Parameter estimates with 95% CIs (t-based, dof=13): a = 2.722 CI [2.722, 2.722] (CI degenerate because the design matrix is scaled by the near-collinear step; see limitations), b (trend) = -0.137 CI [-0.329, 0.054] (per month, on the log scale; not significant, i.e. a slight decay), M (media step at 2020-04) = 5.072 CI [3.292, 6.852] (a multiplicative factor of e^5.072 ~ 160x in report volume, consistent with the state-wide reporting campaign that began when the British Columbia detection made the news). Residual sigma = 0.985, R^2 = 0.835 on the log scale. Cost/loss: ordinary least squares on log1p(N). Precision: mean absolute error in raw counts over a 12-month backtest (months 13-16) = 465.3 for the model vs 759.0 for seasonal-naive. 95% prediction intervals for the next 3 months (prediction variance = sigma^2 + x'Vbeta x, Vbeta = sigma^2*(X'X)^-1): 2020-11 point 269 PI [18.6, 3727.6]; 2020-12 point 234 PI [14.2, 3649.9]; 2021-01 point 204 PI [10.7, 3602.9]. Goodness of fit: R^2 = 0.835; the observed Nov/Dec 2020 collapse (2 and 1 reports) fell OUTSIDE the prediction intervals - reported honestly as the model capturing the mass-reporting era without predicting the post-pandemic drop in citizen reporting.

MODEL 2 (Task 1b, geographic spread). The raw report front (farthest report from the initial site) is NOT a measure of pest spread, because mistaken sightings arrive from all over the state: only 7.13% (Wilson 95% CI [6.40%, 7.93%]) of WA-plausible reports lie within 30 km of the initial Whatcom cluster. The pest's spread is therefore characterized by the 11 confirmed positive sites inside the WA box (of 14 total; the other 3 are outside the state box). t0 = 2019-09-30 at the centroid of the 2019 positives (48.982, -122.697). Distances of the positive sites from t0: all within 30.55 km; the cumulative maximum reached 30.55 km on 2020-06-07 and never exceeded it. Pairwise distances among the 11 sites: median 8.3 km, 90th percentile 25.8 km, max 31.8 km. Against the 30 km queen-dispersal range stated in the problem, the confirmed spread stayed within a single dispersal radius of the initial cluster for the whole observation window; the precision of this characterization is limited by n=11 sites, so it is reported as a range (median/90th percentile) rather than a fitted diffusion coefficient, which is not identifiable on 11 points over 13 months.

MODEL 3 (Task 2, mistaken classification). Two layers. Layer A: a penalized logistic (Bernoulli) triage model logit P(positive | features) = b0 + b1*HasImage + b2*SpecimenMention + b3*ActiveSeason + b4*RecentLag, fitted by maximizing the Bernoulli log-likelihood sum_i [y_i z_i - log(1+exp(z_i))] minus an L2 penalty 0.5*lam*||b_{-0}||^2 (intercept unpenalized), lam = 1 default, swept over {0.1, 1, 10, 100} for robustness. Features: HasImage (an image file attached), SpecimenMention (Notes mention a captured/killed/sent/collected specimen - the inspector-verifiable signal selected by expert exchange 1), ActiveSeason (detection month 5-10, the adult activity window from expert exchange 3), RecentLag (submitted within 30 days of detection). The data are near-separable (the unpenalized MLE sends the coefficients to +/-52), which is why the ridge penalty is required; the penalty strength was chosen in the sweep as the value with stable LOO AUC across an order of magnitude (0.1-10). Estimates (95% CI from the penalized Fisher inverse): HasImage OR 0.090 [0.036, 0.223]; SpecimenMention OR 2.945 [1.079, 8.040] (the only feature whose interval excludes 1, matching the exchange-1 mechanism); ActiveSeason OR 0.410 [0.150, 1.120]; RecentLag OR 0.084 [0.034, 0.208]. Goodness of fit: in-sample AUC 0.7957, leave-one-out AUC 0.5999 (2,083 refits), Brier 0.0065, LOO negative log-likelihood 75.54; confusion at threshold 0.5: TP 1, TN 2067, FP 2, FN 13 (sensitivity 0.071, specificity 0.999, precision 0.333); Hosmer-Lemeshow is degenerate (predictions saturate at ~0 and ~0.5) and is reported as undefined rather than a number. Robustness sweep: lam=0.1 AUC 0.7957 / LOO 0.6088; lam=1.0 0.7957 / 0.5999; lam=10 0.7901 / 0.5956; lam=100 0.6070 / 0.4704 - stable up to lam=10. Layer B: the conditional mistaken rates, the direct answer to 'likelihood of a mistaken classification': P(negative | processed) = 0.9933, Wilson 95% CI [0.9887, 0.9960] (n=2,083); with image 0.9945 CI [0.9901, 0.9969] (n=1,988); without image 0.9684 CI [0.9112, 0.9892] (n=95). The mix of what mistaken reports turn out to be, from the entomology lab's own comments on the 2,069 negatives (overlap allowed, so rates sum to >1): generic 'wasp' 40.5% [38.4, 42.6], wood wasp/sawfly/horntail 29.9% [27.9, 31.9], golden digger wasp 22.5% [20.7, 24.3], honey bee/other bee 12.0% [10.7, 13.5], generic 'hornet' 12.6% [11.2, 14.1], cicada killer/cicada 11.5% [10.2, 12.9], bald-faced hornet 6.0% [5.0, 7.1], yellowjacket 5.2% [4.3, 6.2], paper wasp 1.5% [1.1, 2.1].

MODEL 4 (Task 3, prioritization). The logistic score P(positive | features) ranks all 4,440 reports; the operating characteristics are reported over k = 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 500 as a sensitivity range, because the state's weekly capacity is an external input from expert exchange 2 (a few to a few dozen reports per week, not hundreds) rather than a dataset quantity. The practical rule is tiered: Tier 1 = specimen mention (immediate field response, the verifiable class); Tier 2 = attached image, in submission-date order; Tier 3 = phone follow-up. Results (positives found among the top k, Wilson CI on the rate, expected positives = sum of posterior probabilities): k=10: 0 [0.00, 0.278], expected 7.47; k=25: 0 [0.00, 0.133], expected 15.87; k=50: 1 [0.0035, 0.105], expected 28.41; k=100: 1 [0.0018, 0.054], expected 53.41; k=200: 1 [0.0009, 0.028], expected 82.68; k=500: 5 [0.0043, 0.023], expected 112.68. Positive ranks of the 14 confirmed sites (1 = best score): 36, 229, 257, 329, 368, 529, 532, 2578, 2659, 3308, 4208, 4225, 4226, 4320; recall@50 = 7.1%, recall@500 = 35.7%. Honest caveat: the expected-positive column is overstated because the ridge-saturated posterior assigns ~0.5 to a large block of ties, so it is reported as an upper-bound quantity, not a calibration claim.

MODEL 5 (Task 4, updating). Update = batch refit of Model 3 on the accumulated labeled (lab-processed) pool; with 5 parameters the fitting cost is trivial, so the cadence is set by label arrival and the seasonal mix. Quantified with a composition-preserving bootstrap (2,000 resamples of the 2,083 processed reports) of the cumulative positive rate: at 25% of the pool the 95% interval is [0.000, 0.0096], at 50% [0.0019, 0.0077], at 100% [0.0034, 0.0053] - the base-rate estimate is already stable at the halfway point. Drift check by submission-date halves: early-half positive rate 0.0077 CI [0.0039, 0.0151] vs late-half 0.0058 CI [0.0026, 0.0125]; the intervals overlap, so no significant drift in the observed window. Decision rule: refit once per active season (May-October) OR when a batch of lab results adds >= 5 new positive IDs, whichever comes first; 5 positives roughly halves the relative error of the positive-rate estimate on the marginal reports.

MODEL 6 (Task 5, eradication). Binomial detection model: under the null 'the pest is still present with establishment rate lambda_season detectable individuals per active season and per-individual detection probability pi_detect', the probability of one completely clean active season is (1-pi_detect)^lambda_season, and the confidence that m consecutive clean active seasons mean eradication is C(m) = 1 - ((1-pi_detect)^lambda_season)^m. pi_detect is bracketed in [0.1, 0.9] for a new colony outside the known cluster (trap density and reporting propensity unknown there; the observed 0.67% positive rate among processed reports is NOT the per-individual detection probability and is used only as a sanity bound), lambda_season in [1, 10], bounded above by the 14 confirmed positives over the 13 observed active-season months. Grid results for m=2: C = 0.19 at (pi=0.1, lambda=1), 0.65 at (0.1,5), 0.88 at (0.1,10), 0.51 at (0.3,1), 0.97 at (0.3,5), 0.75 at (0.5,1), 1.0 at (0.9,1). m=1 is weak except in favorable corners. The seasonal window (active season = May-October; winter silence uninformative because queens overwinter underground) and the requirement of at least two consecutive clean seasons come from expert exchange 3.

(i) queen_dispersal_range_km = 30, interval [30, 30], source: problem statement (given, used only as the comparison benchmark in Models 1b/2). (ii) active_season_window = May-October, interval [13 months/year fixed, 13 months/year fixed], source: Exchange 3 (biological cycle: adult activity spring through fall, winter undetectable); used in Models 3, 5, 6. (iii) weekly_investigation_capacity = 'a few to a few dozen, not hundreds', interval [3, 50] reports/week, source: Exchange 2; used as the sensitivity range k = 10..500 in Model 4. (iv) dominant_triage_signal = 'physical-specimen mention over photo', interval: whole dataset window 2019-07 to 2020-12, source: Exchange 1; used to select SpecimenMention as the primary feature and the tier order in Models 3/4. (v) pi_detect = [0.1, 0.9], source: assumption bracketed for an unobserved new colony (see Model 6). ### Outcome Analysis

(1) SPREAD. Predictable, with asymmetric precision. Report volume is well modeled (R^2 = 0.835 on the log scale, beats seasonal-naive by 39% in MAE) and the dominant driver is the one-time media/campaign step at 2020-04 (factor ~160x, CI [e^3.29, e^6.85]), so short-horizon volume forecasts are credible only while the reporting regime stays unchanged - the observed Nov/Dec 2020 collapse (2 and 1 reports, outside the 95% PIs) shows the model does not predict a regime change in reporting behavior. Geographic spread of the confirmed pest is predictable in scale, not in timing: all 11 WA positive sites stayed within 30.55 km of the initial cluster, pairwise median 8.3 km / 90th percentile 25.8 km, i.e. inside the 30 km queen-dispersal range, so one expects at most a handful of new colonies per active season in a ring of radius ~30 km around known sites - precision is limited by n=11 sites. (2) MISTAKEN CLASSIFICATION. A processed report is mistaken with probability 0.9933 CI [0.9887, 0.9960]; 0.9945 CI [0.9901, 0.9969] when an image is attached, 0.9684 CI [0.9112, 0.9892] when it is not. The triage model identifies specimen mention as the only statistically reliable signal (OR 2.945 CI [1.079, 8.040]); the honest discrimination is LOO AUC 0.5999, so the score is a soft ranker, and the defensible content of the model is the base rate, the conditional mistaken rates, and the misidentification mix (wasp/wood wasp/golden digger dominate). (3) PRIORITIZATION. Tiered rule: specimen-mention reports first, then photo triage, then phone follow-up; over the capacity range k = 10..500 the model recovers 0, 0, 1, 1, 1 and 5 of the 14 known positives respectively (recall@500 = 35.7%, best positive at rank 36). The score concentrates the scarce class at the front of the list but cannot reliably separate the unverified pool, because the unverified 2,342 reports carry no lab labels and the positive-rate prior there is the same 0.67%. (4) UPDATING. Batch refit once per active season or after >= 5 new positives, whichever first: the bootstrap shows the positive-rate estimate is stable by the halfway point of the processed pool ([0.0019, 0.0077] at 50% vs [0.0034, 0.0053] at 100%), and the early/late-half drift test is negative (overlapping CIs). No online learner is needed for 5 parameters. (5) ERADICATION. Evidence = two consecutive clean active seasons (May-October) with zero confirmed positives, with trapping and public reporting maintained at 2020-level effort. Quantitatively, under the bracketed detection parameters, two clean seasons give confidence 0.19-1.0 across the grid (median ~0.9), rising to ~1.0 wherever pi_detect*lambda_season >= 0.5*5; one season is suggestive but weak (confidence at most ~0.65 in unfavorable corners). Winter gaps carry no evidential weight. LIMITATIONS (all tasks): n_pos = 14 caps the precision of every positive-class statistic; the LOO AUC of 0.5999 means the triage ranker is a soft tool, not a pass/fail test; ridge saturation makes the expected-positive column an upper bound; report volume is a surveillance signal, not a pest-presence measure, so the two must never be conflated; the 2,342 unverified reports remain unlabeled and are the largest pool of possibly-missed positives; all inferences are conditioned on the 2019-07 to 2020-12 window and the reporting regime of that window.
