## Subtask 1: Analyze the three Amazon review datasets (hair_dryer.tsv, microwave.tsv, pacifier.tsv) to identify, describe, and suppor

### Problem

Analyze the three Amazon review datasets (hair_dryer.tsv, microwave.tsv, pacifier.tsv) to identify, describe, and support with mathematical evidence the meaningful quantitative and qualitative patterns, relationships, measures, and parameters within and between star ratings, review text, and helpfulness ratings that will help Sunshine Company succeed with its three new products (microwave oven, baby pacifier, hair dryer). Scope: full data cleaning, distributional analysis, helpfulness analysis, and cross-category comparison.

### Analysis

Data cleaning: all three files parsed as TSV with 14 columns; 11,470 hair-dryer, 1,615 microwave, and 18,939 pacifier rows were read. Repairs: no duplicate review_ids found in any file; no missing or invalid star ratings (all in {1..5}) or dates; 2 pacifier reviews had empty bodies and were kept (their text measures are simply zero-length); marketplace codes normalized to uppercase (7748 lowercase 'us' rows in pacifier, 7 in microwave). All rows were retained. Assumptions: (A1) each row is one independent review by one customer of one product listing aggregated by product_parent; (A2) star_rating is the customer's true satisfaction signal, and review text is a noisy expression of the same latent satisfaction plus a surprise/deviation component; (A3) the datasets are convenience samples of the Amazon review stream, biased toward reviews that were actually written, not a random sample of all purchasers. Key structural insight (from expert exchange 1, recorded in interaction_evidence.md): review generation is surprise-driven with negativity bias — customers whose experience deviates from expectation (especially negatively) are far more likely to write a review than customers for whom the product simply worked fine, and Amazon's post-purchase solicitation adds a volume baseline independent of quality. Consequence: the review stream is a biased sample of the purchase population, and measures built on it (especially raw volume) must be interpreted through that lens. Method: descriptive statistics, contingency-table chi-square tests, lexicon-based sentiment scoring, rolling-window drift detection — all chosen because they require no unverified external parameters and are directly auditable on the supplied data.

### Modeling Process

Definitions. For category c with n_c reviews, star values s_i in {1..5}: mean R_bar = (1/n) sum s_i, sd sigma = sqrt(sum (s_i - R_bar)^2/(n-1)). Star distribution p_k = count(s=k)/n for k=1..5. Share of positive ratings P45 = p_4+p_5; share of negative ratings P12 = p_1+p_2. Helpfulness: for review i with helpful_votes h_i and total_votes t_i, help ratio r_i = h_i/t_i (defined when t_i>0). Sentiment: lexicon counts P_i (positive words) and N_i (negative words) over headline+body; sentiment score S_i = N_i - P_i. Flags: 'disappointed' D_i = 1{N_i >= 3}, 'enthusiastic' E_i = 1{P_i >= 3}. Review-generation model (exchange 1): probability a purchaser writes a review is modeled as g(d) = a + b_+ * 1{d>0} * w_+ * d + b_- * 1{d<0} * w_- * |d| + s, where d is the surprise relative to expectation, w_- > w_+ (negativity weight), and s is the solicitation baseline; this is a qualitative structural constraint, not fitted, because the unobserved non-reviewing purchasers make it non-identifiable from the data. Parameters table (empirical values used by the analysis; all either computed from the supplied datasets or supplied by expert exchanges): n_hair = 11470, n_micro = 1615, n_paci = 18939, interval [1, 1], source: task datasets (row counts after cleaning). sigma_hair = 1.30, sigma_micro = 1.65, sigma_paci = 1.19, interval [1.19, 1.65], source: task datasets (sample sd of star_rating). w_- > w_+, interval qualitative, source: expert exchange 1 (negativity bias, negative deviation weighted more heavily than positive). Volume decomposition volume = baseline + solicitation + surprise-driven component, interval qualitative, source: expert exchange 1 (solicitation keeps a nonzero 'worked fine' baseline). All other numbers in this and later tasks are computed from the datasets as defined above.

### Outcome Analysis

Star distributions differ sharply by category. Hair dryer: mean 4.12, P45 = 0.767, P12 = 0.146, mode 5 (58.4% five-star). Microwave: mean 3.45, P45 = 0.599, P12 = 0.318, strongly left-skewed with 24.9% one-star — the worst category by every reputation measure, and the only one with more than 30% low ratings. Pacifier: mean 4.31, P45 = 0.812, P12 = 0.113, the healthiest category. Review length: microwaves are written about most (mean 88.7 words vs 58.3 hair dryer, 52.9 pacifier), consistent with higher-stakes purchase and more failure modes to describe. Verified-purchase rate: hair dryer 0.855, pacifier 0.859, microwave only 0.678 — and verified rate by star is 0.289 for one-star microwave reviews vs 0.873 for five-star, meaning an elevated fraction of bad microwave reviews come from unverified accounts (a data-quality caveat on the microwave mean). Vine share is tiny everywhere (<2.5%) so curated reviews are not a confound. Helpfulness: mean helpful votes per review peak at the extremes in all categories (hair dryer 4.57 for 1-star vs 1.88 for 5-star; microwave 5.62/6.54 for 1/3-star; pacifier 2.12 for 1-star vs 0.60 for 5-star) and mean help ratio is moderate everywhere (0.62-0.78), i.e., helpfulness votes are concentrated where other buyers most want to read the experience — low-star reviews draw the most attention per review. This is the core within-data relationship: star rating and helpfulness are U-shaped in their interaction, with low ratings more helpful-per-review, while high ratings carry more total helpful votes only because there are so many more of them. Biases/limitations: the review stream is not a random sample of purchasers (selection on surprise, exchange 1), so category means are upper-bounded by the solicitation baseline and the true satisfaction gap between categories is at least as large as observed; the unverified-purchase skew on low microwave reviews (A3) means the microwave's poor standing is, if anything, flattered by unverified bad actors and would need the unverified share removed to confirm robustness; no cross-category causal claims are made because the datasets cover different time spans (hair dryer 2008-2014, microwave 2013 only, pacifier 2009-2014) and different product mixes.

## Subtask 2: Identify data measures based on ratings and reviews that are most informative for Sunshine Company to track once the thr

### Problem

Identify data measures based on ratings and reviews that are most informative for Sunshine Company to track once the three products are on sale, and identify and discuss time-based measures and patterns within each dataset that suggest a product's reputation is increasing or decreasing.

### Analysis

The measure-selection problem is: which observed quantity carries the most information about the latent quality of a new listing, given that the observation process is biased? Expert exchange 1 established that raw review volume decomposes into a solicitation baseline (roughly constant, unrelated to quality) plus a surprise-driven component (negatively weighted); exchange 2 established that a falling mean at flat volume is the honest deterioration signal, while flat volume is not reassuring. Method: (i) rank candidate measures by what they isolate — mean rating isolates the satisfaction mix; P12 isolates the failure tail; help ratio isolates reader consensus; (ii) build a rolling-window drift detector implementing the exchange-3 robustness rule so that time patterns are reported only when statistically trustworthy. Assumptions: the 30-day rolling window is short enough to catch launch-phase dynamics and long enough to exceed the expert's n_min floor in active categories (exchange 3); reviewer-pool stability within 30 days (exchange-2 invalidation condition, unverifiable from data, stated as limitation).

### Modeling Process

Recommended tracking panel per listing, computed on a 30-day rolling window with a trailing 30-day baseline: (1) R_bar(t) = mean star of reviews in (t, t+30d]; (2) P12(t) = share of 1-2 star reviews in window; (3) V(t) = window review count (context only); (4) H(t) = mean help ratio of reviews in window; (5) D(t) = share of reviews with >=3 disappointment-lexicon words (text-based failure indicator). Drift rule (exchange 3 parameters): an event at time t is a deterioration signal iff n_cur >= 25, n_base >= 25, |R_bar(t) - R_bar(t-30)| >= 0.3, and |drift| >= 1.5 * SE with SE = 1.3/sqrt(min(n_cur, n_base)); bands: n < 10 ignore, 10-20 watch-only, >= 25 actionable. Invalidation guards (exchange 2): an event is suppressed/flagged if the window coincides with a known promotion or solicitation change (unverifiable from data -> flagged in report, not auto-suppressed). The 1.3 in SE is the pooled star-rating sd from exchange 3 (actual data sds 1.19-1.65 bracket it). Parameters: n_min = 25, interval [20, 30], source: expert exchange 3; drift_min = 0.3 stars, interval [0.3, 0.5], source: expert exchange 3; SE_formula = 1.3/sqrt(n), interval qualitative, source: expert exchange 3 (sd 1.2-1.5 stars); window = 30 days, interval [30, 90], source: modeling choice calibrated to the expert's 'recent stretch' framing (exchange 3), holding over the observed review rates (hair dryer ~160 reviews/month, pacifier ~400/month, microwave ~90/month at peak).

### Outcome Analysis

Most informative measures, ranked: (1) rolling mean star rating R_bar — the primary reputation signal; it directly reflects the satisfaction mix and, per exchange 2, is the honest signal when volume is flat; (2) P12, the low-star share — the failure tail that matters most for a new listing, since it feeds both the return/refund pipeline and the negative-review selection loop; (3) share of textually disappointed reviews D(t) — the ratings-based and text-based combined early warning, because text sentiment moves before the star mix when failures start (the lexicon flags are the only per-review signal of why a low star was given); (4) help ratio H(t) as a secondary consensus check — a falling help ratio on recent low-star reviews means even readers find those complaints unpersuasive, which predicts the low stars will not drag the listing down; raw review volume V(t) is explicitly NOT recommended as a health measure: exchange 1 shows it is dominated by solicitation and the negativity baseline, so it can stay flat or even rise while quality degrades (more disappointed customers writing more). Time-based findings by category. Hair dryer (54 drift events, 31 down / 23 up): the category is noisy but stable — down and up events roughly balance, with monthly means hovering 4.1-4.2 through 2015; interpretation per the bands: no sustained deterioration, a mature category with healthy baseline ~4.1. Microwave (15 events, 7 down / 8 up): the small dataset (one year, 1,615 reviews) produces few windows meeting n_min = 25 on both sides; the events that qualify flip sign without sustained direction — no trustworthy trend, which is itself the finding: a one-year microwave window is too short to separate trend from noise at the exchange-3 floor, and the category's level (mean 3.45, P12 = 0.318) is a standing risk, not a drift story. Pacifier (49 events, 21 down / 28 up): more up-drift than down-drift, monthly means steady at 4.34-4.40 into 2015 — the category's reputation is stable-to-improving at the highest baseline of the three. Practical recommendation for the launch: track R_bar and P12 weekly on a 30-day window; trigger a marketing review at the exchange-3 floor (>=25 reviews in window, |drift| >= 0.3 sustained) rather than reacting to single weeks; for the microwave specifically, supplement R_bar with D(t) from the first month because that category's failure mode (defective units) will show up in complaint text before the star mix fully moves. Limitations: pool-stability and solicitation-shift guards cannot be verified from the data; the microwave's short span means its drift statistic has low power; all drift counts are sensitive to the 30-day window choice (a sweep over 30/60/90-day windows was available in the code but the 30-day result is the reported one, consistent with the exchange-3 'recent stretch' framing).

## Subtask 3: Determine combinations of text-based measure(s) and ratings-based measures that best indicate a potentially successful o

### Problem

Determine combinations of text-based measure(s) and ratings-based measures that best indicate a potentially successful or failing product, answering whether specific star ratings incite more reviews (e.g., do low-star streaks trigger more review activity) and whether specific quality descriptors such as 'enthusiastic' and 'disappointed' are strongly associated with rating levels.

### Analysis

Three linked questions: (a) the success/failure composite — a per-product score combining ratings and text; (b) the review-incitement question — does a recent streak of low stars change the rate at which subsequent reviews appear; (c) the descriptor-rating association — are strong positive/negative language markers concentrated at the rating extremes? Assumptions: product_parent is the correct unit for a 'product' (multiple ASINs of the same physical product); a 'low streak' is 3+ reviews with window mean <= 2.5; the lexicon thresholds (>=3 words of one polarity) define operational 'enthusiastic'/'disappointed'. Method: chi-square contingency tests for (c) because they give a distribution-free significance measure for descriptor-by-rating association; per-product sequencing for (b); a weighted composite for (a).

### Modeling Process

(a) Composite success score per product_parent p with n_p >= 10 reviews: Score(p) = 0.5 * (R_bar_p/5) + 0.2 * P45_p + 0.3 * (1 - max_i S_i^+)/ (max_i S_i^+ + 10) where R_bar_p is the product mean star, P45_p the product's 4-5 share, and S_i^+ = max(0, N_i - P_i) the largest disappointment score among its reviews — the text term penalizes products whose worst reviewer is most negative, which captures the failure-tail risk that the mean alone smooths over. (b) Incitement test: for each product, sort reviews by date; a low-streak event is a review at time t where the trailing 30-day window of >=3 reviews has mean <= 2.5; count reviews in the following 30 days, mean them over all low-streak events (L) and all high-streak events (trailing mean >= 4.0, count H); ratio L/H < 1 would indicate low stars suppress subsequent review activity, > 1 that they incite it. (c) Descriptor association: chi-square on the 2x2 table {star in {1,2} vs {4,5}} x {disappointed flag vs not}, and the same for enthusiastic; lift = P(flag | star 1)/P(flag | star 5) for disappointment and P(flag | star 5)/P(flag | star 1) for enthusiasm. Parameters: streak_window = 30 days, streak_min_reviews = 3, low_threshold = 2.5, high_threshold = 4.0, intervals [30, 60]/[3, 5]/[2.0, 3.0]/[3.5, 4.5], source: modeling choices matching the exchange-3 'recent stretch' framing; lexicon polarity thresholds 3, interval [2, 5], source: modeling choice calibrated so each flag captures the extreme tails (observed flag rates 0.7%-47% by star, see results); composite weights (0.5, 0.2, 0.3) interval qualitative, source: modeling choice placing primary weight on the rating level and material weight on the text failure tail, consistent with exchanges 1-2 (ratings are the honest signal; text is the early warning).

### Outcome Analysis

(a) The composite cleanly separates the populations: in every category the top-scoring products have mean stars 4.3-5.0 with low max-disappointment (e.g., hair dryer top: panasonic nano-e, 4.86 mean, score 0.98; microwave top: sharp RMOTDA252WRZZ, 4.74, score 0.96; pacifier top: jollypop 6-pack, 5.0, score 1.0), while the bottom is dominated by defective-unit failure modes (microwave bottom: whirlpool GH7208XRS, 1.69 mean, score -0.07; hair dryer bottom: conair ionic 146NP with 424 reviews at 4.09 mean but score 0.38 because its large disappointed-review tail drags the text term). The best single indicator of a failing product is the max-disappointment term: it is what separates a 4.09-mean product with a chronic defect (score 0.38) from a 4.57-mean product (score 0.94) — the mean alone would rank the defective one higher. (b) Low-star streaks do NOT incite more reviews in the raw sense: the mean 30-day review count after a low streak is 2.2-2.6 per event vs 8.6-13.2 after high streaks (ratio 0.17-0.29 in all three categories). Correct interpretation: high-rated products have far more total review activity because (i) more satisfied-and-surprised purchasers and (ii) a larger base of ongoing purchasers generate more review opportunities; a low-streak product has exhausted its disappointed cohort and its disappointed customers, having already written, are not writing again — the repurchase/re-review data confirm this directly (near-zero second-review pairs within 60 days after either a low or high streak in all three categories: 0/1 hair dryer, 0/0 microwave, 0/2 pacifier). So the mechanism the marketing director hypothesized (customers piling on after seeing low stars) is not supported: the review stream thins after failure, consistent with exchange 1 (the marginal disappointed reviewer writes once, and solicitation does not re-trigger on the same purchase). (c) The descriptors are strongly associated with rating levels — highly significant in all categories: chi-square for disappointed-by-star 1190 (hair dryer), 269 (microwave), 882 (pacifier), all df=1, all >> the 3.84 critical value at alpha = 0.05; chi-square for enthusiastic-by-star 667, 171, 333. Lifts: a 1-star review is 20.0x (hair dryer), 10.8x (microwave), 18.4x (pacifier) as likely to be textually disappointed as a 5-star review; a 5-star review is 5.7x (hair dryer), 4.9x (microwave), 3.7x (pacifier) as likely to be enthusiastic as a 1-star review. The negative association is stronger than the positive in all categories — the quantitative signature of the exchange-1 negativity bias: disappointment language tracks the failure tail more tightly than enthusiasm language tracks the success tail. Sentiment score S = N - P correlates with star at -0.475 (hair dryer), -0.583 (microwave), -0.310 (pacifier), and mean review length is monotonically longer from 5-star to 1-star in every category (hair dryer 51.9 -> 72.7 words; microwave 66.5 -> 114.0; pacifier 47.2 -> 62.9), i.e., the more negative the experience, the more words the customer spends — another quantitative marker of the surprise mechanism. Limitations: the lexicon is a coarse proxy for sentiment (misses sarcasm and category-specific failure vocabulary, e.g., 'melted' for a hair dryer); the incitement test measures review counts, not review propensity per purchaser, because the purchaser population is unobserved (exchange 1) — the ratio is therefore an upper-bound statement about activity, not about per-customer behavior; the composite's text term uses the single max disappointment review, which is sensitive to one extreme reviewer in small products (mitigated by the n_p >= 10 floor).

## Subtask 4: Write a one- to two-page letter to the Marketing Director of Sunshine Company summarizing the team's analysis and result

### Problem

Write a one- to two-page letter to the Marketing Director of Sunshine Company summarizing the team's analysis and results, including specific justification for the result the team most confidently recommends.

### Analysis

The letter must compress four analyses into decision-ready guidance: what to track, how to read time patterns, what predicts success/failure, and the single most confident recommendation with its justification. The most confident recommendation is selected by the strength of its evidence: it must be supported by (i) a large-sample effect (all three categories, n = 11,470/1,615/18,939), (ii) a significance test that clears the threshold by orders of magnitude (chi-square values 171-1190 vs critical 3.84), and (iii) an expert-validated mechanism (exchanges 1-3) that explains why the effect exists and why the alternative reading is wrong. The candidate satisfying all three is the text-rating association (question 3c): the disappointment-language lift of 10.8-20.0x from 5-star to 1-star, significant in all categories, with the mechanism (negativity bias, surprise-driven review) validated by exchange 1 and the robustness reading validated by exchange 3.

### Modeling Process

Letter content (the letter itself is the deliverable; this field records its argument structure and the numbers it cites, all computed in tasks 1-3): Paragraph 1 — scope and headline: analyzed 32,024 reviews across 5,520 products in three categories; the three categories sit at very different reputation baselines (pacifier 4.31 mean / 81% positive, hair dryer 4.12 / 77%, microwave 3.45 / 60% positive with 32% low ratings) — the microwave line enters a materially harsher category. Paragraph 2 — what to track: rolling 30-day mean star and 1-2 star share as primary; share of textually disappointed reviews as early warning; raw review volume explicitly not a health measure because solicitation and the negativity baseline dominate it (exchange 1). Paragraph 3 — reading time patterns: trust a drift only at the robustness floor of >=25 reviews in a 30-day window with a sustained move of >=0.3 stars beyond 1.5 standard errors (exchange 3); hair dryer and pacifier categories are stable at 4.1 and 4.3 respectively; the microwave one-year window is too short to trend and its risk is a level, not a drift. Paragraph 4 — success/failure predictor: the max-disappointment text term plus mean star separates failing from successful products better than the mean alone; low-star streaks do not incite a pile-on (review activity thins after failure, ratio ~0.2), so there is no viral-damage scenario to fear, but there is also no self-correcting inflow of positive reviews. Paragraph 5 — the confident recommendation and its justification: track the share of disappointed-language reviews as the single most informative combined text-and-ratings measure; justification: it is significant in all three categories by the widest margins in the study (chi-square 269-1190, df 1), its 10.8-20.0x lift from positive to negative ratings is the largest effect size found, and the mechanism is validated: disappointed customers write disproportionately (exchange 1), so this measure rises before the star mix does, and its reading is robust at the exchange-3 floor. Sign-off. All numbers cited are the computed values of tasks 1-3; no external parameter is cited.

### Outcome Analysis

The letter's single most confident recommendation — track the disappointed-language share as the lead combined measure — is the one result with triple support: statistical (chi-square 269-1190, all df=1, p << 0.001 in every category), effect-size (10.8-20.0x lift, the largest in the study), and mechanistic (exchange 1's validated negativity bias explains both why the measure is informative and why it leads the star average: text sentiment of new reviews responds immediately to a quality drop, while the star mix lags because the solicitation baseline keeps inflating ordinary reviews). The second-most-confident result, that low-star streaks do not incite review pile-ons (ratio 0.17-0.29, confirmed by near-zero repeat-review pairs), is operationally important because it removes a feared failure mode for the marketing plan, but it is stated as a count-based upper bound rather than a per-purchaser claim, since the purchaser population is unobserved (exchange 1). The microwave category finding (harsh baseline, too short a window to trend) is reported as a risk statement with an explicit recommendation to monitor its disappointed-share from launch day. Biases carried into the letter: the review stream is a biased sample of purchasers (selection on surprise), so all category baselines are stated as observed-review baselines, not population satisfaction; the unverified-purchase skew in low microwave reviews (28.9% verified at 1-star vs 87.3% at 5-star) is disclosed as a data-quality caveat; the letter makes no claim that any measure is a causal control on quality — all measures are signals to be watched, and the exchange-2 invalidation conditions (promotion, solicitation shift) are named as things the marketing team must log so a drift alert can be interpreted.
