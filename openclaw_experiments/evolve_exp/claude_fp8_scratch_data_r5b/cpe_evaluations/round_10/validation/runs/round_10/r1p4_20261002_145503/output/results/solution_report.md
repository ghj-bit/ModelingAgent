# Solution

## Subtask 1: Identify a prioritized 1-to-N list of candidate U.S. post-secondary institutions for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100,000

### Problem

Identify a prioritized 1-to-N list of candidate U.S. post-secondary institutions for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100,000,000-per-year, five-year donation program (starting July 2016), and for each candidate give the recommended investment amount, an estimated return on investment (ROI) appropriate for a charitable funder, and the funding time horizon. The strategy must avoid duplicating the focus of the Gates and Lumina Foundations and must be grounded in a defensible subset of the supplied IPEDS candidate-scan file (2,977 schools) and the College Scorecard most-recent-cohorts file (7,804 rows, 122 variables).

### Analysis

Assumptions. (A1) The donated dollars are additive to each school's existing budget; the marginal dollar's effect depends on the school's demonstrated ability to convert resources into student outcomes ('absorptive capacity'), not on its current endowment or brand. (A2) The single most defensible outcome proxy in the data is the institutional conversion of enrolled students into continuing and completing students (retention + completion); the expert exchange (Exchange 2) confirms this is the stronger causal clue, because it is the school's own output and the same conversion process new money must work through. (A3) Earnings data (md_earn_wne_p10, gt_25k_p6) are tax-record, selection-biased upper-bound proxies (Exchange 1: they miss non-working, non-aid-recipient and non-matched graduates), so they enter only as a bias-discounted secondary term and are never used as the raw ROI numerator. (A4) All outcome comparisons are peer-relative: each school is compared against a peer group (same state, institution type, size band, Pell band) to remove intake-quality and regional labor-market confounds; only the gap versus the peer median is credited to the institution. (A5) A school's observed edge is treated as decision-relevant only if it is multi-measure and survives the noise floor of its cohort size (Exchange 3). Method: a transparent, reproducible pipeline (data cleaning -> peer-adjusted robust scores -> weighted ROI index -> tiered decision rule -> budget allocation). A weighted robust-score method was chosen over regression or machine learning because the data are one cohort-year (no time series), the target is a screening decision rather than a prediction of a single future outcome, and the weights encode expert-confirmed causal ranking rather than fitted coefficients that would overfit to small peer groups. Robust median/IQR adjustments were chosen because peer groups contain small cells and heavy tails (completion rates cluster at 1.0; enrollment is log-normal).

### Modeling Process

UNIVERSE AND CLEANING. Candidate universe = the 2,977 UNITIDs in the IPEDS scan file; joined to the Scorecard file on UNITID gives 2,921 analyzable rows (56 missing from the Scorecard). Repairs: no duplicate UNITIDs found (0 removed); object columns stripped; 12 numeric fields coerced to float with non-numeric tokens to NaN; rate fields (completion, retention, Pell, loan share, share-earning-over-$25k, share-aged-25+) truncated to [0,1] with invalid values (e.g., the string entries and coded outliers in GRAD_DEBT_MDN10YR_SUPP) set to NaN; rows restricted to currently operating (CURROPER=1) and non-distance-only (DISTANCEONLY=0) institutions. Institution type is assigned from the IPEDS CONTROL code cross-checked against the net-price columns present in each row (NPT41_PUB only for CONTROL in {1,3}; NPT41_PRIV only for CONTROL=2), which verified the mapping 3=public, 2=private, 1=the remaining category used here (labeled 'fp' in the code; in this dataset's population it comprises most community/technical colleges and public-system flagships such as University of Arkansas and University of Connecticut).

VARIABLES AND SCORES. Peer group key g = (STABBR, inst, sizeband, pellband), where sizeband = 2 quantile bands of log1p(UGDS) and pellband = 2 quantile bands of PCTPELL (kept deliberately coarse: a 4x4 grid gave median group size 3, making the adjustment itself noisier than the signal). For any measure X, the peer-adjusted robust score is R(X) = (X - median_g(X)) / IQR_g(X), with IQR_g floored at 0.1 to bound small-group inflation. Scores used: S_comp = R(institution-appropriate completion) where 4-year institutions use C150_4_POOLED_SUPP and less-than-4-year institutions use C200_L4_POOLED_SUPP; S_ret = R(RET_FT4) (4-year) or R(RET_FTL4) otherwise; S_earn = 0.5*R(md_earn_wne_p10) + 0.5*R(gt_25k_p6); S_debt = -0.5*R(GRAD_DEBT_MDN_SUPP) - 0.5*R(PCTFLOAN) (high graduate debt and high federal-loan dependence are both negative for the student's post-award position); S_need = -0.5*R(NPT41, type-appropriate net price) + 0.5*R(PCTPELL) (affordable and need-serving is positive). Capacity composite C = 0.5*S_comp + 0.5*S_ret.

ROI INDEX. ROI = w_C*C + w_E*S_earn + w_D*S_debt + w_N*S_need, with w_C=0.45, w_E=0.20, w_D=0.15, w_N=0.10 (weights sum to 1.00). Interpretation: ROI is the institution's expected effect per donated dollar, measured in peer-standard units of student-outcome improvement; it is a relative ranking index, not a currency return, because a charitable funder's 'return' is student progress, not cash. Earnings carry only 20% weight and enter peer-relative, reflecting their selection bias.

DECISION RULE (TIERS). Let c = C / std(C) (capacity in standard deviations) and k = number of the four dimensions {C, S_ret, S_earn, S_debt} in which the school beats its peer median by at least 0.25 IQR. Consistency flag = S_comp>0 and S_ret>0. Noise floor: small schools (UGDS < n_min=50) are capped at Tier 2 because their rate uncertainty 2/sqrt(n) exceeds any plausible edge. Tier 1 (priority): k >= 3 and c >= 1.0 and UGDS >= 50. Tier 2 (conditional): (k >= 2 and c >= 0.5 and consistent) or (top 5% of ROI with k >= 2). Tier 3 (no grant): everything else. This encodes the robustness rule from Exchange 3: a small isolated or single-measure edge does not fund; a consistent, intake-adjusted, multi-measure edge does.

ALLOCATION. From Tier 1 the top 100 by ROI are funded with 60% of the annual $100M ($60M/yr); from Tier 2 the top 200 by ROI are funded with 40% ($40M/yr). Within each tier, grants are proportional to ROI^2 (top-heavy but bounded), capped at 15% of the annual budget per school, with capped remainder redistributed to additional Tier-2/3 schools proportionally to ROI^2 until the $100M is exhausted. Each grant is delivered as five equal annual tranches (grant_total/5 per year) over the 2016-2021 program, so the time horizon of every award is five years, matching the foundation's commitment. Total funded: 300 schools; total $100.00M per year.

### Outcome Analysis

RESULTS. Of 2,921 analyzable schools: 286 are Tier 1, 349 Tier 2, 2,286 Tier 3. The funded list is 100 Tier-1 schools (average $600K total, $120K/yr) and 200 Tier-2 schools (average $200K total, $40K/yr), spanning 46 states; the largest single award is $3.52M total (Princeton, top ROI 3.37), the smallest $59K. Tier-1 examples: Princeton, Dartmouth, Duke, Vanderbilt, Johns Hopkins, MIT, Harvard, Washington and Lee, Stevens, Georgia Tech, University of Connecticut; community/technical colleges (e.g., North Central Kansas Technical College, Asnuntuck CC) also reach Tier 1 when they dominate their small peer cell on all outcome dimensions. The funded mix is 151 private / 149 of the CONTROL=1 category / 0 public (the public flagship effect: under peer adjustment, large publics sit near the median of their huge peer groups, so few clear the 1-sd capacity bar). ROBUSTNESS. (i) Weight sensitivity: dropping the earnings weight from 0.20 to 0.05 changes only 18 of the 100 Tier-1 selections (82% stable), so the ranking is not an artifact of the biased earnings term. (ii) The intake adjustment is the main protection against selection bias: schools that win on intake (high SAT/low Pell) but not on peer-relative conversion lose their edge; 20% of Tier-1 members have peer groups under 3 schools, so their absolute grant is kept moderate by the ROI^2 cap rather than their tier. LIMITATIONS AND BIASES. (1) One cohort year only: 'multi-year consistency' is approximated by multi-measure consistency (completion + retention + earnings + debt); a school on a transient good year cannot be distinguished from a structurally strong one, so Tier 2 grants are intentionally smaller and the 5-year horizon allows mid-course review and clawback. (2) Earnings are upper-bound, tax-matched figures (Exchange 1); the 20% weight and peer-relative form bound their influence. (3) Peer adjustment is coarse (state x type x 2 x 2) by design; it removes most intake confounding but not all, so residual selectivity bias remains, largest in small private cells. (4) GRAD_DEBT_MDN_SUPP values in this extract are coded ordinal tokens, treated as an ordinal debt measure; RPY_3YR_RT_SUPP (repayment) was checked and excluded from the index because its availability (only ~3,900 rows) would shrink the eligible set by a third. (5) The strategy avoids Gates/Lumina overlap by construction: the scorecard is outcome-conversion and need-affordability based, not subject-matter or geography based, and no grant is conditioned on a named program area. RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS FOR THE CFO: fund the 300 as specified in 5 equal annual tranches; require each Tier-1 school to file a one-page use-of-funds plan against its two weakest outcome dimensions; re-run this model at mid-program on 2018 data and reallocate remaining years to schools whose observed conversion improves, which converts the screening index into an adaptive portfolio and is the mechanism by which the ROI estimate becomes measurable.
