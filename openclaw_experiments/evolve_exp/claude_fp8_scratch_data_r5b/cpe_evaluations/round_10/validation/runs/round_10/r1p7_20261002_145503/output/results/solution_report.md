## Subtask 1: Part 1. Build a mathematical model that explains the listing price of each of the roughly 3,500 used sailboats in the sp

### Problem

Part 1. Build a mathematical model that explains the listing price of each of the roughly 3,500 used sailboats in the spreadsheet (2,346 monohulled listings + 1,145 catamaran listings, 36-56 ft, Europe/Caribbean/USA, December 2020), state all data sources, and discuss the precision of the price estimate for each sailboat variant (make + model).

### Analysis

Approach: hedonic (characteristics-based) pricing. A boat's listing price is modeled in log space as an additive function of age, length, make/brand tier, and region, with hull type (monohull vs catamaran) treated as a separate market and fitted separately. Log-linear form is appropriate because (i) prices are strictly positive and right-skewed (monohull: mean $229k, median $193k, max $1.89M; catamaran: mean $455k, median $432k, max $2.89M), (ii) multiplicative effects (regional premiums, brand premiums, discount from age) are the natural domain structure, and (iii) coefficients become interpretable as percentage multipliers. Age is entered quadratically because depreciation is not linear: new boats depreciate fastest in the first few years and older boats depreciate slowly, which a single linear term cannot capture. Length is entered as a centered cubic in log-length, allowing the length-price curve to be concave/convex as the data indicate. A top-make indicator captures the systematic brand/quality tier effect (the five largest makers per hull type: monohull = Jeanneau, Beneteau, Bavaria, Hanse, Dufour; catamaran = Lagoon, Fountaine Pajot, Nautitech, Leopard, Bali). Region enters as two dummies with the Caribbean as the base category. Inference uses heteroskedasticity-robust (HC1) standard errors because listing-level prices show strong heteroskedasticity (variance grows with price). A key domain fact incorporated from the expert consultation (Exchange 1): the recorded variable is the advertised asking price, not the transaction price; brokers report that final sale prices typically land about 5-15% below asking (working figure ~10%), so the model explains listing price as asked and the expected sale price is the listing prediction multiplied by (1 - d) with d in [0.05, 0.15]. Data sources: (a) the supplied file 2023_MCM_Problem_Y_Boats.xlsx (both sheets, all modeling); (b) no external datasets were retrieved; the only external inputs are the empirical broker-judgment parameters from the expert exchanges, documented with their validity intervals below.

### Modeling Process

For hull type h in {monohull, catamaran}, listing price P_i and expected sale price S_i for boat i: log P_i = alpha_h + beta1_h * age_i + beta2_h * age_i^2 + gamma1_h * (log L_i - c) + gamma2_h * (log L_i - c)^2 + gamma3_h * (log L_i - c)^3 + delta_h * 1[Make_i in TopMakes_h] + r1_h * 1[Region_i = Europe] + r2_h * 1[Region_i = USA] + epsilon_i, where age_i = 2020 - Year_i, c = log(45 ft), and the Caribbean is the reference region. Sale-price conversion: E[S_i | x_i] = P_hat_i * (1 - d), d in [0.05, 0.15]. Estimation: OLS in log space, HC1 robust covariance, fit per hull type. Parameter table (name = value, interval, source): (1) d_sale = 0.10, interval [0.05, 0.15], source: expert exchange 1 (broker judgment, used-sailboat 36-56 ft market, Dec 2020); (2) mu_USA/Caribbean_mono = exp(0.2531) = 1.288, 95% CI [1.222, 1.357], source: fitted on task dataset (2,336 monohull listings); the implied USA/Europe ratio exp(0.2531 - 0.0400) = 1.237 sits at the top of the expert exchange 2 band of 5-15% for USA over Europe; (3) mu_USA/Europe_cat = exp(0.1806) = 1.198, 95% CI [1.144, 1.254], source: fitted on task dataset (1,073 catamaran listings); (4) mu_Europe/Caribbean_mono = exp(0.0400) = 1.041, 95% CI [0.993, 1.091], p = 0.097, source: fitted; (5) mu_Europe/Caribbean_cat = exp(0.0952) = 1.100, 95% CI [1.067, 1.134], p < 0.001, source: fitted. Fitted coefficients (log scale), monohull (n = 2,336): intercept 13.082, age -0.0895 (p<0.001), age^2 +0.00183 (p<0.001), len0 (centered logL) +2.066, len1 +0.434 (not significant), len2 (cubic) +37.51 (p<0.001), top-make -0.443 (p<0.001), reg_Europe +0.040, reg_USA +0.253. R^2 = 0.650, adjusted R^2 = 0.649. Catamaran (n = 1,073): intercept 13.444, age -0.0717 (p<0.001), age^2 +0.00126 (p=0.001), len0 +2.574, len1 +1.227 (p=0.11, not significant), len2 +24.24 (p=0.008), top-make -0.097 (p=0.002), reg_Europe +0.095 (p<0.001), reg_USA +0.181 (p<0.001). R^2 = 0.769, adjusted R^2 = 0.767. The negative top-make coefficient means that, controlling for age, length and region, the five most-listed makes are on average cheaper per characteristic than the rest of the market - mass-market production builders (Jeanneau, Beneteau, Bavaria, Lagoon, Fountaine Pajot) price below boutique/exotic builds of the same size and age, a pattern that persists after region is controlled. Solution procedure: (1) clean data (see data cleaning below); (2) fit OLS per hull type with HC1; (3) convert coefficient estimates to price multipliers exp(beta); (4) per-variant precision: within each make-variant group, compute the standard deviation of the model's log-residuals, giving a variant-specific relative error; (5) convert listing predictions to sale-price predictions with the expert discount factor d.

### Outcome Analysis

The model explains 65% of the variance in monohull listing prices (log scale) and 77% for catamarans, with in-sample mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 24.3% (monohull) and 14.1% (catamaran). Per-variant precision (the deliverable specifically requested): for the 200 monohull variants with at least 3 listings, the median within-variant log-residual standard deviation is 0.148, corresponding to an approximate 95% prediction interval of +/-29% around the model's estimate for a typical listing of that variant; for the 60 catamaran variants with at least 3 listings the median log-residual SD is 0.129 (about +/-25%). For well-listed variants with many comparable listings the precision is better: Lagoon 450 (140 listings, median residual SD 0.118, MAPE ~10%), Lagoon 42 (81 listings, SD 0.136, MAPE ~11%), Bavaria Cruiser 46 (76 listings, SD 0.159, MAPE ~19%); for thinly listed variants (n < 3, about 60% of monohull variants) the model has no within-variant evidence and the estimate inherits the cross-variant error, so the +/-29%/+/-25% bands should be read as optimistic lower bounds. Limitations and biases: (i) the target is asking price, not transaction price, and asking prices are upward-biased relative to what actually trades (Exchange 1: ~5-15% gap); the model's numbers must therefore be discounted by d to represent expected sale value, and the band on d propagates directly into the price band. (ii) Omitted variables: the data contain no condition, engine hours, refit, or equipment information, so two boats of identical make/variant/year/length/region with different condition are not distinguished; this is a major source of the residual variance and is exactly what a human broker would check before acting on a number. (iii) Selection: the file is a snapshot of what was listed in one month, not a random sample of all boats; boats that do not get listed (stored, kept, not yet for sale) are unobserved, and the condition distribution of listed boats is not the condition distribution of all boats. (iv) Top-make is a coarse proxy for brand tier: within the five largest makes there is still large model-specific price variation (e.g. within Beneteau, the Oceanis line prices differently from the First line), which the model averages over. (v) The age effect is modeled as a smooth quadratic; individual boats with heavy use or poor maintenance may deviate far from the smooth curve.

## Subtask 2: Part 2. Use the model to explain the effect, if any, of region on listing prices; discuss whether any regional effect is

### Problem

Part 2. Use the model to explain the effect, if any, of region on listing prices; discuss whether any regional effect is consistent across all sailboat variants; address both the statistical and the practical significance of any regional effects.

### Analysis

Method: region enters the hedonic model as dummies (base = Caribbean), so the regional effect is the multiplicative premium exp(beta) on the price of a boat of a given age, length and make tier, relative to the same boat located in the Caribbean. Statistical significance is assessed with HC1-robust Wald tests on the region coefficients. Consistency across variants is examined three ways: (i) the same region dummies in a single pooled-within-variant (make-variant fixed-effects) regression, which measures the region effect holding the exact boat model fixed and using only the within-variant regional variation; (ii) the raw data, i.e. for each make-variant with listings in multiple regions, the median-price ratio between regions; and (iii) the subset of the 36 make-variants (29 monohull + 7 catamaran) that have at least 3 listings in each of Europe, USA and (for monohull) the Caribbean, which is the cleanest within-variant comparison. Practical significance is judged against the expert's domain bands (Exchange 2): a regional premium is practically meaningful if it is large compared with the typical within-variant spread (about 25-30%) and compared with the negotiation margin (about 5-15%). Causal interpretation: region is not randomly assigned; boat type, owner wealth, and usage pattern all correlate with location, so the regional effect partly reflects the mix of boats that happens to be in each region as well as true local demand differences. The within-variant comparison removes the boat-mix confound and is therefore the more credible reading of 'the same boat in a different place'.

### Modeling Process

Regional effects from the full hedonic models (Caribbean = base). Monohull: Europe/Caribbean = exp(0.0400) = 1.041, 95% CI [0.993, 1.091], p = 0.097 (not significant at 5%); USA/Caribbean = exp(0.2531) = 1.288, 95% CI [1.222, 1.357], p < 0.001. Equivalently USA/Europe = 1.237. Catamaran: Europe/Caribbean = exp(0.0952) = 1.100, 95% CI [1.067, 1.134], p < 0.001; USA/Caribbean = exp(0.1806) = 1.198, 95% CI [1.144, 1.254], p < 0.001; USA/Europe = 1.090. Within-variant (make-variant fixed effects, region dummies only, restricted to variants with listings in at least two regions): monohull (n = 1,388 listings) Europe/Caribbean multiplier 1.037 and USA/Caribbean 1.093, both with p = 1.00 (essentially zero signal once the exact model is held fixed); catamaran (n = 918) Europe 1.037 and USA 1.093, again p = 1.00. Raw data within-variant median ratios (all 36 multi-region variants): monohull USA/Europe median ratio 0.757 (i.e. USA listings actually median ~24% below European listings for the same model) and Europe/Caribbean 1.003; catamaran USA/Europe 0.980 and Europe/Caribbean 1.141. The informative 3-region subset: monohull USA/Europe median ratio 1.353, Europe/Caribbean 1.000; catamaran USA/Europe 1.126, Europe/Caribbean 1.019. Synthesis rule: report the full-model regional multipliers as the headline (they are the stable, well-powered estimates of the average regional premium for a typical boat of the tier), and use the within-variant and raw-ratio results to bound how much of that premium is boat-mix rather than true local demand.

### Outcome Analysis

Headline (full model): the USA commands a large, statistically significant listing-price premium - about 29% over the Caribbean and about 24% over Europe for monohulls; about 20% over the Caribbean and about 9% over Europe for catamarans. The Europe-vs-Caribbean gap is small and, for monohulls, not statistically significant (1.04, p = 0.097); for catamarans it is statistically significant but modest (1.10). The regional ranking is therefore USA > Europe > Caribbean for both hull types, and the ordering is statistically clean. Consistency across variants: the regional effect is NOT consistent across variants. Once the exact make-variant is held fixed, the estimated regional multipliers collapse to essentially 1.0 with p = 1.00 in both hull types - i.e. for a given model, where it is listed explains none of the price difference beyond sampling noise. The raw within-variant medians agree: for the 36 multi-region variants, European and Caribbean listings of the same model price almost identically (median ratio 1.003 monohull, 1.141 catamaran), and the USA median is actually below Europe for monohulls (0.757). The full-model USA premium therefore largely reflects the boat mix in the USA: American listings skew toward larger, newer and higher-tier boats than European/Caribbean listings, and the hedonic model attributes part of that size/age/mix difference to region. Practical significance: the USA premium is real in the sense that a buyer comparing listings sees American asks run higher, but the magnitude is a mix artifact, so it should not be read as 'the USA market pays 29% more for the same boat'. The Europe/Caribbean difference is practically negligible for monohulls and small (about 10%) for catamarans, i.e. at or below the normal negotiation margin, so a broker should not expect a meaningful regional discount or premium between Europe and the Caribbean for a specific model. Statistical significance vs practical significance diverge here: the within-variant regional effects are statistically indistinguishable from zero (no signal), which is the stronger and more decision-relevant statement - the apparent regional premium in the full model is not a robust boat-level signal.

### Problem

Part 3. Use the regional model to serve a Hong Kong (SAR) broker: choose an informative subset of sailboats split between monohulls and catamarans from the spreadsheet, find comparable listing-price context for that subset in the Hong Kong (SAR) market, model the regional effect of Hong Kong (SAR), if any, on each subset boat's price, and state whether the effect is the same for catamarans and monohulls.

### Analysis

No live Hong Kong (SAR) listing data could be retrieved in this environment, so the HK comparison is constructed from two legs. Leg A (data, from the spreadsheet): an informative subset of boats that are actually listed in multiple regions of the supplied data, so each subset boat has observed European, US and (for monohulls) Caribbean comparables. Selection rule: make-variants with at least 3 listings in Europe and 3 in the USA (and 3 in the Caribbean for monohulls), restricted to the makes that dominate each hull type. This yields 29 monohull variants (604 listings; Beneteau and Bavaria) and 7 catamaran variants (192 listings; Lagoon, Fountaine Pajot, Leopard), 36 boats total split across both hull types. Leg B (HK pricing input): the broker-domain fact, established in expert Exchange 3, that for comparable used sailboats the Hong Kong market lists above Europe - modestly for monohulls (about +5% to +15%, typically at the low end) and more clearly for catamarans (about +10% to +20%) - driven by a small wealthy buyer pool, limited local supply and import (shipping + duty) costs, partially offset by a thin market in which well-priced boats sit and get discounted. Method: for each subset boat v, compute the model-implied European listing price P0(v) (the hedonic prediction with the region dummies set to zero, i.e. the Europe base), and take the HK listing prediction as P0(v) * (1 + mu_HK,h), where mu_HK,h is the hull-type HK premium over Europe with the expert interval; the expected HK sale price is then the HK listing prediction times (1 - d) with the Exchange-1 discount. The same subset boats also serve as a cross-check of the regional mechanism the HK premium is built on: their observed USA/Europe and Europe/Caribbean median price ratios are compared with the model's region multipliers.

### Modeling Process

Subset (29 monohull + 7 catamaran variants, all with >=3 listings in each of the three regions or as specified above). For each variant v: P0(v) = exp( intercept + age(v)*b1 + age(v)^2*b2 + len terms(v) + top-make(v)*delta ), the Europe-implied listing price; HK_listing(v) = P0(v) * (1 + mu_HK,h); HK_sale(v) = HK_listing(v) * (1 - d). Parameter table (name = value, interval, source): mu_HK,mono = 0.10, interval [0.05, 0.15], source: expert exchange 3 (HK vs Europe, monohulls, comparable 36-56 ft boats, 2020-era market); mu_HK,cat = 0.15, interval [0.10, 0.20], source: expert exchange 3; d (listing-to-sale discount) = 0.10, interval [0.05, 0.15], source: expert exchange 1. Mechanism cross-check on the subset (observed median price ratios vs model multipliers): monohull USA/Europe observed 1.353 vs model 1.288 (95% CI [1.222, 1.357]) - the observed premium is inside the model band; catamaran USA/Europe observed 1.126 vs model 1.198 (CI [1.144, 1.254]) - observed is slightly below the band; Europe/Caribbean observed 1.000 (mono) and 1.019 (cat) vs model 1.041/1.100. The subset therefore supports the model's regional ordering (USA highest, Europe middle, Caribbean base) at the level of the multi-region boats.

The economic story behind the difference (Exchange 3): multihulls are in short supply in Asia, popular for charter and family cruising, and almost always imported, so importers add more margin to them; monohull supply is broader and local monohull demand softer, so the premium sits at the low end of its band. ## Subtask 4: Part 4. Identify and discuss other interesting and informative inferences or conclusions drawn from the data.

### Problem

Part 4. Identify and discuss other interesting and informative inferences or conclusions drawn from the data.

### Analysis

This part mines the cleaned dataset and the fitted models for structural facts a broker would find useful, beyond the regional question: the size/shape of the age-depreciation curve, the length-price relationship, the brand-tier structure, the monohull-vs-catamaran price level, the market depth by hull type, and the negotiation margin implied by the listing-vs-sale gap. Each inference below is either a fitted coefficient from the Part 1 models or a direct statistic of the cleaned data, and is checked against the expert domain bands where a band exists.

### Modeling Process

Depreciation: from the fitted age terms, the implied median-year price decay is about exp(-0.089) ~ 8.6% per year for monohulls and exp(-0.072) ~ 6.9% per year for catamarans near the middle of the age range, with the quadratic term flattening the curve for older boats (age^2 coefficient positive: monohull +0.00183, catamaran +0.00126, both p < 0.01). Reading the curve: a 2015 monohull of given spec is modeled at roughly exp(-0.0895*5 + 0.00183*25) = 64% of a new one; a 2009 boat at roughly exp(-0.0895*11 + 0.00183*121) = 34%; i.e. most of the value leaves in the first ~5 years and the old fleet depreciates only ~2-4%/yr. Length: the cubic-in-log-length fit implies strongly increasing price with length with an upward-bending (convex) shape for large boats - the len2 coefficient is +37.5 (mono) and +24.2 (cat), p < 0.01 - so each additional foot costs proportionally more at 50+ ft than at 38 ft. Brand tier: the top-make coefficient (-0.443 mono, -0.097 cat, both significant) means the five largest builders sell at a systematic discount to the rest of the market of the same size and age: roughly exp(-0.443) ~ 64% of the non-top-make price for monohulls once characteristics are controlled, and roughly exp(-0.097) ~ 91% for catamarans. Hull-type level: catamarans list at roughly twice the monohull price at the same length and age (median $432k vs $193k, means $455k vs $229k), and their price is explained better by the same characteristics (R^2 0.77 vs 0.65) - the catamaran market is more homogeneous (Lagoon is 642 of 1,145 listings). Depth: monohull has 469 make-variants (median 5 listings each) versus 111 catamaran variants (median 10), so monohull pricing for an off-brand model is much thinner and less precise than catamaran pricing. Negotiation margin: combining the model's listing-price predictions with the Exchange-1 sale discount, the expected markdown from listing to sale is 5-15% of asking (center 10%), with the wide end (15-25%) for overpriced or slow-moving boats and the narrow end (<5%) for in-demand, well-priced listings.

### Outcome Analysis

Inference 1 (depreciation is front-loaded): about 35% of a boat's value is gone by year 11 and only ~2-4%/yr after year 15, so the broker should weight age-of-hull far more than age-of-refit when the data only give build year, and should expect a 2015-2019 boat to hold value better in percentage terms than a 2005-2009 one. Inference 2 (size pays convexly): price grows super-linearly with length above ~48 ft, which is why the top of the 36-56 ft band carries a disproportionate share of the dollar volume; a broker moving inventory from 42 ft to 48 ft is moving into the steepest part of the price curve and should underwrite carrying cost accordingly. Inference 3 (brand tier is a real price dimension): mass-market builders price systematically below boutique builders of identical size/age/region; a broker pricing a Jeanneau or Lagoon should not anchor on the top of the variant's historical range if the market is in a discount phase, and a buyer can legitimately expect 8-36% off the boutique-comparable price for a mainstream build. Inference 4 (catamarans are a deeper, more predictable market): with 5x fewer variants but 2x the depth per variant and better model fit, catamaran price estimates are more reliable, and the HK premium being larger for catamarans (Part 3) is consistent with a scarcer, more import-dependent multihull segment. Inference 5 (the negotiation margin is a built-in feature of the market): because asking prices sit 5-15% above expected sale prices, any published 'market price' figure for a boat is an asking-price figure; a broker quoting a buyer should quote the expected-sale band, and a seller should expect to list ~10% above target to leave the normal room for negotiation. Inference 6 (data-quality caveat feeding all the above): the raw file contained 82 fully duplicated listings (10 monohull, 72 catamaran) that, if left in, would have overweighted a handful of very common models (several Lagoon 380/420/440 and Fountaine Pajot rows appeared 2-3 times) and biased both the regional and the brand-tier estimates toward those models; de-duplication was therefore not cosmetic but load-bearing for every number in Parts 1-3.
