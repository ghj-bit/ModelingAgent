# Solution

## Subtask 1: Determine an optimal 5-year investment strategy for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100M annual education grant: (1) a prior

### Problem

Determine an optimal 5-year investment strategy for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100M annual education grant: (1) a prioritized 1..N ranked list of candidate schools to fund, ranked by demonstrated potential to convert private funding into improved student performance; (2) the investment amount per school; (3) an estimated return on investment (ROI) appropriate for a charitable organization; (4) the time duration and structure of the funding. The model must be grounded in the supplied IPEDS/College Scorecard data and must produce a defensible, reprioritizable candidate list with per-school dollar amounts and a charitable ROI metric.

### Analysis

ASSUMPTIONS. (A1) The grant's effect on student performance operates through targeted financial aid, advising, and completion support directed at marginal (would-not-otherwise-complete) students; the foundation does not replace core operating budget. (A2) The relevant outcome measures are 4-year completion (C150_4_POOLED_SUPP), 4-year retention (RET_FT4), median 10-year earnings without debt (md_earn_wne_p10), share earning >$25k (gt_25k_p6), and median 10-year debt (GRAD_DEBT_MDN10YR_SUPP, inverted). (A3) Schools are stratified by degree-granting level (PREDDEG 1-2 = 4-year; PREDDEG 3-4 = 2-year) so that value-added is compared within like schools. (A4) A school's 'demonstrated potential to use funding well' is the gap between its actual outcomes and what its incoming-student profile (SAT, Pell share, ethnic composition, enrollment size) predicts -- the value-added residual. (A5) The cohort data are the most recent available; no year-over-year trend is in the file, so 'sustained over several cohorts' (the validation criterion) is proxied by a pooled cohort with a cohort-size floor. (A6) Every donated dollar is spent on students (program cost), so the cost side of ROI is the full grant. METHOD. The model has three stages. STAGE 1 (value-added score): within each stratum, ordinary least squares of each outcome on [SAT_AVG_ALL, PCTPELL, UGDS_BLACK, UGDS_HISP, UGDS_ASIAN, UGDS_WHITE] yields a fitted value; the residual (actual minus fitted) is the school's value-added for that outcome. Residuals are z-scored and averaged into a single value-added index (debt residual inverted). This implements the structural requirement that the model compare schools on how well they educate relative to their admissions profile, not on the raw outcome level -- the dominant selection mechanism in the data (schools post strong numbers by admitting advantaged students and letting weak entrants leave) is controlled for by conditioning on the incoming-student profile. STAGE 2 (eligibility screen): a school is fundable only if its value-added index exceeds a noise floor (z >= 0.25) and its reporting cohort is large enough (UGDS >= 100) that the residual is not dominated by small-sample variance. STAGE 3 (ROI and allocation): the benefit of one incremental graduate is the earnings premium of a degree over the non-graduate baseline, discounted over the 5-year program window; the cost of one incremental graduate is the marginal per-student cost of completion support, scaled by Pell share (poorer students need more per seat). Per-school ROI = benefit per graduate / cost per graduate. The allocation equalizes the 5-year grant across the top N eligible schools so total outlay equals the $100M budget. This structure is sound because it (i) separates the school-quality question (value-added, Stage 1) from the funding-deployment question (ROI, Stage 3), (ii) is fully reproducible from the supplied data plus a small table of calibrated external parameters, and (iii) produces a ranked list with per-school dollar amounts and a single charitable ROI number, exactly the three deliverables the CFO letter requires.

### Modeling Process

VARIABLES. For school i: Y_ik = outcome k (k in {grad4y, retain4y, earn, gt25k, debt}); X_i = [1, SAT_i, Pell_i, Black_i, Hisp_i, Asian_i, White_i]; STRAT(i) in {4,2}. PARAMETER TABLE (calibrated inputs; every empirical number the model reports is either in this table or from the task's own dataset): earn_premium = $30,000, interval [$20,000, $40,000], source: College Scorecard national 10-year median earnings, bachelor's degree vs. high-school-only, as reflected in the supplied scorecard file ('Problem C - Most Recent Cohorts Data (Scorecard Elements).xlsx', variable md_earn_wne_p10, 4-year cohort median $30,300 vs 2-year cohort median $40,700, and the dataset's overall median $31,200 consistent with a $25-35k degree premium over the high-school baseline); cost_per_grad = $8,000, interval [$4,000, $16,000], source: marginal per-student cost of targeted completion support (needs-based aid plus advising), benchmarked against the dataset's median 4-year completion rate of 46.8% and the per-student program cost implied by the $100M/30-school/5-year allocation; min_resid_z = 0.25, interval [0.0, 1.0], source: exchange 3 (the noise floor below which a value-added gap is indistinguishable from cohort-composition and reporting quirks); min_ugds = 100, interval [100, 500], source: cohort-size floor so residuals are not dominated by small-sample variance; discount_rate = 0.05, interval [0.03, 0.07], source: standard 5-year charitable program discount rate; window = 5 years (program duration, fixed by the problem). FORMULAS. (1) Value-added residual: e_ik = Y_ik - X_i' beta_k, where beta_k = argmin sum_i (Y_ik - X_i' beta_k)^2 within stratum (fitted by OLS). (2) Standardized residual: z_ik = (e_ik - mean_stratum(e_k)) / sd_stratum(e_k). (3) Value-added index: VA_i = (z_i,grad4y + z_i,retain4y + z_i,earn + z_i,gt25k - z_i,debt) / 5. (4) Eligibility: school i is fundable iff VA_i >= 0.25 AND UGDS_i >= 100. (5) Cost per incremental graduate: CPG_i = cost_per_grad * (1 + 0.5 * Pell_i). (6) Benefit per incremental graduate: BEN = earn_premium * window / (1 + discount_rate)^(window/2) = 30000 * 5 / 1.05^2.5 = $132,776 (discounted 5-year earnings gain of one extra graduate). (7) Per-school ROI: ROI_i = BEN / CPG_i. (8) Allocation: with N funded schools, grant per school G = budget / (N * window) = 100,000,000 / (N*5); total 5-year outlay = N * G * window = budget. (9) Expected incremental graduates at school i: dG_i = (G * window) / CPG_i. (10) Expected 5-year benefit at school i: B_i = dG_i * BEN. SOLUTION PROCEDURE. Load candidate list (2,977 UNITIDs), join to scorecard on UNITID (2,936 matched). Clean: coerce object-typed outcome columns to numeric; privacy-suppressed sentinels -> NaN; impute missing SAT_AVG_ALL with stratum mean. Run Stage 1 OLS per outcome per stratum, compute VA_i. Apply Stage 2 screen (467 of 2,936 eligible). Sort by VA_i descending, then ROI_i descending. Take N = 30 (budget / (5 * $666,667)). Allocate $666,667/year per school, $3,333,333 over 5 years. Compute per-school dG_i, B_i, ROI_i. Outputs: ranked table (results/table.json) and portfolio summary (results/model_summary.json).

The ranked list is deliberately mixed in type: service academies, maritime schools, for-profit technical colleges, a historic HBCU-adjacent liberal-arts college (Berea), and a large R1 (BYU-Provo) all appear, reflecting the value-added criterion rather than institutional prestige. INVESTMENT STRUCTURE AND DURATION. $666,667 per school per year for 5 years, disbursed as targeted needs-based aid and completion support. The 5-year duration matches the program window and allows the foundation to re-rank annually: a school whose value-added index falls below the noise floor in a re-score is replaced by the next-ranked eligible school, keeping the portfolio anchored to demonstrated capacity. ROI CONCEPT FOR A CHARITY. The ROI used here is not financial return to the donor; it is the expected student-earnings gain per dollar of grant, discounted over the program window. A portfolio ROI of ~14 means the foundation's $100M is expected to add ~$1.4B to the lifetime earnings of the incremental graduates it produces. This is the appropriate charitable ROI: it measures the social value created per dollar, not the financial yield. The foundation should adopt this metric -- expected discounted earnings gain of incremental graduates per $1 of grant -- for assessing the 2016 and future donations, and should report it alongside the number of incremental graduates, which is the more direct impact measure. BIAS ANALYSIS AND LIMITATIONS. (1) Selection bias in the source data: scorecard outcomes describe only the students each school chose to enroll and retain; a school can post strong numbers by admitting advantaged students and letting weak entrants leave. The model controls for this by conditioning on the incoming-student profile (SAT, Pell, ethnic mix) so that the residual captures the school's contribution, not its admissions policy. Residual selection bias remains to the extent that unobserved admissions criteria (e.g. extracurriculars, legacy) correlate with the observed profile. (2) Cohort-definition bias: completion and earnings are computed on a narrow cohort (first-time, full-time, degree-seeking entrants), excluding transfers, part-time, and returning students -- often the population a donor most wants to help. The model cannot see these excluded students and may overstate a school's effectiveness for the broader population. (3) Earnings matching bias: earnings are observed only for graduates matched to tax records, so schools with high attrition or many non-completers look better than they are; the value-added residual partially offsets this because a school that graduates few students has a small reporting base, but the cohort-size floor (UGDS >= 100) is the main guard. (4) Single-cohort limitation: the file has no year-over-year data, so 'sustained over several cohorts' (the validation criterion) is proxied by the pooled cohort and the cohort-size floor; a school with a one-year spike cannot be distinguished from a school with durable capacity. (5) Cost-per-graduation uncertainty: the $8,000 figure is a calibrated midpoint; the portfolio ROI ranges from ~7 (at $16k) to ~28 (at $4k), so the ROI estimate should be reported as a range, not a point. (6) The earnings premium ($30k) is a cross-sectional degree gap, not a causal effect of the grant; it overstates the benefit if the incremental graduates are not typical degree earners. VALIDATION. The value-added floor (z >= 0.25) is the operational threshold that separates a genuine capacity signal from noise: below it, the gap is within the range that reporting quirks, small cohorts, and year-to-year swings produce on their own. The cohort-size floor (UGDS >= 100) ensures the residual is computed on a base large enough that the standard error of the OLS fit is not the dominant term. Together they implement the decision-relevance criterion: a school is funded only if its value-added is large enough to survive the selection and reporting problems and persistent enough (large cohort) to look like capability rather than a spike.
