## Subtask 1: Task 1: Build a mathematical model that explains the listing price of each of the ~3,500 used sailboats (2,346 monohulle

### Problem

Task 1: Build a mathematical model that explains the listing price of each of the ~3,500 used sailboats (2,346 monohulled + 1,145 catamarans, 36-56 ft, listed Dec 2020 in Europe, the Caribbean, and the USA). Include useful predictors, document all data sources (including any supplemental), and discuss the precision of the price estimate for each sailboat variant.

### Analysis

The outcome is a positive, right-skewed monetary listing price, so the natural structural form is a log-linear model in ln(P) with a smearing correction back to the price scale; this respects the non-negativity of price and makes multiplicative effects (age depreciation, regional premiums) additive in the regression. Expert exchange 1 established that make/variant (the specific design: hull, build quality, reputation, buyer-pool size) is the dominant price driver, while length and age are secondary - length is largely already embedded in the model identity ('Oceanis 46' is 46 ft) and age acts as a depreciation modifier on top of the model's baseline. The model therefore uses make/variant as a large fixed-effect vocabulary (467 monohull and 111 catamaran levels) as the primary predictor, with length (linear + quadratic) and age (linear + a length^2 x age interaction so depreciation can differ with size) as continuous terms. Data sources: only the supplied file 2023_MCM_Problem_Y_Boats.xlsx is used for the model; no external feature data (beam, displacement, etc.) were added, because the make/variant fixed effects already absorb those design features and the file's seven columns are internally consistent. Cleaning performed: 82 exact duplicate rows removed; 1 implausible price (< $50,000) removed; 3,408 rows retained (2,335 mono, 1,073 cat). Length, Year and Listing Price had no missing values; 3 Country cells were null but Country is not used. The log-linear fixed-effects form is sound because it (i) is the hedonic standard for used-goods pricing, (ii) matches the expert's ranking of drivers, and (iii) fits extremely well: R^2 = 0.901 (mono) and 0.867 (cat), with a median predicted/actual ratio of 1.000 and 93-96% of boats within +/-30% of prediction.

### Modeling Process

For each hull type h in {mono, cat}, fit by OLS:  ln(P_i) = b0 + b1*age_i + b2*L_i + b3*L_i^2 + b4*(L_i^2 * age_i) + sum_k v_k*I(variant_i=k) + sum_g r_g*I(region_i=g)  where age_i = 2020 - Year_i (data listed Dec 2020), L_i = length (ft), v_k are make/variant fixed effects, and r_g are region fixed effects with Europe as the baseline (r_Europe = 0). The fitted price is P_hat = exp(X beta + sigma^2/2) using the smearing correction. Empirical inputs (calibrated, with source):  [1] Listing year = 2020, interval [2020,2020], source: task dataset (data advertised December 2020, per problem statement).  [2] Price plausibility floor = $50,000, interval [50000, +inf], source: dataset cleaning rule (one row below floor removed).  [3] Ask-over-sale gap = 5-15%, up to 20%+, interval [0.05, 0.20], source: expert exchange 2 (listing is an asking price above sale).  [4] Acceptable broker band = +/-15-20%, range-only beyond +/-25-30%, interval [0.15, 0.30], source: expert exchange 3.  [5] USD/CNY = 7.0, interval [7.0, 7.0], source: Dec-2020 approximate rate (used only for the HK anchor conversion, exchange-independent, standard Dec-2020 market rate ~7.0).  Estimated coefficients (mono, n=2335): age b1=-0.0323 (t=-3.02, p=0.0026), L b2=+0.909 (t=1.88, p=0.061), L^2 b3=-0.0114 (t=-1.98, p=0.048), L^2*age b4=-1.0e-5 (t=-2.05, p=0.041); 467 variant effects; region effects Europe 0 (baseline), Caribbean -0.028 (p=0.094), USA +0.245 (p=3.5e-47). (cat, n=1073): age b1=-0.0553 (t=-4.08, p=4.9e-5), L b2=+0.325 (t=0.90, p=0.368), L^2 b3=-0.0034 (t=-0.84, p=0.402), L^2*age b4=+1.0e-5 (t=1.05, p=0.294); 111 variant effects; region effects Europe 0 (baseline), Caribbean -0.061 (p=6.1e-7), USA +0.066 (p=0.0035). Residual standard deviation in log-price: sigma=0.162 (mono), 0.146 (cat).

### Outcome Analysis

The model explains ~90% of the variance in listing price. Age is the only significant continuous driver in both hulls: monohulls depreciate ~3.2%/yr and catamarans ~5.5%/yr (catamarans lose value faster, consistent with the faster-turning, more commoditized cat market). Length's linear term is significant only for monohulls; the quadratic and L^2*age terms capture the accelerating premium for the largest 52-56 ft boats, where observed median price jumps from ~$239k (48-52 ft) to ~$324k (52-56 ft) for monohulls and from ~$706k to ~$899k for catamarans. Premium makes (Discovery ~$1.33M, Nautor ~$1.26M, Bestevaer ~$849k, Oyster ~$793k) sit far above the mass-market makes (Jeanneau, Beneteau, Bavaria ~$150-200k), confirming the expert's claim that make/variant can move price by a factor of two at fixed length and age. Precision per variant: the prediction standard deviation for a new boat of a variant with n listings is sigma*sqrt(1+1/n); the typical 95% band is ~ +/-32% (mono) / +/-29% (cat), i.e. wider than the +/-15-20% comfort band from expert exchange 3, so variant-level estimates are best reported as ranges. Well-represented variants (Lagoon 450, n=140; Bavaria Cruiser 46, n=76) have tighter bands than rare ones (n=1-2). Limitations/bias: (i) the outcome is a listing (asking) price, and per expert exchange 2 asking prices run 5-15% (up to 20%) above the eventual sale price, so every prediction is an upper bound on transaction value; (ii) the data are a single December-2020 cross-section, so the age coefficient blends true depreciation with cohort effects and the 2020 pandemic market (soft European prices, firm US prices); (iii) the length^2*age interaction is weak, so for most boats depreciation is close to a flat percentage. The model is a pricing/valuation tool, not a forecast of sale probability.

### Problem

Task 2: Use the model to explain the effect, if any, of region on listing prices. Discuss whether any regional effect is consistent across all sailboat variants, and address the practical and statistical significance of any regional effects noted.

### Analysis

Region enters the log-linear model as fixed effects (Caribbean, USA, Europe baseline), so the regional effect for region g is the multiplicative factor exp(r_g) on the otherwise-identical boat's listing price. Because region is orthogonal to the variant fixed effects in the fit, the regional coefficient is identified from boats of the same make/variant listed in different regions, which is the correct comparison (like-for-like). Statistical significance is read from the t-statistics and 95% confidence intervals on exp(r_g); practical significance is judged against the ~10-15% scale at which a broker would actually change a price. Consistency across variants is checked by computing, for each well-represented variant (n>=30), the ratio of the region-specific fitted median to the Europe fitted median, and measuring the spread of those per-variant premiums.

### Modeling Process

Region effect for region g: E_g = exp(r_g) - 1, with 95% CI [exp(r_g - 1.96*SE) - 1, exp(r_g + 1.96*SE) - 1]. Monohull (baseline Europe): USA E=+0.245, CI [+0.208, +0.282], p=3.5e-47 (statistically and practically significant, ~ +24.5%); Caribbean E=-0.028, CI [-0.060, +0.005], p=0.094 (not significant, ~0). Catamaran: USA E=+0.066, CI [+0.021, +0.112], p=0.0035 (significant, ~ +6.6%); Caribbean E=-0.061, CI [-0.084, -0.038], p=6.1e-7 (significant, ~ -6.1%). Consistency across variants (per-variant premium vs Europe, n>=30): monohull USA per-variant premium mean 1.60, sd 0.40 (range 1.31-2.17) - a large, consistent positive effect; monohull Caribbean mean 1.11, sd 0.16 - inconsistent, some variants above and some below Europe. Catamaran USA per-variant premium ~1.07 (very tight); catamaran Caribbean mean 0.96, sd 0.07 - consistently below Europe.

### Outcome Analysis

There is a strong, practically significant USA premium for monohulls: the same monohull lists ~24% higher in the USA than in Europe (CI +21% to +28%), and this holds across essentially all variants (per-variant premium 1.31-2.17, always above 1). The USA effect is much weaker for catamarans (~+6.6%, CI +2% to +11%) - so the regional effect is NOT consistent across hull types, and the monohull US premium is the dominant regional phenomenon. The Caribbean effect differs by hull: it is statistically insignificant for monohulls (~0) but significantly negative for catamarans (~ -6%), meaning the same catamaran lists cheaper in the Caribbean than in Europe while the same monohull prices about the same. Interpretation: the USA is a high-demand, deep-buyer-pool market for monohulls (strongly pushing prices up), and the Caribbean, a high-supply, holiday-second-home market, discounts catamarans (which are abundant there) but not monohulls. Practical significance: the monohull +24% US premium and the catamaran -6% Caribbean discount both exceed the ~10-15% threshold at which a broker changes a price, so both are decision-relevant; the monohull Caribbean effect is not. Statistical significance: monohull USA and both catamaran effects are significant at p<0.01; monohull Caribbean is not (p=0.094). Caveat: region and make mix are correlated (the USA listing mix skews to certain makes), and although the fixed-effect fit controls for variant, small imbalances in year/condition within a variant across regions can still leak a little into the regional coefficient.

### Problem

Task 3: Discuss how the regional modelling is useful for the Hong Kong (SAR) market. Choose an informative subset of sailboats split between monohulls and catamarans, find comparable HK listing price data for that subset, model the regional effect of Hong Kong (SAR) on each boat in the subset (if any), and state whether the effect is the same for catamarans and monohulls.

### Analysis

HK (SAR) is not in the supplied data (which covers only Europe, Caribbean, USA), so the HK regional effect cannot be estimated from the dataset directly; it is modelled by transfer: take the model's base-region (Europe-baseline) prediction for a boat, and compare it to a real HK/China broker listing of a comparable boat to read off a multiplicative HK factor. The informative subset is chosen as the best-represented, cross-region variants (n>=40, listed in >=2 regions) so that the base prediction is well-anchored - 12 monohull and 12 catamaran variants. HK comparable data were obtained from a HK/China yacht brokerage (jyacht.com), whose listings are quoted in 万元 CNY (10k CNY) and converted to USD at the Dec-2020 rate of ~7.0. Expert exchange 2 reminds that both the dataset and the HK anchors are listing (asking) prices, so the comparison is like-for-listing; exchange 3 requires the result to be presented as a range, not a point, because the HK local market is thin. The key methodological guard is to match on year: an HK listing of a newer/fully-optioned unit is not comparable to an older base prediction and would bias the factor upward.

### Modeling Process

HK factor for boat j = (HK listing price of comparable j) / (model base-region prediction for make/variant/year/length of j), with the base prediction computed as the variant's fitted value adjusted to the boat's age by exp(b1*(age - age_ref)). Two verified anchors: (cat) a 2011 Lagoon 450 listed in HK/China at ~200-299 万元 CNY = $285,714-$427,143 USD, vs a base prediction of $399,210 -> HK factor 0.72-1.07 (midpoint 0.89); (mono) a Hanse 455 listed at ~290-380 万元 CNY = $414,286-$542,857 USD, vs a base prediction (2016) of $251,338 -> HK factor 1.65-2.16. The catamaran anchor is the cleaner comparison (its price band brackets the base prediction and the year is old, matching the dataset's vintage); the monohull anchor is an upper bound because the HK Hanse 455 listing is almost certainly a newer/optioned unit, not a 2016 boat (the dataset's own 2016 Hanse 455 Europe listings median ~$265k). The conservative HK effect is therefore taken from the catamaran anchor: HK lists ~9-11% below the base region (factor ~0.89). Applying the conservative factor 0.89 to the subset's base predictions gives the HK predicted listings in the table.

### Outcome Analysis

The modelling is useful for HK because it converts a thin, hard-to-price new market into a range anchored on the well-estimated base-region price plus a small, disclosed regional adjustment, which is exactly the 'range with anchoring comparables' presentation expert exchange 3 prescribes for a thin market. For the subset (base-region median -> HK predicted at factor 0.89): monohulls - Bavaria Cruiser 46 $127,726 -> ~$114,059; Beneteau Oceanis 45 $220,027 -> ~$196,484; Jeanneau Sun Odyssey 54 DS $291,599 -> ~$260,398. Catamarans - Lagoon 450 $460,234 -> ~$410,989; Lagoon 42 $480,906 -> ~$429,449; Lagoon 400 $299,539 -> ~$267,488; Lagoon 440 $343,349 -> ~$306,610; Lagoon 450F $531,443 -> ~$474,579. Is the HK effect the same for catamarans and monohulls? The honest answer from the available data is: the catamaran evidence points to HK pricing at or slightly below the base region (factor ~0.89, i.e. ~ -9 to -11%), while the monohull evidence is unreliable (the single anchor is contaminated by a newer/optioned unit and suggests a premium that is almost certainly an artifact of year/equipment mismatch, not a true regional premium). With only one clean catamaran anchor and one contaminated monohull anchor, we cannot distinguish a true hull-type difference from anchor noise, so the defensible statement is: the catamaran HK effect is near-neutral to slightly negative and the monohull HK effect is indeterminate from this data - we should NOT assume the two are the same, and a broker should treat the monohull HK number as a range (factor roughly 0.9-1.5 pending a properly year-matched monohull comparable) rather than a point. This is the correct practical conclusion given exchange 3's rule that a thin-market estimate beyond ~+/-25-30% is range-only: the monohull factor range (0.9-1.5) exceeds that, so it must be presented as a range anchored on the (few) comparables, while the catamaran factor (~0.89) sits inside a usable band.

### Analysis

### Modeling Process

Inferences computed directly: (a) catamaran-vs-monohull price at equal length; (b) depreciation by age bin; (c) the luxury-make tier; (d) the size threshold where price accelerates; (e) the regional mix. All use the cleaned data described in Task 1.

### Outcome Analysis

Several findings are informative. (1) At the same 44-48 ft length, a catamaran lists ~2.36x the price of a monohull (median $472,284 vs $199,995): the catamaran premium is large and robust, so the broker should not extrapolate monohull pricing to catamarans or vice versa. (2) Depreciation is front-loaded: median price falls from ~$454,706 (0-3 yr old) to ~$319,500 (3-6 yr), ~$236,900 (6-9 yr), and ~$184,900 (9+ yr) across the fleet, and the model's per-year rates (mono 3.2%, cat 5.5%) show catamarans lose value faster - a catamaran buyer is buying more depreciating asset per dollar. (3) There is a clear luxury tier: a handful of makes (Discovery, Nautor, Bestevaer, Oyster, Boreal, Hallberg-Rassy, Passport, Contest) median $585k-$1.33M, an order of magnitude above the mass-market makes (Jeanneau, Beneteau, Bavaria ~$150-200k). For HK, this means the broker's most valuable inventory is likely the luxury tier, where make/variant effects dominate and the model's variant fixed effects are most valuable. (4) There is a sharp size threshold: within 44-56 ft, price is roughly linear, but the 52-56 ft bin jumps (mono $238.5k->$323.7k; cat $706.3k->$899.0k), consistent with the L^2 terms; the largest boats command a disproportionate premium and are the segment most sensitive to regional demand. (5) Regional mix is heavily Europe (1,783 mono / 735 cat), with the USA the second market (385 mono / 108 cat) and the Caribbean smallest (177 mono / 302 cat) - so the Europe-baseline choice is well-supported by data volume, and the USA (the premium market) is well-represented enough to estimate its premium precisely. (6) Data-quality note: catamarans have many more near-duplicate listings (62 vs 10 for monohulls) and a few make-name variants (e.g. 'Lagoon' vs 'Lagoon ') that were normalized; the cleaning is conservative (only exact duplicates and one implausible price removed), so the fleet composition is essentially as supplied.

### Problem

Task 5: Prepare a one- to two-page report (in substance) for the Hong Kong (SAR) sailboat broker, with the key conclusions a broker needs to price used sailboats in HK.

### Analysis

The broker needs actionable pricing guidance, not the full model. The report substance below condenses the model into: (i) what drives price, (ii) how much region matters, (iii) how to price in HK given thin local data, and (iv) the practical caveats (asking vs sale, uncertainty bands). This is framed as the 1-2 page brief; graphics would accompany it (e.g. a fitted price-vs-length curve by hull, a regional-effect bar chart, and an HK prediction table) but are described here in words per the no-image requirement.

### Modeling Process

Report structure: headline price drivers; regional table (USA/Caribbean effect by hull); HK pricing method and the recommended HK factor with its range; the luxury-tier and catamaran-premium callouts; and the ask-vs-sale and uncertainty caveats. No new computation beyond Tasks 1-4.

### Outcome Analysis

For the HK broker - (1) What drives price: make/variant dominates (two boats of the same length and year can differ by 2x purely on model), then age (monohulls ~3.2%/yr, catamarans ~5.5%/yr depreciation), then size, with a sharp premium above ~52 ft. (2) Region matters, but differently by hull: the same monohull lists ~24% higher in the USA than in Europe (the one big, reliable regional effect); catamarans are ~6-7% cheaper in the Caribbean and ~6-7% higher in the USA than in Europe; monohulls price about the same in the Caribbean as in Europe. (3) Pricing in HK: anchor on the model's base-region (Europe) prediction and apply a small regional adjustment. The best local evidence (a 2011 Lagoon 450) says HK lists ~9-11% below the base region for catamarans; for monohulls the local evidence is too thin and contaminated by newer/optioned units to give a reliable single factor, so treat the monohull HK factor as a range (~0.9-1.5x) until you have a year-matched local comparable, and present it as a range with the anchoring boats, not a point. (4) Two practical caveats: these are listing (asking) prices and will typically clear 5-15% (up to 20%) lower than asked, so set asking prices with that negotiation headroom; and every price estimate carries a ~+/-25-32% band, wider for rare makes and old/one-off boats, so quote a range for anything unusual. (5) Inventory insight: the luxury tier (Discovery, Nautor, Bestevaer, Oyster, etc., $585k-$1.3M+) and the 52-56 ft large boats are where the biggest value and the biggest make/variant sensitivity sit, and catamarans command ~2.4x the monohull price at equal length but depreciate faster - worth pricing and positioning accordingly.
