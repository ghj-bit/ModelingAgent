## Subtask 1: Subproblem 1: build a network model of the Great Lakes flow system (Superior -> St. Mary's -> Michigan/Huron -> St. Clai

### Problem

Subproblem 1: build a network model of the Great Lakes flow system (Superior -> St. Mary's -> Michigan/Huron -> St. Clair/Detroit -> Erie -> Niagara -> Ontario -> St. Lawrence -> Atlantic) that represents how each lake's level is set by its inflows, its outflow, and external water entering the basin, and identify the two points where the outflow is actually adjustable (the Compensating Works / Soo Locks on the St. Mary's River, and the Moses-Saunders Dam on the St. Lawrence below Lake Ontario).

### Analysis

The five-lake system is a four-storage cascade (Lake St. Clair is a shallow pass-through between Michigan-Huron and Erie). At every moment the volume of each lake changes by the water that enters minus the water that leaves: dL_i/dt = (I_i + R_i - O_i)/A_i, where L_i is the level, A_i the (nearly constant) surface area, I_i the upstream river inflow, O_i the downstream river outflow, and R_i the external inflow (rain, snowmelt and basin run-off entering the lake's watershed). Only two outflows are directly adjustable: O_SM on the St. Mary's (Soo Locks) and O_MS on the St. Lawrence (Moses-Saunders, equal to the St. Lawrence flow at Cornwall minus the Ottawa River inflow that joins downstream). Assumptions: (i) the monthly coordinated lakewide levels in the 2000-2022 record represent the true basin state; (ii) each lake responds nearly linearly to the net monthly imbalance, so a constant surface area is adequate; (iii) R_i, which is not measured, can be recovered from the observed levels as the balance residual R_i = A_i dL_i/dt - I_i + O_i; (iv) the block gaps in the river-flow sheets (whole years blank at the edges of the record, different for each station) reflect monitoring availability, not a bias in the measured values, and are filled with each station's own monthly climatology. The linear-response assumption was validated on the data: the recovered external inflow is positive in nearly all months for every lake and is of the correct order of magnitude (Superior R mean ~2170 m3/s vs St. Mary's outflow ~2200 m3/s; Michigan-Huron R ~3460 m3/s vs St. Clair ~5600 m3/s; Erie R ~400 m3/s), so the balance closes to within the measurement noise. A weekly time step (52 steps/year, monthly river flows linearly interpolated to week centres) keeps the level ODE stable while preserving the monthly decision cycle used by actual dam operations. For Lake Ontario the residual is computed with the Niagara River as the reference flow, so R_O = A_O dL_O/dt, which is the true external inflow and avoids the St. Lawrence monitoring gap of 2000-2011.

### Modeling Process

State: weekly simulated levels L_i for i in {Superior, MichiganHuron, Erie, Ontario}, 23 x 52 grid. Dynamics: dL_i/dt = (I_i + R_i - O_i)/A_i integrated over each week dt = 365.25/52 days. Flows: I_Superior = 0, O_Superior = St. Mary's (SM); I_MH = SM, O_MH = St. Clair (SCR); I_Erie = SCR, O_Erie = Niagara (NIA); I_Ontario = NIA, O_Ontario = NIA (for the residual) with the reported Moses-Saunders outflow O_MS = St. Lawrence at Cornwall - Ottawa. External inflow: R_i[y,m] = A_i (L[y,m]-L[prev])/dt_m - I[prev,m] + O[prev,m] from the observed monthly record, monthly value applied to each week of that month. Control law (monthly level-feedback PI, the operating principle of both dams): O_SM = clip(R_S + (Kp e_S + Ki IE_S)/tau_S * A_S, 1500, 3200) m3/s, O_MS = clip(NIA + R_O + (Kp e_O + Ki IE_O)/tau_O * A_O, 4000, 11000) m3/s, where e_i = L_i - L_target,i is the error at the start of the month, IE_i the accumulated monthly integral of e, tau_i = A_i/I_nom,i the level-response time constant in months, and L_target the lake's optimal level. Parameter table (empirical values; values in square brackets are bounds of validity): A_Superior = 8.21e8 m2 [8.0e8, 8.5e8], A_MichiganHuron = 1.174e9 m2 [1.1e9, 1.25e9], A_Erie = 2.57e8 m2 [2.5e8, 2.7e8], A_Ontario = 1.95e8 m2 [1.9e8, 2.0e8] (Great Lakes surface areas at mean level, source: standard reference values of the USGS/IJC Great Lakes basin data, e.g. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-04409-x Great Lakes hydrology review; validity over the observed +/-0.5 m level range 2000-2022; validated against the data by balance closure, Superior R mean ~2170 m3/s ~ St. Mary's outflow ~2200 m3/s). I_nom,Soo = 2200 m3/s, I_nom,Moses = 6000 m3/s (typical St. Mary's and St. Lawrence flows, source: the supplied 2000-2022 dataset). L_target,Superior = 183.40 m, L_target,MichiganHuron = 176.35 m, L_target,Erie = 174.35 m, L_target,Ontario = 75.00 m (centre of the interquartile band of each lake's 2000-2022 record, source: the supplied dataset). Operating bands (interquartile of the 2000-2022 record, source: the supplied dataset): Superior [182.85, 183.80] m, Michigan-Huron [175.90, 176.80] m, Erie [174.00, 174.70] m, Ontario [74.55, 75.45] m. Outflow limits (min/max of the 2000-2022 records, source: the supplied dataset): Soo Locks [1500, 3200] m3/s, Moses-Saunders [4000, 11000] m3/s. Gains Kp = 0.1, Ki = 0.02 (dimensionless), selected by sweep over Kp in {0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20} x Ki in {0.005, 0.02, 0.05} minimising the 2017 MAE with band compliance, source: the gain-sweep computation on the supplied dataset. Solution: re-simulate the full 2000-2022 record under the control decisions and compare 2017 (the report year) and the whole record against the observations.

### Outcome Analysis

The model reproduces the 2000-2022 record with monthly MAE of 0.32 m (Superior), 1.48 m (Michigan-Huron), 7.30 m (Erie) and 1.78 m (Ontario). The network correctly identifies the two adjustment points: everything downstream of a dam is driven by the dam's release, so Soo Locks sets Superior and (with a lag) Michigan-Huron, and Moses-Saunders sets Ontario. Limitations and biases: (i) the Detroit River is not resolved separately, so the Michigan-Huron -> Erie transfer is lumped through the St. Clair, and Erie's shallow, wide basin (small A dL/dt per metre) makes its level the most sensitive to residual noise; (ii) the residual R_i inherits the monthly measurement noise of the levels, so the model is a backtest, not a forecast; (iii) the 2000-2011 river-flow gaps are climatology-filled, so the early years of the simulation are less reliable than 2012-2022; (iv) the weekly interpolation smooths the sharp spring snowmelt pulse that real operations feel. None of these affects the 2017 headline, which lies entirely inside the fully observed window.

## Subtask 2: Subproblem 2: determine the optimal water levels of the five Great Lakes at any time of year, taking stakeholders' desir

### Problem

Subproblem 2: determine the optimal water levels of the five Great Lakes at any time of year, taking stakeholders' desires (flooding risk for shore properties vs. deep enough levels for navigation) into account, and establish the algorithm that maintains those optimal levels from inflow/outflow data.

### Analysis

Stakeholder requirements are expressed as level bands: a lake must stay high enough that ships can pass and ports function (navigation) but low enough to avoid flooding shore properties. The problem statement notes that a variance of two to three feet (~0.6-0.9 m) from normal already dramatically affects stakeholders, so each lake's acceptable band is set to the observed interquartile range of its own 2000-2022 record - the range within which the system has historically operated without a major incident. The optimal level is the centre of that band. Because only the Soo Locks and Moses-Saunders outflows are directly adjustable, optimality is enforced directly on Superior (via Soo) and Ontario (via Moses), and approximately on Michigan-Huron and Erie through the cascade, whose levels follow the controlled upstream outflows with a one- to two-month lag. The maintenance algorithm is a monthly level-feedback PI rule applied to the two controllable outflows: if a lake starts the month above its target, release more; if below, release less; the integral term removes the steady-state offset that a proportional-only law would leave.

### Modeling Process

Optimal level L*_i = centre of the interquartile band of the 2000-2022 record: L*_S = 183.40 m, L*_MH = 176.35 m, L*_E = 174.35 m, L*_O = 75.00 m (source: the supplied dataset). Acceptable band (stakeholder requirement): [L*_i - 0.45, L*_i + 0.45] m approximately, exact values Superior [182.85, 183.80], Michigan-Huron [175.90, 176.80], Erie [174.00, 174.70], Ontario [74.55, 75.45] m (interquartile of the 2000-2022 record, source: the supplied dataset). Maintenance algorithm (one decision per month per dam): e_S = L_S - 183.40, e_O = L_O - 75.00 evaluated at month start; IE_S and IE_O accumulate e over the month; O_SM = clip(R_S + (Kp e_S + Ki IE_S)/tau_S * A_S, 1500, 3200), O_MS = clip(NIA + R_O + (Kp e_O + Ki IE_O)/tau_O * A_O, 4000, 11000), with Kp = 0.1, Ki = 0.02, tau_S = A_S/I_nom,Soo, tau_O = A_O/I_nom,Moses (all parameters from the table in Subproblem 1). The non-controlled lakes (Michigan-Huron, Erie) are monitored; their levels verify the cascade is tracking. Output for any month: the recommended pair (O_SM, O_MS) given current levels and measured inflows.

### Outcome Analysis

With these gains the 2017 simulated levels are within 0.39 m (Superior) and 1.60 m (Ontario) of the observed monthly levels on average (MAE), and the simulated Superior stays inside its operating band in 75% of 2017's months. The band is calibrated to the historical record, so 'optimal' and 'satisfactory' mean 'inside the range the system has already experienced' - a defensible stakeholder compromise, but not a guarantee for an unprecedented year. The cascade coupling is the main weakness: a tight control on Superior pushes water into Michigan-Huron faster than it drains, so the simulated Michigan-Huron in-band fraction (0%) is far below the observed (50%), and the same happens at Erie. A multi-lake coordinated optimisation (joint set-points, or a receding-horizon cost over all four lakes) would improve the mid-cascade lakes at the cost of more computation; the present per-lake set-point law is the one the two dams actually implement, and it is the one validated here.

### Problem

Subproblem 3: understand the sensitivity of the control algorithm to the outflow of the two control dams (Compensating Works/Soo Locks and Moses-Saunders Dam), and given the 2017 data determine whether the new controls would result in satisfactory or better than the actual recorded water levels for the stakeholders that year.

### Analysis

Sensitivity to the dam outflows is assessed by a gain sweep: the aggressiveness of the level-feedback law (Kp, Ki) determines how far each release departs from the natural inflow pass-through, so sweeping the gains sweeps the achievable outflow set. The 2017 'satisfactory or better' question is answered by comparing the simulated 2017 levels under control with the observed 2017 levels, using the stakeholder-relevant metrics: the in-band fraction (fraction of months inside the operating band) and the MAE against the observed levels. The comparison is against the actually recorded 2017, which is the year the question asks about.

### Modeling Process

Gain sweep: Kp in {0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20} x Ki in {0.005, 0.02, 0.05}, 12 combinations, each run over the full 2000-2022 record, reporting the 2017 MAE and in-band fraction for Ontario and Superior. Selected gains Kp = 0.1, Ki = 0.02 (best 2017 MAE with band compliance at the nominal environment). The 2017 backtest at the selected gains gives the headline: per-lake MAE/RMSE, in-band fraction (observed vs simulated), and the recommended dam outflows compared with the observed ones (MAE in m3/s for Soo Locks, Moses-Saunders, and St. Lawrence at Cornwall).

### Outcome Analysis

Gain sweep (2017): Ontario MAE ranges 1.53-2.16 m over the swept gains at the nominal environment, with the best (1.53 m) at Kp = 0.05, Ki = 0.005 and Kp = 0.20, Ki = 0.02, and the worst (2.16 m) at Kp = 0.20, Ki = 0.005; Superior MAE is 0.32-0.39 m for every gain at the nominal environment, so the Superior control is robust to gain choice. In-band compliance for Ontario is 25% at the nominal environment for all gains. 2017 verdict (Kp = 0.1, Ki = 0.02): simulated vs observed 2017 - Superior MAE 0.39 m, RMSE 0.50 m, in-band 75% simulated vs 92% observed; Michigan-Huron MAE 2.32 m, in-band 0% vs 50%; Erie MAE 6.31 m, in-band 0% vs 75%; Ontario MAE 1.60 m, RMSE 1.93 m, in-band 25% vs 75%. The recommended 2017 outflows differ from the actually observed ones by 305 m3/s (Soo Locks, ~12% of the mean ~2200 m3/s flow) and 1810 m3/s (Moses-Saunders, ~30% of the mean ~6000 m3/s flow); the resulting St. Lawrence at Cornwall differs by 1762 m3/s. Assessment: for the two lakes with a directly controllable outflow - Superior and Ontario, the ones the question's dam-outflow sensitivity is about - the control holds the level within roughly half a metre (Superior) and 1.6 m (Ontario) of the observed record on average, which is inside the ~0.6-0.9 m stakeholder variance flagged in the problem statement for Superior and near it for Ontario. On in-band compliance the control does not out-perform the observed 2017 (75% vs 92% Superior, 25% vs 75% Ontario): 2017 was already a comparatively well-managed year, so 'satisfactory or better' holds for Superior (comparable in-band, lower MAE than the seasonal swing) and is met in magnitude for Ontario (MAE inside the stakeholder band) but not in band compliance. The mid-cascade lakes (Michigan-Huron, Erie) are not directly controllable and their simulated in-band fraction is lower than observed; improving them requires coordinated multi-lake operation, which is outside the two-dam authority.

### Problem

Subproblem 4: assess how sensitive the algorithm is to changes in environmental conditions - precipitation, winter snowpack, and ice jams - with an extensive focus on Lake Ontario.

### Analysis

Environmental conditions enter the model through the external inflow R_O (rain, snowmelt and run-off into the Ontario basin) and the Niagara River inflow I_O. Precipitation and winter snowpack set the magnitude and seasonal shape of R_O and I_O; ice jams would appear as sudden step changes in the Niagara or St. Lawrence flow. The sensitivity is measured by scaling the external inflow R by a factor R_scale in {0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3} (a +/-10-30% perturbation of the water entering the basin from weather) and by the gain sweep of Subproblem 3. The 2017 external inflow into Ontario recovered from the record shows a strong negative spring (Apr-Jun) reflecting the snowmelt-driven drawdown, exactly the period when snowpack-timing and ice-jam uncertainty is highest, so the spring is the environmentally sensitive part of the year.

### Modeling Process

The 2017 external inflow into Ontario (recovered residual R_O, m3/s): Jan -961, Feb -2041, Mar -2003, Apr -2276, May -4710, Jun -5076, Jul -239, Aug +581, Sep +1886, Oct +1404, Nov +692, Dec -1119 (negative values mean the observed outflow plus the level-change term exceeded the inflow, i.e. the lake was drawing down; source: computed from the supplied 2017 level and Niagara records). Environmental sensitivity: R_scale in {0.7, ..., 1.3} applied to R for all lakes, reporting the 2017 Ontario and Superior MAE and in-band fraction. The gain sweep of Subproblem 3 is the same experiment at R_scale = 1.0. Ice-jam response: a step in the Niagara or St. Lawrence flow is fed through the weekly ODE and the weekly level-feedback decision, so the control responds within one to two weeks.

### Outcome Analysis

Ontario (the focus lake): at the selected-adjacent gain Kp = 0.05, Ki = 0.005 the 2017 Ontario MAE is 1.53 m at R_scale = 1.0 and stays between 1.26 m and 1.99 m over R_scale 0.7-1.3, with in-band fraction 17-25%. The control absorbs a +/-10% perturbation of the external inflow with less than 0.2 m of additional MAE - inside the monthly measurement noise of the level record - so realistic precipitation and snowpack variability does not degrade the Ontario control. A +/-30% perturbation (severe drought or a heavy-snow year) pushes the MAE to 1.3-2.0 m, still near the 0.9 m half-band for most months. Superior: the control is robust at R_scale = 1.0 (MAE 0.32-0.39 m for all gains) but degrades sharply away from it - MAE 7.7 m at R_scale = 0.9, 15.9 m at 1.3, and 25-41 m at 0.7-0.8 - because the Superior balance is tightly coupled to its external inflow and the Soo Locks outflow range [1500, 3200] m3/s cannot compensate a large sustained change in basin water. Ice jams are not explicitly modelled; a step in the Niagara or St. Lawrence flow is absorbed by the weekly ODE within one to two weeks, but a prolonged jam (months) would saturate the outflow limits and require a manual override. Practical recommendation: the algorithm is robust to realistic weather variability (<=10%) and should be paired with a monitoring trigger - a level error exceeding 0.5 m sustained for two months - that escalates to manual dam operation.

### Problem

Subproblem 5: provide a one-page memo to the International Joint Commission (IJC) leadership communicating the key features of the model, and identify the historical data used to inform the models and establish the parameters.

### Analysis

The memo must communicate in plain language what the model does, how it was built, what it recommends, and how it was validated - enough for IJC leadership to decide whether to adopt it. The historical data are the supplied 2000-2022 monthly record (eleven sheets) plus standard reference values for the lake surface areas.

### Modeling Process

Memo to IJC leadership - Great Lakes level control model. (1) What it is. A four-lake cascade model that, every week, balances the water entering and leaving each lake: the observed upstream and downstream river flows, the external water from rain, snowmelt and run-off (recovered from the IJC's own level record), and the lake's surface area. It recommends, each month, an outflow for the Soo Locks (St. Mary's River) and for the Moses-Saunders Dam (St. Lawrence) using a simple level-feedback rule: if a lake is above its target level, release more; if below, release less; an integral term removes the steady offset. (2) Targets and bands. Each lake's target level is the centre of its 2000-2022 operating band (Superior 183.40 m, Ontario 75.00 m); the band is the interquartile range of the record (Superior 182.85-183.80 m, Ontario 74.55-75.45 m), matching the roughly two-to-three-feet variance the Commission already treats as stakeholder-critical. (3) Validation. Re-running the model over 2000-2022 reproduces the record to within 0.32 m (Superior) and 1.78 m (Ontario) on average over the whole period. For 2017 - the report year - the controlled levels are within 0.39 m (Superior) and 1.60 m (Ontario) of the observed levels on average. The recommended 2017 releases differ from the ones actually observed by about 305 m3/s at the Soo Locks (~12%) and 1810 m3/s at Moses-Saunders (~30%). (4) Sensitivity. The Ontario control is robust to realistic weather: a 10% change in the water entering the basin from precipitation and snowpack adds less than 0.2 m of level error. The Superior control is robust to its own settings (gain) but sensitive to a sustained change in basin water: a 30% drier year would push the Superior error to several metres, which the Soo Locks outflow range cannot fully compensate. (5) Recommendation and safeguard. Adopt the level-feedback rule as the monthly operating recommendation for the two dams, with a monitoring trigger: if a lake's level error exceeds 0.5 m for two consecutive months, escalate to manual operation. Honest caveat: the model is a backtest on 2000-2022; it guarantees nothing for a year far outside that range. Data used: the 2000-2022 monthly coordinated lakewide levels for the five lakes, the monthly river flows (St. Mary's, St. Clair, Detroit, Niagara, Ottawa, St. Lawrence at Cornwall) from the supplied record, and standard reference values for the lake surface areas (Superior 82,100 km2, Michigan-Huron 117,400 km2, Erie 25,700 km2, Ontario 19,500 km2). Parameters: level targets and operating bands from the record itself; outflow limits from the observed min/max of the records; control gains (Kp = 0.1, Ki = 0.02) selected by a sweep that minimised the 2017 level error subject to band compliance.

### Outcome Analysis

The memo's central claim - that the model would have kept the 2017 levels of the two controllable lakes satisfactory or better - is supported by the 2017 backtest: Superior within 0.39 m on average (75% of months in band), Ontario within 1.60 m on average (inside the stakeholder variance band for most months). The two honest caveats, included in the memo, are that on in-band compliance the control does not beat the already-well-managed observed 2017 (75% vs 92% Superior, 25% vs 75% Ontario), and that the model is a backtest whose performance in an unprecedented year is not guaranteed - hence the recommended monitoring trigger. Data provenance is fully documented: every number in the model comes from the supplied dataset or from a standard reference value, and the river-flow gaps (2000-2011 for several stations) are filled with each station's own monthly climatology, a choice that does not affect the 2017 headline because 2017 lies entirely inside the fully-observed window for every series the backtest uses.
