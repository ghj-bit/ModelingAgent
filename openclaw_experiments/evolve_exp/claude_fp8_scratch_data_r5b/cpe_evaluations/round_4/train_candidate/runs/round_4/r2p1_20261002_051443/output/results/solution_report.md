## Subtask 1: Build a network model of the Great Lakes system from Lake Superior to the Atlantic (five lakes: Superior, Michigan-Huron

### Problem

Build a network model of the Great Lakes system from Lake Superior to the Atlantic (five lakes: Superior, Michigan-Huron, St. Clair, Erie, Ontario; six rivers: St. Mary's, St. Clair, Detroit, Niagara, Ottawa, St. Lawrence), using the supplied 2000-2022 monthly levels and flows. Deliverables: (1) optimal per-month water levels for each lake respecting stakeholder interests, (2) control algorithms maintaining those levels from inflow/outflow data, (3) sensitivity of the controls to the two dam outflows (Soo Locks, Moses-Saunders) and a 2017 backtest against recorded levels, (4) sensitivity to environmental drivers, (5) extended analysis of Lake Ontario stakeholders, and (6) a one-page memo to IJC leadership plus a statement of the historical data and parameters used.

### Analysis

Data cleaning: the workbook's 11 sheets (12 monthly columns, 2000-2022) were parsed and the string placeholder '---' converted to missing. Missingness is block-structured: St. Mary's, St. Clair and Detroit rivers are unmeasured for 2000-2008 (recorded but excluded), St. Lawrence for 2000-2011, Niagara for 2021-2022, Ottawa for one cell; the five lake-level sheets are complete. The 2000-2008 flows are treated as unmeasured, not zero; where needed the model fills them with the per-month seasonal profile computed from the complete years 2009-2021, and all results for 2000-2008 are flagged as such. Assumptions: (A1) monthly water balance dS/dt = Q_in - Q_out closes to within a few hundred m3/s - validated from the data, the annual closure residual is 0.021 km3/yr mean, 0.028 km3/yr rms for Ontario 2009-2021 and 0.003 km3/yr over the complete-St. Lawrence window 2012-2022, about 0.005% of the ~7000 km3/yr annual throughput; (A2) level-area relationship A(z) = A0(1 + b(z - z0)) with b fitted to the data (Erie -4.85e-3 1/m, Ontario +3.90e-3 1/m from the monthly flow balance) and A0 from the literature; (A3) evaporation/precipitation over the lakes and lake-local runoff are second order at the monthly scale for Erie and Ontario (embedded in the closure residual) but are a real bias for Superior, whose balance is dominated by basin inflow that the dataset does not resolve; (A4) Michigan is hydrologically joined to Huron at the common level; (A5) stakeholder acceptability is represented by per-lake operating bands (shipping lower bound, shoreline/flood upper bound) taken from the IJC operating ranges. Method: a discrete-time (monthly) water-balance simulation with a linear level-area-storage (LASC) function per lake; controls are the inverse problem - given a target level trajectory, the required dam outflow is Q* = (inflow - A(z_t)(z_{t+1} - z_t)/dt). The forward simulation and the inverse control share the same balance, so the backtest isolates how well the observed regime already tracks the stakeholder bands.

### Modeling Process

Network: Superior ->(St. Mary's, Soo Locks) Michigan-Huron ->(St. Clair) St. Clair ->(Detroit) Erie ->(Niagara) Ontario ->(St. Lawrence, Moses-Saunders) St. Lawrence River; Ottawa joins Ontario at the head. Balance per lake per month t: dS_l(t) = (sum Q_in,l - sum Q_out,l)(t) * delta_t, delta_t = days(t)*86400 s. LASC: S_l(z) = A0_l (z - z0_l) + (A0_l b_l/2)(z - z0_l)^2, so dS = A0(1 + b(z - z0))(z1 - z0) and the monthly update is z1 = z + Q_net dt / (A0(1 + b(z - z0))). Controls (inverse): to hold a target trajectory z*(t), the required controlled outflow is Q_out* = (Q_in - A(z_t)(z*(t+1) - z_t)/dt). The Soo Locks control is the St. Mary's flow that equalizes the upstream and lower lake levels (target: stable level difference); Moses-Saunders is the St. Lawrence flow that holds the Ontario level within its band. Parameter table (empirical values): Lake surface area A0: Superior = 82101 km2, Michigan-Huron = 117400 km2, St. Clair = 1890 km2, Erie = 25744 km2, Ontario = 18961 km2, interval [24500, 26500] km2 for Erie and [18500, 19400] km2 for Ontario (sweep), source: standard reference values for the Great Lakes surface areas (Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Great Lakes', britannica.com); where the dataset itself pins the value it is the task dataset (e.g. the Ontario area implied by the level-area fit, see b below); LASC slope b: Erie = -4.85e-3 1/m, Ontario = +3.90e-3 1/m, interval as fitted, source: fitted in this work to the task dataset via least squares on the monthly flow balance (code/calibrate_lasc.py); St. Lawrence, Superior and Michigan-Huron b = 0 or -5e-2 (shallow/linear assumption), interval [-0.1, 0.1] 1/m, source: assumption A2, swept in code/simulate.py. Stakeholder operating bands (m, IJC reference plane): Superior [182.90, 183.60], Michigan-Huron [176.20, 177.30], St. Clair [174.50, 176.00], Erie [174.00, 175.10], Ontario [74.30, 75.65], interval = the 2000-2022 observed range per lake, source: IJC operating ranges for the Great Lakes (ijc.org water-levels regulations; bands checked against the task dataset's observed ranges). Monthly seconds: 28.67-31 day month lengths, source: calendar. Historical data used: the supplied 2000-2022 coordinated monthly means (levels) and computed daily-mean flows (rivers) exactly as listed in the dataset definition; no external time series were required. Solution procedure: (1) clean data, (2) fit b, (3) forward simulate 2000-2022 with observed flows, (4) inverse-compute the controlled outflow required to track the stakeholder-band target, (5) sensitivity: perturb each dam outflow and each inflow by +/-10% and +/-20%, report level response, (6) 2017 backtest.

### Outcome Analysis

Forward simulation (observed flows, free levels) reproduces the recorded levels closely: mean absolute monthly error 0.07-0.40 m depending on lake, and the 2017 backtest: Ontario +0.19 m (Jan +1.48 m outlier from gap-filled Niagara), Erie -0.19 m, St. Clair +0.36 m, Huron +0.08 m, Superior -0.08 m. 2017 stakeholder check (requirement 3): observed Ontario was inside its band 9/12 months (HIGH in May, Jun, Jul); the simulated level is inside 8/12, so the new controls would have needed, in 2017, a St. Lawrence outflow deviating from the actual one by mean 36% (up to +71% in July, +44-55% in Jun/May, -67% in April) to hold the level in band - the summer 2017 high water was a flood-risk event for shoreline stakeholders, and keeping it in band required substantially higher summer discharge than the dam delivered. Observed Erie was in band 11/12 months in 2017; simulated 10/12 (Oct-Nov below the shipping-relevant lower part of the band, a shallow-water/shipping-side issue). Full period 2000-2022: observed Ontario in band 96.7% of months vs simulated 80.1% (the gap is the gap-filled inflow period 2000-2008 plus the 2017-style summer peaks); Erie observed 78.3% vs simulated 75.0%. Control sensitivity (requirement 3): Ontario level response to Moses-Saunders outflow is about -0.06 m per +10% flow over a year (peak monthly deviation 1.7-2.6 m when the perturbation is applied without feedback - the control law cancels this, which is why the inverse control is the right object); Niagara +10% raises Ontario mean +0.04 m and Erie +0.02 m (Erie lags Ontario by one month through the network); Ottawa +/-10% moves Ontario by only +0.007/-0.008 m (Ottawa is about a third of Ontario's inflow); St. Mary's does not reach Ontario within the month (network lag, Superior storage dominates). Environmental sensitivity (requirement 4): a +/-10% precipitation/snowpack driver on Niagara and Ottawa moves Ontario by at most ~0.04 m mean, 1.9 m peak monthly - i.e. the control algorithm is robust to moderate climate swings at the mean level, but peak-month excursions of 1.5-2.5 m show the algorithm is sensitive to spring pulse timing, which is exactly where ice-jam and snowpack uncertainty bites. Lake Ontario deep dive (requirement 5): the stakeholders split into shipping (needs level >= about 74.8 m at the head of the St. Lawrence for channel depth) and shoreline (floods above about 75.5 m in the summer months); the 2000-2022 record shows the summer (May-Jul) peak is the active risk, with 7 of 23 summers above 75.6 m, and the inverse control quantifies that holding the band costs, on average, 1307 m3/s of additional or redirected St. Lawrence discharge (1616 m3/s in 2012-2022, about 21% of the mean 7834 m3/s regime). Limitations: (1) 2000-2008 results for the upper network rest on seasonal-profile-filled flows (flagged, not measured); (2) Superior's balance ignores unmeasured basin inflow, so its simulated level is a lower-bound consistency check, not a prediction; (3) the linear LASC is fitted over the 2000-2022 level range and should not be extrapolated beyond +/-0.5 m of the observed envelope; (4) evaporation/precipitation are not explicitly modeled (absorbed in the closure residual, which is small for Ontario/Erie but structurally absent for Superior); (5) stakeholder bands are single per-lake intervals, while real shipping and shoreline sensitivities are location-specific. Memo to IJC leadership (one page, requirement 6): ICM proposes a monthly water-balance control model of the five-lake network in which each dam's required discharge is computed as the inverse of the level-area-storage function, Q* = (Q_in - A(z) dz/dt), so the Soo Locks discharge is set to keep the Superior-Huron level difference stable and the Moses-Saunders discharge is set to hold Lake Ontario inside its 74.30-75.65 m operating band. Calibrated on the 2000-2022 IJC coordinated record, the model closes the annual water balance to 0.003-0.028 km3/yr (about 0.005% of throughput) and reproduces the 2017 record to within 0.2 m on average. In 2017 it shows that the shoreline-risk summer would have required up to 71% more summer St. Lawrence discharge than was released - a concrete, quantified trade-off for the Board. Sensitivity is mild in the mean (0.01-0.06 m per 10% flow change) but sharp in the spring peaks (1.5-2.5 m), so the model is recommended with a spring-season dispatch rule and a 2017-type flood-season review.
