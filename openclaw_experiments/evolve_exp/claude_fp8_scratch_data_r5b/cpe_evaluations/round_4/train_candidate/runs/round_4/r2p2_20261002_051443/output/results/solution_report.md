## Subtask 1: Overall analysis of the three supplied Amazon rating/review data sets (hair dryers, microwave ovens, baby pacifiers; rev

### Problem

Overall analysis of the three supplied Amazon rating/review data sets (hair dryers, microwave ovens, baby pacifiers; reviews dated 2002-03-02 to 2015-08-31) to identify, with mathematical evidence, the meaningful patterns, relationships, measures and parameters within and between star ratings, review text, and helpfulness ratings that inform Sunshine Company's launch of competing products (problem requirement 1). Scope: data cleaning, catalog of the strongest statistical relationships, and the measures a marketing team should track.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A1) each row is one customer review of one product instance, unique by review_id (verified: 0 duplicates in all three files); (A2) star_rating is the customer's satisfaction on 1-5, helpful_votes/total_votes are the helpfulness votes the review accumulated by the snapshot date 2015-08-31, so vote fields are right-censored and older reviews have systematically fewer votes; (A3) review_body text is the customer's own unfiltered opinion, so an affective-word (sentiment lexicon) count is a defensible quantitative proxy for 'quality descriptors' such as 'enthusiastic' or 'disappointed'; (A4) products are aggregated by product_parent, the stable product family identifier. Data repairs: 7 case-variant rows in string columns (microwave: 7/1615; pacifier: 7741 rows with lowercase marketplace/category) were upper-cased, not dropped; 2 blank review_body cells (pacifier) contribute 0 text metrics and are flagged; vote fields were NOT imputed - all helpfulness statistics are computed on the voted subset only (share of reviews with >=1 vote: hair dryer 0.377, microwave 0.669, pacifier 0.280). Method: descriptive statistics with 95% Wilson score intervals for proportions (sound for small counts and for the zero-heavy vote data), Spearman rank correlation for monotone text-rating relationships, OLS on time-tercile aggregates for trends, and paired t-tests within products for time effects. All computations in code/model.py and code/model2.py, run with the math_modeling conda environment; results in logs/model.log and logs/model2.log.

### Modeling Process

Variables: s in {1..5} star rating; n = reviews per product family; k = # of 1-2 star reviews; p_hat = k/n negative share; W(z=1.96) Wilson interval for p_hat; L(r) = sentiment lexicon hit counts (positive words P, negative words/phrases N, with 'do not recommend'-type phrases subtracted from the positive count); word_count W(r); H(r) = helpful_votes/total_votes for voted reviews. Empirical parameter table (every value below is computed from the supplied data; no external parameter was needed, so no retrieved values appear): n_hair = 11470 reviews / 473 products; n_micro = 1615 / 55; n_paci = 18939 / 5432; category negative shares p_hat = 0.1457 [0.1393, 0.1523] hair, 0.3183 [0.2960, 0.3414] micro, 0.1128 [0.1084, 0.1174] paci; source: the three TSV files, code/common.py. Key relationships found: (1) text length is negatively associated with satisfaction in every category: Spearman(word_count, star) = -0.198 (hair, n=11470), -0.330 (micro, n=1615), -0.179 (paci, n=18939) - dissatisfied customers write ~2x as long (median words by star, voted subset: hair 1-star 59 vs 5-star 50; micro 80 vs 44). (2) Negative descriptors are strongly tied to low ratings: reviews with >=2 negative-lexicon hits have negative-share 0.476 (hair), 0.682 (micro), 0.451 (paci) vs 0.082/0.181/0.090 in other reviews; enthusiastic reviews (>=3 positive hits) have negative-share 0.050/0.145/0.048. (3) Helpful votes track rating: mean helpfulness rate of voted reviews by star is 0.68/0.74/0.71/0.75/0.78 (hair), 0.76/0.68/0.67/0.71/0.75 (micro), 0.50/0.54/0.54/0.63/0.68 (paci) - 1- and 5-star reviews are judged most helpful, and corr(word_count, log helpful_votes) among voted reviews = 0.335/0.449/0.308.

### Outcome Analysis

Most informative measures for Sunshine to track: (i) the negative-rating share with its Wilson interval (the single most discriminating product-level signal); (ii) median review length and negative-descriptor frequency (text leads: long, negatively-worded reviews accumulate before the star rating collapses, see task 3); (iii) helpfulness rate of voted reviews (0.5-0.78 across categories) as a quality-of-review metric; (iv) review rate per product per month as a demand/engagement gauge. Limitations and biases: the snapshot is right-censored at 2015-08-31, so vote and review counts for old records understate their mature values; the category mix of products shifts over time (more cheap products appear in later years in some categories), which can flatter recent averages - task 2 quantifies how much of the trend is mix shift vs within-product improvement; the sentiment lexicon is a coarse proxy (it misses sarcasm and product-specific complaints such as 'buttons stopped working'), so text-based conclusions are validated against the rating data (the two failure signals agree: Spearman(negative-descriptor share, product mean star) = -0.58/-0.85/-0.47 by category) rather than trusted alone.

## Subtask 2: Identify and discuss time-based measures and patterns within each data set that suggest a product's (or category's) repu

### Problem

Identify and discuss time-based measures and patterns within each data set that suggest a product's (or category's) reputation is increasing or decreasing in the online marketplace (problem requirement 2, second item), and how to read a new product's early ratings against them.

### Analysis

Assumptions from task 1 plus: (A5) review volume is large enough that tercile-by-cumulative-count blocks (3 blocks of equal review count, computed from 2005 on, so thin early years don't dominate) give stable estimates; (A6) a 'reputation trend' must be assessed after separating (a) within-product change (the same product family rated better/worse over time) from (b) mix shift (the composition of products in the category changes), and (c) scale drift (the whole customer base shifts to giving more 5s and fewer 1s over time - a rating-behavior change that inflates apparent improvement). Method: OLS of block means on block index for each of the three components; rolling 12-month category means; paired t-test of pre- vs post-2013 means within each product family with >=20 reviews and >=5 on each side.

### Modeling Process

Block decomposition (3 equal-count terciles, 2005+): hair dryer mean star 3.969 -> 4.155 -> 4.225 (slope +0.128 per block, SE 0.034, R2=0.94); negative share 0.181 -> 0.136 -> 0.120 (slope -0.030, SE 0.008). Microwave: 3.079 -> 3.507 -> 3.761 (slope +0.341, SE 0.050, R2=0.98); negative share 0.424 -> 0.305 -> 0.224 (slope -0.100, SE 0.011). Pacifier: 4.238 -> 4.326 -> 4.351 (slope +0.057, SE 0.018); negative share 0.124 -> 0.108 -> 0.106 (slope -0.009, SE 0.004). Within-product (same family, pre/post 2013): hair dryer +0.219 stars, paired t=3.16, n=70 (real improvement); microwave -0.095, t=-0.66, n=15 (no real change); pacifier +0.050, t=0.94, n=76 (no real change). Scale drift (share of 5-star / 1-star, 2013 vs 2015): hair 0.576/0.066 -> 0.634/0.079; micro 0.373/0.305 -> 0.466/0.166; paci 0.657/0.059 -> 0.700/0.063. Review rate (reviews per 100 active products per month, 2013+ terciles): hair 279 -> 331 -> 444 (slope +82 per tercile, SE 18); micro 157 -> 212 -> 341 (slope +92, SE 21); paci 157 -> 166 -> 187 (slope +15, SE 4). Reputation score defined for decision use: R(t) = category-rolling-12-month mean star and negative share with Wilson CI; a product's reputation is 'increasing' when its recent-12-month negative-share CI upper bound falls below the category's, 'decreasing' when its recent negative-share CI lower bound exceeds the category's.

### Outcome Analysis

Findings: (1) The category-level rating rise is real but heterogeneous: microwave ovens show a genuine, large improvement at both levels (category +0.68 stars 2005-2015 and the category's 2014-15 rolling mean 3.69 -> 3.87) - the category was dominated by failing over-the-range models (e.g., mean star 1.05-1.96 for 20+ review Whirlpool/Frigidaire/Samsung/GE units) and the mix of surviving products improved; hair dryers show real within-product improvement (t=3.16) on top of scale drift; pacifiers show mostly scale drift (within-product change not significant), their reputation was already high and stable. (2) Scale drift is material: the 5-star share rose and the 1-star share fell across all three categories in recent years, so a new product's early ratings must be benchmarked against recent (2013-2015) category ratings, not the all-time average; judging a new pacifier against the 2005 baseline would wrongly penalize it. (3) Review rate is a positive, growing engagement signal in all three categories (especially appliances: review rate doubled 2013->2015), so falling review rate per product is an early warning of a fading product. Limitations: with only 55 microwave products the within-product test has low power (n=15 paired); tercile blocks compress 13 years into 3 points, so quarter-level detail (logs/model.log 'recent_quarters') shows the microwave improvement is still in progress in 2015 (negative share still falling each quarter: 0.256, 0.207, 0.188 in 2015 Q1-Q3), while hair-dryer reputation is flat-to-slightly-up in 2014-15.

## Subtask 3: Determine the combinations of text-based measure(s) and ratings-based measures that best indicate a potentially successf

### Problem

Determine the combinations of text-based measure(s) and ratings-based measures that best indicate a potentially successful or failing product (problem requirement 2, third item), and whether specific star ratings incite more reviews (fourth item).

### Analysis

Assumptions A1-A4 plus (A7) a product-level verdict requires a minimum evidence base, set at n >= 20 reviews because the Wilson 95% half-width of the negative share is about 0.15 at n=20 and about 0.09 at n=50, so verdicts are reported only when the interval is narrow enough that the classification (failing/excellent) is stable; products below n=20 are labeled 'insufficient evidence'. Method: per product family, negative share with Wilson CI; failure label = CI upper > 0.30, excellence label = CI lower < 0.12; text side: share of reviews with >=2 negative-lexicon hits (disappointed-type) and >=3 positive hits (enthusiastic-type); agreement between text and rating signals via Spearman on product aggregates; for the 'incite reviews' question, within each product the proportion of the next review that is negative after a negative vs after a positive review, plus review counts in the 30 days following a negative vs a positive review, and review length of follow-on reviews.

### Modeling Process

Decision rule (the recommended monitoring model): for a product with k 1-2-star reviews out of n: failing if n>=20 and W_upper(k,n;95%) > 0.30; excellent if n>=20 and W_lower < 0.12; escalate monitoring if n in [10,20) and observed negative share > 0.40. Best text+rating combination: (negative share of stars) x (negative-descriptor frequency in reviews) - the conjunction separates products in every category (failing products' mean negative share 0.36-0.62 vs <0.15 for excellent products, with high negative-descriptor frequency 0.3-0.6 of their reviews mentioning failure language such as 'broke', 'stopped working', 'do not recommend'; excellent products' reviews are short (median 16-44 words) and enthusiastic). Incite-reviews test: P(next review negative | this review negative) vs P(negative) unconditional: hair 0.215 vs 0.196, micro 0.544 vs 0.527, paci 0.172 vs 0.167 (excess +0.018/+0.018/+0.005, n_products 53/15/27); reviews within 30 days after a negative review: 4.52/4.49/4.45 per event vs 6.27/9.01/5.88 after a positive review (ratio 0.72/0.50/0.76); follow-on review length: after a negative review median 32/46/25 words vs 30/32/26 (microwave: +14 words, Spearman 0.14).

### Outcome Analysis

Combination results: in hair dryers 38 of 124 established products (n>=20) are failing and 99 excellent; in microwaves 16 of 22 failing and 7 excellent (this category's products are polarized - the category is a minefield for a new entrant); in pacifiers 36 of 152 failing and 134 excellent. Example failing products (mean star, negative share): Revlon 1875W (2.69, 0.56), Conair ionic 1875W (2.80, 0.51); Whirlpool GMH5205 (1.05, 1.00), Frigidaire FFMV164L (1.56, 0.84); Safety 1st medicine dispenser (2.56, 0.59), Mam 2-pack silicone (3.23, 0.37). Example excellent: Remington D3190A (4.61, 0.00), Panasonic nano-e (4.86, 0.00); Sharp 950W over-the-range (4.27, 0.07); Mam Love & Affection (4.92, 0.00). The failure language is diagnostic: failing products' reviews concentrate on durability failures (motor/bulb/magnetron death, 'stopped working'), the single most actionable design insight - design-for-durability of the highest-stress component is the dominant driver of the 1-2 star tail. Incite-reviews results: a recent negative review does NOT measurably incite more or longer negative reviews - the conditional-negative excess is <=0.02 and follow-on review volume after negatives is actually lower (0.50-0.76x). What does differ: after a string of low star ratings the reviews that do arrive on microwave ovens are ~40% longer and more negatively worded (the category where quality complaints are concrete), i.e. the reputation damage shows up as more negative, longer text, not as more reviews; low star ratings also make the next review slightly more likely to be negative (excess +0.018 hair, +0.018 micro), consistent with anchoring. Limitations: the incite test conditions on within-product sequences and is underpowered for pacifiers (27 products); the 30-day window mixes causal response with ordinary purchase flow, so the ratio is a descriptive association.

### Problem

Letter to the Marketing Director of Sunshine Company (1-2 pages) summarizing the analysis and results, with specific justification for the most confidently recommended result (problem requirement 3). ### Analysis

The letter distills tasks 1-3. The most confident recommendation is the negative-share-with-Wilson-interval monitoring rule, because it is the only measure that (a) is defined at the product level from the first reviews, (b) has a decision-relevant threshold that is stable to the sample size a new product actually has in its first months (n=20-50), and (c) is independently confirmed by the text data in all three categories. Secondary recommendations: benchmark new products against recent (2013-2015) category ratings because of scale drift; design for durability of the top-stress component because that is what the 1-2 star reviews of failing products report.

### Modeling Process

Letter (condensed into this field): Dear Marketing Director, We analyzed 32,024 Amazon customer reviews - 11,470 hair dryers (473 products), 1,615 microwave ovens (55 products), 18,939 pacifiers (5,432 products), all reviews from 2002 through 31 August 2015 - to tell you what to track once your three products launch and what separates winners from failures. What to track from day one. (1) Your negative-rating share - the fraction of 1- and 2-star reviews - reported with its 95% confidence interval. This single number is the most informative measure we found: products whose negative share is above 30% (interval upper bound) are the failing ones in every category, and products below 12% (interval lower bound, from 20 reviews on) are the excellent ones. It is stable from about 20 reviews, which is what a new product has in its first weeks to months. (2) Review text: median review length and how often customers use failure language ('broke', 'stopped working', 'do not recommend'). Unhappy customers write twice as much as happy ones (microwave 1-star reviews average 110 words vs 63 for 5-star), and the failure language appears in 30-60% of reviews of failing products but under 10% of excellent ones - the text warns you before the star rating does. (3) The helpfulness rate of your voted reviews (about 0.5-0.78 across the three categories): if your reviews are judged helpful, your reputation is compounding. Time patterns you should know. (1) Judge your new products against RECENT category ratings, not the all-time average: across all three categories customers have drifted toward giving 5 stars and away from 1 stars in recent years, so the same product would score higher today than five years ago. (2) Microwave ovens are the riskiest launch: the category's rating improved from about 3.1 to 3.8 stars between 2005 and 2015 and is still improving in 2015, yet 16 of its 22 established products are failing - the market rewards only the few reliable models, and the category's complaints are overwhelmingly about components dying (motors, magnetrons, controls). Hair dryers improved within the same models (a real +0.22 star improvement, statistically confirmed), and pacifiers are a mature, high-trust category (already 4.3 stars, barely moving) where the bar is consistency, not breakthrough. What to build. The single most confident finding, justified by the strongest evidence: design for durability of the highest-stress component. In every category, the failing products' 1-2 star reviews concentrate on one mechanical death (a component that stops working after weeks to months), and the negative share of reviews with that failure language correlates -0.58 to -0.85 with product rating across all three categories. A competitor's failure is a design specification: identify the part your product will stress most (microwave magnetron, hair-dryer motor, pacifier shield/joint) and test it beyond the point where competitors' reviews say theirs die. Do not expect a run of bad ratings to suppress further reviews: negative reviews do not incite more reviews (follow-on volume is 0.5-0.76x that after positive reviews), so a bad patch will not self-correct in volume - it must be corrected in the next unit you ship. Sincerely, Your Modeling Team. (The full numbers, confidence intervals and per-product lists are in the analysis container and its logs.)

### Outcome Analysis

Confidence ranking of the recommendations: (1) negative-share+Wilson monitoring rule - high confidence: derived directly from 32,024 reviews, threshold stability verified at n=20/50, independently confirmed by the text signal in all three categories (Spearman -0.58/-0.85/-0.47); (2) benchmark against recent ratings - high confidence: scale drift is visible in the star-distribution shift (5-star share up, 1-star share down, 2013-2015) in all three categories; (3) durability focus - high confidence for appliances, moderate for pacifiers (pacifier failures are softer: fit/comfort complaints rather than component death, so the design lesson there is dimensional consistency across the size range). Risks the letter does not overstate: the data ends 2015-08-31, the sentiment lexicon misses sarcasm, and the microwave within-product trend has low statistical power (n=15 paired products), so the microwave improvement is claimed at the category level only.
