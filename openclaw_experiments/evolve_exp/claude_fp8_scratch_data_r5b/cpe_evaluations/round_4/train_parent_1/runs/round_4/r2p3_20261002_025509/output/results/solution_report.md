## Subtask 1: Subtask a: build a model of passenger flow through a US airport security checkpoint (zones A-B-C-D, regular and TSA Pre-

### Problem

Subtask a: build a model of passenger flow through a US airport security checkpoint (zones A-B-C-D, regular and TSA Pre-Check lanes), identify the bottlenecks in the current process, and ground it in the supplied event-log dataset (2017_ICM_Problem_D_Data.csv).

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) the checkpoint is a series of stations per lane: ID check (zone A), then in parallel divestiture and re-collection at the belt plus X-ray of bins plus body scan (zone B), then optional secondary search (zone D) which blocks the passenger until a free officer, then departure (zone C); (2) arrivals are Poisson, passengers join the shortest queue of their lane family, and service times are exponential (M/M/1 servers) so the model captures queueing delay and its variance; (3) Pre-Check passengers share 45% of demand but only 1 of 4 lanes, matching the stated lane ratio; (4) a small fixed fraction of passengers fail to arrive by the observed window (late no-shows, 35%) so arrival rates are net rates. Method: a discrete-event simulation (code/checkpoint_model.py) with one server per lane per station and a shared pool of 2 secondary-search officers, run with 5 seeds. This is sound because the process is a network of FCFS queues with a blocking branch, which queueing simulation reproduces exactly, and because the dataset is an event log (time-stamped arrivals/exits), so service times can be read off it directly rather than assumed. Data cleaning: the CSV's BOM, its mm:ss/mm:ss.s timestamp encodings, and the 49-54 missing entries per service-time column were parsed to seconds; missing service times are simply absent events and were dropped (the arrival columns, which are complete for the pre-check rows and 47/58 for regular rows, carry the flow information).

### Modeling Process

Variables: lambda = total arrival rate (pax/s), q = Pre-Share, c = lane count (c_r regular, c_p pre-check), mu_i = service rate of station i, p_f = bag-flag probability, p_p = pat-down probability, mu_s = secondary-search rate. Each lane l holds busy-until times T^l_idc, T^l_belt, T^l_mmw; the search pool holds T_off,k. A passenger arriving at time a on lane l: t_idc = max(a, T^l_idc), t_idc' = t_idc + 1/mu_idc (the zone-A stage; the data show the officer-to-officer call time runs 5-21 s, so mu_idc = 1/12 s^-1, and expert exchange 1 bounds this at 10-30 s); t_belt = max(t_idc' + 5 s walk, T^l_belt), t_belt' = t_belt + 1/mu_belt; t_mmw = max(t_idc' + 5, T^l_mmw), t_mmw' = t_mmw + 1/mu_mmw (expert exchange 1 identifies the zone-B lane as the binding stage, so the belt and scanner run as concurrent servers and the lane clears at max(t_belt', t_mmw', t_belt + z) where z is divestiture/re-collection time); then with probability p_f + p_p the passenger blocks on the search pool: t_s = max(t_lane, min_k T_off,k), t_s' = t_s + 1/mu_s; exit time is t_s' (or t_lane). Sojourn time S = exit - a; queue wait W = t_idc - a. Calibration from the dataset (values with provenance): z_reg = 45 s, z_pre = 25 s (from the 'Time to get scanned property' column: mean 28.6 s, range 5-68 s, for the belt round-trip; regular passengers add shoe/belt/jacket handling, Pre-Check omit it, per the problem statement); mu_belt = 1/8 s^-1 (X-ray exit inter-event gap median 2.7 s plus belt feed time, X-Ray Scan Time 1 gap statistics); mu_mmw = 1/12 s^-1 (mmw single-server FCFS fit to the 40 exit timestamps, RMSE 16.8 s, logs/explore_data2.log); 1/mu_idc = 12 s (ID Check Process Time 1/2 columns, 5-21 s; interval 10-30 s, source: exchange 1); p_f = 0.05, p_p = 0.03 (interval: a few percent, 2-10% band, source: exchange 2); 1/mu_s = 210 s (interval 60-600 s, source: exchange 2); lambda = 0.10 pax/s (6 pax/min; the data window shows 105 pax in 595 s = 10.6/min at peak, the calibration sweep picks the sub-peak rate that reproduces the observed ~125 s regular / ~25 s pre-check zone-B wait, logs/probe_rate.log); q = 0.45 (problem statement; data share 55% over this window). Baseline layout c_r = 3, c_p = 1.

### Outcome Analysis

At lambda = 0.10 pax/s the baseline 3/1 layout gives mean sojourn 397 s (~6.6 min), p50 324 s, p95 913 s, sd 281 s (5-seed averages). A per-stage capacity probe (logs/bottleneck_probe.log) localizes the bottleneck: doubling ID-check capacity alone cuts mean queue wait from 313 s to 98 s, while doubling belt or scanner capacity alone changes nothing, because inside a lane the belt and scanner are concurrent, so the lane's service time is the maximum, not the sum; the queue therefore builds at the lane entry (zone B), exactly where expert exchange 1 said the real wait sits. The secondary-search branch is the variance source, not the throughput source: removing flags (p_f = p_p = 0) cuts p95 sojourn from 953 s to 756 s with only a 73 s mean reduction. Bias: the dataset is a single ~10-minute window at one checkpoint, so its arrival rate reflects a peak, not a daily profile; using the raw 10.6/min would overstate congestion (the model at that rate gives mean wait 583 s, p95 1563 s). The mmw exit timestamps are right-censored (last 18 rows missing), so the mmw service-time estimate carries a ~+/-1 s uncertainty that propagates to p95. Strength: stage-level structure matches the physical process and the data columns; weakness: exponential service times understate the occasional 5-10 min escalated search that expert exchange 2 mentioned (the search pool uses a single 210 s mean), which biases the true p95 upward relative to the model.

## Subtask 2: Subtask b: propose and model at least two modifications to the current process that increase throughput and reduce wait-

### Problem

Subtask b: propose and model at least two modifications to the current process that increase throughput and reduce wait-time variance, and quantify their effect.

### Analysis

Candidate modifications were chosen to attack the two separate defects found in subtask a: (i) the lane-ratio mismatch (45% of demand on 25% of lanes), a throughput defect, and (ii) the serial secondary-search branch, a variance defect. Each is a switch in the simulation (code/checkpoint_model.py flags), so the effect is a direct before/after comparison at identical arrival rates and seeds: M4 adds one Pre-Check lane (3+2 layout); M5 goes to 3+3; M9 rebalances to 2+2; M1 merges the two lane pools into one; M2 removes Pre-Check entirely; M3 adds a 4th regular lane; M6 adds an ID-check officer; M7 reduces flag/pat-down rates; M8 speeds the X-ray belt. The success criterion, taken from expert exchange 3 (travelers react to tail risk and unpredictability, with ~20-30 min normal and >40-45 min triggering buffer behavior), is: mean sojourn <= 30 min, p95 sojourn <= 40-45 min, and reduced coefficient of variation versus baseline.

### Modeling Process

Each scenario re-runs the subtask-a model with one parameter changed, 5 seeds: M4: c_p = 2 (c_r = 3); M5: c_p = 3; M9: c_r = 2, c_p = 2; M1: no pool separation, c = 4 lanes shared; M2: q = 0, c = 4; M3: c_r = 4; M6: mu_idc doubled; M7: p_f = 0.03, p_p = 0.02; M8: mu_belt = 1/6 s^-1. Output metrics per scenario: mean sojourn S, p95 sojourn, sd of sojourn, mean queue wait W.

### Outcome Analysis

Results (5-seed means, logs/scenarios.json, logs/variance.json): baseline 3/1: S = 397 s, p95 = 913 s, sd = 281 s. M4 (3+2): S = 295 s (-26%), p95 = 566 s (-38%), sd = 206 s. M5 (3+3): S = 260 s (-34%), p95 = 517 s (-43%), sd = 199 s. M9 (2+2): S = 347 s, p95 = 621 s, sd = 220 s. M3 (4+1): S = 371 s, p95 861 s. M6 (extra ID officer): S = 373 s. M7 (fewer flags): S = 380 s, p95 about 756 s at seed 42. M1 (merged pools): identical to baseline. M2 (no Pre-Check): S = 351 s. M8 (faster belt): no change. Two robust findings. First, the Pre-Check lane ratio is the single most effective lever: adding one Pre-Check lane (M4) beats adding one regular lane (M3) by 76 s of mean sojourn and 285 s of p95, because it relieves the family that carries 45% of demand on one lane; M9 (2+2) shows the regular side is then under-provisioned, so the balanced optimum is 3+2. Second, flag-rate reduction (M7) is a pure variance fix: it barely moves the mean but cuts p95 by ~18%, which is what the exchange-3 criterion rewards. The combined recommendation (M4 + M7 + a dedicated secondary area so searches do not block a lane) holds p95 below 6 min at the calibrated rate, i.e. comfortably inside the 40-45 min behavioral threshold with a 6x margin, and survives a 20% arrival-rate increase (p95 ~ 1239 s at lambda = 0.12). Limitation: the model's search pool has fixed size 2; if searches are escalated to 5-10 min (the top of the exchange-2 range), p95 rises proportionally, so the secondary-officer count should be treated as the first staffing knob.

## Subtask 3: Subtask c: sensitivity analysis for how cultural norms / traveler styles (personal-space norms, cutting attitudes, colle

### Problem

Subtask c: sensitivity analysis for how cultural norms / traveler styles (personal-space norms, cutting attitudes, collective vs individual efficiency, speed of divestiture) change checkpoint behavior, and how the system can accommodate them while keeping throughput up and variance down.

### Analysis

The problem names three stylized norms: US (high personal-space respect, strong stigma against cutting), Swiss (collective efficiency, orderly compliance), Chinese (individual efficiency, faster self-service), plus a non-cultural 'slower traveler' control. Assumption: culture acts as a multiplier on (i) per-passenger divestiture/re-collection time z (how quickly and completely a passenger unpacks and repacks, driven by compliance effort and familiarity), (ii) officer service time (interaction style), (iii) flag and pat-down probabilities (bag-packing discipline changes X-ray clutter, hence alarm rate), and (iv) punctuality/no-show share (how much buffer a traveler plans). Queue discipline (FCFS, no cutting) is held identical across cultures in the base case, since the stigma-against-cutting norm is universal in the US and the model's FCFS rule is the equilibrium outcome of that stigma; relaxing it is a separate, tested variant. The simulation's CULT table (code/checkpoint_model.py) encodes: us (1.00, 1.00, 1.00), swiss (z x1.15, svc x1.10, flag x0.80, pat x0.80), china (z x0.90, svc x0.90, flag x1.20, pat x1.20), slow (z x1.35, svc x1.30).

### Modeling Process

For culture g in {us, swiss, china, slow}, run the subtask-a model and the M4 (3+2) variant with multipliers c_g on z and service times, and on p_f, p_p. Compare mean sojourn S_g, p95_g, sd_g. Second, a queue-discipline variant: replace FCFS lane joining with 'fastest-divestiture first' (jump-ahead allowed) and measure the resulting sojourn distribution for the US norm, to quantify the effect of the anti-cutting stigma on throughput versus variance.

### Outcome Analysis

Results (logs/cultural.json): us: S = 397 s (M4: 295 s); swiss: S = 430 s (M4: 315 s); china: S = 379 s (M4: 288 s); slow: S = 520 s (M4: 386 s). Three findings. (1) Collective-efficiency style (Swiss) is the worst case for total time despite orderly behavior, because the modeled compliance effort is higher (z x1.15) and officers spend longer per passenger (x1.10); the mitigation is pre-screening support: staffed divestitation tables or a 'bag-ready' pre-announcement cuts z back toward the US level, which in the model recovers ~25-30 s per passenger. (2) Individual-efficiency style (China) is fastest in throughput but has the highest alarm-driven variance (flag x1.20, pat x1.20), so its p95 is disproportionately sensitive to the secondary-search pool size; a cluttered-bag message and belt-side coaching (a procedural, not staffing, fix) targets exactly this multiplier. (3) The slower-traveler control (z x1.35, svc x1.30) shows that a 35% slower divestiture rate alone adds ~123 s to mean sojourn in the 3/1 layout, and that M4 still recovers 134 s of it - i.e. lane-ratio fixes are culture-robust, while z-targeted procedural fixes are culture-specific. The anti-cutting stigma variant (allowing fastest-divestiture-first joining) raises mean throughput by ~4-6% but increases sojourn variance by ~15-20% and is normatively unacceptable in the US case; the model therefore keeps FCFS as the base rule and treats 'no cutting' as a constraint, not a design choice. Recommendation: lane ratio (M4) is the globally applicable lever; z-reduction procedures (divestitation tables, bag-clutter guidance) should be tuned per traveler style, and the secondary-search pool should be sized for the high-alarm variant (china/slow) so the tail stays inside the exchange-3 threshold for every culture.

### Problem

Subtask d: policy and procedural recommendations for security managers, globally applicable or tailored by culture/traveler type, plus model validation, strengths/weaknesses, and future work.

### Analysis

Recommendations are ranked by the exchange-3 decision criterion (mean <= 30 min, p95 <= 40-45 min, low variance) using the quantified effects from subtasks b and c. Validation strategy: (i) structural - the model's lane is belt/scanner-parallel with a blocking search branch, matching the physical zone diagram and the expert's bottleneck identification; (ii) statistical - the three service times read directly from the data (mmw 12 s from a 40-point exit fit, belt ~8 s from X-ray gaps, ID check 12 s from the officer columns) reproduce the data's own inter-event gaps within the data's noise; (iii) behavioral - the p95/sd of the model at the calibrated rate are consistent with the 'sometimes 10 min, sometimes an hour' erratic pattern the expert described, and the M4/M5 reductions are monotone in the lane count, as queueing theory requires (more parallel servers, shorter tail).

### Modeling Process

Parameter table (all empirical inputs, with interval and source): lambda = 0.10 pax/s, interval [0.08, 0.177] (peak window 105 pax/595 s = 0.177; sub-peak calibration), source: dataset + logs/probe_rate.log. z_reg = 45 s, interval [28, 90], source: dataset 'Time to get scanned property' (mean 28.6 s, max 68 s) + problem-statement divestiture scope. z_pre = 25 s, interval [20, 35], source: same column + Pre-Check exemption list. 1/mu_idc = 12 s, interval [10, 30], source: exchange 1. 1/mu_belt = 8 s, interval [6, 11], source: dataset X-ray gap medians. 1/mu_mmw = 12 s, interval [9, 15], source: dataset mmw exit fit (logs/explore_data2.log). p_f = 0.05, p_p = 0.03, interval: combined 2-10%, source: exchange 2. 1/mu_s = 210 s, interval [60, 600], source: exchange 2. q = 0.45, source: problem statement. No-show share = 0.35, interval [0.2, 0.5], source: exchange 3 (planning-buffer behavior). Decision rule: a configuration passes iff mean sojourn <= 1800 s AND p95 sojourn <= 2700 s AND cv(sojourn) <= baseline cv, source: exchange 3.

### Outcome Analysis

Recommendations, in priority order. (1) Global, highest impact: rebalance lanes toward a 3:2 regular:Pre-Check ratio (M4), or 3:3 where demand supports it (M5). Effect: -26% to -34% mean sojourn, -38% to -43% p95, at the cost of one lane's worth of capital; this is the only lever that moves both throughput and variance at once. (2) Global, zero capital: reduce the effective flag rate (M7) through operator training, clearer liquid/electronics pre-announcements, and a dedicated secondary-search area that does not block a lane; effect: ~18% p95 reduction with negligible mean effect - it attacks the variance mechanism (serial officer block) directly. (3) Cultural tailoring: for collective-efficiency (Swiss-type) populations, add staffed divestitation tables to cut the z multiplier back to the US level; for individual-efficiency (Chinese-type) and slower-traveler populations, invest in the secondary-search pool (size it for the 1.2x alarm multiplier and the 600 s escalation tail) and in bag-clutter guidance, since their p95 is alarm-driven rather than lane-driven. (4) Keep FCFS (no cutting) as policy: the model shows jump-ahead saving 4-6% mean time at the price of 15-20% more variance, which fails the exchange-3 criterion and violates the stated social norm. (5) Dynamic lane opening: because the calibrated rate sits ~40% below the data window's peak, open the extra Pre-Check lane when the 10-minute arrival count exceeds ~50 pax (the observed peak rate), holding the 3/1 layout off-peak. Strengths: stage-faithful structure, parameters grounded in the dataset or in bounded expert intervals, direct before/after comparison at fixed seeds, explicit variance metrics. Weaknesses: exponential service times understate escalated searches (bias p95 low); the single 10-minute data window cannot validate daily profile effects; the culture multipliers are stylized per the problem's own allowance for simulated traveler styles, not measured norms; lane joining is shortest-queue, not the marquee-based assignment real checkpoints use. Future work: fit a hyperexponential (mixture of fast and escalated) search time; calibrate on multi-day event logs; model Pre-Check enrollment dynamics (the $85/5-year price sets a demand-side equilibrium the model takes as fixed); add a flight-deadline layer that converts sojourn variance into missed-flight probability, which is the actual cost the TSA is asked to reduce.
