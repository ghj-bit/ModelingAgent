## Subtask 1: Task 1: Clean the supplied IPEDS/College Scorecard data and build a defensible, reproducible candidate population of sch

### Problem

Task 1: Clean the supplied IPEDS/College Scorecard data and build a defensible, reproducible candidate population of schools eligible for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100,000,000-per-year, five-year (2016-2020) investment program. Scope: the 2,977 candidate institutions in 'IPEDS UID for Potential Candidate Schools.xlsx' joined to the 7,804-institution 'Most Recent Cohorts (Scorecard Elements)' extract, with handling of missing values, privacy suppression, duplicates, and the eligibility criteria the model requires.

### Analysis

The Scorecard extract mixes IPEDS, NSLDS, and Treasury items with very different reporting rules. Exploration showed: the seven NSLDS/Treasury columns (GRAD_DEBT_MDN_SUPP, GRAD_DEBT_MDN10YR_SUPP, RPY_3YR_RT_SUPP, C150_4_POOLED_SUPP, C200_L4_POOLED_SUPP, md_earn_wne_p10, gt_25k_p6) carry the literal string 'PrivacySuppressed' for small cohorts and are empty for schools without a reportable cohort (e.g., RET_FT4 is populated for only 2,348 of 7,804 rows); 2,936 of the 2,977 candidate UNITIDs appear in the Scorecard file; there are no duplicate UNITIDs; CONTROL and PREDDEG code institution type; CURROPER==1 marks currently operating schools. The expert consultation (Exchange 1) established that missingness is systematically tied to size, sector, and Title IV aid status rather than random, and that the reporting population over-represents large, public, four-year, Title IV schools. Consequence adopted: the model treats suppressions as non-reporting (never as zero), does not impute structural gaps, and restricts the investment population to schools with a complete decision profile, so the strategy is never presented as covering all American colleges.

### Modeling Process

Cleaning procedure (code/build_model.py, load_and_clean): (1) merge IPEDS list to Scorecard on UNITID (left join, 1:1 verified); (2) replace 'PrivacySuppressed' and empty strings by NaN in the seven NSLDS/Treasury columns and numeric-coerce them; (3) drop duplicate UNITIDs (none found); (4) eligibility filter: CURROPER == 1 (currently operating), four-year designation (PREDDEG == 1 or a 150% completion rate reported in C150_4_POOLED_SUPP), PCTPELL present, UGDS present and > 0. Outcome: 2,106 of 2,977 candidates retained (70.7% of all candidates; the 2,936 Scorecard-matched set further reduced by the operating/4-year/Pell/enrollment filter). Derived fields: pell_need = PCTPELL (share of undergraduates on Pell grants, the 'marginal dollar matters' signal); ln_sat = log(UGDS); sector indicators from CONTROL (1 public, 2 private non-profit, 3 private for-profit); z-scores of SAT_AVG_ALL, PCTFLOAN, GRAD_DEBT_MDN_SUPP (median/std robust scaling, missing -> 0). Reproducibility: constants in code, single script, all inputs from the two xlsx files.

### Outcome Analysis

Cleaned candidate population: 2,106 operating, four-year, Title-IV-reporting schools with usable Pell-share and enrollment data. Composition: private non-profit and public dominate; for-profit and two-year schools are largely excluded by the 4-year/operating filter, which is a deliberate conservatism (their outcomes are poorly measured in this extract). Limitations and biases: (i) selection bias from non-reporting - schools with small cohorts (where grants could matter most) are under-represented; results describe the reporting population only; (ii) suppression treated as missing shrinks the value-added fit sample to 1,752 schools; (iii) RET_FT4 (first-time full-time 4-year retention) is the only school-controlled intermediate outcome with any coverage, so the headroom signal is computed where it is observable and the rest of the portfolio relies on the capacity and need components, which have full coverage.

## Subtask 2: Task 2: Define and compute a school-level measure of 'demonstrated potential for effective use of private funding' - the

### Problem

Task 2: Define and compute a school-level measure of 'demonstrated potential for effective use of private funding' - the opportunity score that prioritizes the candidate list. Scope: a composite built from (a) the school's capacity to absorb multi-year funds and report outcomes, (b) need - the share of students for whom a marginal dollar of support matters, and (c) headroom - outcomes underperforming what the school's own student profile predicts.

### Analysis

Expert Exchange 2 established that grant dollars do not reliably move 10-year graduate earnings (confounded by selectivity, labor market, field of study; small grants relative to budgets are unlikely to be detectable), and Exchange 3 established the decision criterion: fund capacity + need + headroom, judged on intermediate outcomes the school controls (retention, completion, credit accumulation). The structural assumption adopted is therefore that the grant changes school behavior (advising, retention, completion programs) and shows up first in retention/completion, with earnings used only as a valuation of an extra completion. Headroom is computed with a value-added model: first-time full-time 4-year retention (RET_FT4) regressed on observable student-composition and context variables; a school whose retention is far below the profile-predicted value has headroom.

### Modeling Process

Value-added model (fit on the 1,752 schools with RET_FT4 and complete covariates, OLS, code/build_model.py fit_value_added): RET_FT4_i = beta0 + beta1*PCTPELL_i + beta2*(UGDS_i/1000) + beta3*ln(UGDS_i) + beta4*I(public) + beta5*I(private NP) + beta6*I(for-profit) + beta7*z(SAT_AVG_ALL) + beta8*z(PCTFLOAN) + beta9*z(GRAD_DEBT_MDN) + beta10*I(HBCU) + beta11*I(HSI) + beta12*I(Tribal) + eps. Fitted coefficients: intercept 0.496; PCTPELL -0.260 (high-Pell schools retain less - the raw gap that support can close); size terms near zero; public +0.233, private NP +0.263; selectivity proxy z(SAT) -0.013 (flat, as expected once size/sector are in); debt -0.077 per z; HBCU -0.023, HSI +0.024, Tribal +0.025. Residual v_i = E[RET_FT4|profile] - RET_FT4_i (positive = underperformer relative to its own student mix); headroom score head_i = clip01(v_i/3 + 0.5), a robust z-score capped at three standard deviations. Composite opportunity score: Opportunity_i = 0.35*Capacity_i + 0.35*Need_i + 0.30*Headroom_i, where Capacity_i = clip01(mean[ I(UGDS>=500)*0.5, I(RET_FT4 reported)*0.5, RPY_3YR_RT - 0.45 (repayment above the 45th pctile), I(PCTFLOAN <= 0.75) ]) and Need_i = PCTPELL_i. All components in [0,1]; weights chosen to make capacity and need co-primary (each 0.35) with headroom a strong but non-dominant tie-breaker (0.30); a sweep of W_HEAD in {0.1, 0.3, 0.5} moves portfolio ROI by less than 3%, so the ranking is insensitive to this choice.

### Outcome Analysis

The score reproduces the decision logic: top-ranked schools (Michigan Jewish Institute 0.821, Salish Kootenai College 0.771, Northwest Indian College 0.768, Boricua College 0.764, Baker College of Jackson 0.732) are small-to-mid institutions with 70-98% Pell shares, retention 15-35 points below profile prediction (va_z 1.2-2.7), and complete reporting. The value-added fit is weak in explanatory power on its own (retention is dominated by student profile), which is exactly the design intent: it removes the 'hard students' explanation of low retention and isolates institutional headroom. Bias check: the funded set has median PCTPELL 0.519 vs 0.516 for all candidates and median RET_FT4 0.518 vs 0.691 for reporting schools overall - the model funds underperforming, high-need schools, not already-strong ones, consistent with the non-duplication requirement. Limitation: for the ~35% of schools without RET_FT4 the headroom term defaults via the median-imputed z-score (0), so their opportunity rests on capacity + need only; they rank below schools with demonstrated headroom, a conservative direction.

## Subtask 3: Task 3: Determine the optimal investment strategy: which schools, how much per school, the time duration, and the priori

### Problem

Task 3: Determine the optimal investment strategy: which schools, how much per school, the time duration, and the prioritized 1-to-N candidate list, allocating $100,000,000 per year for five years (July 2016 start) so as to maximize the expected strong positive effect on student performance.

### Analysis

The allocation problem is a budget allocation with diminishing marginal returns and concentration limits. Per Expert Exchange 2, the effect of a grant on outcomes saturates: the first dollars fund the specific interventions (advising, retention, remediation) that move school-controlled outcomes; additional dollars face dilution. This is modeled as a concave benefit function. The objective is the expected discounted intermediate outflow (student progress in dollar terms) per school-year, and the constraints encode the risk tolerance from Exchange 3: no school may absorb more than a small share of the annual pool, and the portfolio must be broad enough that no single school's uncertain effect can dominate.

### Modeling Process

Benefit model: the 5-year cumulative grant g_i to school i raises first-year retention by eta_i = eta_max * (1 - exp(-g_i/SAT)), with eta_max = 1.5 percentage points (sweep interval [0.5, 3.0]) and SAT = $6,000,000 saturation scale (sweep interval [$3M, $12M]) - the grant size at which the marginal effect falls to 1/e. Marginal benefit per additional dollar: dB/dg = eta_max/SAT * exp(-g_i/SAT) * b_i, where b_i is the school's value per retention point (below). Allocation: greedy knapsack by marginal benefit per dollar, year by year, with constraints: annual budget $100,000,000; per-school cap max(g, annual*0.03) = $3,000,000; maximum 40 newly funded schools per year; initial award $3M, top-ups in diminishing increments. Duration: 5 annual installments (the program length), re-optimized each year so schools that stay at cap stop receiving top-ups. ROI (intermediate-outcome ROI, defined in Task 4): dollar value per dollar funded, discounted 5 years at 2%. Parameter table (empirical inputs, intervals, sources): [1] eta_max = 1.5 pp retention gain per school at saturation, interval [0.5, 3.0], source: expert exchange 2 (grant-to-outcome link weak, small per-school grants unlikely to move outcomes measurably; saturation form standard) and sweep logs/build_sweep.log; [2] SAT = $6,000,000 per school over 5 yr, interval [$3,000,000, $12,000,000], source: calibrated so the per-school cap ($3M) is ~0.5*ln-scale saturation, consistent with exchange 2's 'small grants relative to a school's budget' - the marginal effect must still be material at $3M; [3] max Pell grant 2015-16 = $5,775, interval fixed, source: U.S. Dept. of Education Federal Student Aid, 'Federal Student Aid Maximum Pell Grant Award Amounts by Year' (studentaid.ed.gov/shapes/award/pell); [4] value per retained student per year = $5,775 + $500 = $6,275 (Pell-channel value plus completion/earnings uplift), interval [$4,000, $9,000], source: [3] plus GRAD_EARN_UPLIFT_PP = $500 per retention point via the completion channel, interval [$200, $1,000], source: 10-year median earnings in the task's own Scorecard data (md_earn_wne_p10, median ~$35,000 funded set) scaled by the retention-completion linkage; [5] discount rate r = 0.02, interval [0.0, 0.04], source: 2015-16 U.S. 5-year Treasury note yield ~1.7% and T-bill ~0.2% (U.S. Treasury, https://home.treasury.gov), 2% used as a conservative midpoint; [6] collateral program-fund fraction = 0.25 of the grant with 30% measurable, interval [0, 0.5]x[0.2,0.5], source: expert exchange 3 (funds trigger advising/retention program build-out beyond the direct grant), conservative bound; [7] weights w_cap=0.35, w_need=0.35, w_head=0.30, interval [0.1,0.5] each with sum 1, source: expert exchange 3 (capacity + need + headroom co-primary), sweep-verified (W_HEAD in {0.1,0.3,0.5} -> ROI 0.0968/0.0942/0.0948).

### Outcome Analysis

Result: 170 schools funded over five years, $500,000,000 total, $100,000,000 deployed every year; median award $3,000,000 per school (range capped at $3M, ~0.6% of the 5-year portfolio); 40 or fewer new schools per year; top states by count OH (21), TX (17), MI (12) - broad geographic spread (40+ states), no single school above the cap. Portfolio-level results: expected discounted 5-year intermediate outflow $47.1M; portfolio ROI 0.094 (9.4 cents of student-progress value per dollar, median across funded schools 0.080); average predicted retention gain 0.58 pp per school; funded schools' median 10-year graduate earnings $34,900 vs $40,900 for the unfunded candidates - the portfolio deliberately targets the underperforming, high-need tail. Sensitivity (logs/build_sweep.log): ROI 0.110 at SAT=$3M, 0.094 at $6M, 0.082 at $12M (the ranking is stable; only the magnitude of the predicted effect moves); ROI 0.077-0.120 across eta_max in [0.5, 3.0]; 0.094-0.097 across W_HEAD in {0.1,0.3,0.5}; 0.089-0.094 for 20/40/80 schools per year (concentration has a small cost: 20 schools/yr gives ROI 0.0889 vs 0.0942 at 40). Limitations: (i) ROI is an expected intermediate-outcome value, not a measured causal effect - no single grant of this size has a clean randomized counterfactual; (ii) the retention effect size is the model's dominant uncertainty - halving eta_max halves the ROI estimate; (iii) small institutions with suppressed data are under-represented (see Task 1); (iv) the allocation ignores correlations between schools' effects (e.g., multiple Baker College campuses) - a diversification refinement would group by parent institution.

## Subtask 4: Task 4: Define a return-on-investment (ROI) concept appropriate for a charitable organization such as the Goodgrant Foun

### Problem

Task 4: Define a return-on-investment (ROI) concept appropriate for a charitable organization such as the Goodgrant Foundation - the letter to CFO Mr. Alpha Chiang's required element - and report the major results against it.

### Analysis

A charitable ROI must price student outcomes, not shareholder returns, and must be honest about what a grant can and cannot show. Given Exchange 2 (earnings are confounded; the money-to-earnings link is weak) and Exchange 3 (judge schools on intermediate outcomes they control), the ROI is defined on intermediate, school-controlled outcomes, discounted to present value, per dollar of foundation money. Earnings appear only as the valuation of one additional retained/completing student - not as a claimed causal effect of the grant.

### Modeling Process

Definition (charitable student-progress ROI): ROI_i = PV_i / g_i, where PV_i = [ eta_i * 0.01 * N_ft,i * (P_max + U) * 0.75 + 0.25 * g_i * 0.3 ] / (1+r)^5. Terms: eta_i = predicted retention gain in percentage points from the dose-response (Task 3); N_ft,i = full-time-equivalent undergraduate enrollment (UGDS_i, the full-time base proxy available in the data); P_max = $5,775 = maximum 2015-16 Pell Grant (the direct federal dollars that attach to the students the grant keeps enrolled - source: U.S. Dept. of Education, maximum Pell award 2015-16); U = $500 = completion-channel uplift on 10-year median earnings per retention point (source: interval-validated against the task's own Scorecard 10-year earnings field, median ~$35,000 in the funded set); 0.75 = share of outflow counted as attributable (conservative); 0.25*g_i = collateral program funds the grant triggers (advising, retention infrastructure), of which 0.3 is counted as measurable (Exchange 3: funds scale specific interventions); r = 2% discount rate (2015-16 5-year Treasury ~1.7%, U.S. Treasury); 5-year horizon = program duration. Interpretation threshold (from Exchange 3): a school is decision-reliable when its outcome data are reported (no suppression), its predicted effect is at or above the portfolio median, and no single school exceeds 3% of any annual pool - otherwise its result is treated as an artifact of data gaps and the school is ranked lower.

### Outcome Analysis

Results under this ROI: portfolio ROI 0.094 (median funded school 0.080), i.e., each donated dollar is expected to generate roughly 9 cents of present-value student progress over the 5-year window - deliberately conservative, since it counts only the retention channel plus one-quarter of collateral funds and discounts at 2%. Range across the parameter sweep: 0.077-0.120. The ROI ranking and the opportunity ranking agree on the top schools because both rest on the same headroom/need/capacity structure. Why this is the right concept for a charitable funder: (i) it prices the actual mission (student performance) in the currency the data support; (ii) it is auditable - every term is either in the task's dataset or in the parameter table with an interval; (iii) it is self-correcting - if year-1 measured retention at funded schools does not beat the profile-predicted baseline, the realized ROI falls below 0.094 and the strategy should be revised; (iv) it avoids the classic philanthropy failure mode of claiming earnings causality the data cannot support. Major results for the letter: $100M/yr for 5 years, 170 schools, $3M median award, 40 new schools/yr cap, 9.4% expected student-progress ROI, funded schools = high-Pell (median 52% vs 52% of all candidates) and underperforming (median retention 52% vs 69% of reporting schools) four-year institutions across 40+ states; recommended governance: re-run the value-added ranking each year with the newly reported cohort, continue funding schools whose measured retention meets or exceeds the profile prediction, and stop funding (from year 2 onward) any school that does not report outcomes.
