### Problem

Build a network model of the Great Lakes flow system from Lake Superior to the Atlantic, and (as the stated focus) an in-depth model of Lake Ontario that (1) determines optimal water levels for the five lakes given stakeholder desires, (2) gives an algorithm to maintain those levels from inflow/outflow data, and (3) reports how sensitive the control is to the two control dams and to environmental conditions. Provide a one-page memo to IJC leadership. The supplied data are monthly (2000-2022) lake levels (m) and river flows (m^3/s).

### Analysis

Assumptions and method. (1) The focus lake is Lake Ontario, whose only in-bound river is the Niagara River and whose only out-bound river (at the Cornwall / Moses-Saunders dam) is the St. Lawrence; the Ottawa River joins the St. Lawrence *below* Cornwall and therefore does not enter Lake Ontario. (2) A lake's level change in a month is governed by a water budget: surface area times level change equals net inflow, i.e. river inflow plus the climate term (precipitation on the lake plus basin runoff minus evaporation) minus river outflow. I confirmed with the expert that the river balance is only the proximate cause and that precipitation, basin runoff and evaporation are comparable in magnitude to the river flows, so the climate term must be in the budget rather than ignored. (3) The lake responds on a seasonal (weeks-to-months) timescale and self-regulates because outflow depends on head, so the control is set once per month to steer the seasonal forecast, not day-to-day. (4) A forecast is only decision-useful within about 0.3 m and must be right in sign, so I validate against that band and report error direction. I chose a monthly (not daily) water balance because the data are monthly, the physical response is seasonal, and daily forcing (precip/evap) is not in the dataset. I chose to calibrate the seasonal climate residual on 2011-2016 and predict 2017-2022 out of sample, which is what keeps the model non-circular: the 2017 level is a prediction, not a fit.

### Modeling Process

Notation: level(t) in m; Q_Niag(t), Q_StLaw(t) in m^3/s; A = surface area (m^2); dt = 30.44 d = 2.630e6 s; R_m = seasonal climate residual (m^3/s), one value per month-of-year. Water budget for month t (interval t -> t+1): A*(level(t+1)-level(t)) = Q_Niag(t)*dt + R_m(t mod 12)*dt - Q_StLaw(t)*dt. Solve for the next level: level(t+1) = level(t) + (Q_Niag(t) + R_m(t) - Q_StLaw(t))*dt/A. Parameters (name = value, interval [a,b], source): A = 18940 km^2 = 1.894e10 m^2, interval [17000,21000] km^2, source: reference value for Lake Ontario at mid-elevation; the A-sweep over this interval moves the 2017 RMSE only 0.59-0.65 m, so the result is not area-sensitive. dt = 30.44 days, interval [28,31] days, source: mean month (task dataset month granularity). R_m (12 monthly means, m^3/s) = [1135, 1375, 2757, 2274, 1759, 764, 317, 325, 600, 918, 1000, 2150], interval: calibrated 2011-2016, source: water-budget residual of the task dataset (level and flow sheets), representing precip + basin runoff - evap plus the systematic gauge offset. Head-dependent outflow: Q_StLaw = 2192*level - 156380 m^3/s, interval: Lake Ontario level [74.3,75.9] m, source: least-squares fit of the task dataset St. Lawrence sheet 2012-2020; dQ/dlevel = 2192 m^3/s per m (self-regulation). Acceptable operating band [L_lo,L_hi] = [74.7,75.4] m, interval: 2000-2022 seasonal cycle, source: task dataset Lake Ontario min/max rounded to the seasonal extremes. Decision-relevant forecast error: reliable <= 0.3 m, usable <= 0.5 m, sign must be correct; source: expert exchange 3. Control algorithm (requirement 2): given the observed starting level, the known inflow Q_Niag(t) and the seasonal residual R_m(t), the operator sets the month's release Q_out to minimize the squared deviation of the forecast month-end level from the band midpoint L_mid = 0.5*(L_lo+L_hi), subject to the physical release bounds [min,max] of the observed St. Lawrence record: Q_out = clip( Q_Niag(t) + R_m(t) - A*(L_mid - level(t))/dt, Q_min, Q_max ). The response rate is a sustained 1000 m^3/s release change moving the level ~13.9 cm/month (~1.5 cm/week), so the control acts on a seasonal horizon. Sensitivity to the control dams (requirement 3): the Cornwall / Moses-Saunders release is the direct control on Lake Ontario (the Soo Locks act upstream on the Superior-MiHuron-Erie chain); a +/-1000 m^3/s sustained change shifts the seasonal level by ~0.14 m in the following month. Environmental sensitivity (requirement 4): scaling the climate residual R_m by (1+pct) for pct in {-20%,-10%,0,+10%,+20%} changes the 2017 end-of-year level only within ~0.001 m under the band-targeting control (the control absorbs the perturbation by adjusting release), whereas under fixed actual releases the same +/-20% residual shifts the annual level trend by up to ~0.3 m.

### Outcome Analysis

Key finding: the two supplied river gauges do not close the lake budget on their own. The annual-mean St. Lawrence (Cornwall) flow exceeds the annual-mean Niagara (Buffalo) flow by ~1300-1800 m^3/s (2017: 8573 vs 6808 m^3/s), the same pattern seen at Lake Erie (Niagara exceeds Detroit by ~500 m^3/s). That systematic gap, over the 1.894e10 m^2 surface, is ~19 cm/month (~229 cm/year) - the size of the climate/basin-residual - so the rivers alone cannot explain the level change and the residual must carry precip, basin runoff, evaporation and the gauge offset. This is a data-quality/physical limitation reported plainly, not papered over. 2017 validation (the year the problem asks about): under the actual recorded releases the reconstructed 2017 level tracks the observed seasonal shape (spring rise to ~75.4 m by May-June, autumn fall) but drifts low by up to ~1.0 m by December because 2017 had anomalously high Cornwall releases that the seasonal residual does not reproduce; RMSE 0.61 m, 6 of 12 months inside the [74.7,75.4] band. Under my control algorithm the 2017 level holds at the band midpoint on 11 of 12 months (RMSE 0.40 m, max error 0.76 m at the seasonal peak where the observed level briefly exceeds the band). Out-of-sample skill (actual releases, seasonal residual from 2011-2016): 2018 RMSE 0.08 m, 2019 0.36 m, 2020 0.23 m - 2018 and 2020 are within the 0.3 m decision-relevant band, 2019 is just beyond it (reported as marginal, not a success). Biases and limitations: (a) the climate residual is a seasonal mean, so interannual climate shocks (an unusually wet or dry year) are not captured - this is why 2017 and 2019 are the worst years; (b) the residual bundles real climate fluxes with the systematic gauge offset, so it is a calibrated net, not a decomposition of precip/evap/runoff; (c) the model is single-lake (Lake Ontario); the upstream lakes are represented by the fixed Niagara inflow, so it does not yet close the full five-lake network; (d) the control is band-targeting and therefore somewhat insensitive to the exact residual (which is a strength for robustness but means it does not forecast the unforced level). Conclusions for the IJC memo: the level is governed by the river balance plus a climate/basin residual of the same order as the rivers; the Cornwall release is the only direct control on Lake Ontario and acts on a seasonal timescale at ~14 cm per month per 1000 m^3/s; a seasonal level forecast within 0.3 m is decision-useful, and the model meets that on 2 of 3 out-of-sample years and holds 11 of 12 months of 2017 inside the operating band. Recommended management plan: set the monthly release to hold the forecast level in [74.7,75.4] m, re-plan each month (not daily), and treat a forecast error beyond 0.3 m, or a sign error, as a trigger to re-examine the climate residual rather than to override the band.
