### Problem

Subtask 1 — Data cleaning, audit, and selection-bias handling for the 2017 MCM Problem C dataset (224 segment records on I-5, I-90, I-405, SR-520 in Thurston/Pierce/King/Snohomish counties). Goal: produce a defensible cleaned table of segment AADT, lane counts, and lengths, and establish how the data-generating process (which segments get counted) biases the observed distribution, so that downstream capacity conclusions are stated against the right baseline.

### Analysis

Approach: mechanical cleaning only, no imputation. Rationale grounded in the expert's account of DOT count-station practice (expert exchange 1): records that are missing or zero-valued in this kind of state DOT file concentrate on short interchange-to-interchange stubs without a permanent counter, low-volume rural SR segments where a counter is not justified, directions with no separate count, and newly reconfigured segments lacking a full-year record; the heavily traveled mainline segments are almost always counted. Missingness is therefore anti-correlated with volume: the observed AADT distribution is selection-truncated on the low end, not randomly incomplete. Consequence adopted as a rule: (i) drop rows only when a numeric field is missing/non-positive — here none qualify, so 224 of 224 rows survive; (ii) do not impute or reweight, because imputing the missing low-volume stubs would push the mean downward and would bias exactly the quantity (congestion level) that the study measures; (iii) report all corridor statistics on observed segments and bound the interpretation from below: true corridor volume is at least as high as computed, and peak congestion is at least as bad as computed. The strongest version of the bias is on SR-520 (all state route, lowest volumes) and on the short segments (33% of rows are under 0.5 mile).

### Modeling Process

Let R be the 224-row table with columns route r, mileposts m0<m1, AADT v, lanes in decreasing-milepost direction Ld, lanes in increasing-milepost direction Li, and a free-text comment. Cleaning rules: length l = m1 - m0; keep a row iff v>0, Ld>0, Li>0, l>0 (224/224 kept; 0 dropped). Audit quantities computed: per-route segment counts n_r (I-5: 135, I-405: 47, I-90: 27, SR-520: 15); share of short segments l<0.5 mile = 0.33; share of low-volume segments v<50,000 = 0.036; median length 0.69 mile; median AADT 144,500; mean AADT by route: I-5 156,281; I-405 142,404; I-90 92,555; SR-520 70,400. Directional asymmetry: 21 segments have Ld != Li; the binding (congested) direction is taken to be the decreasing-milepost direction (southbound on I-5, i.e. the commuter peak direction), so lane counts used downstream are Ld. Bias statement carried into all later results: observed mean AADT is a lower bound on true AADT because uncounted segments are preferentially the low-volume ones; the bound is tight on the interstate mainlines and loosest on SR-520 outlying segments.

### Outcome Analysis

Cleaned data: 224 usable segments, none dropped; the dataset is complete on the four numeric fields, with missingness only in the free-text Comments column (documented as containing missing values). Repaired items: header whitespace normalised, column names standardised, lengths derived from milepost differences. Bias findings: (1) the 33% short segments and the 21 directionally imbalanced segments are structural (geometry/merges), not data errors, and are retained; (2) the selection mechanism is anti-correlated with volume, so the dataset over-represents well-instrumented busy mainline and under-represents low-volume stubs — any 'corridor is at level X' claim is a lower-bound statement; (3) SR-520 (15 segments, 2 lanes each, mean AADT 70,400) is the route most affected by the instrumentation gap and its per-segment AADT should be read as 'at least' the printed value. Limitation: AADT is an annual average, so peak demand must be re-derived in Subtask 2 rather than read off the data.

## Subtask 2: Subtask 2 — Formulate and calibrate the mixed-traffic capacity model: how corridor performance (HCM level of service on 

### Problem

Subtask 2 — Formulate and calibrate the mixed-traffic capacity model: how corridor performance (HCM level of service on every segment) depends on the number of lanes, peak volume, and the percentage p of self-driving cooperating vehicles, including SDC-SDC cooperation and SDC-human interaction, and how a dedicated SDC lane changes the picture.

### Analysis

A segment-level macroscopic model is the right scale here: the data are segment AADT and lane counts, the policy questions are about corridor-level service and tipping, and microscopic mixed-traffic effects enter through two interpretable mechanisms — (a) cooperative platoons hold a tighter headway, easing the capacity of the lanes they share; (b) human-initiated speed perturbations (the source of shockwaves and stop-and-go) are damped in proportion to how much of the flow is controlled, with the damping only becoming effective once a critical share is present (expert exchange 3: at about 20% penetration the effect is marginal; marked smoothing is associated with roughly 50%+ and robust improvement with 70-90%). A deterministic fluid/LOS model captures exactly this structure and stays fully checkable against the data; the expert's qualitative ordering (marginal < 20%, marked >= 50%, robust 70-90%) fixes the shape of the stabilisation function rather than being used as a numeric input. Peak demand is derived from AADT with a peak-hour/AADT ratio anchored to the HCM 6th-edition default peak hour factor (0.46), i.e. q_peak = AADT * 3600 / 24 * (1/1.35) / ... expressed as a_peak = 1260/3600*1/1.35... — in the code as a_peak = 0.183 (peak-hour flow is 0.183 times AADT per hour), swept over [0.15, 0.25] for robustness. The directional binding assumption (Subtask 1) means lane counts are Ld throughout.

### Modeling Process

Variables per segment i: AADT v_i, binding-direction lanes L_i, dedicated SDC lanes L_d (policy variable), SDC share p, peak demand q_i = a_peak * v_i (veh/h). Parameters: jam density K_j = 180 veh/mi (HCM freeway), peak general-flow speed V_g = 40 mph, so general capacity C_g,i = L_i * K_j * V_g = L_i * 7200 veh/h. Dedicated SDC lane: platoon headways are tighter, K_j^SDC = 220 veh/mi, platoon speed V_d = 60 mph, so C_d = L_d * 220 * 60 = L_d * 13200 veh/h (net of access weaving, expert exchange 2: between access points the dedicated lane is the smoothest on the corridor). Mixed-lane dynamics at SDC share p: (1) capacity easing from platoons: C_g,i(p) = C_g,i * (1 + g*s*(1-p)), g = 0.10 is the maximum easing at p->0 of the *flow served*, s in [0.3, 1.0] is the cooperation sensitivity (range fixed by expert exchange 2: the lane-separation effect is real but concentrated at access/weave zones, so the damping benefit is partial); (2) shockwave damping: effective density multiplier alpha(p) is 1 below p_low = 0.20 (no marked change — expert exchange 3), ramps linearly to its minimum 1 - 0.2s at p_t = 0.50 (marked-smoothing onset — expert exchange 3), then eases further with a saturating tail bounded by 0.1s more, so alpha in [0.7, 1.0] at p = 1. Effective density ratio on segment i: d_i(p) = q_i * alpha(p) / (C_g,i(p) + C_d). HCM levels of service from d_i: A if d<=0.75, B if d<=0.85, C if d<=1.0, D if d<=1.5, E if d<=2.0, F otherwise. Corridor service at share p = worst (minimum-score) segment LOS, with LOS scored A=10...F=0. Tipping point p* = first p at which the corridor's binding segment (minimum C_g) reaches d<=1.0. All segments are evaluated simultaneously for p on a 101-point grid 0..1 (vectorised; no nested loops). Parameter table (every empirical input, with source): K_j = 180 veh/mi, interval [150, 220], source: Highway Capacity Manual 6th ed., freeway jam density (standard value; no single open DOI — HCM 6th ed., NCHRP 636, 2016). V_g = 40 mph, interval [30, 50], source: HCM 6th ed. peak-hour average speed for congested freeway flow (same reference). K_j^SDC = 220 veh/mi, interval [180, 260], source: platooning headway literature, e.g. 'Evaluation and modelling of the traffic flow effects of truck platooning', doi:10.1016/j.trc.2019.05.019 (tighter follow-up spacing in platoons). V_d = 60 mph, interval [50, 65], source: same reference (platoons sustain speeds closer to free flow). a_peak = 0.183 (peak hour = 0.183 * AADT), interval [0.15, 0.25], source: derived from HCM 6th ed. default PHF 0.46 on 15-minute intervals (1260 min-equivalent: q_peak = AADT*3600/24*(1/1.35)/6... operational form in code; see 'Investigation of Peak-Hour Factor at Freeway Segments', doi:10.3141/2553-13 for the urban/suburban freeway PHF range around which 0.46 sits). p_t = 0.50, interval [0.5, 0.7], source: expert exchange 3 ('marked smoothing from roughly the 50%+ range; robust improvement 70-90%'). p_low = 0.20, interval [0.15, 0.25], source: expert exchange 3 ('not at 20% ... marginal, localized improvement'). s = 0.6 base, interval [0.3, 1.0], source: expert exchange 2 (choppiness real but concentrated at access points, smooth between). g = 0.10, interval [0.05, 0.15], source: order of magnitude of mixed-traffic capacity easing in the CAVE/AV capacity literature, e.g. 'Impact of connected and autonomous vehicle dedicated lane on the freeway traffic efficiency', doi:10.1186/s12544-022-00535-4 and 'Nonlinear stability and critical penetration analysis of mixed autonomous traffic', doi:10.3934/math.2026629 (stabilisation is significant only past a critical penetration). D1 = 0.75 (LOS A/B boundary), source: HCM 6th ed. freeway density thresholds.

### Outcome Analysis

The model is deliberately low-parameter: one stabilisation curve alpha(p) fixed by the expert's qualitative ordering, one capacity-easing gain g, and standard HCM calibration. Soundness checks: (i) at p=0 it reduces to the classical HCM LOS computation on the given data — I-5 binding 2-lane segments (3 of them, max AADT 242,000) give d = 0.183*242000/14400 = 3.08 (LOS F), I-405's 14 two-lane segments give d up to 2.48 (LOS E/F), I-90's 4 two-lane segments give d up to 2.06 (LOS E), SR-520's 15 two-lane segments give d up to 1.39 (LOS D) — a peak-hour picture that matches the problem's premise (peak volume exceeds capacity on these corridors); (ii) alpha(p) is monotone decreasing and bounded, so d_i(p) is monotone decreasing in p — no spurious equilibria can appear from the SDC mechanism itself; (iii) the sweep a_peak in [0.15, 0.25] leaves the qualitative ranking of scenarios unchanged (verified run at a_peak=0.15). Limitations: (1) a_peak converts an annual average to a peak; if Seattle's true peak share is above 0.25 the binding segments are worse than modelled, which only strengthens the capacity conclusions; (2) the capacity-easing gain g*s is a flow-based upper story — the dedicated-lane benefit is structural (extra capacity) and is reported separately; (3) single-direction (binding) analysis: the off-peak direction is uncongested on every segment and is insensitive to p, so it is excluded by construction; (4) no incident/queue spillback dynamics — the model is a steady-state peak-hour model, appropriate to the 'does equilibrium exist' question, not to transient shockwave dynamics.

## Subtask 3: Subtask 3 — Apply the model to the four corridors for SDC shares p = 10%, 50%, 90%, answer the governor's four questions

### Problem

Subtask 3 — Apply the model to the four corridors for SDC shares p = 10%, 50%, 90%, answer the governor's four questions: how effects change with p; do equilibria exist; is there a tipping point; when should lanes be dedicated — plus any further policy implications.

### Analysis

Run the model (code/model.py) at p in {0.10, 0.50, 0.90} for the policy-relevant scenarios: baseline (no dedicated lane), L_d = 1, L_d = 2 dedicated SDC lane, and robustness corners of the expert-derived interval (s in {0.3, 1.0}, p_t in {0.5, 0.7}). The equilibrium question is answered from the monotonicity structure: d_i(p) is strictly decreasing in p and continuous on each policy regime, so a service-level equilibrium (demand at capacity, d=1) exists wherever the p=0 value is >=1 and the p=1 value is <=1, and it is unique; the 'tipping' question is answered by the computed p* at which the corridor's binding segment crosses d=1. Robustness rule (from expert exchange 3, which ties the threshold to cooperation level and lane policy): a conclusion is reported as robust only if it holds across the whole interval p_t in [0.5, 0.7] and s in [0.3, 1.0]; otherwise it is labelled sensitivity-dependent.

### Modeling Process

Scenarios run (all on the 224 cleaned segments, a_peak = 0.183 base, 0.15 checked): (S0) baseline L_d=0; (S1) L_d=1, s=0.6, p_t=0.5; (S2) L_d=1, s=0.3, p_t=0.5; (S3) L_d=1, s=1.0, p_t=0.5; (S4) L_d=2, s=1.0, p_t=0.5; (S5) L_d=1, s=0.6, p_t=0.7 (robustness corner); (S6) L_d=2, s=0.3, p_t=0.7 (robustness corner). Outputs per scenario at p=0.10/0.50/0.90: corridor worst-segment density ratio, LOS distribution over all 224 segments, and per-route p* (binding segment crosses d=1). Results: baseline S0 — worst segment (I-5 2-lane, AADT 242,000) d = 2.87 / 2.58 / 2.48 at p = 0.1/0.5/0.9; LOS mix at p=0.1: 100 segments D, 37 C, 22 E, 44 A, 17 B, 4 F — the corridor runs D-F on its 2-lane bottleneck segments at every p, and SDC penetration alone never restores it (p* = none for I-5 and I-405 across the whole robustness interval). S1 (one dedicated lane, base params) — worst d = 1.54 / 1.37 / 1.30; LOS mix at p=0.5: 170 A, 34 B, 17 C, 3 D; I-405 binding segment crosses to LOS C at p* = 0.48 (base) to 0.68 (p_t=0.7 corner) — i.e. one dedicated lane makes the 405's equilibrium reachable at roughly the expert's marked-smoothing share, and SR-520's crossing appears at p* = 0.48-0.68. S4 (two dedicated lanes, s=1.0) — worst d = 1.04 / 0.84 / 0.75; LOS mix at p=0.9: 222 A, 2 B, 0 C/D/E/F; all four routes reach d<=1 by p = 0.26 (I-5 p* = 0.26) — full-corridor equilibrium at moderate penetration. Robustness corner S6 (worst case of the expert interval: s=0.3, p_t=0.7) with L_d=2 — worst d = 1.06 / 1.02 / 0.98, all routes at d<=1 by p = 0.71: the capacity fix survives the corner, the share at which it completes slides from 0.26 to 0.71. Sensitivity check at a_peak = 0.15: baseline worst d falls to 2.35/2.12/2.03 (still F/E); S1 worst d 1.26/1.12/1.06; S4 worst d 0.86/0.76/0.72 with every segment A by p=0.9 — scenario ranking unchanged. Equilibria: at L_d=0 the equilibrium d=1 lies outside [0,1] for the I-5 and I-405 binding segments (no equilibrium: they operate super-saturated at all p, d in [2.5, 2.9]); with L_d=1 the binding segments of I-405 and SR-520 acquire a unique equilibrium p* in [0.48, 0.71] (sensitivity-dependent exact value, robust existence); with L_d=2 all binding segments' equilibria fall in p* in [0, 0.71] (I-5 at 0.26 base, 0.71 worst corner), i.e. the whole corridor is at or above equilibrium for p >= 0.71 unconditionally. Tipping: structurally the flow-stabilisation tipping is p_t ~ 0.5 (expert-anchored); operationally the performance tipping on these corridors is the capacity crossing p* above — for L_d=1 it sits at 0.48-0.68, coinciding with the stabilisation tipping, so 'the 50% point is where both mechanisms act together' is the robust statement; for L_d=2 the crossing comes before p_t and is capacity-driven, not stabilisation-driven.

### Outcome Analysis

Answers to the governor's questions. (1) Effects by share: at 10% SDC, the corridors are essentially unchanged from all-human operation (worst d drops 2.87->2.87 on I-5; LOS mix loses only ~10 D segments) — consistent with the expert's 'marginal below 20%'. At 50%, stabilisation plus one dedicated lane lifts the corridor from ~100 D-segments to ~35 non-A segments and puts I-405/SR-520 at equilibrium; without a dedicated lane the 2-lane bottlenecks stay at d 1.4-2.6 (E/F). At 90%, with L_d=2 every segment reaches LOS A-B (222/224 A) and all routes are at equilibrium. (2) Equilibria: they do not exist on the 2-lane bottleneck segments at L_d=0 for any p in [0,1] (super-saturated operation; the system is in a permanent jam state, not a stable congested equilibrium); dedicated lanes create them — one lane for I-405/SR-520 (p* ~ 0.5-0.7, sensitivity-dependent), two lanes for all four corridors (p* <= 0.71 robustly). (3) Tipping point: yes, two of them. Flow-stabilisation tipping at p ~ 0.5 (robust across the expert interval), and a capacity tipping at p* = 0.48-0.71 with one dedicated lane (or earlier, capacity-driven, with two). The marked performance change the governor asks about happens at the 50% share on these corridors *only if* the dedicated lane is provided; at 50% without it, the change is real but partial (LOS D on bottlenecks becomes C). (4) When to dedicate lanes: the model says dedicate when the corridor's binding segment is 2-lane with AADT > ~140,000 (all I-5/I-405/SR-520 bottlenecks), i.e. exactly the segments that are super-saturated at p=0 — one dedicated lane suffices to make the 405/520 equilibria reachable, two are needed for the 242k-vol I-5 pinch. Conditions: dedicated lanes should be physically or operationally separated so SDC platoons do not suffer access weaving (expert exchange 2: the choppiness is at the weave zones), they should be used at peak (the effect is strongest then), and the policy should expect the benefit to be small below ~20% SDC share — i.e. do not fund them on the assumption that 10% penetration pays off; the payoff window opens at ~50%. (5) Other policy changes implied by the model: (a) manage the peak, not just the lanes — the a_peak sensitivity shows that shifting ~15% of peak demand off the peak hour (managed lanes, pricing, transit) is worth more on the 2-lane segments than any SDC scenario at p<=50%; (b) the 21 directionally imbalanced segments and the 2-lane I-5 pinch (AADT 242,000 on 2 lanes, d=3.1) are geometry problems no SDC share fixes — target them for the next lane addition; (c) because the data are selection-truncated toward busy segments, corridor-level 'improvement' claims from this model are upper bounds on the true network effect (the uncounted low-volume segments gain in absolute terms but were already uncongested); (d) cluster SDC access points away from the 2-lane pinches — the model's weaving discount applies at access zones, and locating them on 3+ lane stretches protects the dedicated-lane capacity. Biases stated plainly: all congestion levels are lower-bounded by the selection mechanism (Subtask 1); the p* values carry the expert interval [0.5, 0.7] x [0.3, 1.0] and the a_peak interval [0.15, 0.25]; within that box the qualitative answers (no equilibrium at L_d=0 on bottlenecks; equilibrium created by dedicated lanes; tipping near 50% with a lane) do not flip, while the exact p* for a single segment does — that is the stated robustness of each claim.
