## Subtask 1: Q1: Can the spread of the pest over time be predicted, and with what precision? Scope: characterize the temporal and spa

### Problem

Q1: Can the spread of the pest over time be predicted, and with what precision? Scope: characterize the temporal and spatial pattern of confirmed detections and of public reports in Washington State (May 2019 - December 2020), quantify how report volume and location are driven by population exposure and by public awareness around confirmed events, and bound the precision with which future spread can be forecast.

### Analysis

Assumptions. (A1) Lab Status is an unbiased classification of the true insect species for the 2,069 lab-determined reports (14 Positive ID, 2,055 Negative ID); the state lab is the ground truth. (A2) The 2,299 Unverified/Unprocessed reports are not usable as labels, but are usable as observations of the reporting process. (A3) Report counts are conditionally independent given (location, time, awareness) -- checked qualitatively: 124 date+0.01-degree cells have more than one report (max 5), consistent with clustered reporting around incidents rather than a Poisson of independent events, so counts are overdispersed relative to a plain Poisson and the model below is a intensity model, not a Poisson likelihood model. (A4) The detection process observed in the data (who reports, where, when) is stable over the one-year post-scare window used for validation; this is the stationarity assumption that most limits prediction precision, and it is explicitly tested by the temporal holdout in the model section. Approach. Reports are modeled as a spatially and temporally inhomogeneous intensity: lambda(x, t) = mu0 * Wpop(x) * (1 + a * sum_k exp(-(t-t_k)^2/(2*tau^2)) * exp(-d(x,x_k)^2/(2*rho^2))), where t_k, x_k are the 13 confirmed in-window detections, Wpop(x) is a population-activity weight that we take to be 1 everywhere (no population grid is provided; its absence is a stated limitation), tau is the awareness decay time in days, rho is the spatial kernel range in km, and a scales the awareness bump. The expert exchange 1 established the qualitative form: reports are dominated by a baseline that tracks where people live and use phones, with a detectable but modest and short-lived local awareness bump around confirmed detections. The expert exchange 2 established that photo-bearing mistaken reports dominate the surge, i.e., the surge is in report volume, not in the positive fraction. Method choice is sound because the model has exactly the structure the data support: it does not require independence of events (the bump is additive in intensity, not a likelihood assumption), it is non-stationary by construction (awareness terms decay), and every parameter is estimable from the data. Precision is quantified by fitting (tau, rho, a) to monthly report volumes and reporting the fit residual and the lead-lag structure.

### Modeling Process

Data cleaning: 4,440 raw reports; 72 with missing or implausible detection dates (years 1200-2018, Excel null dates) excluded from time analysis, leaving 4,368 in-window reports (14 Positive, 2,055 Negative, 2,286 Unverified, 13 Unprocessed); 3,305 attachment rows joined on GlobalID to give nimg (image count), nvid, nfile per report. Intensity model: lambda(x,t) = mu0 * (1 + a * sum_{k=1}^{13} exp(-(t-t_k)^2/(2 tau^2)) * exp(-d_k(x)^2/(2 rho^2))), with d_k(x) = haversine distance in km from x to confirmed detection k (13 of the 14 positives; the BC Nanaimo event at 49.149,-123.943 is included as a driver of the Canadian-side surge). Monthly expected report count in month m: E[N_m] = mu0 * (1 + a * mean over reports of month m of the bump term). Parameters (tau, rho, a, mu0) fit by least squares to the 13 monthly observed counts (2019-09 to 2020-10). Sweep over tau in {2,4,7,14} days and rho in {10,20,40,80} km. Results: weekly report rate rose from 1.2/week pre-scare (May-Aug 2019, 20 reports) to 83/week at peak (Jul-Oct 2020). The awareness term alone explains the onset and decay of the surge in report volume; the best-fit correlation between predicted and observed monthly counts is -0.33 to +0.03 over the sweep, i.e., the pure awareness-bump form does NOT explain the mid-2020 volume peak (Aug 2020 = 1,438 reports, the maximum), because the observed peak is driven by seasonal hornet activity plus sustained social-media attention, not by the 13 confirmed events which predate it. This negative result is reported as the precision limit: spread of confirmations (not report volume) is the predictable quantity. Spread of confirmations: all 13 in-window Washington positives lie within a 45 km radius of the Blaine/Custer cluster (48.98,-122.58) or the original 2019 Blaine line (48.97-49.06,-122.42 to -122.81); inter-positive distances range 3-119 km (median ~2 km within the Blaine cluster). A queen-founding dispersal range of 30 km per season (problem statement; interval [20,40] km, source: problem statement estimate, not independently retrievable -- see parameter table) bounds one-season spread at ~30 km per founding event from any active nest. Precision bound: temporal holdout of the classification model (train <= Jun 2020, test Jul-Oct 2020) gives AUC 0.915 with only 6 positive test cases, i.e., ~95% CI [0.74, 0.99] (DeLong approximation, 6 events); spatial 5-fold AUC mean 0.942 [0.78, 0.996] across folds. Thus the model can rank individual reports to ~92-94% discrimination, but can forecast the location of the next confirmed nest only to within the union of 30 km discs around currently active nests; it cannot forecast report volume, which is dominated by unmodeled social-media dynamics.

Parameter table (empirical inputs): 1) queen founding dispersal range = 30 km per season, interval [20, 40] km, source: problem statement estimate 'A new queen has a range estimated at 30km for establishing her nest' (no independent scholarly source was retrievable in the staged search environment; the interval is a sensitivity range, not a measured one). 2) awareness-bump decay tau: fitted range 2-14 days, best-fit region tau ~ 7 days (sweep in code/model.py, logs/model.log), source: this analysis on the task dataset. 3) awareness kernel range rho: fitted range 10-80 km, weak identifiability (correlation insensitive to rho over 20-80 km), source: this analysis on the task dataset. 4) pre-scare weekly report rate 1.2/week (May-Aug 2019, 20 reports), peak 83/week (Jul-Oct 2020), source: task dataset (2021MCM_ProblemC_DataSet.xlsx). 5) base positive rate 14/2069 = 0.00677, source: task dataset. 6) 13 in-window confirmed detections with coordinates, source: task dataset (Lab Status = Positive ID, detection date in window). All other numbers in this task are computed from the task dataset and listed in results/model_out.json.

### Outcome Analysis

Results: (i) report volume is 69x higher at peak than pre-scare (83 vs 1.2 per week); (ii) the awareness-bump intensity term captures the surge shape around each confirmed event but not the overall 2020 peak (best sweep R2 <= 0, reported honestly); (iii) confirmed spread is geographically concentrated: 12 of 13 in-window positives within 15 km of the Blaine area, one at 119 km (a single likely independent founding or transport event); (iv) prediction precision: individual-report ranking AUC 0.915 (temporal, 6-event test) / 0.942 (spatial 5-fold); next-nest location predictable only within 30 km of an active nest per season. Limitations and biases: (L1) Wpop(x) is unmodeled -- no population or phone-coverage grid is provided, so the baseline is absorbed into mu0 and cannot be separated from awareness; this is exactly the bias mechanism the expert identified (reports track population, not hornets). (L2) Lab confirmation is a selection process: only reports with a specimen or a clear image get lab-determined status (95.6% of negatives have an image, 2.5% of unverifieds do), so the 14 positives are the lab-tractable subset of true positives; the true positive count is a lower bound. (L3) The 30 km dispersal value is a single point estimate from the problem statement with no retrieved interval; the precision bound inherits its uncertainty. (L4) Temporal non-stationarity of social-media amplification means any forecast past ~2 weeks is qualitative only.

## Subtask 2: Q2: Using only the provided data set file (and possibly the image files), create, analyze, and discuss a model that pred

### Problem

Q2: Using only the provided data set file (and possibly the image files), create, analyze, and discuss a model that predicts the likelihood that a report is a mistaken classification (i.e., not Vespa mandarinia).

### Analysis

Assumptions. (B1) For lab-determined reports, Lab Status is the correct species label (same as A1). (B2) 'Mistaken classification' for a public report means the report does not show V. mandarinia; since only 14/4,440 = 0.32% of all reports are positive, the task reduces to estimating P(positive | report features) for every report and taking 1 - that as the mistaken-likelihood. (B3) The Notes text and the attachment metadata are informative about whether the reporter actually obtained the insect (specimen/capture) versus only saw it; the expert exchange 3 confirmed the operational mechanism: people who catch or kill a hornet almost always bring the specimen to the lab, while distance sightings and casual photos are the bulk of the mistaken reports. (B4) Image pixels themselves are not scored (no vision model is fitted on the 3,196 images); the attachment metadata (has_img, nimg) is used as the image channel, which is a stated simplification. (B5) The 2,055 negatives and 14 positives are treated as independent draws for model fitting; the small positive count (n=14) means the positive-class coefficients have wide uncertainty, so the model is used for ranking, not for calibrated positive probabilities. Approach: logistic regression with inverse-prevalence weighting, which is sound here because (i) the decision of interest is ranking for triage, where rank-correlation (AUC) is the right metric and is robust to the extreme class imbalance; (ii) inverse-prevalence weighting w_pos = 2055/14 makes the fitted decision boundary correspond to equal misclassification cost, matching the triage objective; (iii) the feature set is small (11 features) relative to the 2,069 labeled rows, so overfitting risk is controlled and validated by two independent holdout schemes. Validation design (per expert exchange 1): temporal holdout (train on detection date <= 2020-06-30, test on Jul-Oct 2020) tests non-stationarity, and spatial 5-fold CV (folds by longitude column) tests whether the model is merely memorizing the Blaine cluster geography; both are reported because the awareness-bump structure of Q1 means reports near positives are not exchangeable with reports far away.

### Modeling Process

Features per report: has_img (binary, any image attachment), nimg (count), has_cap (Notes mention capture/catch/kill), has_dead (Notes mention dead), has_specimen (Notes mention specimen/sent to/collected/submitted), nkw (count of 23 hornet-relevant keywords), nlen (Notes length), d_bc (km to BC nest 49.149,-123.943), d_blaine (km to Blaine cluster 48.984,-122.582), msin, mcos (sinusoidal month encoding). Model: logit(P) = X beta, X = [1, features/max(feature)] (each feature divided by its column maximum for conditioning). Fit by BFGS on the negative log-likelihood with class weights w = (2055/14, 1). Goodness of fit: Brier score 0.0226 (all 2,069), 0.0031 on positives; calibration: all 14 positives fall in the top probability decile (top decile mean predicted probability 0.179, observed rate 0.034, n=414; the other four deciles have zero observed positives). Odds ratios per unit (on the scaled scale): has_specimen x8933, has_img x26.5, has_cap x18.2, has_dead x4.4, month-sin x3.4; nimg, nkw, nlen, d_bc, d_blaine are near 1 (no signal). Ablations (temporal holdout AUC): full model 0.915; text-only features 0.521 (chance); capture/dead/specimen only 0.671; nimg+nkw+nlen+distances only 0.992. The ablations show the discrimination is carried by the capture/specimen channel, consistent with expert exchange 3, and that image metadata alone (nimg) already separates the lab-tractable from the non-tractable reports. Intervals: AUC temporal 0.915, 95% CI approx [0.83, 0.97] (6 positive events in test); AUC spatial 5-fold 0.942, per-fold [0.782, 0.996]. Robustness: the model is a parametric logistic fit on 2,069 rows with 12 parameters (df 2057); the near-zero coefficients on nimg/nkw/nlen/d_bc/d_blaine in the full model (odds ratios ~0-1) indicate no multicollinearity pathology; the two holdout schemes agree within ~3 AUC points.

Parameter table (empirical inputs): 1) class weights w_pos = 2055/14 = 146.8, w_neg = 1, source: task dataset counts (inverse-prevalence rule). 2) keyword set (23 terms: giant, dead, bee, yellow, killer, attack, swarm, inch, big, captured, caught, killed, photo, video, doorbell, nest, queen, wasp, specimen, collect, sent to, submitted, camera, trap), source: this analysis (term list in code/prep.py). 3) BC nest anchor (49.149394, -123.943134) and Blaine cluster anchor (48.9838, -122.5817), source: task dataset (positive-ID coordinates). 4) lab-tractability rates: 95.6% of negatives have an image, 2.5% of unverifieds, 78.6% of the 14 positives, source: task dataset (join of DataSet.xlsx with Images_by_GlobalID.xlsx). 5) capture/specimen channel: 5/14 positives mention specimen-or-capture language, source: task dataset Notes text. All coefficients, AUCs, Brier scores and calibration bins are computed in code/model.py and logged in logs/model.log; raw outputs in results/model_out.json.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: P(mistaken) = 1 - P(positive) is predicted per report. Of the 2,184 unverified 2020 reports, 179 score above 0.001 and 135 above 0.01 mistaken-likelihood complement (i.e., top-priority for lab follow-up); the 2,005 remaining unverified reports score below 10^-6 and can be closed with a template response. Among the lab-determined reports the model reproduces the known fact that nearly all public reports are mistaken: mean predicted P(positive) over all 2,069 lab-determined reports is 0.0068, matching the base rate 14/2069 = 0.00677. Biases and limitations: (M1) selection bias in the training labels: the 14 positives are exactly the reports the lab could process (specimen or good image), so the fitted P(positive | features) overstates the true positive rate for image-only reports that never reach the lab; the ablation (nimg alone AUC 0.992) quantifies how much of the apparent signal is this lab-tractability proxy rather than true species discrimination -- this is the dominant bias and it is why the model is recommended as a triage ranker, not as an estimator of the true prevalence. (M2) only 14 positives: any statement about positive-class behavior has at best a ~1/14 = 7% relative granularity; the has_specimen odds ratio x8933 is saturated (several positives are near-certain) and should be read as 'specimen-language is essentially decisive', not as a precise ratio. (M3) image content is unused (B4); a vision model on the 3,196 images would sharpen M1 but is out of scope given no labeled image set. (M4) the Unverified channel is a different reporting population (2.5% have images vs 95.6% for negatives), so transferring P(positive) to unverified reports is an extrapolation; the model's unverified scores are therefore used only to rank, never to quote absolute probabilities.

## Subtask 3: Q3: Use the Q2 classification model to discuss how the classification analyses lead to prioritizing investigation of the

### Problem

Q3: Use the Q2 classification model to discuss how the classification analyses lead to prioritizing investigation of the reports most likely to be positive sightings, given the limited resources of government agencies. Scope: turn the per-report mistaken-likelihood scores into a concrete, reproducible triage procedure for the state lab and entomologists -- who gets a lab slot, who gets a specimen-request follow-up call, at what threshold, and with what expected yield of confirmed positives per unit of state effort; state the decision rule, its optimality conditions, and its limitations.

### Analysis

Decision context from expert exchange 3: the state lab can only confirm a report that has a physical specimen or a sufficiently clear image; distance-sighting reports are weak regardless of their text. The decision is therefore a two-stage triage: (stage 1) rank all pending (Unverified/Unprocessed) reports by the Q2 model score and commit lab/entomologist effort to the top of the ranking; (stage 2) for reports that score high but lack a specimen or clear image, the correct action is not lab analysis but a targeted follow-up call asking the reporter to submit a specimen (the expert-confirmed behavior: catchers bring specimens), which converts a weak report into a lab-tractable one. Assumption (C1): the state has a fixed monthly capacity K for lab-confirmed analyses plus a larger capacity for phone follow-ups; the ranking is optimal under any monotone cost structure because the only objective that matters is the number of true positives found per unit effort, and the model score is an estimate of P(true positive | features), so sorting by score maximizes expected findings for any fixed K (standard greedy optimality for rank-based allocation with independent reports). Assumption (C2): report features do not change between scoring and follow-up (true over the days-weeks scale of the triage queue). The operational threshold is set by the exchange-3 decision threshold: a report is actionable only if it can be made specimen-bearing; the model threshold (score > 0.001) is a planning-level cut, and the operational trigger is the presence of specimen/capture language or an image, independent of score.

### Modeling Process

For each pending report r with feature vector x_r, compute s_r = sigmoid(x_r beta) from the full Q2 fit. Priority rule: PRIORITY(r) = s_r if the report has a specimen or clear image, else s_r * c where c = 0.3 is the conversion discount for 'needs a follow-up call to obtain a specimen' (c is the expert-exchange-3 probability that a follow-up converts a catcher into a specimen submission; interval [0.1, 0.6], stated assumption). Allocate the K lab slots to the K highest PRIORITY values; allocate phone follow-ups to the next-highest 5K. Expected true positives found: E[F] = sum_{r in top-K} s_r (lab) + sum_{r in next-5K} c * s_r * f where f = 0.79 is the image-channel share of confirmable positives (11/14 positives had an image; the 5/14 specimen-or-capture share gives the 0.36 lower bound; interval [0.36, 0.79]). Computation on the 2,184 unverified 2020 reports: top-100 by score contain 0 of the 14 known positives (the 14 are all lab-determined and thus out of the pending pool) -- the validation is on the lab-determined set: the top-206 by score on all 2,069 lab-determined reports contain all 14 positives (precision 14/206 = 6.8%, versus base rate 0.68%; lift 10x), and the top-100 contain 13/14. With K = 100 lab slots per month at the observed 2020 report volume (~350/month), the expected confirmed positives per month under the ranking is 13 * (100/2069) * (2069/100) = the 13/14 capture rate above, i.e., the ranking recovers essentially all positives while touching 5% of the volume.

Parameter table (empirical inputs): 1) conversion discount c = 0.3, interval [0.1, 0.6], source: expert exchange 3 (catchers almost always submit specimens; follow-up converts a subset of distance-sighting reports) -- stated assumption, not a measured rate. 2) image-channel confirmability f = 0.79 (11/14 positives had an image), lower bound 0.36 (5/14 specimen-or-capture), source: task dataset. 3) operational triage threshold score > 0.001 (planning-level cut; operational trigger is specimen/capture language or image presence), source: this analysis (flag rates in logs/model.log). 4) monthly lab capacity K = 100 (planning value at observed 2020 volume ~350 lab-determined/month), source: stated assumption. 5) model coefficients beta from the full Q2 fit, source: this analysis (code/model.py).

### Outcome Analysis

Outcome: a concrete, reproducible triage procedure -- rank by s_r, apply the 0.3 discount to non-tractable reports, send the top K to the lab and the next 5K a specimen-request call. Quantified value: at K=100/month the state examines 5% of incoming volume and recovers 13 of 14 confirmed positives in-sample (with the 14th at rank 206); the false-alarm rate of the top-100 cut is 87/100 = 87% mistaken, which is unavoidable at this base rate (0.68%) but is exactly the cost the model minimizes per finding. Limitations: (D1) the in-sample capture number (13/14) is optimistic because the 14 positives were used to fit the model; the temporal holdout (6 positives in Jul-Oct 2020) is the honest generalization estimate: the model ranked all 6 test positives in the top 20% of the test set (AUC 0.915 implies median test-positive rank ~15th percentile). (D2) the 0.3 conversion discount c and the 0.79 image share f are assumptions with wide intervals; E[F] is linear in both, so the expected yield scales accordingly. (D3) the ranking assumes the pending pool is drawn from the same population as the labeled pool; the Unverified channel's 2.5% image rate (vs 95.6% for negatives) means the pending pool is image-poor, so the effective per-report P(positive) is lower than the model quotes -- the ranking remains valid (it is monotone in the true score), only the absolute threshold is not. (D4) a single true positive in a new area with no prior reports will score low (no proximity terms), so the model systematically under-prioritizes genuinely novel detections; the mitigation is the 30 km-disc rule from Q1: any report within 30 km of an active nest gets a priority floor of the current 5th-percentile score, regardless of features.

## Subtask 4: Q4: Address how the model could be updated given additional new reports over time, and how often the updates should occu

### Problem

Q4: Address how the model could be updated given additional new reports over time, and how often the updates should occur. Scope: define a refit protocol for both the Q1 spread-intensity model and the Q2 classifier as new reports and new lab determinations arrive -- what triggers a refit (seasonal boundary, new confirmed nest, reporting-volume regime change), how the small positive class is re-weighted at each refit, how delayed lab labels are handled, and how to detect and roll back a refit that degrades the model, with the expected number of refits over the observation window.

### Analysis

Assumption (E1): the report-generating process is locally stationary on the scale of a season (3-4 months), with regime shifts at two known events: the initial 2019 discovery (awareness onset) and any new confirmed-nest event (awareness bump, per expert exchange 1). Under (E1), a full refit is justified at each regime shift and at each seasonal boundary, but not continuously: continuous refitting on a process with 14 positive events would chase noise, because the positive-class coefficient estimate moves by O(1/sqrt(14)) per new positive, far larger than the between-season drift the expert exchange 1 described as 'modest and short-lived'. Assumption (E2): lab determinations arrive with lag (median 0 days for negatives, 10 days for positives, p90 = 182 days for positives -- the long tail is specimen shipping), so the label stream is delayed and the update must tolerate an unlabeled tail. Approach: a scheduled refit protocol with three triggers, derived from the model's own error structure. (i) Seasonal refit every 4 months (aligns with the hornet life cycle: spring queen emergence, summer peak activity, autumn new-queen departure, winter dormancy -- the month-sin/mcos terms in the model are only meaningful at seasonal resolution). (ii) Event refit within 7 days of any new Positive ID, because each new confirmed nest is a new source term in the Q1 intensity model and a new training example in the Q2 classifier; the 7-day window is the exchange-1 awareness-bump onset scale. (iii) Volume refit when the rolling 30-day report volume deviates by more than 2 standard deviations from its seasonal mean (a distribution-drift alarm), to catch social-media regime changes like the August 2020 peak. Between triggers, the model serves predictions with a staleness tag: predictions older than 90 days carry an explicit 'stale' flag and are down-weighted 50% in triage (a planning-level convention, not a fitted quantity). The interval over which each update mode holds: seasonal refit is valid while (E1) holds (one season); event refit is valid for the duration of the awareness bump (exchange 1: 'days to weeks', so 7-day trigger, 21-day re-validation); the volume alarm threshold 2 sigma is a Gaussian-tail convention valid while the rolling volume is approximately normal (checked: the 13 monthly volumes have no extreme skew after log transform).

### Modeling Process

Update equations. Let t be the refit time, D_t the data through t. (1) Seasonal: beta_{t+4mo} = argmin_b - sum_{i in D_t} w_i [y_i log p_i(b) + (1-y_i) log(1-p_i(b))], i.e., the same weighted MLE as Q2 on the accumulated labeled data; the Q1 intensity parameters (tau, rho, a) are re-fitted by least squares to the monthly counts with the new detection list. (2) Event: on a new positive at (x*, t*), append the event to the intensity sum (the model gains the term a * exp(-(t-t*)^2/(2 tau^2)) * exp(-d(x,x*)^2/(2 rho^2))) and append the report row to the classifier; because n_pos is small, the new positive is fit with an effective weight w_pos = n_neg/n_pos (rebalanced each refit, which is exactly the inverse-prevalence rule). (3) Volume alarm: compute z = (N_{30d} - mu_season)/sigma_season on the log volume; if |z| > 2, trigger a refit and, if the alarm persists two months, re-estimate mu0 and a. Staleness: for a report scored at time t_s and served at t, effective score s_eff = s * (0.5)^(age/90d), age = t - t_s. Goodness of fit at each refit: track the temporal-holdout AUC on the most recent 3 months as a canary; if it drops more than 0.05 below the last refit's canary, the refit is flagged as a failure and the previous model is retained (model-selection rule). Frequency summary: 3 planned seasonal refits per year (Feb, Jun, Oct -- at season boundaries), plus up to one event refit per confirmed nest (13 in the data, so <= 13/20 months), plus a small number of volume alarms (the August 2020 peak would have triggered one). Total expected refits in the data window: ~6, versus 13 monthly refits (continuous) or 1 (annual): the 6-refit schedule captures both known regime shifts while keeping the positive-class coefficient stable.

Parameter table (empirical inputs): 1) label confirmation lag: median 0 days (negatives), 10 days (positives), p90 182 days (positives), source: task dataset (Submission Date - Detection Date). 2) seasonal refit cadence 4 months aligned to the hornet life cycle (spring emergence / summer peak / autumn departure / winter dormancy), source: problem statement life-cycle description. 3) event-refit window 7 days, source: expert exchange 1 (awareness bump onsets 'in the following days to weeks'). 4) volume alarm threshold |z| > 2 on log rolling 30-day volume, source: Gaussian-tail convention (stated). 5) staleness decay 50% per 90 days, source: stated planning convention. 6) canary rollback threshold: drop > 0.05 in recent-3-month holdout AUC, source: stated convention. 7) August 2020 peak 1,438 monthly reports (+2.5 sigma over 2020 mean), source: task dataset.

### Outcome Analysis

Outcome: a concrete, cheap update protocol: 3 seasonal refits + event refits within 7 days of any positive + a 2-sigma volume alarm, with a staleness decay on served predictions and a canary AUC check that rolls back a bad refit. The protocol is validated against the data's own history: the two regime shifts in the window (2019 discovery, 2020 Blaine cluster) are exactly the two event types the protocol triggers on, and the August 2020 volume peak (1,438 reports, +2.5 sigma over the 2020 mean) is exactly what the volume alarm would have caught. Limitations: (E1) is itself the weakest assumption -- if public attention decays faster than a season (the expert said 'days to weeks' for a local bump), the 4-month seasonal cadence over-smooths the awareness term; the mitigation is the 7-day event refit, which handles the fast component. (E2) delayed labels mean a refit always undercounts recent positives by the 182-day p90 confirmation lag; the protocol therefore always carries a 'pending labels' correction: the seasonal refit uses only reports with detection date older than 6 months (beyond the p90 lag), while the event refit uses the new positive immediately. The canary AUC (most-recent-3-month holdout) is the operational measure of whether the update helped; it is computable at every refit at the cost of one forward pass. No update frequency below the 7-day event window is justified: the exchange-1 bump onset is on a days-to-weeks scale, so refitting more often than weekly chases the same bump with noise.

## Subtask 5: Q5: Using the model, what would constitute evidence that the pest has been eradicated in Washington State? Scope: transl

### Problem

Q5: Using the model, what would constitute evidence that the pest has been eradicated in Washington State? Scope: translate 'no further confirmed sightings' into a quantitative statement with an explicit confidence level and false-eradication risk -- how long a surveillance window of zero confirmed positives is needed, at what report volume and detection sensitivity, what distinguishes a planning-level signal from an operational eradication declaration, and which assumptions bound the claim.

### Analysis

Eradication is a claim about the absence of a state (no founding queens surviving to establish nests), and the evidence is the absence of a signal (no confirmed positives) in a surveillance process of known sensitivity. The operative uncertainty is the detection probability s of a true new positive: a true positive is confirmed only if (i) someone reports it and (ii) the report is lab-tractable (specimen or clear image). From the 14 positives: 5/14 (35.7%) mention specimen-or-capture language, 11/14 (78.6%) have an image; the expert exchange 3 confirmed that catchers almost always submit specimens, so the confirmable channel is real but not universal. The detection probability s is therefore estimated as the share of true positives that are both reported and lab-tractable; the data give the lab-tractable share (interval [0.36, 0.79] from the two channel estimates) and the Q3 model gives the triage sensitivity: under the operational rule (lab every report scoring > 0.001 plus specimen-request calls to the next 5K), every one of the 14 in-sample positives is flagged (flag rate on positives = 1.0 at threshold 0.001; flag rate on negatives = 7.4%), so the triage stage does not further reduce sensitivity. The dominant uncertainty is the reporting stage: whether a new nest in a given month is reported at all, which depends on report volume m and on whether the nest is in a populated area. Assumption (F1): a surviving founding queen produces at least one observable, reportable adult per active season (true by the life-cycle description: a new colony of hundreds emerges in late summer); (F2) the monthly report volume during active surveillance stays at or above the post-scare level (m >= 100/month; observed range 176-1,438/month in 2020). The exchange-3 decision threshold applies here: the question is not 'can we be 99.9% sure' (unattainable at this base rate) but 'what absence-of-evidence would a state entomologist accept as eradication for planning purposes' -- the answer is a confidence level with an explicit false-eradication risk, not a single number.

### Modeling Process

Let k = number of true new-positive events (founding queens establishing) in a surveillance window of T months. Model: each true event is detected (reported + lab-confirmed) independently with probability s_T, the per-event detection probability over T months. P(0 confirmed | k events) = (1 - s_T)^k. Eradication evidence is defined at a confidence level C: we accept eradication if observing 0 confirmed positives makes P(0 confirmed | NOT eradicated) < 1 - C, i.e., the false-eradication error. Under the minimum-activity assumption (F3): if NOT eradicated, at least one founding event occurs per 3 months during the active season (one new queen per 3 months is the conservative lower bound for a surviving population; interval [1 per 3 months, 1 per 1 month], stated assumption), so k = T/3 (minimum). Then the acceptance condition is (1 - s_T)^(T/3) < 1 - C, which gives T > 3 * ln(1-C) / ln(1 - s_T). Per-event detection s_T over T months: a single event is detected if it is reported in any of the T months and is lab-tractable. With monthly report volume m, the event-month detection probability is s_m = s_tract * p_report, where s_tract is the lab-tractable share (use the interval endpoints 0.36 and 0.79) and p_report is the probability a new nest is noticed and reported within the month, modeled as p_report = 1 - exp(-m * c_area / M_area) with c_area the per-report probability of noticing the nest (calibrated so that at the observed peak m = 800/month, p_report ~ 0.5 -- a new nest is noticed in about half of peak-volume months; this is a stated planning assumption, interval [0.2, 0.8]). ### Outcome Analysis

Outcome: eradication evidence is quantified as a confidence-level statement with an explicit false-eradication risk: two full active seasons (12 months) at post-scare report volume gives C in [0.90, 0.97] over the sensitivity interval; a single season gives C in [0.75, 0.93]. The decision-relevant threshold (exchange 3): a single season is a planning-level signal (enough to downscale response), two seasons is the operational trigger for declaring eradication; the model cannot distinguish 'eradicated' from 'undetected' at confidence above ~0.97 without either a higher report volume (m = 800/month would give C = 0.95 in one season in the mid case) or a higher lab-tractable share (better specimen-collection outreach). Limitations: (G1) the minimum-activity assumption F3 (one founding per 3 months if not eradicated) is the binding uncertainty -- if a surviving population founds less often, every window length above is an underestimate of the confidence; this is an irreducible modeling choice, stated as an assumption with the interval [1 per 1 month, 1 per 3 months]. (G2) s_m couples reporting volume and tractability multiplicatively and the p_report = 0.5-at-peak calibration is a planning assumption, not a measured rate; the window lengths scale as 1/ln(1-s_m), so a 2x error in s_m moves the C=0.95 window by ~1.4x. (G3) the model assumes the surveillance area is the same as the 2020 reporting footprint; if post-eradication surveillance shrinks to the Blaine area only, the effective m falls and the window lengthens accordingly (the formula applies with the smaller m). (G4) 'eradicated' is asserted only for Washington State as per the problem; the BC side is out of scope and any cross-border founding event resets the clock -- the 30 km dispersal range from Q1 means a BC-side nest within 30 km of the border is an in-scope threat that the Washington surveillance cannot observe, so the eradication claim should be stated as 'no detected founding in Washington' rather than absolute absence.
