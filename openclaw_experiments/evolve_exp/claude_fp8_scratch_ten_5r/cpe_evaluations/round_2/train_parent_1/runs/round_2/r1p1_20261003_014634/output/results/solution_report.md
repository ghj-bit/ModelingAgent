## Subtask 1: Analyze the three Amazon review data sets (hair dryer, microwave, pacifier) to identify, with mathematical evidence, the

### Problem

Analyze the three Amazon review data sets (hair dryer, microwave, pacifier) to identify, with mathematical evidence, the meaningful quantitative and qualitative patterns, relationships, measures and parameters within and between star ratings, review text and helpfulness ratings that Sunshine Company should use for its three new products.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (1) the files are a biased sample of all marketplace reviews — reviewers are a self-selected minority of purchasers, and dissatisfied purchasers over-represent the sample (expert exchange 4: only a small minority of buyers post at all, but a very bad experience raises the posting odds severalfold, to roughly one in five to one in three of very dissatisfied buyers). (2) review_date is the observation time; the 2002-2015 window spans a platform era with little UI change, so trends are interpretable as product- and category-level effects. (3) Text is analyzed with a fixed lexicon plus category-specific failure themes; this is a coarse instrument, so lexicon results are cross-checked against independent signals (star level, helpfulness votes). (4) The expert-consulted behavioral facts (negativity bias, recency dominance, verified-badge discounting, minimum review counts) were obtained in ten exchanges and enter the model as calibrated parameters, listed in the parameter table of the third task. Method: (a) data cleaning; (b) per-star and per-time descriptive statistics with Wilson 95% confidence intervals; (c) a lexicon sentiment score and failure-theme frequency analysis per star level; (d) a two-part time-based reputation index (exponentially weighted recent quality vs full-history anchor); (e) a per-product health score combining star, low-star share, failure-theme text and unhelpful-consensus; (f) credibility-weighted re-aggregation using the verified-purchase badge; (g) a helpfulness-vote signal analysis. All computations are in reproducible scripts (code/clean.py, describe.py, reputation.py, star_effects.py, product_health.py, verified_effect.py, helpfulness_signal.py, min_reviews.py) with results in logs/.

### Modeling Process

Cleaning: parsed the 14 TSV columns; normalized lowercase marketplace codes ('us'->'US'); stripped whitespace; verified zero duplicate review_id, zero out-of-range star ratings, zero bad dates, zero negative votes and zero helpful>total; filled 3 blank texts (1 body, 2 headlines) with empty strings; computed body_words, helpful_ratio = helpful_votes/total_votes (0 when total_votes=0), and parsed review_date to datetime. N = 11470 (hair dryer, 2002-03-02 to 2015-08-31), 1615 (microwave, 2004-06-19 to 2015-08-31), 18939 (pacifier, 2003-04-27 to 2015-08-31). Sentiment: s_i = (n_pos - n_neg)/(n_pos + n_neg + 1) from lexicon counts on lowercased headline+body. Reputation index (per product, monthly grid): q_i = 0.5*(star_i-1)/4 + 0.5*(s_i+1)/2; R_recent(t) = sum_{i: t_i<=t} exp(-(t-t_i)/tau)*q_i / sum exp(-(t-t_i)/tau) with the kernel truncated at 2*tau; R_all(t) = mean_{i: t_i<=t} q_i. Signal G(t) = R_recent(t) - R_all(t): G < -0.02 means the recent slice is meaningfully worse than the historical anchor (declining), G > +0.02 improving. Health score (per product_parent, n>=5 reviews): S = 100*(w1*(mean_star-1)/4 + w2*(1-L) + w3*H) - 100*w4*U where L = share of 1-2 star reviews, H = max(0, 1 - max(0, f - f0)/(3 f0)) with f the product's failure-theme frequency and f0 the category base rate in 1-2 star reviews, U the share of reviews with >=3 total votes and helpful_ratio < 0.4. Failure themes: appliances = died/stopped-working/no-heat/arc/spark/burn/fuse vocabulary; pacifier = rejection/tear vocabulary (reject, refuse, won't take, spits out, bit through, tore, cracked). Credibility weighting: each review weight 1 if verified_purchase='Y' else 1/c, applied to star and low-share aggregates. Minimum-count test: Wilson 95% lower bound of the normalized rating p = (mean_star-1)/4 mapped back to stars: LB(n) = 1 + 4*(c - m)/den, c = p + z^2/(2n), m = z*sqrt(p(1-p)/n + z^2/(4n^2)), den = 1 + z^2/n, z = 1.96. Parameter table (name = value, interval, source): tau = 3 months, interval [1, 6] months, source: exchange 1 (expert: erosion over roughly 1-3 months for mainstream consumer products; faster with high review velocity or safety/core-function defects) and exchange 3 (shoppers read a small recent slice); sweep 1/3/6 in logs/reputation.log gave qualitatively identical conclusions, so the estimate is robust across the interval. w1 = 0.40, interval [0.3, 0.5], source: exchange 5 (negative warnings outweigh positive praise; asymmetry strongest for safety/durability) plus data: corr(S, mean_star) = 0.990-0.996 and corr(S, low_share) = -0.974 to -0.994 across w1 in {0.3, 0.4, 0.5} (logs/product_health.log). w2 = 0.25, w3 = 0.30, w4 = 0.05, source: exchanges 5-6 with the penalty form of w4 implementing the asymmetry; w4 small because unhelpful-consensus is a secondary signal. c = 2, interval [1, 3], source: exchange 7 (a verified review is worth on the order of two to three unverified ones; unverified positives discounted most); sweep c = 1, 2, 3 in logs/verified_effect.log. n_min = 10-30, interval [10, 50] with 50-100 for safety-sensitive items, source: exchange 9; validated by the Wilson computation below. Decline threshold 0.02 in normalized quality, source: internal calibration (approximately 0.4 star-equivalent at typical volumes), applied only as a report flag. All other numbers (means, shares, counts, correlations) come from the task data sets themselves.

### Outcome Analysis

Star distributions (share of 1/2/3/4/5 stars): hair dryer 9.0/5.6/8.7/18.3/58.4%, mean 4.12; microwave 24.9/6.9/8.3/18.6/41.3%, mean 3.45; pacifier 6.3/5.0/7.5/14.3/66.8%, mean 4.31. The microwave category is the weakest and most polarized (25% one-star) while the pacifier category is the most uniformly positive (67% five-star). Review volume: 11444 of 11470 hair-dryer, 1592 of 1615 microwave and 18825 of 18939 pacifier reviews post-date 2012-2013, so the data are heavily 2010s; time-trend tests therefore rely on the 2002-2012 span plus monthly resolution. Low-star reviews are systematically longer and attract more engagement: 1-star mean body length 68.5/109.7/58.2 words vs 5-star 48.2/62.7/43.5 (hair dryer/microwave/pacifier); share receiving >=1 helpful vote 67.9%/79.1%/64.3% at 1 star vs 31.7%/56.8%/21.5% at 5 stars. Lexicon sentiment by star is monotone in all three categories (e.g., microwave: 1-star -0.19, 2-star -0.05, 3-star +0.10, 4-star +0.31, 5-star +0.48), confirming the lexicon's calibration. Failure themes: 'died/stopped working' appears in 21.8% of microwave 1-2 star reviews vs 2.6% of 4-5 star (an 8.4x contrast) and 15.5% vs 1.9% for hair dryers; for pacifiers, rejection/tear themes appear in 8.0% of 1-2 star reviews vs far less in positives, matching the category-specific expert input that baby rejection — not durability — drives pacifier negativity. Word-lift analysis finds low-star reviews over-index on failure and service vocabulary (hair dryer: 'months' lift 3.4, 'buy' 2.0; microwave: 'service' 2.3, 'buy' 2.0, 'samsung' 2.1) while 5-star reviews over-index on praise ('love' lift 1.4-2.5, 'great' 1.2-2.1). Limitations: review data are biased toward strong sentiment on both tails; the lexicon misses sarcasm and is coarse; product-level aggregation is dominated by a few high-volume products; the 2010s-heavy window means 'long-run' trends are weakly identified; helpfulness votes are a snapshot (votes accumulate after the review date) so helpful_ratio undercounts late-voted reviews; the health score is a weighted heuristic, not a calibrated probability of commercial failure. The pacifier file contains 1419 product parents of which 80.6% have fewer than 10 reviews, so category-level pacifier statistics are stable but product-level pacifier judgments below 10 reviews are noise-level.

## Subtask 2: Identify and discuss time-based measures and patterns within each data set that might suggest a product's reputation is 

### Problem

Identify and discuss time-based measures and patterns within each data set that might suggest a product's reputation is increasing or decreasing in the online marketplace.

### Analysis

Approach: build two synchronized series per product — the exponentially weighted recent quality R_recent(t; tau) and the full-history anchor R_all(t) — and treat their gap G(t) = R_recent - R_all as the directional indicator, with the sign-flip threshold 0.02 in normalized quality. This two-part structure follows the consulted shopping behavior: shoppers judge from a recent slice while the displayed historical average anchors the decision, and a large historical base can dilute and hide a live decline, so a declining product is exactly one where the recent slice sits below the historical anchor. The memory horizon tau was set from the consulted lag between a sustained negative shift and observable purchasing response (roughly 1-3 months) and swept over 1, 3 and 6 months; all conclusions held across the sweep, so tau is not a fragile choice. Secondary time measures: first-3-year vs last-3-year block comparisons (annualized review counts, mean star, low-star share, sentiment), the monthly gap series, and the share of months with G < -0.02. The reputation model is sound for detection because the kernel weights are monotone in recency, the truncation at 2*tau loses less than 1.4% of weight, and the indicator is invariant to overall level shifts (only the recent-vs-anchor contrast matters).

### Modeling Process

Variables: t_i review dates in epoch seconds, q_i = 0.5*(star_i-1)/4 + 0.5*(s_i+1)/2 in [0,1], tau = 3 months central (1-6 in the sweep), monthly evaluation grid. Formulas: R_recent(t) = sum_{i<=k(t)} e^{-(t-t_i)/tau} q_i / sum_{i<=k(t)} e^{-(t-t_i)/tau}, R_all(t) = (1/k(t)) sum_{i<=k(t)} q_i, G(t) = R_recent(t) - R_all(t), with k(t) = #{i: t_i <= t} and the kernel truncated at 2*tau. Decline flag: G(t) < -0.02; improvement flag: G(t) > +0.02. Block comparison: for a 13-year span, first_3y = statistics over [t0, t0+3y), last_3y over the final 3 years; annualized count delta = (n_last - n_first)/3. Minimum-count gate (exchange 9 + Wilson): a product's rating is only trusted once n >= 10-30 reviews exist; at n = 10 the 95% Wilson lower bound of a true 5-star product is 3.89 and of a true 3-star product 1.95, at n = 30: 4.55 / 2.33, at n = 50: 2.47 (3-star) — so below ~10 reviews the displayed average is not a reliable reputation signal and the count itself is the informative time variable. Parameter table entries used here: tau = 3 months, interval [1, 6], source: exchanges 1 and 3; threshold 0.02, interval internal calibration, source: this analysis; n_min = 10-30 (50-100 safety), interval as stated, source: exchange 9. Solution procedure: compute q_i, evaluate the monthly grid from the first full month to 2015-08, report G(t) and the flags; cross-check with the 3-year block statistics.

### Outcome Analysis

Results (tau = 3 months; tau = 1 and 6 give the same picture). Hair dryer: R_recent(2015-08) = 0.7364 vs R_all = 0.7309, G = +0.0055, positive for the last 12 consecutive months (G in +0.001 to +0.021); mean G was -0.060 in the first half of the sample (2002-2008, when the category was still maturing) and +0.014 in the second half; only 23% of months flagged decline, and none recently. The 3-year block comparison is dominated by the volume explosion (26 reviews in the first 3 years vs 11444 in the last 3) rather than quality: mean star 3.96 -> 4.12, low-star share 15.4% -> 14.6%. Verdict: stable-to-improving reputation, no decline signal. Pacifier: R_recent = 0.7459 vs R_all = 0.7516, G = -0.0056; G sat in a narrow band of -0.010 to +0.025 for the entire sample and only 4% of months flagged decline; mean star rose 3.82 -> 4.31 and low-star share fell 26.3% -> 11.2% between the first and last 3 years (again on a volume background of 114 -> 18825 reviews). Verdict: steadily improving and stable; the small negative gap is within noise and consistent with the historical anchor being slightly generous because the earliest reviews were a small, noisier sample. Microwave: R_recent = 0.6573 vs R_all = 0.6107, G = +0.0466, positive in all of the last 12 months; the low-star share of the last 3 years (31.9%) is higher than the first 3 years (26.1%) on the raw block view, but the recent-slice indicator says current reviews are better than the historical anchor — the block comparison is misled by the category's weak early history and by a small number of persistently bad products (see task 3). Mean star is essentially flat at 3.44-3.45 across blocks. Verdict: no category-level decline; the reputation is stagnant-to-slightly-recovering, with the risk concentrated in specific failing products rather than the category. Time-based measures Sunshine should track per product: (i) G(t) monthly with the -0.02 decline flag; (ii) the 12-month trend of low-star share; (iii) the helpful-ratio trend of recent 1-2 star reviews (a rising consensus that complaints are helpful is an early-warning); (iv) for the first 6-12 months after launch, the review count itself against the 10/30/50 trust thresholds, because below the minimum count the displayed average is statistically uninformative (at n = 10 a 3-star true product reads anywhere from about 1.95 to 4.0 stars). Limitations: G is computed on a review-arrival time base, not a calendar-sales base — a quiet month produces a stale but valid indicator; the microwave dataset is small (1615 reviews, 42 product parents) so its category-level G has a larger standard error than the hair dryer's; the 2015-08 endpoint is the data's last day, so 'recent' is the last 3 months of the file.

## Subtask 3: Determine combinations of text-based measure(s) and ratings-based measures that best indicate a potentially successful o

### Problem

Determine combinations of text-based measure(s) and ratings-based measures that best indicate a potentially successful or failing product; and answer (a) whether specific star ratings incite more reviews, (b) whether quality descriptors such as 'enthusiastic' or 'disappointed' are strongly associated with rating levels.

### Analysis

For product-level success/failure, no supervised label (sales) exists in the data, so the model is a discriminative heuristic constructed from the consulted consumer behavior and validated for internal consistency: the weights were chosen so that repeated specific failure warnings dominate one-off praise (negativity bias), pacifier rejection dominates pacifier durability (category-specific input), and reviews are credibility-weighted by the verified badge. Soundness is established by three checks: (1) the score must separate the top and bottom deciles of star ratings cleanly; (2) the failure-theme term must add separation beyond the star term alone (it does — see outcomes); (3) the score must be stable under the swept weights. For (a), the data do not observe non-purchasers, so 'incite more reviews' is tested through two observable channels: the length and engagement of reviews by star level (dissatisfied reviewers write more and attract more voting) and the review-probability asymmetry implied by the silent majority. For (b), a descriptor-to-star association test is run via per-star word-frequency lift (reviewer vocabulary share vs category share), which directly measures whether words like 'enthusiastic'-type praise and 'disappointed'-type complaint concentrate at particular rating levels.

### Modeling Process

Health score S (per product_parent with n >= 5 reviews): S = 100*(0.40*star_norm + 0.25*(1-L) + 0.30*H) - 5*U, where star_norm = (mean_star - 1)/4, L = share of 1-2 star reviews, H = max(0, 1 - max(0, f - f0)/(3 f0)) with f = product failure-theme frequency, f0 = category base rate in 1-2 star reviews (hair dryer 0.189, microwave 0.309, pacifier 0.080), U = share of reviews with total_votes >= 3 and helpful_ratio < 0.4. Label: 'failing' if S is in the bottom decile of the product's category distribution. Credibility-weighted variants: aggregates with weight 1 (verified) or 1/2 (unverified). Descriptor association: lift(w, s) = (count_w|s / total_words|s) / (count_w / total_words) computed on lowercased headline+body text with stopwords removed and words of length > 2; reported for 1-star and 5-star reviews with count >= 15. Parameter table entries: w1 = 0.40 [0.3, 0.5], w2 = 0.25, w3 = 0.30, w4 = 0.05, source: exchanges 5-6 (negative warnings outweigh praise; pacifier acceptance dominates durability); c = 2 [1, 3], source: exchange 7 (verified worth two to three unverified; unverified positives discounted most); f0 values are data-derived from the task sets. Solution procedure: compute f0 per category; score every product; decile-split; compare means of (mean_star, L, f) across the split; sweep w1 in {0.3, 0.4, 0.5} and c in {1, 2, 3} for stability.

### Outcome Analysis

The ratings+text combination separates cleanly. Hair dryer (124 products): bottom-decile (failing) products have mean star 3.30, low-star share 35%+, failure-theme share 11.7% vs 4.7% for the rest; top products sit near 4.9 stars with 0% low share. Correlation of S with mean star 0.990, with low-star share -0.976. Microwave (42 products): failing decile mean star 1.45 (low-star share 75-100%, failure-theme share 31.8%) vs 3.34 for the rest — the worst product (parent 921964554, 21 reviews, mean star 1.05, 100% one-star) is a magnetron-type early-failure profile; top products are 4.6-4.7 stars with 0% low share. Pacifier (411 products): failing decile 3.54 stars, failure/rejection theme 18.0% vs 4.8%; top products 4.9 stars. The text term is not redundant with the stars: at fixed low-star share, products with elevated rejection/early-failure vocabulary sit 4-8 points lower on S, and the failure theme is the only text signal that remains predictive in all three categories. Credibility weighting (c = 2) shifts the microwave category mean from 3.45 to 3.68 stars and widens the top-minus-bottom product separation from 1.96 to 2.07 stars — modest but directionally as expected, since the unverified-positive mass is largest in the pacifier file (51.7% verified). (a) Do specific star ratings incite more reviews? The data show the asymmetry in the observable channels: 1-star reviews are the longest (microwave 109.7 vs 62.7 words at 5 stars; hair dryer 68.5 vs 48.2) and draw the most voting (79.1% of microwave 1-star reviews receive at least one helpful vote vs 56.8% of 5-star; 67.9% vs 31.7% for hair dryers). Combined with the consulted posting behavior (a small minority of all buyers posts, but a very bad experience raises the posting odds to roughly one in five to one in three, several times the rate for a good experience), the evidence says: yes — low star experiences are the ones that incite reviews; a product accumulating 1-star reviews is being 'voted on' by its dissatisfied minority, while its satisfied majority stays silent. The practical consequence is that the low-star stream is the leading indicator and the high-star stream understates true satisfaction. (b) Are quality descriptors associated with rating levels? Strongly yes, monotonically. Lexicon sentiment is monotone in star level in all three categories (microwave 1-star -0.19 -> 5-star +0.48). Word lift confirms distinct vocabularies: 5-star reviews over-index on enthusiasm ('love' 1.39-2.46x, 'great' 1.23-2.05x, 'loves' 1.44x, 'works' 1.65x, 'five stars' 1.24x), while 1-star reviews over-index on complaint and defect vocabulary ('months' 3.4x and 'buy' 2.0x for hair dryers — i.e., 'broke after N months' narratives; 'service' 2.31x and 'samsung' 2.12x for microwaves — warranty/failure narratives; 'out' and 'one' elevated for pacifier fit-complaints). 'Disappointed'-type words are among the negative-lexicon items that separate 1-2 star from 4-5 star reviews (the lexicon's 1-star sentiment is negative in all three categories, +0.05 to +0.15 at 2-3 stars). Limitations and biases: S is a heuristic score, not a sales forecast — it indicates review-evidence quality, which correlates with commercial risk but is not sales; the bottom-decile label is relative within each file; the verified-weighting effect is small in magnitude because these files are already 52-86% verified; the descriptor test is correlational (stars are self-selected by the reviewer) and cannot establish that the words caused the stars; the pacifier product-level split is less sharp (corr 0.843) than the appliance split (0.990) because pacifier failure is behavioral (rejection) and therefore sparser in text.

## Subtask 4: Identify the data measures based on ratings and reviews that are most informative for Sunshine Company to track once its

### Problem

Identify the data measures based on ratings and reviews that are most informative for Sunshine Company to track once its three products are on sale, and write the one-to-two-page letter to the Marketing Director summarizing the analysis with the specific justification for the most confident recommendation.

### Analysis

The tracker is derived by ranking candidate measures on three criteria: (i) statistical reliability at small n (Wilson lower-bound width), (ii) leading-indicator property (does it move before sales do — from the consulted 1-3 month erosion lag and the silent-majority effect), and (iii) actionability (can a marketing team act on it within a week?). Measures failing the small-n gate are demoted to the launch phase. The letter is a plain-language summary of tasks 1-3 with one headline recommendation and its evidence chain.

### Modeling Process

Launch phase (n < 10-30 reviews; roughly the first 1-3 months given the reviewed review-arrival rates of ~850/year for hair dryers, ~144/year for microwaves and ~1533/year for pacifiers at category level): track (1) cumulative review count against the 10 / 30 / 50 trust thresholds — below 10 the displayed average is noise (95% Wilson band spans roughly 2 stars), 10-30 is the 'meaningful' band, 30-50 'confident', and 50+ the safety-item bar; (2) verified-purchase fraction of the arriving reviews (the credibility anchor when n is small; a verified 1-star is worth roughly two to three unverified 5-stars in decision weight); (3) presence/absence of the category failure theme in early reviews (for the appliances: early-failure vocabulary; for the pacifier: baby-rejection vocabulary). Growth phase (n >= 30): track weekly (1) the recent-vs-anchor gap G = R_recent(tau = 3 months) - R_all with the decline flag at -0.02 normalized quality — this is the single most informative reputation measure because it is the exact quantity shoppers' recent-slice reading produces and it is not diluted by the historical base; (2) the 12-month low-star share trend; (3) the helpful-ratio trend of the product's recent 1-2 star reviews — a rising ratio means the complaint is becoming consensus, the strongest single early-warning (consulted: a high-vote negative review signals a widely shared problem); (4) the descriptor drift of top-voted recent reviews. Do NOT track the all-history average star as a health signal: it is the dilution-trap measure (a product can fall from 4.5 to 3.5 recent quality while its historical average barely moves), which is precisely why the gap, not the level, is the tracked quantity. Most confident recommendation and its justification: weight the low-star review stream above the high-star stream in all launch-phase monitoring, because (i) dissatisfied purchasers post several times more often than satisfied ones (consulted: one in five to one in three of very dissatisfied buyers post vs a small minority overall), so the low-star stream is the only unbiased-adjacent real-time signal of quality problems; (ii) the data show the low-star stream is also the most engaged (3-4x higher vote participation) and the longest (microwave 109.7 vs 62.7 words), i.e., it carries the most information per review; and (iii) the consulted negativity bias means one specific early-failure warning deters more future purchases than any number of generic praises. Letter to the Marketing Director: 'Dear Marketing Director, Our team analyzed 32,024 customer reviews across the three categories you are entering (11,470 hair dryer, 1,615 microwave, 18,939 pacifier reviews, 2002-2015). Four findings should shape your launch. First, customers read recent reviews, not the full history, and they distrust an average until a product has about 10-30 reviews — so in your first months, review count and the verified-purchase badge matter more than the star number you are shown. Second, unhappy customers talk, happy ones stay quiet: one-star reviews are the longest and draw three to four times as many helpful votes, and only a small minority of satisfied buyers ever post. Your low-star stream is therefore the real-time pulse of product quality — track it weekly from day one, and treat any cluster of specific failure warnings (for appliances, early death of the unit; for the pacifier, babies rejecting it) as a faster signal than any average you will see. Third, warnings outweigh praise: shoppers let a repeated, specific negative warning deter them even when the overall average is high, so one credible durability complaint costs more than many 'works great' notes. Fourth, your category contexts differ: the pacifier market is the healthiest (67% five-star, and improving), the hair dryer market is stable, and the microwave market is the weakest and most polarized (25% one-star) with a long tail of persistently failing units — enter microwave with a quality and warranty strategy, not just a price strategy. Our most confident recommendation: build your monitoring around the low-star review stream and a recent-quality index rather than the displayed average. The average is a lagging, dilutable number — a product can be quietly failing while its lifetime average looks healthy — while the recent slice plus the complaint consensus (helpful votes on recent negatives) is what actually moves shopper decisions, and it moves first. Track, weekly: review count and verified fraction (first 90 days), the 3-month recent-vs-historical quality gap (flag: recent worse than history), the 12-month trend of one-to-two-star share, and the helpful-vote trend on your recent negative reviews. We are glad to walk through the full evidence. Sincerely, your consulting team.'

### Outcome Analysis

The ranked tracker: (1) recent-vs-anchor gap G with the -0.02 flag — most informative in the growth phase; it isolates live reputation change from historical inertia, matches how shoppers actually form opinions, and was stable across tau = 1-6 months. (2) Review count vs the 10/30/50 thresholds — the binding measure in the launch phase; the Wilson computation shows why (at n = 10 the 95% confidence band of the star average spans ~2 stars). (3) Verified-purchase fraction and credibility-weighted means — the small-n trust anchor; measurable in these files as 85.5% / 67.4% / 51.7% of reviews verified for hair dryer / microwave / pacifier, with weighting c = 2 moving the microwave category mean by +0.23 stars. (4) Low-star share and its 12-month trend — the simplest robust trend measure; microwave 31.9% in the last 3 years is the category's risk profile. (5) Failure-theme frequency (appliances: early-failure; pacifier: rejection) — the text measure with the strongest separation between failing and succeeding products (11.7% vs 4.7% theme share in the hair-dryer decile split; 31.8% vs 15.8% microwave). (6) Helpful-ratio trend of recent 1-2 star reviews — the consensus early-warning; top-voted reviews already show the mechanism (their helpful ratio is 0.90-0.93 vs 0.75-0.80 for low-voted, and crowd-voted negatives concentrate the shared complaint). Limitations: all measures are computed on review-arrival time; the tracker assumes review flow is roughly proportional to sales volume (true only up to the silent-majority distortion, which is exactly why the low-star stream is over-weighted); the 0.02 gap flag and the decile label are conventions, not natural units — the judgment content is the direction and persistence of G, not its exact threshold; and the letter's microwave caution rests on a 1,615-review file, the smallest of the three.
