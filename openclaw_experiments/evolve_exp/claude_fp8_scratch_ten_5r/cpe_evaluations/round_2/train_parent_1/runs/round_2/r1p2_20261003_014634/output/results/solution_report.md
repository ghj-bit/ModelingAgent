# Solution

## Subtask 1: Model the spread of synthetic opioids (fentanyl family) and heroin across the counties of the five states in the supplie

### Problem

Model the spread of synthetic opioids (fentanyl family) and heroin across the counties of the five states in the supplied NFLIS drug-identification data (2010-2017); identify the likely origin locations of each drug in each state; define drug-identification thresholds and predict where and when drug crises occur; associate reported use with U.S. Census ACS socio-economic data and modify the model accordingly; and test a counter-strategy with parameter bounds. Note: the problem text names OH/KY/WV/VA/TN, but the shipped data actually contains OH, KY, WV, VA and PA (no Tennessee), so the analysis is run on the five states actually present.

### Analysis

The data are NFLIS DrugReports counts by county, substance, and year (2010-2017) plus seven ACS DP02 socio-economic files. After cleaning (dedupe, '(X)'/'****' suppressed cells to NaN, county FIPS parsed from GEO.id2, state FIPS map {39:OH,21:KY,53:WV,51:VA,42:PA}) the panel has 3,480 county-year rows. Three empirical facts shape the modeling. (1) The five-state data show the classic displacement sequence: prescription pills declining, heroin peaking in OH in 2015 (23,347) then falling to 15,045 by 2017, and fentanyl exploding (OH x300 from 2010 to 2017: 56 to 16,728). (2) Fentanyl growth is hyper-exponential and still pre-saturation in 7 yearly points, so a free saturated Gompertz/Bass fit is not identifiable (the optimizer runs the carrying capacity to 0 or infinity); the identifiable structure is a compound-growth rate plus a reporting-system ceiling. (3) County-level fentanyl penetration (share of counties reporting) flattens at 0.94-0.97 in OH/PA by 2017 - the reporting system is at its county-coverage ceiling, which caps reported counts even as true use keeps growing. The ACS files contain no income/poverty/unemployment columns and the education columns (HC01_VC93/94) are suppressed ('(X)') for every county, so the socio-economic regression can only use household-structure and demographic covariates; also only ACS_10/11/12 (2005-2011 windows) carry data, so the covariates are effectively static. Each of these is handled explicitly below.

### Modeling Process

DATA CLEANING. NFLIS: no missing DrugReports; no duplicate (county,year,substance); DrugReports cast to int; FIPS_Combined kept as the 5-digit county code. Substance classes: HER=Heroin; FENT=all names containing 'fentanyl'; OPIOID_ALL=HER+FENT; shares = class/TotalDrugReportsCounty. ACS: skip the two header rows, parse Estimate columns HC01_*, map '(X)'/'****'/'(M)' to NaN, keep county rows for the five states. Repairs: missing HC01 columns in some ACS years filled with NaN; education columns dropped (all suppressed); derived rates FEM_HH_RATE=FEM_HH_NO_HUSB/POP*1000, ALONE_RATE=LIVING_ALONE/POP*1000, OLD_RATE=POP_65/POP*1000, LOG_POP=log10(POP).

ASSUMPTIONS. (A1) County-level NFLIS counts are a locally representative proxy for local drug-incident activity (county of the submitting agency ~= seizure location), used for relative spread, not absolute incidence. (A2) Reported counts are not true use; NFLIS covers ~88% of state/local forensic cases and year-to-year changes mix real trend with reporting artifacts, so share indicators and the stable 2013-2017 reporting regime are used. (A3) First appearance of a new drug is driven by supply connectivity + receptive market + detection artifact, not city size. (A4) Alert thresholds are state/county-relative, not a fixed national standard. (A5) A county is a lasting origin only if its signal persists and is geographically coherent (not a one-off artifact). (A6) County spread saturates as counties fill, while total burden keeps growing (two-layer structure).

PART 1 - GROWTH AND SPREAD. State level, per substance X: S_t = sum of X over counties. The growth model is a Bass diffusion pinned at the reporting-system ceiling. The Bass model dS/dt=(p+q S/K)(1-S/K) reduces to compound growth at rate g=p+q while S<<K and saturates at K as S->K. p, q, K are jointly non-identifiable in 7 pre-saturation points, so K is pinned to the data-driven reporting-system bound K = S_2017 / pen_2017 (pen_2017 = fraction of the state's counties reporting X in 2017) and g is estimated from the stable 2013-2017 regime as the CAGR g=(S_2017/S_2013)^(1/4)-1. The 2018-2024 path is the discrete Bass approach S_{t+1}=min(K, S_t + g(1-S_t/K)S_t), which grows at ~g while S<<K and flattens as S->K (a monotone, non-oscillatory step). A 95% prediction interval is built in log-space from the 2014-2017 fit residual, widening as sqrt(horizon). County spread is an SIR-like recurrence c_t=c_{t-1}+beta c_{t-1}(N-c_{t-1})/N - gamma c_{t-1}, N=counties in the state, fitted by multi-start Nelder-Mead (beta=diffusion, gamma=county exit below detection).

ORIGIN INFERENCE. A county is a candidate origin for X if, in the emergence window (HER 2010-2013, FENT 2013-2015): (i) first-appearance year within the window; (ii) persistence: reported in >=2 of the 3 years after first appearance (suppresses one-off lab artifacts); (iii) receptive market: the county's same-class (HER_SHARE) or pre-window OPIOID_SHARE (FENT) exceeds the state median in the window. Score =(persistence, share), ranked within state; city size is not a criterion.

THRESHOLDS AND WHERE/WHEN. Warning threshold T_warn = 90th percentile of the state's own 2010-2017 yearly series (state-relative, also reported as 2x the state median). Crisis threshold = 3-year growth (S_t/S_{t-3}-1)>50%. 'When' = first projected year crossing T_warn. 'Where' = counties predicted to cross the state-relative county-level T_warn, confirmed as a real signal by the exchange-10 filters: persistence (>=2 of the last 2 years), a >=1.5x break over the county's own baseline, and geographic coherence (>=2 elevated counties in the state, i.e. a shared supply corridor, not an isolated county).

PART 2 - SOCIO-ECONOMIC ASSOCIATION. County OPIOID_SHARE (a reporting-robust indicator) is regressed on standardized ACS covariates (FEM_HH_RATE, ALONE_RATE, OLD_RATE, LOG_POP, HH_SIZE), state-demeaned to control for the state reporting regime, with VIFs. ACS is matched to each NFLIS year by the nearest 5-year window that has data (falls back to ACS_10/11/12 because ACS_13-16 are all suppressed). The exchange-4 prior (joblessness > education > poverty) cannot be tested on the supplied data (no income/poverty/unemployment/education columns); the available distress proxy is household structure. The dominant covariate is reported and the poverty/unemployment ordering is recorded as a prior this data cannot confirm. The model is modified accordingly: the covariate that dominates (OLD_RATE, negative) is carried into the interpretation of which counties are most vulnerable, and the Part-1 growth rate g is read as varying with county distress through this association.

PART 3 - COUNTER-STRATEGY. The strategy (exchange 5) is sustained upstream supply disruption, reducing the spread coefficient by (1-eta_s), plus county treatment/naloxone capacity c (a recovery term r*c), where capacity must be BUILT from a low rural baseline c0~0.3 (exchange 8): c_t = c0+(c_target-c0)(t/t_build). The county-spread dynamics drive the projected state count S_t=S_2017*(pen_t/pen_2017). Success = the strategy's 2024 level below the no-intervention baseline by a 10% margin. Failure modes (exchange 5): disruption-only without capacity (drug reappears) and capacity-only without sustained disruption (spread continues). Parameter bounds are computed by sweep over (eta_s, c_target).

PROVENANCE TABLE (external/expert-sourced values; no other external data were fetched - the search.py tool was available but all empirical values below come from the supplied datasets or the expert exchanges). reporting_coverage = 0.88, interval [0.8, 0.9], source: exchange 2 reply (corroborated by the problem statement's 88% figure). alert_multiple = 2.0, interval [1.5, 2.0], source: exchange 7 reply (state-relative threshold; 90th percentile and 2x median both reported). signal_break = 1.5, interval [1.5, 2.0], source: exchange 10 reply (sustained break over the county's own baseline). baseline_capacity c0 = 0.3, interval [0.2, 0.5], source: exchange 8 reply (rural treatment/naloxone coverage is thin and uneven). emergence windows HER=2010-2013, FENT=2013-2015, source: exchange 3 + exchange 6 replies (displacement sequence). All fitted model parameters (g, K, beta, gamma, covariate betas, eta_s, c_target) are estimated from the supplied NFLIS/ACS data by the reproducible scripts in code/ (prepare.py, model1.py, model2.py, model3.py; constants exposed via CLI, sweep supported).

### Outcome Analysis

PART 1 - GROWTH (2018-2024 projection, state-level reported identifications). FENT is at or near the reporting ceiling and saturates: OH g=2.50, K=17,318 (pen_2017=0.97), S_2017=16,728 -> S_2024~17,318 (95% PI essentially at the ceiling); PA g=2.26, K=10,333, S_2017=9,716 -> ~10,333; KY g=2.20, K=4,055, S_2017=2,636 -> ~4,055; VA g=0.97, K=2,740, S_2017=2,008 -> ~2,740; WV g=2.16, K=780, S_2017=298 -> ~780. HER has passed its peak and decays: OH g=-0.048, S_2017=15,045 -> ~14,847; PA g=-0.026 -> ~13,178; KY g=-0.062 -> ~2,908; VA g=-0.031 -> ~3,704; WV g=-0.203, S_2017=751 -> ~264. This matches the exchange-6 prior (fentanyl fastest, heroin flat/declining as fentanyl displaces it). Because the reported-count ceiling is a bound on the reporting system, not on true use, the 2024 fentanyl figures are lower-bound readings: true use is not capped by county reporting coverage.

PART 1 - ORIGIN INFERENCES (leading candidates per state). FENT: KY - Harrison, Boone, Carroll, Owen; OH - Fayette, Logan, Jefferson, Seneca; PA - Armstrong, Somerset, Washington, Lycoming; VA - Suffolk City, Portsmouth City, Warren, Culpeper; WV - Harrison, Marion, Wood, Kanawha. HER: KY - Boone, Pendleton, Campbell, Bracken; OH - Mercer, Delaware, Logan, Shelby; PA - Lackawanna, Blair, Butler, Armstrong; VA - Hampton City, Suffolk City, Harrisonburg City; WV - Monongalia, Hancock, Harrison, Marshall. Caveat: small counties rank high on the share criterion (a few fentanyl identifications in a low-volume county is a large share), so these are candidate origins subject to a population floor, not confirmed sources; the persistence filter already removes one-off artifacts.

PART 1 - THRESHOLDS / WHERE / WHEN. All five states already crossed their fentanyl warning threshold by 2017 (T_warn reached in 2018 in the projection), and the 3-year>50% crisis condition is met from 2018 - the fentanyl crisis is already underway in every state. The 'where' prediction flags, per state, the counties that cross the state-relative county threshold and pass the signal-confirmation filters (e.g. OH: Hamilton, Cuyahoga, Montgomery, Franklin, Butler, Trumbull, Clermont, Clark; PA: Philadelphia, Allegheny, Delaware, Luzerne; KY: Jefferson, Fayette, Kenton, Campbell; VA: Fairfax, Henrico, Chesterfield, Prince William; WV: Wood, Cabell, Logan, Morgan, Hampshire). Heroin warning thresholds are NOT crossed in the projection (the series is declining), so no new heroin crises are predicted by 2024.

PART 2 - SOCIO-ECONOMIC ASSOCIATION. Pooled state-demeaned OLS on OPIOID_SHARE: R^2=0.073 (per-state R^2 0.01-0.16), VIFs 1.2-4.4 (mild collinearity among the distress covariates). The dominant covariate is OLD_RATE (population 65+ per 1000) with a negative coefficient (-0.017); FEM_HH_RATE is positive (+0.013). FENT_SHARE is weakly explained (R^2=0.016) while HER_SHARE is somewhat stronger (R^2=0.070). Interpretation: the supplied ACS covariates are weak predictors of reported opioid share - the household-structure/demographic proxies are not the economic-distress variables (poverty, unemployment, low education) that the expert prior says matter most, and those columns are not in the data. The model is modified by carrying OLD_RATE/FEM_HH_RATE as the best-available vulnerability indicators and stating explicitly that the exchange-4 ordering is untestable here; the fentanyl crisis is better characterized by the diffusion/growth dynamics (Part 1) than by these cross-sectional covariates.

PART 3 - COUNTER-STRATEGY (OH, representative; same pattern in KY). No-intervention baseline: penetration 0.97 (2017) with the spread near-saturated. Strategy grid (eta_s x c_target): every cell with sustained disruption >=0.3 and capacity target >=0.6 beats the baseline by >65%; the 10% success margin is met for eta_s>=0.1 with c_target>=0.4. Failure modes confirmed (exchange 5): (i) disruption-only (eta_s=0.5, no capacity build) FAILS - penetration rebounds to 0.199 in 2024 vs 0.001 for the paired strategy (the drug reappears without treatment capacity); (ii) capacity-only without sustained disruption is contained in OH because the spread is already near-saturated, but in a pre-saturation state (lower penetration) it would not contain the spread. Parameter bounds: minimum sustained disruption eta_s ~= 0.1-0.3 and minimum capacity target c_target ~= 0.4-0.6 are needed together; removing either lever fails.

MEMO TO THE DEA / NFLIS CHIEF ADMINISTRATOR. The fentanyl wave has already saturated county reporting in Ohio and Pennsylvania (94-97% of counties) and is approaching saturation in Kentucky and Virginia; the reported-count series will therefore flatten even as true use continues to grow, so a flat reported number should not be read as a flat epidemic. Heroin is in decline in every state as fentanyl displaces it, and no new heroin crises are projected. The most likely fentanyl origin clusters sit along the interstate/supply corridors of each state (e.g. the Louisville-Eastern Kentucky Harrison/Boone/Carroll corridor, the Columbus Dayton-area Fayette/Logan in Ohio, the Pittsburgh Armstrong/Somerset in Pennsylvania), subject to a population floor. Recommended alert rule: flag a county when its reported fentanyl count is >=1.5x its own baseline and sustained for two years, in a county that is part of a multi-county elevated corridor (not an isolated spike), using state-relative thresholds. Counter-strategy: pair sustained upstream supply disruption (cut the inter-county spread coefficient by at least ~30%) with built-out county treatment/naloxone capacity (target >=0.6 of counties covered); disruption without treatment lets the drug reappear, and treatment without sustained disruption lets the spread continue in states not yet saturated. Priority for intervention should be the still-rising states (KY, VA, WV) before they reach the reporting ceiling, because once saturated the reported data lose their early-warning value.

LIMITATIONS. (1) Reported counts are not true use (88% lab coverage; reporting artifacts), so all projections are on the reported-identification system. (2) The fentanyl 2024 point is a lower bound capped by the reporting ceiling. (3) The ACS socio-economic files lack income/poverty/unemployment and have suppressed education columns, and only 2005-2011 windows carry data, so the exchange-4 distress prior is untestable and the covariates are weak/static. (4) The small-county share criterion can over-rank low-volume counties as origins; a population floor should be applied operationally. (5) The cross-indicator corroboration of an alert (ED visits, naloxone, deaths) is not in the supplied data, so alerts are framed as reported-identification signals. (6) The data contain PA, not TN; results are for the five states actually present.
