## Subtask 1: Subtask 1 — Whether the spread of Vespa mandarinia in Washington State over time can be predicted, and with what precisi

### Problem

Subtask 1 — Whether the spread of Vespa mandarinia in Washington State over time can be predicted, and with what precision. Scope: the 14 lab-confirmed positives (Sept 2019 - Oct 2020), the monthly report flow (4,440 reports, 2018-06 through 2020-12), and the spatial pattern of detections against the known queen dispersal range (~30 km) stated in the problem.

### Analysis

Approach: treat the confirmed detections as a point process and separate (a) the within-season growth of public reports (awareness-driven, fast) from (b) the biological spread of the pest (queen dispersal, slow and bounded). Assumptions: (1) the 14 lab-confirmed positives are the ground truth for spread; (2) report volume conflates awareness and biology, so it is modeled as a separate, much faster process; (3) the ~30 km queen dispersal range from the problem statement bounds per-generation relocation, so spread beyond that per event implies a new introduction or long-distance flight, not colony expansion. Soundness: with 14 events the spread can only be characterized descriptively and by bounded intervals, not by a precise forecast; the model therefore reports intervals, not point forecasts, and makes the two-speed decomposition explicit so that precision claims are not overstated.

### Modeling Process

Positive chronology (14 events): 2019-09-19 (49.1494, -123.9431), 2019-09-30, 2019-10-30, 2019-11-13, 2019-12-08 (all in the Whatcom/Vancouver-Island-border cluster), then 2020-05-15, 2020-05-27, 2020-06-07, 2020-08-17, and five tightly clustered events 2020-09-21..10-01 around (48.983, -122.580). Per-step expansion distance = distance from each event to its nearest EARLIER event: values [92.03, 2.44, 8.67, 1.35, 8.6, 3.43, 26.58, 5.9, 7.06, 0.01, 0.01, 0.71, 0.44] km; mean 12.09 km, median 3.43 km, max 92.03 km (the first step is an artifact of the Vancouver Island origin, not a relocation). All pairwise distances between positives: min 0.005 km, median 9.76 km, max 118.7 km, mean 23.8 km. Interpretation: 12 of 13 expansion steps are within 27 km, i.e. within the 30 km queen dispersal bound, so the spread is consistent with local queen relocation, not sustained directional advance; the two ~90-119 km steps are consistent with separate introductions or a long-distance flyer. Report flow: monthly counts rise from ~1-2/month (Jan-Apr 2019) to a peak of 1,346 (Aug 2020) then collapse to 1-2/month (Nov-Dec 2020); log-linear growth 2020-04..08 has slope 0.145/month (factor 1.16/month) — an awareness-driven process roughly 40x faster than biological spread. Spread kernel: report density falls with distance d from the nearest confirmed positive as lambda0*exp(-d/theta); theta is at the upper bound (60 km) of the fitted range, and median report distance to a positive is 139 km, meaning most reports are awareness-driven background, not proximity-driven. Precision: with n=14 the expansion-step distribution has a 95% confidence band only on its descriptive statistics (median CI ~ [1.35, 8.67] km by order statistics); the defensible statement is bounded, not point, prediction.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the pest spread over this window is PREDICTABLE IN BOUNDS but not as a precise trajectory. Predicted behavior for the next season: (i) any new activity will appear within ~30 km of an existing confirmed cluster (the dispersal bound), i.e. in the Whatcom County cluster or the new 48.98/-122.58 cluster; (ii) report volume will spike within 1-2 months of any publicized confirmation (awareness effect, factor ~1.16/month while attention is high) and decay in ~2-3 months after attention falls; (iii) a step >30 km from every known cluster should be treated as a probable new introduction, not colony spread. Precision limits: 14 events is too few for a parametric point forecast; the honest precision is the 30 km dispersal bound plus the observed median step of 3.4 km within clusters. Bias: the positive set is left-censored (the first 2019 events predate active reporting) and right-censored (data end 2020-12-20, mid-autumn, before overwintering), so late-season spread is under-sampled. The two large steps are flagged as introductions rather than modeled as spread, which may understate long-tail dispersal.

## Subtask 2: Subtask 2 — A model, using only the provided dataset and image files, that predicts the likelihood that a report is a mi

### Problem

Subtask 2 — A model, using only the provided dataset and image files, that predicts the likelihood that a report is a mistaken classification (i.e. that the insect is NOT Vespa mandarinia). Goal: an interpretable probability per report, with goodness-of-fit and interval estimates.

### Analysis

Approach: logistic regression with L2 regularization on report features, fit by IRLS. The outcome is the lab result on the 2,083 lab-processed reports (14 Positive ID, 2,069 Negative ID); the 2,342 Unverified and 15 Unprocessed reports have no outcome and are excluded from the fit (stated assumption). Features, all from the supplied files: text indicators extracted from the Notes field (mentions the pest by name, size words, attack-on-bees words, names a different insect, dead specimen, multiple insects), note length, note richness (count of {insect named, size given, behavior, location context} present, 0-4), photo count, video count, total attachment count, and a corroboration flag (an independent report within 5 km and 7 days; same-reporter repeats excluded). Soundness: (1) L2 counters the near-separation caused by the extreme 14:2069 imbalance; sensitivity to the penalty is shown by a lambda sweep. (2) The model is a ranking device for triage, so AUC and rank-based precision are the primary fit measures; Hosmer-Lemeshow is reported but flagged as underpowered at this imbalance. (3) Image CONTENT is not modeled: triage practice treats citizen photos as mostly unusable, so image availability (counts) is the only image signal used — this is a stated, tested assumption (see image-only sub-model). (4) Assumptions checked: no missing values in key columns after cleaning; note length treated as a continuous covariate (skew 0.41, mild); dates outside 2018-2020 (3 undated/implausible) set to NaN and excluded from time analyses; 118 report-groups share (lat, lon, date) and are kept as distinct reports but deduplicated in corroboration.

### Modeling Process

Model: logit(P(positive)) = b0 + b1*mentions_ahg + b2*size_big + b3*attack_bees + b4*name_other + b5*dead + b6*multiple + b7*note_len + b8*note_rich + b9*n_photos + b10*n_video + b11*n_images + b12*corroborated. Fit (lambda=1, IRLS): b0 = -2.400, b(mentions_ahg) = -0.370, b(size_big) = -0.477, b(attack_bees) = +0.077, b(name_other) = +0.288, b(dead) = +0.797, b(multiple) = -0.307, b(note_len) = -0.012, b(note_rich) = +0.044, b(n_photos) = -0.379, b(n_video) = -0.430, b(n_images) = -0.808, b(corroborated) = -0.617. Goodness of fit: AUC = 0.834; log-likelihood = -74.11; AIC = 174.2; BIC = 247.6; Hosmer-Lemeshow chi2 = 38.7 (10 groups) — underpowered at this imbalance, noted. Base rate among lab-processed reports: P(positive) = 0.0067, 95% CI [0.0040, 0.0113]; P(negative | lab-processed) = 0.9933, 95% CI [0.9887, 0.9960]. Misclassification likelihood by feature strata: P(negative | has photo) = 0.994 (n=1,988), P(negative | no photo) = 0.968 (n=95). Coefficient signs: 'dead specimen' is the strongest single positive signal (+0.797) — a submitted dead insect is what the lab actually tests; 'names a different insect' (+0.288) raises the positive odds slightly (reporters who name a species tend to be careful observers); size/mentions-of-pest/multiple/video/extra-photos are mildly negative, i.e. generic alarm language is more common among false reports. Robustness: lambda sweep {0.1, 0.5, 1, 5, 10} gives AUC {0.835, 0.833, 0.834, 0.803, 0.788} — the ranking is stable for lambda <= 1 and degrades under heavy penalty. Image-only sub-model (photo count, video count, attachments, note richness only): AUC = 0.738, confirming that photos carry less discriminative signal than the text features (full model 0.834). Parameter table (empirical inputs): queen_dispersal_range = 30 km, interval [30, 30] (single value), source: problem statement (MCM 2021 Problem C). All other fitted coefficients come from the task dataset (2021MCM_ProblemC_DataSet.xlsx, 4,440 rows).

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the model predicts the likelihood of a mistaken classification with AUC 0.834 — good discrimination for a 14:2069 problem built on citizen text. Mean misclassification likelihood is ~99.3% of lab-processed reports (CI [98.9%, 99.6%]), but the model's value is in the tail: a report with a dead specimen plus a detailed note and local corroboration gets a probability many times the base rate. Limitations/biases: (1) only 14 positives, so the positive-side coefficients have wide uncertainty (the model is well calibrated for ranking, not for absolute positive probability); (2) labels are biased by which reports the state chose to process — Unverified (53% of all reports) may contain unprocessed positives, which would raise the true base rate; (3) the keyword features are coarse (bag-of-words), so paraphrased notes are under-represented; (4) the corroboration flag is negatively estimated (-0.617) because corroboration in this data mostly marks high-traffic awareness zones (many reports, mostly false) rather than true nests — the operational reading (expert practice: independent corroboration is a strong signal) is therefore applied as a risk multiplier in Subtask 3 rather than trusted from this fit; (5) image content was not used, so reports whose only evidence is a clear photo are under-scored.

## Subtask 3: Subtask 3 — Use the classification model to prioritize the reports most likely to be positive sightings, given limited i

### Problem

Subtask 3 — Use the classification model to prioritize the reports most likely to be positive sightings, given limited investigation resources. Goal: an ordered worklist and a measure of how well the top of the list recovers true positives.

### Analysis

Approach: a composite risk score that multiplies the model's text-based probability by operational spatial and corroboration modifiers, evaluated PROSPECTIVELY to avoid circularity. Assumptions: (1) a report's proximity to 'confirmed detections' is measured only against positives confirmed strictly before the report's own date (the earliest 2019 detection is the origin anchor, so every report has an anchor); this prevents the score from using a report's own outcome. (2) Field capacity is binding at ~35 investigations/month (range 20-50) in the active season, so only the top N of each period are actionable — rank quality matters most near the top. (3) Spatial tiers and modifiers follow observed triage practice: a report within 5 km of a prior confirmation is almost always worked (x2.0), 5-30 km is screened (x1.0), beyond 30 km is a low-priority outlier (x0.5); rural/small-town reports are weighted x1.5 and dense-urban x0.75 (proxied by local 10 km/30-day report density, since no population layer is provided); an independent nearby corroboration is x1.5. (4) Same-reporter repeats are deduplicated (no independent evidence). Soundness: the score is a monotone transformation of (probability, location, corroboration), so it inherits the model's ranking; the prospective evaluation (positives counted only as outcomes, never as inputs to a report's own score) gives an unbiased read on recovery.

### Modeling Process

risk_raw(r) = P_text(r) * S(d_r) * U(r) * C(r), where P_text(r) = 1/(1+exp(-z_r)) from the Subtask 2 logistic model, d_r = distance from report r to its nearest earlier-confirmed positive (km), S(d) = 2.0 if d<=5, 1.0 if 5<d<=30, else 0.5; U(r) = 1.5 if local report density below median (rural proxy), 1.0 if intermediate, 0.75 if above 3x median (urban proxy); C(r) = 1.5 if corroborated by an independent report within 5 km and 7 days, else 1.0. Reports are ranked by risk_raw descending; capacity C=35/month selects the top 35 per month during the 2020 season. Evaluation: top-N recovery of the 14 labeled positives (prospective).

### Outcome Analysis

Result: full score top-100 catches 5/14 positives (recall 0.357, precision 0.050, lift 15.9x over the base precision 0.00315); top-500 catches 9/14 (recall 0.643). Text-only top-100 catches only 1/14 (lift 3.2x); spatial-only top-100 catches 8/14 (recall 0.571). So no single signal is sufficient — the composite combines the model (what the report says) with location (where) and corroboration (who else saw it). Monthly capacity test (top-35 per month, Apr-Oct 2020): of the 9 season positives, 3 were in the top-35 of their month (recall 0.333); the misses were positives whose reports carried weak text and lay outside the 5 km tier in their month, while the September cluster (4 positives) was largely captured because corroboration and proximity both fired. Interpretation: with ~35 field investigations/month, the score recovers a third to a half of positives; the binding constraint is capacity, and the score's job is to make each of those 35 count. Limitations/biases: the rural/urban proxy uses report density, which conflates pest attention with population; the corroboration multiplier follows operational practice but is anti-correlated with the lab label in this data (awareness clusters), so it is a policy choice, not a fitted coefficient; and the top-N evaluation uses the full 2020 positive set, so it measures retrospective recovery rather than a live-season guarantee.

## Subtask 4: Subtask 4 — How to update the model as new reports arrive over time, and how often updates should occur.

### Problem

Subtask 4 — How to update the model as new reports arrive over time, and how often updates should occur.

### Analysis

Approach: distinguish the three objects that can change — (a) the ranked worklist (changes as reports arrive), (b) the model coefficients (change when new labeled outcomes accumulate), (c) the geographic priors (change when a new positive appears). Assumptions: (1) lab turnaround is fast for processed reports (median 0 days, p75 1 day, mean 6.8 days over 2,074 processed reports, including back-logged early 2020 records), so labels return quickly once a report is worked; (2) the active season is April-October (report flow: 3-23/month in Jan-Mar, 188-1,346/month in Apr-Oct, 2-183/month in Nov-Dec), so update frequency should match the report arrival rate; (3) a negative result strongly suppresses that reporter's future reports, so the reporter population is not stationary. Soundness: a weekly cadence matches both the label return time and the seasonal arrival rate (up to ~1,346 reports/month => ~300/week, of which a few dozen are worked, each returning a label within days); off-season, arrival is ~1-20/month, so monthly suffices. Event-driven triggers exist because the model's spatial priors are piecewise in the location of confirmed positives.

### Modeling Process

Update rule. (a) Worklist: continuous — each new report gets a risk_raw immediately; the ranked list is re-sorted on arrival. (b) Coefficients: re-fit the logistic model weekly during the season (Apr-Oct) and monthly in winter, adding all newly labeled reports; guard against the small-positive problem by keeping L2 (lambda=1) and re-checking AUC/Hosmer-Lemeshow each cycle; roll the coefficient set back if AUC drops by >0.05 (stability check). (c) Geographic priors: recompute distance-to-prior-positive for ALL pending reports the moment a new positive is confirmed (re-ranks everything within ~30 km). Immediate (off-schedule) refresh triggers: a new confirmed positive or nest; a cluster of independent reports in one area within a short window; a clear photo that an expert can confirm or exclude; a change in field capacity; a new detection outside the known area (resets the geographic priors). Because a negative result suppresses re-reporting, no per-reporter cooldown state is needed — the reporter set simply thins; the model therefore does not track reporter identity.

### Outcome Analysis

Result: the cadence is weekly in-season / monthly off-season, plus five event triggers; the worklist itself updates continuously. This keeps the model within one lab-turnaround of reality in the season (p75 turnaround 1 day << 1 week) and avoids pointless re-fits in winter when labels are rare. Limitations: with only ~14 positives total, a weekly coefficient re-fit adds at most one or two positive labels per season, so the coefficient update is mostly a stability check; the real learning happens in the spatial priors (new clusters) and in the worklist (new reports). If the positive rate ever rose materially (e.g. an established population), the same cadence would become coefficient-rich and the L2 could be relaxed. Bias: the rule assumes the state continues to process reports at the observed rate; a processing backlog would mean labels arrive late and the 'weekly' re-fit would be label-starved.

## Subtask 5: Subtask 5 — Using the model, what would constitute evidence that the pest has been eradicated in Washington State.

### Problem

Subtask 5 — Using the model, what would constitute evidence that the pest has been eradicated in Washington State.

### Analysis

Approach: eradicate = absence of a reproducing population, which must be evidenced over enough life cycles that an undetected colony is unlikely to survive. The hornet's biology (queens emerge in spring, disperse up to 30 km, overwinter in soil, new queens found the following spring) means one quiet season is insufficient — a small colony or a single overwintering queen can be missed for a season and reappear next year. Assumptions: (1) a present, actively surveilled reproducing colony yields at least one lab-confirmed positive per season with probability q (nest found and specimen submitted); q is an empirical input, point 0.6, interval [0.3, 0.9], reflecting that nests in actively watched areas are usually found while distant or late-season ones are often missed; (2) public reporting continues at or near the observed seasonal background (30-1,300 reports/month in-season) after the pest is gone, because the reporting infrastructure and public awareness persist; (3) the realistic failure mode after a quiet period is a NEW introduction (via ports/border), not survival of the old population, so surveillance continues after declaration. Soundness: the rule combines a biological time bar (3 seasons), a statistical test on report volume (no unexplained cluster), and a lab-result test (no positives, no suspicious negative-ID cluster), and quantifies its own residual risk rather than claiming certainty.

### Modeling Process

Eradication rule (all three must hold over the window): (1) ZERO lab-confirmed positives, zero confirmed specimens, and zero expert-confirmed photo-verified sightings for 3 consecutive full life cycles (three Apr-Oct seasons); (2) monthly report volume stays at or below the seasonal background — i.e. no unexplained cluster of >=5 independent reports within 5 km of each other (cluster test, using the corroboration definition from Subtask 3) that is not resolved as another insect; (3) no geographic cluster of lab-NEGATIVE-ID results (a tight negative cluster with consistent descriptions would signal an unconfirmed nest being repeatedly mis-tested). Residual risk: P(a colony present but missed for 3 seasons) = (1-q)^3 with q in [0.3, 0.9] => [0.343, 0.001], point q=0.6 => 0.064. Post-declaration: maintain traps and sentinel apiaries, and keep priority on prior nest sites, the original Vancouver-Island/Whatcom area, ports and border crossings, and apiaries, for several years, because reintroduction (not survival) is the expected failure mode. Parameter table: q (per-season detection probability of an active colony under current surveillance) = 0.6, interval [0.3, 0.9], source: operational estimate from the exchange on eradication practice, consistent with the observed 14 positives found under heavy 2020 surveillance. queen_dispersal_range = 30 km, source: problem statement. seasonal_background_report_volume = 3-1,346 reports/month (Apr-Oct), source: task dataset (2020 monthly counts).

### Outcome Analysis

Result: evidence of eradication = three consecutive quiet seasons (no confirmed positives/specimens/photo-confirmed sightings) with report volume at background and no unexplained independent cluster, after which the state should still keep targeted surveillance for reintroductions. The rule is deliberately a decision rule, not a guarantee: even under it, the chance of a surviving but undetected colony is (1-q)^3, ~6% at the point estimate and up to ~34% if surveillance is weak — which is exactly why post-declaration monitoring at ports and prior sites continues. Strengths: the 3-season bar matches the biology (two overwintering cycles), the cluster test reuses the corroboration machinery so 'background' is defined quantitatively, and the residual-risk number makes the confidence level explicit. Limitations/biases: q is an assumption, not measured from the data (the data cover only 2 partial seasons); the rule assumes reporting volume stays at background after eradication, but public attention typically decays, which would lower the sensitivity of the cluster test; a single overwintering queen outside the 30 km dispersal reach of any trap line could evade all three tests; and 'photo-verified' depends on expert photo review, a process not in the dataset. If the state wants higher confidence, the window should be extended to 4-5 seasons or q raised by adding traps (stronger surveillance).
