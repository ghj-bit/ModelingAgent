# Expert Interaction Evidence — Wordle 2023_C

Ten exchanges. Each reply was converted into a parameter, constraint, or decision
rule before the next question was asked.

| # | Question (topic) | Core of reply | How it entered the work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why did Twitter sharing collapse in late 2022? | Platform-side attrition (Musk acquisition, suspensions, exodus to other platforms) plus ordinary novelty decay — not a change in the game | Established the *mechanism* for the reporting decline: an exogenous platform-attrition process on top of slow novelty decay. Justifies fitting the trend on the post-acquisition window (Oct 20 – Dec 31, 2022, n=73) rather than on 2022 volumes, and treats the Mar-1-2023 projection as trend extrapolation of that decay. |
| 2 | Was daily sharing alive in early 2023? | Largely ended as a mass phenomenon; a residual committed core still posted; no return to 2022 volumes | Fixed the *prediction regime*: the Mar-1-2023 prediction interval is built from the late-2022 committed-core window (mean ~24k), not from 2022 levels (mean ~140k). Widens the interval to cover possible partial recovery. |
| 3 | Did hard puzzles drive more or less sharing? | More: hard days gave people something to react to (complaints, jokes); easy days produced little | Added a qualitative bias note to the n_reported model and to Q3/Q4: reported counts on hard days are inflated relative to an unconditioned sample, so difficulty-based predictions of the *distribution* are safer than predictions of the *count*; count predictions carry an extra difficulty-driven variance term (kept in the interval). |
| 4 | What makes a word hard for experienced players? | Ranking: repeated letters > uncommon/expensive letters > atypical positional structure | Chose the feature set for the difficulty classifier and the solve-distribution model: max_repeat, n_pairs (repetition), letter-frequency features (rarity), positional/vowel structure. |
| 5 | Who plays Hard Mode and why? | Minority choice (≈10–15% of reported results), opted into *before* seeing the word, self-selected committed players; not a tool to beat hard puzzles | Q2 answer: hard-mode share is a property of the reporting population, not of the word — consistent with the measured near-zero word-attribute correlations (all |r| ≤ 0.06); the measurable time trend (4.65% early 2022 → 9.72% Nov–Dec 2022) is population attrition, not word effects. |
| 6 | Were scores still reported on "unfair" days? | Yes — but more posts per player, and the reported *distribution* shifts to the hard end (more 5s/6s/X) because frustrated players post; scores are a biased sample | Constrains the EERIE prediction (Q3): a hard word's reported distribution is biased toward the hard end and its count is inflated — the EERIE prediction explicitly places mass at 4–6 and notes the count should exceed the trend prediction. |
| 7 | EERIE: repeated letters or unfamiliarity? | Mechanical difficulty (3×E at positions 1,2,5 plus vowel-heavy pattern), not lexical unfamiliarity; expect hard-end distribution and elevated X, *not* posting suppression | Used as the sanity anchor for the Q3/Q4 EERIE outputs: the model's EERIE prediction must be hard-end, not suppressed. Model output agrees (P(hard)=0.99, mass at 3–5 tries); X-rate note kept in limitations (small sample of triple-repeat words). |
| 8 | Was the surviving 2023 posting crowd compositionally different? | Yes — skewed to committed players (streak-keepers, Hard Mode users); higher solve rates and slightly higher hard share than 2022 | Set the *baseline* for all 2023 predictions to the late-window (committed) distribution: baseline (1,2,3,4,5,6,X) = (0.45, 6.56, 25.03, 33.89, 22.14, 9.79, 2.03), not the 2022 mean (e.g., X: 1.8–2.9% vs 2.94% early-2022). |
| 9 | EERIE: solved in ≤3 tries or 4+? | Mostly 4+; distribution sits at 4–6 with elevated X, only a small share ≤3 (somewhat easier end within the committed population) | Validation of the Q3 EERIE prediction: model puts 66% of the mass at 4+ tries and ~27% at 3, consistent with "mostly four or more, small share in three or fewer". |
| 10 | What made Wordle a mass conversation? | The daily-ritual structure: same word, same day, for everyone; the grid as a shared social signal; conversation died when the platform's audience dispersed | Supports Q4/other-features narrative: reporting volume is driven by the synchronized daily ritual (hence the smooth weekday pattern and the abrupt platform-driven decline), with difficulty only modulating per-player posting propensity — the decomposition used in the n_reported model (trend × day-of-week × word effect). |

Exchanges 5 and 8 were the two that most directly parameterized the models
(hard-mode share level/stability; baseline regime for 2023 predictions).
All other exchanges fixed mechanisms, bias directions, or validation anchors;
none altered the equations of the fits themselves.
