# Interaction Evidence

Exchange 1 (structural): Asked which vital rate degrades first when a patch shrinks or shades over.
Reply: juvenile survivorship Sj is most sensitive (hatchlings need open, sun-exposed sand); mate-finding fails last; adult food limitation secondary.
Use: Task 3 makes the sandy-fraction dependence of Sj the steepest (largest beta coefficient); Task 1/Task 6 focus on preserving open sand. Interval: Florida upland scrub patches, shade-encroached or shrinking.

Exchange 2 (parameter): Asked the fire return interval to keep scrub open.
Reply: 5–15 years, typical 8–12; xeric inland sites (Lake Wales Ridge, Avon Park) at longer end, ~12 years; >15–20 years shade closes.
Use: Task 6 sets burn return interval I = 12 yr (range [8,15]) for the Avon Park landscape; policy targets fraction of landscape burned per year = 1/I ≈ 8.3%.

Exchange 3 (parameter): Asked typical migration distance for juveniles.
Reply: bulk of successful migration within 100–300 m, declining rapidly beyond; 350 m survey radius matches data.
Use: Task 4 keeps the empirical migration kernel on [0,350] m; Task 5 restricts dispersal to pairs of patches closer than ~350 m (effective dispersal radius, consistent with expert judgment and histogram support).

Exchange 4 (parameter): Asked minimum viable size for a patch population.
Reply: ~200–500 breeding adults as MVP target (500 common); below ~50–100 adults non-viable; low hundreds marginal, need immigration.
Use: Task 5 viability thresholds: viable if local equilibrium adults A* >= 200; marginal (needs immigration) 50 <= A* < 200; not viable A* < 50 (or intrinsic growth r<0 even before thresholds).

Exchange 5 (structural): Asked whether burns are one big fire or spread over years.
Reply: rotating mosaic; burn a fraction of units each year, staggered, so the landscape always mixes recently burned open-sand patches with older shaded ones.
Use: Task 6 policy: staggered burn schedule, 1/I of the total sandy habitat burned per year across units, no whole-landscape simultaneous burn; keeps patch-to-patch variation that the lizards need.

Exchange 6 (parameter/edge): Asked effect of burning on juvenile first-summer survival.
Reply: Sj improves, often substantially, in years 1–3 post-burn; peak years 1–2; very hot complete burn can depress it temporarily.
Use: Task 6 dynamics: Sj gets a temporary multiplicative boost (peak +15%, decaying over ~3 yr) on burned units; moderate, patchy severity assumed. Interval: post-burn years 1–3.

Exchange 7 (structural/Task 1): Asked real-world habitat-loss causes.
Reply: fire suppression/vegetation succession first; then conversion (urbanization, agriculture, phosphate mining), fragmentation, hydrology/off-road/invasive plants (cogongrass, Brazilian pepper).
Use: Task 1 factors list, ranked; informs the 6%/yr vegetation-density growth used in Task 6.

Exchange 8 (Task 1/5): Asked management response for a patch too small or too shady.
Reply: restore habitat (fire/mechanical clearing) rather than prop up population; if still not viable, treat as network element sustained by immigration or accept its loss and concentrate on larger patches; translocation only as last resort.
Use: Task 5 classification: patches with r>0 but A*<200 are "network/marginal" (sustainable via immigration); r<0 or A*<50 are unsuitable/accept-loss candidates; recommendations follow.

Exchange 9 (Task 1): Asked obstacles on a military training range.
Reply: primary mission priority ("compatible use" constraint); fire prohibited/restricted near munitions and impact areas so suppression continues; access windows limit crews; fragmentation by roads/tank trails/berms; funding/staffing.
Use: Task 1 obstacles section; Task 6 note that burn scheduling must respect no-fire zones, pushing some units toward mechanical clearing or longer local intervals.

Exchange 10 (parameter/edge): Asked how long after a burn until hatchlings can use it.
Reply: usable within ~1 yr; best years 1–3; degrades thereafter; poor again by ~10–15 yr without fire.
Use: Task 6 burn-recovery function: benefit term zero at t=0, rises to peak by year 1–2, decays to zero by ~12 yr, matching the burn interval so the schedule never lets a unit pass its peak into decline.
