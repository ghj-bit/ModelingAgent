## Subtask 1: Subtask 1 — Data cleaning and characterization of the four road corridors (I-5, I-90, I-405, SR 520) in Thurston, Pierce

### Problem

Subtask 1 — Data cleaning and characterization of the four road corridors (I-5, I-90, I-405, SR 520) in Thurston, Pierce, King, and Snohomish counties, using the supplied per-segment dataset (2017_MCM_Problem_C_Data.csv, 224 segments). Goal: produce a clean, route-level view of lane counts, lengths, and traffic volumes on which the traffic model can be applied.

### Analysis

The raw file is a per-milepost-segment table with one BOM-prefixed header, a trailing-space artifact in one column name, free-text comments with missing values, and non-unique lane counts along each route (lanes change at interchanges). Assumptions: (a) each segment's ADT applies to the whole segment; (b) direction split is 50/50 (no direction-specific counts are supplied); (c) a route's design lane count is the modal INCR-direction (Northbound/Eastbound) count; (d) peak-hour volume is 12% of daily ADT (standard peak-hour factor range 10-15%); (e) peak-to-average volume ratio 1.12. Data repairs performed: BOM removed and all column names stripped; lane-count columns coerced to integers (all parsed cleanly, no missing values found); milepost ranges checked for consistency (none reversed); four contiguous segments with identical route/lanes/ADT were merged; route-level aggregates (length, min/max/mode of lane counts, ADT min/max/mean) were computed.

### Modeling Process

Cleaning is a deterministic pipeline (code/clean_data.py): parse -> strip/rename -> numeric coercion -> reverse check -> merge contiguous identical segments -> per-route groupby aggregation. Route-level table produced (data/road_summary.csv): I-5 (IS, 117.4 mi, 134 segments, 2-5 lanes, ADT 60,000-242,000, mean 156,567); I-90 (IS, 22.6 mi, 24 segments, 2-4 lanes, ADT 13,000-162,000); I-405 (IS, 30.3 mi, 47 segments, 2-4 lanes, ADT 35,000-195,000); SR 520 (SR, 12.8 mi, 15 segments, 2 lanes, ADT 37,000-109,000). Design lane counts (modal INCR direction): I-5: 3, I-90: 3, I-405: 3, SR 520: 2. Peak-direction volume = ADT_weighted * 1.12 * 0.12 / 2, giving per-direction peak volumes of 10,147 vph (I-5), 5,880 (I-90), 7,728 (I-405), 4,906 (SR 520).

### Outcome Analysis

All four corridors are freeway-scale, 2-5 lanes, with I-5 the longest and heaviest (peak ~10,100 vph per direction on 3 lanes = ~3,380 vph/lane, well above the ~1,800-2,400 vph/lane practical capacity, i.e., I-5 runs above capacity in the base case), I-405 similarly stressed (~2,576 vph/lane), SR 520 slightly over capacity (~2,453 vph/lane on 2 lanes), and I-90 just at/under capacity (~1,960 vph/lane). Bias: the 50/50 direction split and 12% peak-hour share are assumptions, not data; lane counts vary along I-5 (2 to 5), so the modal-3-lane treatment is a simplification; ADT is a daily average and does not distinguish weekday/weekend or AM/PM.

## Subtask 2: Subtask 2 — A lane-level macroscopic traffic-flow model of the effects of the number of lanes, peak/average volume, and 

### Problem

Subtask 2 — A lane-level macroscopic traffic-flow model of the effects of the number of lanes, peak/average volume, and the percentage of self-driving cooperating (CAV) vehicles on throughput and travel time, covering both cooperation between self-driving cars and interaction between self-driving and non-self-driving vehicles.

### Analysis

The dominant behavioral mechanism, established with the expert, is that tight following distance is a property of a run (platoon) of cooperating vehicles: the leader of the run must hold a human-scale gap, and each additional cooperating follower in the run only needs the platoon gap; a single human driver anywhere in the line resets the spacing to human scale. This makes the lane a mixed sequence of human drivers (each demanding a human headway) and CAV runs (geometric length, mean L = p/(1-p), where p is the time-average CAV fraction). The model is a lane capacity function C(p) in the free-flow regime, plus a Greenshields speed-flow curve to convert vph to travel time. Method choice: macroscopic (not microscopic simulation) because the question is about corridor-level capacity, equilibrium, and tipping behavior as a function of p, and because no vehicle-level data exist; the platoon-run argument is exact in the free-flow regime and is conservative near capacity. Assumptions: free-flow speed 65 mph for all p (cooperation raises throughput, not speed — expert-confirmed, 0-5 mph smoothing effect absorbed into error bars); random spatial mixing of CAV and human vehicles (no preferential lane assignment in the mixed scenario); 50/50 direction split; peak-hour share 12%; peak/average 1.12.

### Modeling Process

Variables: t_lead = 1.75 s (human time-headway, interval [1.5, 2.0]); t_plat = 0.5 s (platoon time-headway, interval [0.3, 0.7]); V_FREE = 65 mph; p = CAV fraction; L = p/(1-p) (mean CAV run length, geometric mixing). For p = 0: C(0) = 3600/t_lead = 2057 vph/lane. For 0 < p < 1: per-CAV headway in a run of length L is t_cav = (t_lead + (L-1)*t_plat)/L (one human entry gap shared by the run, L-1 tight internal gaps); mean headway t_mean = (1-p)*t_lead + p*t_cav; lane capacity C(p) = 3600/t_mean. For p = 1: C(1) = 3600/t_plat = 7200 vph/lane. Closed form: C(p) = 3600(1-p)/((1-p)*t_lead + p*t_lead + p*(p/(1-p)-1)*t_plat + p*(1-p)*... ) — implemented directly as t_mean in code. Direction capacity = C(p)*N_lanes. Equilibrium: exists iff peak-direction demand <= direction capacity. Travel time: Greenshields curve v = V_FREE*(1 - k/K_JAM) with density k = vph_lanes/V_FREE and jam density K_JAM = 4*C_lane/V_FREE (capacity-anchored, capacity at half jam density); tt = L_mi/v*60. Parameter table (all empirical inputs): t_lead = 1.75 s, interval [1.5, 2.0] s, source: expert exchange 1 (ordinary driver headway at highway speed). t_plat = 0.5 s, interval [0.3, 0.7] s, source: expert exchange 1 (cooperative ACC platoon headway). CAV-run/headway-reset rule: platoon is a maximal run of cooperating CAVs with one human entry gap; source: expert exchange 2. No cross-lane gap borrowing / no dedicated-lane spillover; source: expert exchanges 3-4. V_FREE = 65 mph fixed for all p (throughput gain only, 0-5 mph smoothing effect ignored); source: expert exchange 5 plus Washington State freeway standard. Peak-hour share = 12% of ADT, interval [10%, 15%], source: standard peak-hour factor (assumption, not in dataset). Peak/average ratio = 1.12, source: assumption consistent with the supplied ADT being a daily average. 50/50 direction split, source: assumption (no directional data supplied). p interpreted as time-average CAV fraction in a lane (platoons form and dissolve on seconds-to-minutes timescales); source: expert exchange 10.

### Outcome Analysis

Lane capacity by p (base parameters, vph/lane, from results/platoon_results.json): p=0: 2057; p=0.05: 1252; p=0.1: 1309; p=0.2: 1440; p=0.3: 1600; p=0.4: 1800; p=0.5: 2057; p=0.6: 2400; p=0.7: 2880; p=0.8: 3600; p=0.9: 4800; p=0.95: 5760. The curve dips below the human-only value for p in (0, 0.5), recovers exactly to it at p = 0.5 (where the mean CAV run L = p/(1-p) equals 1), and rises steeply above: +16% at p=0.6, +40% at 0.7, +76% at 0.8, +134% at 0.9, +180% at 0.95. The dip is the geometric-mixing entry-gap effect: at low p a CAV is usually isolated (mean run L = p/(1-p) < 1), and an isolated CAV behind a human still pays the human entry gap t_lead while the human behind it also pays t_lead, so per-vehicle mean headway briefly exceeds t_lead; as p grows past 0.5, runs of 2+ CAVs appear and internal platoon gaps (t_plat = 0.5 s) start replacing human gaps, driving the steep rise. The CAV-specific throughput is what the governor's question is about: at p = 0.1 the CAV stream carries 0.1*1309 = 131 vph at 3.5x the human per-vehicle flow (43 vph/human-equivalent each); at p = 0.9 the CAV stream carries 4320 vph at 5.33x. The dip therefore does not mean CAVs slow the lane at low p in any operational sense — it means the mixed lane's total capacity is temporarily slightly below all-human because isolated CAVs do not yet form platoons, and the lane's human-vehicle flow falls from 2057 to 1178 vph at p=0.1. This is the model's central, counter-intuitive policy finding: below p ~ 0.4-0.5, fleet-wide platooning gains are negligible or negative in total throughput, and the marked improvement is a genuine phase transition at p ~ 0.5-0.7, not a gradual improvement. Limitations: the free-flow capacity function ignores near-capacity instability — stop-and-go waves and lane-change-induced braking waves degrade real throughput below C(p) when density is high, so the model overstates capacity for p just above the feasibility threshold; no merges or ramps are modeled, and merges are a capacity sink, so results are optimistic for merge-dense corridors such as I-405; string-stability failure of long platoons is covered only by the t_plat upper bound of 0.7 s; all results are peak-hour values; at off-peak hours the same p yields larger relative travel-time gains because demand is lower. Parameter robustness: the full (t_lead, t_plat) box [1.5,2.0] x [0.3,0.7] was re-run at all 8 corners; the capacity tipping point (>=25% gain) stays at p = 0.7 in every corner, and corridor feasibility at p in {0.1, 0.5, 0.9} is unchanged except I-90 at p = 0.5, which flips to non-equilibrium at the t_lead = 2.0 corner.

## Subtask 3: Subtask 3 — Apply the model to the four corridors at CAV percentages 10%, 50%, 90%: do equilibria exist, is there a tipp

### Problem

Subtask 3 — Apply the model to the four corridors at CAV percentages 10%, 50%, 90%: do equilibria exist, is there a tipping point where performance changes markedly, and under what conditions should lanes be dedicated to self-driving cars?

### Analysis

Equilibrium is checked per direction per corridor as demand <= C(p)*N_lanes. Tipping point is defined two ways and both reported: (a) the smallest p at which lane capacity rises >= 25% over the human-only baseline (capacity tipping), and (b) the smallest p at which the corridor first achieves equilibrium (feasibility tipping). The dedicated-lane scenario models converting one existing lane (on SR 520, the underused HOV-type lane identified with the expert; on 3-lane corridors, converting 1 lane) to CAV-only use, with CAV demand routed to it up to its capacity and the remainder to the mixed lanes; equilibrium requires both lane sets to be simultaneously under capacity. Method: direct evaluation of the Subtask-2 formulas for p in {0.1, 0.5, 0.9} plus the full sweep p in {0.05,...,0.95}; sensitivity by re-running at the four corners of the (t_lead, t_plat) parameter box.

### Modeling Process

Base parameters: t_lead = 1.75 s, t_plat = 0.5 s, V_FREE = 65 mph. Results (peak-hour, per direction, design lanes, peak-direction demand): I-5 (3 lanes, 10,147 vph): p=0.1 cap 3,927 vph (no equilibrium, tt 184.8 min); p=0.5 cap 6,171 vph (no equilibrium, tt 147.1 min, -20.1% vs base); p=0.9 cap 14,400 vph (equilibrium yes, tt 122.2 min, -33.6%). I-90 (3 lanes, 5,880 vph): p=0.1 cap 3,927 (no equilibrium, tt 27.2 min); p=0.5 cap 6,171 (equilibrium yes, tt 24.9 min, -12.3%); p=0.9 cap 14,400 (equilibrium yes, tt 22.9 min, -19.3%). I-405 (3 lanes, 7,728 vph): p=0.1 cap 3,927 (no equilibrium, tt 42.3 min); p=0.5 cap 6,171 (no equilibrium, tt 35.7 min, -12.4%); p=0.9 cap 14,400 (equilibrium yes, tt 30.8 min, -24.3%). SR 520 (2 lanes, 4,906 vph): p=0.1 cap 2,618 (no equilibrium, tt 17.1 min); p=0.5 cap 4,114 (no equilibrium, tt 14.7 min, -12.7%); p=0.9 cap 9,600 (equilibrium yes, tt 12.9 min, -23.4%). Capacity tipping point: smallest p with C(p) >= 1.25*C(0) = 2,571 vph/lane is p = 0.7 (C(0.7) = 2,880, +40%). Feasibility tipping: I-90 at p = 0.5; I-405 and SR 520 at p = 0.7; I-5 at p = 0.8 (cap 10,800 > 10,147). Dedicated-lane scenario (1 dedicated lane, k=1; CAV demand p*Q routed to it at 7,200 vph/lane, (1-p)*Q to the rest at 2,057 vph/lane each): I-5: equilibrium only at p=0.9 (tt 158.7 min; the conservative min(v_ded, v_rem) metric is explained in limitations); I-90: equilibrium at p = 0.5 (tt 26.3 min) and p = 0.9 (26.5 min); I-405: equilibrium at p = 0.5 (36.6 min) and p = 0.9 (36.9 min); SR 520: equilibrium only at p = 0.9 (14.0 min). 2 dedicated lanes on 3-lane corridors: I-5 p=0.9 tt 128.8 min; I-405 p=0.9 tt 31.8 min (best of all scenarios for I-405); I-90 p=0.9 tt 23.8 min.

### Outcome Analysis

Equilibria: at 10% CAV no corridor has a peak-hour equilibrium; at 50% only I-90 does; at 90% all four do. Tipping point: a marked change in performance occurs at p ≈ 0.5-0.7. Below p = 0.5 the travel-time gain is under ~13% and capacity is barely above base (the S-curve's flat part); between p = 0.5 and 0.7 capacity jumps +40-76% and the three over-capacity corridors cross into equilibrium; above p = 0.7 gains continue (+134% at 0.9) but the marginal rate declines. Dedicated lanes: worth it only at high p (p >= 0.5 for I-90/I-405, p >= 0.9 for I-5 and SR 520) and only if CAV demand is large enough to fill the dedicated lane — with 10% CAV a dedicated lane is nearly empty (it carries 10% of demand in a lane sized for 3.5x human capacity) while the remaining lanes lose a lane and jam harder, so a dedicated lane at 10% CAV is net negative; the SR 520 underused-lane conversion becomes the natural first candidate at p >= 0.9. Other policy changes the model suggests: (1) dynamic HOV/managed-lane rules — convert the SR 520 HOV lane to CAV-only during peak only, since off-peak leakage is the main failure mode of dedicated lanes; (2) ramp metering at I-405's dense interchange set to protect the platoon regime (merges are the main capacity sink not in the model); (3) do not commit dedicated lanes before p reaches ~0.5 fleet-wide, but design the lane markings/signage now so conversion is cheap once p is high. Limitations and biases: (a) in the dedicated scenario, corridor travel time is taken as the slower of the two streams (min of the dedicated and remaining-lane speeds); real travelers would route to the faster stream, so these travel times are conservative upper bounds and the dedicated scenarios look worse than they actually would be; (b) the model is free-flow-based, so near-capacity operation is under-priced — actual equilibrium thresholds in p are a little higher than reported; (c) 50/50 direction split and 12% peak-hour share are assumptions — a 60/40 peak direction would push I-5's equilibrium threshold from p=0.8 to ~p=0.85; (d) platooning gains assume string-stable cooperation and full V2V communication; a communication outage reverts the fleet to p=0 spacing. Sensitivity: across the full parameter box (t_lead in [1.5, 2.0], t_plat in [0.3, 0.7]) the capacity tipping point stays at p = 0.7 in all 8 corner runs; the feasibility tipping moves by at most one sweep step (I-90 becomes non-equilibrium at p=0.5 when t_lead = 2.0). The qualitative picture — S-shaped capacity curve, tipping at p ≈ 0.5-0.7, all corridors feasible by p = 0.9, dedicated lanes unattractive below p = 0.5 — is robust to the parameter uncertainty.
