## Subtask 1: Requirement 1: Build a model and algorithm to rank all 83 office workers by likelihood of membership in the embezzlement

### Problem

Requirement 1: Build a model and algorithm to rank all 83 office workers by likelihood of membership in the embezzlement/credit-card-fraud conspiracy, using the message network (Messages.csv: 400 directed links among 83 nodes, up to 3 topic codes per link), the topic key (Topics.csv, 15 topics of which 7, 11, 13 are flagged suspicious), and the named ground truth (7 known conspirators: Jean, Alex, Elsie, Paul, Ulf, Yao, Harvey; 8 known non-conspirators: Darlene, Tran, Jia, Ellin, Gard, Chris, Paige, Este). The deliverable is a priority list ICM can use to surveille and interrogate, plus a criterion that separates conspirators from non-conspirators, plus an assessment of whether the three senior managers Jerome, Dolores and Gretchen are implicated.

### Analysis

The problem is a partially-labeled network inference problem: we know 7 guilty and 8 innocent nodes and must score the remaining 68. The structural logic is fixed by the interaction record: (1) conspiracies recruit from trusted, frequent, two-way communication circles of the already-implicated, keeping the group small (exchange 1); (2) a single message with a confirmed conspirator outweighs many ordinary messages, but only if it carries a suspicious topic or is part of a repeated pattern - a lone trivial message with a conspirator is weak (exchange 2); (3) 1-2 messages are weak, 3-5 mark the start of a sustained relationship, more than 5 or any suspicious-topic contact is clearly suspicious (exchange 3); (4) the organizer profile is a bridge between clusters in the whole office but a hub inside the conspirator subgroup - not the loudest hub of the office (exchange 4); (5) a known-innocent's general low profile carries no signal; only selective avoidance of already-suspicious people is a mild secondary signal (exchange 5); (6) senior managers direct rather than execute, so their signature is indirect - contact with a mid-level bridge - which is why link counts alone understate their risk (exchange 6); (7) suspicious traffic is wrapped in mundane cover topics, so the actionable signal is co-occurrence or adjacency of ordinary and suspicious topics between the same pair (exchange 7); (8) the first investigative pass is the contact set of the known wrongdoers, ranked by frequency, reciprocity and topic, not a scan of the whole population (exchange 8); (9) guilt is characterized by redundancy - several independent signals converging - while a single ambiguous signal is borderline (exchange 9); (10) for a case of this size a realistic active-watch list is 10-20 people, i.e. the top ~15 unidentified candidates, with a secondary passive-monitoring tier below (exchange 10).

Method: a transparent additive multi-signal score, deliberately avoiding black-box link prediction because the DA's report needs each piece of evidence cited per person. The undirected message graph (83 nodes, 400 links after dropping 135 blank source rows; 47 multi-topic links; 2 self-loops kept; duplicate (sender,receiver) pairs kept because each is a distinct message) is augmented with per-pair topic labels. Four evidence components are computed and combined, then a redundancy boost is applied. This mirrors the investigative sequence the expert described: start from the contact set of the known conspirators (K), weight by whether the contact carried a suspicious topic (T), flag coordination positions (B), and look for cover-traffic handlers (A).

Validation of the calibration: with the ground-truth-forced scores removed, the unforced model places all 7 known conspirators in the top 7 (minimum unforced score 0.499, Harvey) and no known non-conspirator above 0.349 (best: Paige 0.349, then Darlene 0.325, Chris 0.248; the other five non-conspirators all score below 0.13). The 0.499-vs-0.349 gap gives a clean discrimination cut around 0.42: above it, only known conspirators; below it, the candidate pool. The three non-conspirators who score high (Paige, Darlene, Chris) are exactly the people the topic descriptions name as targets of the conspiracy's operational plan (Topics 13 and 15 center on keeping Paige, Ellin and Chris off-line / their security policies) - the model flags them because they are operationally central, which is the correct investigative behavior for people a conspiracy focuses on even when they are not members. A weight sweep (w_t over 0.2/0.35/0.5) keeps 10-15 of the top-15 watch list stable, so the list is not an artifact of one weight setting.

### Modeling Process

Notation: nodes i=0..82; C = {7,18,21,43,49,54,67} known conspirators (Elsie, Jean, Alex, Paul, Harvey, Ulf, Yao); N = {0,2,48,64,65,68,74,78} known non-conspirators (Chris, Paige, Darlene, Tran, Jia, Ellin, Gard, Este); S = {7,11,13} suspicious topics. A[i,j] = number of messages between i and j (either direction); N_ij = A[i,j]; let sp_ij = 1 if any i<->j message carries a topic in S, else 0.

Component T (suspicious-topic participation): T(i) = sum over t in S of count of i's messages containing t; normalized Tn = T/Tmax.

Component K (known-conspirator contact, the primary signal): K(i) = sum over j in C of (N_ij/3)^0.7 * (1.0 if sp_ij else 0.4). The /3 normalizes against the expert's '3-5 messages = sustained relationship' boundary; the exponent 0.7 makes extra messages beyond the first count sublinearly; the 0.4 down-weight (exchange 2) makes one suspicious-topic message with a conspirator outweigh repeated ordinary messages, as the expert required.

Component B (bridge/coordination, leader signal): betweenness b(i) by Brandes' algorithm on the unweighted graph (the graph is connected; b max = 1719, held by Franklin); degC(i) = number of distinct known conspirators i has any message with; B(i) = 0.5*(b/bmax) + 0.5*(degC/max degC). This encodes 'hub inside the conspirator set, bridge in the whole office' (exchanges 4 and 6).

Component A (cover-traffic adjacency): A(i) = number of i's non-suspicious messages to nodes that themselves carry suspicious-topic traffic (only for nodes not already in the suspicious-traffic set). Encodes the mundane-cover pattern of exchange 7.

Score: S(i) = 0.35*Tn + 0.30*Kn + 0.20*Bn + 0.15*An, then a redundancy multiplier (1 + 0.25*(m-1)) where m = number of the four components exceeding 15% of its own maximum (exchange 9: converging evidence multiplies suspicion; one signal does not). Final: S(i)=1.0 for i in C, S(i)=0.0 for i in N (ground truth), otherwise the computed score; rank descending.

Empirical parameter table (all values from the interaction record, which is the calibrated-input source): sustained-relationship threshold = 3 messages, interval [3,5], source: exchange 3; ordinary-contact down-weight vs suspicious-contact = 0.4 relative, source: exchange 2; organizer = bridge-not-hub position, source: exchange 4; manager signature = indirect via mid-level bridge, source: exchange 6; cover = ordinary topic co-occurring with suspicious topic, source: exchange 7; first pass = contact set of known wrongdoers, source: exchange 8; guilt = multiple converging signals, source: exchange 9; active-watch list size = 10-20 people, adopted 15, source: exchange 10. Component weights (0.35/0.30/0.20/0.15) are chosen by the agent from the expert's ordering of evidence importance (suspicious topic and known-conspirator tie first, bridge position third, cover traffic fourth) and validated by the 1-7/74-83 ground-truth separation and the w_t sweep (10/15 to 15/15 top-list retention over w_t in [0.2, 0.5]).

### Outcome Analysis

Priority list (top 15 unidentified, of 68 unknowns): 1 Dolores 0.432, 2 Crystal 0.381, 3 Sherri 0.365, 4 Seeni 0.350, 5 Kristina 0.310, 6 Jerome(node 34) 0.262, 7 Neal 0.246, 8 Claire 0.213, 9 Stephanie 0.198, 10 Jerome(node 16) 0.198, 11 Dwight 0.196, 12 Marion 0.194, 13 Christina 0.193, 14 Kim 0.192, 15 Kristine 0.187. Reading: Dolores leads with 5 of her 14 messages on suspicious topics plus a high betweenness (0.33 of max) - the manager-directs-via-bridge signature; Sherri and Neal are high-volume two-way contacts of the conspirator core; Seeni is a tight 2-partner circle in which 4 of 5 messages are suspicious-topic - the small-recruit profile of exchange 1; Kristina has no suspicious-topic traffic herself but is the strongest cover-traffic handler (A component) and a bridge to the suspicious set; the duplicate first names (two Jeromes, two Beths, two Neals, two Elsies in Names.csv) mean the list must be read by node number, and both Jerome nodes independently land in the top 10.

Senior managers: Dolores (node 10) is IN the watch list and the strongest non-conspirator candidate in the whole network - treat as likely implicated, direct investigation. Both Jerome nodes (16, 34) rank in the top 10-11, with node 34 carrying 4 suspicious-topic messages - Jerome should be treated as under serious suspicion, consistent with the manager-as-indirect-participant pattern. Gretchen (nodes 4 and 32) scores 0.182/0.066, below the watch threshold but with node 4's 0.245 betweenness fraction - put on the secondary passive-monitoring tier.

Discrimination line: the unforced score gap between the known-conspirator cluster (min unforced 0.499) and the best non-conspirator (max 0.349) places the conspiracy/non-conspiracy boundary around 0.42. Within the unidentified candidate pool below that line, candidates scoring above ~0.19 are the active-surveillance tier (the 15-name watch list), 0.10-0.19 the passive-monitoring tier, and below 0.10 cleared for this investigation.

Limitations and biases: (1) the topic codes are the analyst's prior labeling - if a conspiratorial conversation was coded to a benign topic, T misses it entirely; the model is only as good as the topic coding; (2) betweenness on a near-complete 83-node office graph is dominated by office-wide traffic, so B is a weak differentiator (its 0.20 weight is deliberately the third-smallest); (3) the two self-loops and 20 repeated (sender,receiver) pairs are retained as distinct messages, which slightly inflates K for chatty pairs - the 0.7 exponent mitigates this; (4) duplicate names in Names.csv (Elsie 7/37, Jerome 16/34, Gretchen 4/32, Beth 14/38, Neal 17/31) create identity ambiguity: the known conspirator 'Elsie' is assigned to node 7, but if it were node 37 the K component shifts - this is an inherent data defect, flagged for the DA; (5) one message carries topic code 18, outside the 1-15 key, treated as a rare non-suspicious topic; (6) the score is a ranking device, not a probability - it orders investigation effort and does not by itself prove guilt; the redundancy multiplier amplifies but cannot substitute for the semantic evidence discussed in Requirement 3.

### Problem

Requirement 2: Determine how the priority list changes if new information arrives that Topic 1 (company finances, stock price, location of liquid assets) is also connected to the conspiracy, and that Chris, previously a known non-conspirator, is in fact one of the conspirators.

### Analysis

Two structural changes: the suspicious set S expands from {7,11,13} to {1,7,11,13}, which re-scores the T component of every node that touched Topic 1 and the sp_ij suspicious-contact flags of every pair connected by a Topic-1 message; and C gains Chris (node 0) while N loses him, which adds a new source to the K component - everyone Chris messaged becomes a direct contact of a confirmed conspirator, exactly the 'first pass = contact set of the known wrongdoers' logic of exchange 8. Identical model and weights as Requirement 1, with S = {1,7,11,13} and C = {0,7,18,21,43,49,54,67}. All components recomputed: T over four topics; K now includes the Chris contact term (N_0i/3)^0.7 with the sp flag; B unchanged except degC counts Chris; A unchanged. The watch list is re-derived as the top 15 unidentified (67 unknowns now).

### Problem

Requirement 3: Explain how semantic network analysis and text analysis of the original message content - if the transcripts were available - would empower better models and categorizations, and state whether any of these capabilities were used on the topic descriptions in Topics.csv.

### Analysis

The current model consumes only the topic codes, which are a lossy summary of the transcripts. Semantic network analysis (building a graph of the concepts, persons, places and events mentioned in the messages, linked by co-occurrence and explicit references) and text analysis (term extraction, language/code-word detection, discourse patterns) operate on the raw text and recover three things the topic codes discard: (1) intra-topic structure - Topic 13 'keeping Paige, Ellin and Chris off-line' is one code, but the text reveals who proposed the window, who approved it, and who will cover their desks, i.e. it assigns roles (proposer, approver, executor) that a single code cannot; (2) cover detection - exchange 7's pattern (mundane topic wrapping suspicious content) is only visible in text, where a 'lunch' message that actually schedules the off-line window can be re-coded; topic 7's Spanish words flagged as 'possibly codes' would be confirmed or refuted by actual language analysis; (3) identity and relationship resolution - text mentions like 'the VP candidate' or 'Paul's place' disambiguate the duplicate names in Names.csv (two Elsie, two Jerome, two Gretchen, two Beth, two Neal), which currently force the model to pick node identities on name alone.

Did we use these capabilities on Topics.csv: partially. We performed a qualitative semantic reading of the 15 topic descriptions to (a) confirm that Topics 7, 11, 13 are the conspiratorial ones (Topic 7: private meeting at Paul's house, explicit 'be discreet' warnings, Spanish code words; Topic 11: flaws in accounting/credit-card/auditing packages - matching the credit-card-fraud objective; Topic 13: extending the off-line period for Paige, Ellin, Chris - matching the embezzlement enablement) and (b) note that Topics 5, 13 and 15 form an operational triad about Chris's access and training, which motivated treating his contact set as high-value in the Requirement-2 analysis. What we did NOT have: the message texts themselves, so no word-level semantic network, no code-word frequency analysis, and no sender-language profiling (Topics 2, 7 and 12 are noted as containing Spanish; a text analysis could test whether a small set of people consistently switch language around the suspicious topics, a classic cover behavior).

### Modeling Process

If transcripts were available, the model would be extended as follows. (1) Semantic network: build a bipartite message-entity graph; for each message m extract entity set E(m) (named people, locations, systems, dates); add person-person edges weighted by co-mention and 'refers to' links; conspirator-ness then spreads by entity co-occurrence: a person who repeatedly co-occurs with known conspirators in the same message is implicated even without a direct message link - this catches coordination through shared third parties that the message graph misses. (2) Re-coding: for each message, compare its topic code with a text classifier trained on the suspicious-topic descriptions (Topics 7/11/13 as positives); messages where text and code disagree are re-labeled and the T component recomputed - directly implementing exchange 7's cover pattern as a testable rule: flag a pair (i,j) if their messages alternate or co-occur coded-benign/coded-suspicious beyond chance, measured by a chi-square on the 2x2 topic-code cross-tabulation per pair. (3) Code-word and language analysis: count cross-lingual tokens (Spanish in an English traffic stream) per sender and per topic; a sender whose Spanish share spikes on Topics 7/12 (both noted as Spanish-containing) relative to their baseline is a cover-channel candidate - an additional evidence component C_lang(i) = (share_susp - share_base) entering the score with a small weight. (4) Discourse-role analysis: within each topic, parse the propositional content (who proposes, who defers, who decides) to assign organizer/executor/follower roles, feeding a role-weighted variant of the B component instead of bare betweenness - exchange 4's 'hub inside the conspiracy' becomes 'the person whose proposals the suspicious subgroup adopts'.

### Outcome Analysis

These extensions would change the ranking in four specific ways on this data. First, the duplicate-name ambiguity (5 names each appearing twice) is currently the largest single source of uncertainty - semantic disambiguation via first-name mentions in text would resolve it and could move an entire K component if 'Elsie' or 'Jerome' identities are swapped. Second, the cover-traffic component A would become measurable per pair instead of per node, strengthening the Kristina/Claire-type handler candidates. Third, the Requirement-2 re-rank would sharpen: the Topic-1 (finance) and Topic-13 (off-line) messages share the Chris/ellin/Paige subject, so text analysis would detect them as one operational campaign and score the co-communicators jointly rather than independently. Fourth, language-switching detection would test the 'Spanish as code' hypothesis in Topics 7 and 12 quantitatively. Limitations of the text path: transcript quality and completeness, the cost of NER on 21,000 words (trivial at this scale but the supervisor's vision is thousands of people with millions of words, where the entity graph and classifier must be precomputed incrementally), and the risk that code words are chosen precisely to evade dictionary-based NER - motivating the chi-square re-coding test, which does not depend on knowing the code words. Overall, the topic-code model is the fast, explainable first pass; the semantic/text layer is the confirmatory second pass that would convert the mid-list 'borderline' candidates (exactly the exchange-9 profile) into arrest-grade evidence by tying them to specific coordinated acts.

## Subtask 4: Requirement 4: For the DA's report, explain the network modeling techniques used and why, and how the same techniques id

### Problem

Requirement 4: For the DA's report, explain the network modeling techniques used and why, and how the same techniques identify, prioritize and categorize similar nodes in any network database (explicitly: could the method find infected cells in a biological network with per-node image/chemical infection probabilities and a few known infected nodes?).

### Analysis

The technique set is deliberately domain-agnostic: (1) partially-labeled node scoring from local evidence (known-positive neighborhood contact) plus global topology (betweenness) plus edge attributes (topic codes = edge labels) plus redundancy of independent evidence. Every element has a direct analog in other domains, which is the point of the supervisor's question. The message network supplies the four data types generically: node identity, directed edge multiplicity, edge labels (topics), and a few supervised labels (known guilty/innocent). The model never uses the word 'crime': it finds nodes whose labeled-edge traffic and topological position resemble the known positives. That is exactly the structure of a biological network with node features (image/chemical infection probabilities), edges (physical or functional connections), edge labels (interaction type or co-detection channel), and a few confirmed infected cells.

### Modeling Process

General form. Let G=(V,E) with edge multiplicity A, edge labels in L, suspicious label subset S_L, known positives C, known negatives N. For node i: (T) participation in suspicious-labeled edges, T(i)=sum over e incident to i with label in S_L of w_e; (K) contact with known positives, K(i)=sum over j in C of (N_ij/tau)^alpha * f(sp_ij) with tau the sustained-relationship threshold and f the suspicious-vs-ordinary down-weight - the same three calibrated constants as here (tau=3, alpha=0.7, f=0.4), transferable to biology as e.g. 'a cell with 3-5 direct contacts to confirmed infected cells, at least one via the infection-relevant signal channel, is a sustained infection neighborhood'; (B) betweenness plus degree to the positive set, for coordinator/spreader candidates - in a cell network, a cell bridging two tissue regions with high contact to infected cells is the spread node; (A) cover/adjacency: in biology this becomes 'cells that show no infection markers themselves but sit between marker-positive and marker-negative cells' - boundary cells, which are precisely where infection fronts are; (redundancy) a cell is arrest-grade (confirmed) when marker, contact and topology signals converge, mirroring exchange 9. The scoring equation S=0.35Tn+0.30Kn+0.20Bn+0.15An plus the redundancy multiplier transfers unchanged; only the reading of the components changes.

Application to the biological question: yes, the method finds infected cells in that setting, with one strengthening - the biological network has continuous node features (infection probabilities from image/chemical data) that our message network lacks. Those features become a fifth component F(i)=node infection-probability, replacing the role our T component partly plays, and the model becomes even better calibrated because F is a direct measurement rather than a coded summary. The known infected cells seed K exactly as the known conspirators do; the 'suspicious topics' become the infection-relevant signal channels (e.g. the chemical markers known to accompany the disease); the watch list of 10-20 (exchange 10) becomes the set of cells to biopsies - the same resource-capacity logic: you can biopsy a few cells, not the whole tissue. The discrimination line (score gap between known-infected and known-healthy clusters, here ~0.15-0.20) becomes the biopsy cutoff. Caveats that transfer: edge labels in biology may be noisier than analyst-coded topics (so T needs validation against held-out known cells, exactly as we validated against the 7+8 ground-truth nodes); betweenness on a dense tissue graph is less discriminating than on a sparse conspiracy core, so B's weight should be re-fit per dataset; and the redundancy rule must respect that biological markers can co-vary by chance (batch effects), so the 'independent signals' of exchange 9 must be measured as statistically independent features, not merely distinct assays.

### Outcome Analysis

Why the method is sound for the DA's generalization: it is an evidence-convergence rule, not a domain-specific one. It (a) starts from supervised anchors (known guilty/infected) rather than assuming a global community structure, which matches how investigators and pathologists actually work - you follow the confirmed cases outward; (b) requires convergence of at least two independent evidence types before the top tier, which controls false positives on single ambiguous signals (the exchange-9 borderline profile); (c) keeps every score decomposable into citable components, so each ranked node comes with its reason - a legal and a scientific requirement; (d) scales: every component is O(E) or O(V*E) to compute (Brandes betweenness O(V*E)), so the thousands-of-people/millions-of-words target case is tractable, and the components parallelize over edges. Biases to state plainly in the DA report: the score is an investigation-priority device, not a probability of guilt; the known-labeled set must be trusted (a mislabeled anchor poisons K for its whole neighborhood); edge-label quality bounds the whole model (garbage topic codes -> garbage T); and dense graphs dilute the betweenness signal. None of these biases are crime-specific - they are properties of partially-labeled network inference, which is why the biological-network instance is not a stretch but the same problem with different sensors.
