# Solution

## Subtask 1: Design an optimal five-year investment strategy for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100,000,000 per year education grant pro

### Problem

Design an optimal five-year investment strategy for the Goodgrant Foundation's $100,000,000 per year education grant program (starting July 2016): (1) a prioritized candidate list of 1 to N schools, (2) the investment amount per school, (3) an estimated return on investment (ROI) defined appropriately for a charitable education funder, and (4) the time duration and disbursement structure of the funding. The strategy must avoid duplicating the focus of other large grant organizations such as Gates and Lumina and must maximize the likelihood of a strong positive effect on student performance.

### Analysis

Assumptions: (A1) The program's dominant selection mechanism, established in expert exchange 1, is need-first and capacity-gated: the student population served (low-income/Pell, first-generation, minority share) determines priority, and demonstrated capacity to use funds well gates how much a school receives; prestige rankings and raw size are not primary screens. (A2) Five-year philanthropic funding produces modest, measurable improvements in intermediate outcomes: 1-5 percentage points in retention and 1-3 points in completion (exchange 2), with equity-targeted populations improving relatively more, and effects lagging spending - so the model evaluates retention as the leading indicator and completion as the lagging one. (A3) A usable per-school grant is $0.5M-$5M per year (exchange 3); below roughly $250K/yr the money is too small to fund a real program, and above ~$5-10M/yr a single school cannot absorb it without distorting priorities. (A4) Allocation should be tiered toward the top of the prioritized list, not even, because marginal return diminishes (exchange 4). (A5) Evidence of success is movement in targeted outcomes against a credible counterfactual, dose-response to funding level, and persistence after the grant ends - not enrollment growth or spending totals (exchange 5). (A6) Disbursement is annual and performance-contingent within a multi-year pledge (exchange 6), with a larger first-year startup tranche sized by intervention startup cost and the absorptive ceiling of ~1-2% of annual operating revenue (exchange 9). (A7) Hard exclusions: for-profit/mission-misaligned institutions, institutions too small or unstable to absorb a grant (UGDS < 100), and institutions with no measurable performance signal (exchange 7). (A8) The per-school commitment horizon is 5 years (the common ceiling for a single commitment; exchange 8), consistent with the program window. (A9) The largest grants go where absorptive capacity and execution history are highest, not merely where need is greatest (exchange 10), which the composite score enforces by requiring need, capacity, and ROI potential jointly. Method: a transparent three-component weighted scoring model (additive MCDM) over the cleaned IPEDS/College Scorecard data, followed by a rank-tapered water-filling allocation with per-school bounds. This method is sound because it (i) operationalizes the expert-confirmed mechanism directly as the objective structure, (ii) uses only data actually present in the supplied dataset for the school-level terms, (iii) keeps every empirical input as an explicit calibrated constant with a recorded source, and (iv) produces a fully auditable prioritized list and dollar allocation.

### Modeling Process

DATA CLEANING. Source files: 'Problem C - IPEDS UID for Potential Candidate Schools.xlsx' (2,977 candidate schools; no duplicate UNITIDs found) and 'Problem C - Most Recent Cohorts Data (Scorecard Elements).xlsx' (7,804 rows; no duplicate UNITIDs found). Repairs: whitespace and empty-string normalization on all text fields; numeric coercion of the 12 model-relevant quantitative columns with invalid tokens set to missing; inner join on UNITID giving 2,936 schools with cohort data (41 candidates dropped); missingness after join: PCTPELL 6, UGDS 1, RET_FT4 1,114, RET_FTL4 1,929 (used as a pair, either suffices), GRAD_DEBT_MDN10YR_SUPP 390, RPY_3YR_RT_SUPP 322, C150_4_POOLED_SUPP 1,108, C200_L4_POOLED_SUPP 1,946, md_earn_wne_p10 238, gt_25k_p6 203. Missing handling: PCTPELL and md_earn_wne_p10 median-imputed; debt median-of-10yr falling back to GRAD_DEBT_MDN_SUPP; completion C200_L4 falling back to C150_4; retention RET_FT4 falling back to RET_FTL4. Exclusions (exchange 7): CONTROL==3 for-profit (1 school), UGDS<100 (104 schools), missing PCTPELL and both retention measures (4 schools) => 105 excluded, 2,831 remain; a further data-signal filter requiring PCTPELL plus at least one retention measure leaves N = 2,738 schools analyzed. CONTROL codes verified against institution names in the data: 1 = public (state/local), 2 = private, 3 = for-profit. COMPONENTS (each min-max normalized to [0,1] over the analyzed set). (1) Need / mission fit: need = w_pell * norm(PCTPELL) + w_msr * norm(0.4*HBCU + 0.35*NANTI + 0.25*HSI), with w_pell = 0.8, w_msr = 0.2; final need component renormalized. (2) Capacity / absorptive ability: capacity = w_ret * norm(RET_FT4 or RET_FTL4) + w_comp * norm(completion) + w_stress * 0.5 * (norm(debt) + 1 - norm(median earnings w/o new earnings)), with w_ret = 0.4, w_comp = 0.3, w_stress = 0.3; the stress term raises the value of funding where financial burden is high and earnings low. (3) ROI potential: roi = w_up * (1 - norm(gt_25k_p6)) + w_rr * norm(retention), w_up = 0.6, w_rr = 0.4; headroom where fewer graduates already exceed $25k/yr. SCORE. score = a_n * need + a_c * capacity + a_r * roi, with a_n = 0.4, a_c = 0.3, a_r = 0.3 (weights ordered per exchange 1: population first, capacity second, cost-effectiveness third), renormalized to [0,1]; schools ranked by descending score. The top 50 form the prioritized candidate list (50 = midpoint of the 20-100 schools-per-year realistic range from exchange 3). ALLOCATION. Rank i (i=1..50) receives raw weight (51-i)^2 (taper exponent 2, exchange 4); a water-filling algorithm then enforces the per-school bounds [0.5M, 4.0M]: iterate - distribute the residual 100,000,000 - sum(pinned grants) proportionally to the raw weights of the free schools, pin any school at a bound, repeat until all grants are interior or only the floor/cap remains; a final dollar-level residual is returned to rank 1 so the portfolio sums to exactly 100,000,000. The $4.0M ceiling (rather than the $5M from exchange 3) follows exchange 10's observation that the largest grants must stay inside the absorptive ceiling (~1-2% of operating revenue) and below the distortion threshold. RESULT: 50 grants from $0.5M (rank 50) up to $4.0M (ranks 1-8 at the cap), 28 distinct grant sizes. ROI DEFINITION (charitable, per exchanges 2 and 5). For a school s with grant G_s (annual tranche), UGDS_s students, and predicted effect sizes: delta_retention_pts(s) = 1.0 + 4.0 * capacity_s (band 1-5 pts, exchange 2); delta_completion_pts(s) = 1.0 + 2.0 * capacity_s (band 1-3 pts); delta_earn_share(s) = 0.02 + 0.06 * roi_s (share of the funded student population moving into a higher earnings band). Students benefited over the 5-year window = UGDS_s * 5 (steady-flow approximation of the cohort the intervention acts on). ROI index (outcome dollars per $1M donated, wage-equivalent at $30,000/yr per student-year): ROI_s = [ (delta_retention_pts/100 * students * wage * 0.5) + (delta_completion_pts/100 * students * wage * 3 * 0.4) + (delta_earn_share * students * wage * 2 * 0.3) ] / (G_s / 1e6). The valuation weights (0.5 retention, 0.4 completion with a 3-year wage continuation, 0.3 earnings-band gain with a 2-year horizon) are the modeler's stated assumptions for converting points into a comparable dollar-equivalent; the effect bands themselves are the calibrated inputs. PORTFOLIO OUTPUT. Grant-weighted aggregate ROI index = 8,484,006 outcome-$ per $1M donated; selected portfolio mean composite score 0.744 vs 0.412 for the full analyzed set; mean predicted retention gain 3.54 pts, mean completion gain 2.27 pts; portfolio spans 18 states, heaviest in FL (5 schools, $12.50M), SC (4, $12.38M), TX (5, $11.72M), MS (4, $9.54M), GA (5, $9.31M); mean Pell share of selected schools 0.787 (min 0.412). DURATION AND DISBURSEMENT (exchanges 6, 8, 9). Each school receives a 5-year commitment of 5 * G_s; disbursed in annual performance-contingent tranches with a startup-weighted schedule: year 1 = 1.25 * (G_s/5), years 2-5 = 0.9375 * (G_s/5) each, so the schedule sums to G_s per year of commitment (1.25 + 4*0.9375 = 5), no year's tranche exceeds the $5M/yr absorptive ceiling, and years 2-5 are released only if the school meets the milestone from exchange 5 (targeted retention/completion movement against its peer-matched baseline). PARAMETER TABLE (empirical inputs; every other number is either a dataset value or a declared modeler assumption). (1) eff_ret band = [1.0, 5.0] retention percentage points, interval: 5-year funding window, institutions with measurable baselines, source: expert exchange 2. (2) eff_comp band = [1.0, 3.0] completion percentage points, same interval, source: expert exchange 2. (3) usable annual grant = [0.5M, 5M] per school, sweet spot 1-2M/yr, below ~250K/yr ineffective, source: expert exchange 3. (4) per-school commitment = 5 years (typical ceiling for a single commitment), source: expert exchange 8. (5) startup tranche factor = 1.25x first year, bounded by 1-2%-of-operating-revenue absorptive ceiling, source: expert exchange 9. (6) large-grant cap = 4.0M per school per year to stay inside the absorptive ceiling and below distortion, source: expert exchanges 3 and 10. (7) schools per year = 20-100 realistic range, 50 chosen as midpoint, source: expert exchange 3. (8) wage-equivalent annual value = 30,000 USD per student-year, interval [25,000, 35,000], declared modeler assumption for outcome dollarization (not an exchange value); a sensitivity band of 25k-35k scales the ROI index proportionally (21.2M-30.0M range would require rescaling, the central 30k is used throughout).

The full 50-school table with UNITID, state, score, grant, and predicted effects is in results/investment_strategy.csv. INTERPRETATION. (i) The composite ranking concentrates dollars in high-Pell, minority-serving institutions with credible retention baselines - precisely the profile where donor money is most likely to move student outcomes (exchanges 1, 5), and the geographic spread (18 states, Sun Belt heavy) reflects where low-income access and completion constraints bind. (ii) The tiered allocation spends the entire $100M with the top eight schools at the $4M cap and a long taper to the $0.5M floor, matching the diminishing-returns shape (exchange 4) while keeping every funded school above the $0.5M usability floor (exchange 3). (iii) Predicted portfolio effects: mean +3.54 retention points and +2.27 completion points over the window, with larger effects predicted for larger grants (dose-response, exchange 5); aggregate charity ROI index of ~8.48M outcome-dollars per $1M donated at the $30k wage-equivalent. (iv) The 5-year, annual, performance-contingent disbursement with a 1.25x startup tranche (exchanges 6, 8, 9) keeps the donor's leverage and the ability to reallocate, while giving each school a funded ramp for the specific intervention. LIMITATIONS AND BIASES. (L1) Min-max normalization makes component values relative to the analyzed set, so an out-of-set shock (e.g., a new low-Pell cohort) would shift scores; z-score variants were considered and rejected for interpretability. (L2) RET_FT4/RET_FTL4 and the pooled completion measures carry large missingness (1,114 / 1,946 schools); fallback imputation can overstate capacity for schools with sparse data - the data-signal filter only removes schools with no signal at all, so capacity estimates for sparsely-reported schools are the model's softest input. (L3) For-profit exclusion uses a single CONTROL==3 code; some mission-misaligned private institutions (exchange 7) cannot be identified from the supplied fields and remain in the pool, though the capacity/ROI terms tend to push weak performers down the list. (L4) The effect bands (1-5 retention pts, 1-3 completion pts) are expert-calibrated central ranges, not measured causal estimates; the model assumes the funded intervention is delivered with fidelity. (L5) The ROI index is a stated dollar-equivalent convention (wage value $30k/yr, continuation years 3 and 2, valuation weights 0.5/0.4/0.3) for cross-school comparison, not a financial return; a charitable reader should treat it as an outcomes-per-dollar ranking, not an investment yield. (L6) Five-year steady-flow cohort approximation (UGDS*5) ignores attrition and cohort turnover within the window; it is adequate for ranking but overstates absolute beneficiary counts. (L7) The portfolio is re-run annually with the same rules, but the model does not yet encode cross-year reallocation learning beyond the milestone gates. CONCLUSION. The strategy is a 50-school prioritized list, $100M/year, tiered from $4.0M to $0.5M per school, each on a 5-year commitment with annual performance-contingent tranches (1.25x startup year), scored by need (40%), capacity (30%), and ROI potential (30%), excluding for-profit, sub-scale, and no-signal institutions. It maximizes the likelihood of a strong positive effect on student performance by concentrating the foundation's dollars where the student population needs them, where the institution can absorb and execute them, and where the predicted outcomes-per-dollar are highest, with an explicit, auditable charity ROI definition and evaluation gates for each disbursement year.
