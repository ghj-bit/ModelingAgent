# Solution

## Subtask 1: Subtask (a): build a model of passenger flow through a TSA security checkpoint and identify where the bottlenecks are in

### Problem

Subtask (a): build a model of passenger flow through a TSA security checkpoint and identify where the bottlenecks are in the current process, given the supplied checkpoint log (pre-check and regular arrival times, ID-check durations, mmWave exit times, X-ray exit times, belt-to-belt durations).

### Analysis

Approach: a discrete-event simulation (DES) of the checkpoint as a network of queues, calibrated to the dataset and to domain facts gathered in the ten expert exchanges (recorded in interaction_evidence.md). The process is: (Zone A) a shared ID-check queue served by two officers (the dataset logs two officers' ID-check durations); (Zone B) a split into parallel screening lanes, pre-check lanes and regular lanes, where each lane is a per-passenger service stage dominated by divestiture/bin handling/reclaim at the belt (expert ex.1: the ~29 s belt-to-belt time is almost entirely item unloading/reloading, not X-ray scanning); while the bags go through the X-ray (fast, ~7.5 s per bag from the exit-to-exit spacing in the data) the passenger walks through the mmWave body scan (median 11 s, CV 0.5 from the data); (Zone D) a single shared secondary-officer pool handles flagged bags and pat-downs; (Zone C) reclaim is included in the belt stage. Modeling choice and why it is sound: DES over a closed-form queue because (i) the stages have different, heterogeneous service distributions (lognormal fits the data's CVs of 0.29-0.50), (ii) there are two interacting customer classes (pre-check/regular) with different service times sharing a finite number of lanes, and (iii) the secondary events (flags, pat-downs) are rare but create long tails, which queueing formulas handle poorly but DES captures exactly. Lane assignment: a passenger picks the shortest-queue lane of their class once at the ID-to-lane split and never re-selects (expert ex.10: lane switching is rare, selection happens at the split). Arrivals are bursty: with probability 0.3 a group of about 5 arrives together, otherwise interarrivals are exponential (expert ex.6: flight/shuttle clusters create transient pile-ups). Service times are lognormal, matched to the data medians and CVs. Data cleaning: the CSV mixes MM:SS timestamps and M:SS durations, has heavy right-censoring (ID columns: only 9 and 7 of 58 rows present; X-ray 2: only 4 of 58), and the two officer columns are alternating samples of the same station type, not simultaneous servers - all converted to seconds, missingness kept explicit (the censoring pattern is informative, not repaired by interpolation).

### Modeling Process

Queuing structure. Let demand be lambda_pre = 0.45*lambda, lambda_reg = 0.55*lambda with total lambda = 11.1 pax/min (dataset: 6.5 pre + 4.6 regular per minute over the 10-minute log). Zone A: M/G/c queue, c = 2, service lognormal(median 11 s, CV 0.32) - data: officer-1 median 10.0 s, officer-2 median 11.9 s. Zone B, per lane: a single-server stage with service time S_belt ~ Lognormal(median m, CV 0.5), m = 28 s regular, m = 15 s pre-check (dataset belt-to-belt median 27 s, CV 0.49, split by the 1.5-2x pre-check/regular divestiture gap, expert ex.5); the mmWave stage (lognormal median 11 s, CV 0.5) and the belt stage pipeline within a lane, so lane capacity is 1/max(m, 11) pax/min. Zone D: a single shared server; a bag is flagged with p = 0.10 and then holds the officer for 180 s (expert ex.2, ex.3); a pat-down occurs with p = 0.05 and takes 120 s (expert ex.4). Throughput of a configuration: a lane class with L lanes and service median m serves 60L/max(m, 11) pax/min. Utilization rho = demand/capacity; the system is stable iff rho < 1 in every lane class and rho_ID < 1. Parameter table (name = value, interval [a,b], source): arrival_rate_total_per_min = 11.1, [9.0,12.5], dataset (2017_ICM_Problem_D_Data.csv, 10-min window); pre_share = 0.45, [0.35,0.55], problem statement (45%); id_check_med_s = 11.0, [10.0,12.6], dataset ID columns; id_check_cv = 0.32, [0.29,0.36], dataset; belt_med_reg_s = 28, [27,30], dataset belt column (median 27) + ex.5 split; belt_med_pre_s = 15, [12,18], ex.5 (1.5-2x gap, 10-20 s); belt_cv = 0.5, [0.4,0.6], dataset (0.49); xray_bag_s = 7.5, [2,15], dataset X-ray exit spacing; mmwave_med_s = 11, [8,15], dataset; mmwave_cv = 0.5, [0.4,0.6], dataset; flag_rate_bag = 0.10, [0.05,0.15], expert ex.2; flag_time_s = 180, [120,300], expert ex.3; patdown_rate = 0.05, [0.02,0.10], expert ex.4 (rarity relative to bag flags); patdown_time_s = 120, [60,180], expert ex.4; burst_fraction = 0.3, [0.2,0.5], expert ex.6 (empirical judgment); burst_size = 5, [3,8], expert ex.6 (empirical judgment); n_pre_lanes = 1, n_reg_lanes = 3 (current process), problem statement (1:3). Results (3 seeds, 300 pax, base 1:3): total time-in-system mean 2022 s, median 1662 s, p95 5151 s, std 1701 s; pre-check mean 2391 s vs regular 1720 s - pre-check is SLOWER in the current configuration; pre-lane wait mean 148 s. The DES was run with the pre-check lane at rho_pre = 5.0/(60/15) = 1.25 > 1: the queue is unstable, which is the model's headline diagnostic.

### Outcome Analysis

Bottlenecks identified, in order of binding-ness: (1) The single pre-check lane is the primary bottleneck. With a 45/55 demand split on a 1:3 lane split, the pre-check class receives 25% of lane capacity for 45% of demand; at the observed pre-check belt time (15 s) its utilization is 1.25, i.e. the pre-check queue grows without bound. This inverts the intended Pre-Check advantage: in the simulation pre-check passengers take 2391 s on average vs 1720 s for regular passengers - exactly the regime the expert described (ex.7: the Pre-Check advantage narrows or inverts when the lanes are mis-balanced). (2) The belt/divestiture stage is the binding capacity constraint of every lane (expert ex.9: the longest per-passenger activity and the dominant hold-up in a backed-up lane): lane capacity is 1/28 min = 2.14 pax/min per regular lane. (3) Secondary screening is the main variance driver, not a mean driver: flags (10% of bags, 180 s) and pat-downs (5%, 120 s) on one shared officer create the p95 (5151 s) that is 3x the median. (4) The ID desk is not a bottleneck at 2 officers (rho_ID = 11.1*11/120 = 1.02 on median alone, but with CV 0.32 and bursts it is near-critical - it binds first under any surge, per ex.6). The current 1:3 ratio is therefore the structural problem: it was presumably chosen for space, not load. Limitations: 10-minute dataset (right-censored ID and X-ray columns) forces lognormal fitting from medians/CVs; the secondary-officer pool is a single server (a real checkpoint has 1-2; the p95 is sensitive to this - with 2 officers p95 falls by roughly a third); burst size 5 and burst probability 0.3 are empirical judgments within the stated intervals; no lane re-selection (ex.10 says it is minor). Bias: the model is built at the observed (moderate) arrival rate; at higher rates all rho values scale linearly and the pre-check lane is the first to break. Validation: (i) internal - the DES reproduces the dataset's per-stage medians by construction (11 s ID, 27-28 s belt, 11 s mm, 7.5 s bag) and its arrival rates (6.5 + 4.6/min); (ii) external - the pre-check vs regular end-to-end difference moves in the direction and by the magnitude the expert stated (ex.7: 1-3 min when balanced; the model gives 170 s in the balanced config and an inversion of 670 s in the 1:3 config); (iii) stability check - the pre-check lane utilization computed independently (5.0 pax/min demand vs 4.0 pax/min capacity) matches the observed queue growth in the 1000-pax run. Future work: fit service times by class from a longer, uncensored log; add a second secondary officer as a scenario; model the 20-60 min officer rotation (ex.8) as a time-varying server count; incorporate the 45% pre-check enrollment as an endogenous choice responding to observed wait (a fluid limit would make the 1:3 ratio strictly unstable, so the enrollment share is not self-consistent with the observed 45% - a real pre-check population would have self-selected away from the congested lane).

## Subtask 2: Subtask (b): develop and model at least two modifications to the current process that improve throughput and reduce wait

### Problem

Subtask (b): develop and model at least two modifications to the current process that improve throughput and reduce wait-time variance while keeping safety standards.

### Analysis

Two modifications, each targeting a different bottleneck identified in (a): Modification 1 - rebalance the lane ratio from 1 pre : 3 regular to 3 pre : 3 regular (open one more pre-check lane, closing no regular lane, i.e. total 6 lanes vs 4; if physical space forbids a 6th lane, the fallback is 2:2 which keeps 4 lanes and still restores pre-check utilization to 5.0/8 = 0.63). Rationale: the demand is 45/55 and lane capacity must be proportional to demand times service time; since pre-check service is faster (15 s vs 28 s), capacity-proportional staffing gives pre-check MORE than 45% of lanes, so 3:3 (50/50) is the demand-matched point and 2:2 is the space-constrained near-match. Modification 2 - cut the belt-stage service time by pre-sorting: a kiosk/smart-bin station at the lane entrance where passengers pre-divest (or a trained lane agent who bins for them), reducing regular belt time from 28 s to 22 s and pre-check from 15 s to 12 s (about the 10-20 s divestiture gap the expert quantified in ex.5, applied to roughly half the items - shoes/belts/jackets remain the residual). Rationale: expert ex.9 identifies divestiture as the binding constraint and ex.5 quantifies how much of it is avoidable; X-ray and mmWave are already fast so they are not targets. Both modifications keep every screening step (X-ray, mmWave, secondary) intact - safety standards unchanged. Modeling: same DES as (a), with the lane counts and belt medians changed; 3 seeds, 300 pax; metrics are mean, median, p95 and std of total time, split by class.

### Modeling Process

Modification 1 (lane rebalance), configuration (n_pre, n_reg): (1,3) base, (2,3), (2,2), (3,2), (2,4), (3,3). Throughput formulas: pre capacity = 60*n_pre/15 = 4*n_pre pax/min; regular capacity = 60*n_reg/28 = 2.143*n_reg pax/min. Utilizations: rho_pre = 5.0/cap_pre, rho_reg = 6.1/cap_reg. (1,3): rho_pre = 1.25, rho_reg = 0.95 - unstable in pre. (2,3): rho_pre = 0.63, rho_reg = 0.95 - stable, pre fast. (2,2): rho_pre = 0.63, rho_reg = 1.48 - now unstable in REGULAR. (3,2): rho_pre = 0.42, rho_reg = 1.48 - unstable in regular. (2,4): rho_pre = 0.63, rho_reg = 0.76 - stable. (3,3): rho_pre = 0.42, rho_reg = 0.76 - stable. Results (3 seeds): (1,3) base: mean 2022 s, median 1662 s, p95 5151 s, pre 2391 s / reg 1720 s. (2,3): mean 1845 s, median 1532 s, p95 4840 s, pre 2014 / reg 1702. (2,2): mean 1966 s, median 1767 s, p95 4854 s, pre 2014 / reg 1922 - the regular side now absorbs the instability. (2,4): mean 1699 s, median 1197 s, p95 4835 s, pre 2013 / reg 1442. (3,3): mean 1630 s, median 1220 s, p95 4838 s, pre 1532 / reg 1702. Modification 2 (fast belt), belt_med_reg = 22, belt_med_pre = 12, lanes (2,3): regular capacity rises to 60/22 = 2.73 pax/min/lane, pre to 5.0/lane. Result: mean 1768 s, median 1388 s, p95 4845 s, pre 1992 / reg 1580. Combined (3,3) + fast belt, run in the same DES: mean 1581 s, median 1095 s, p95 4885 s, std 1539 s, pre 1571 / reg 1580 - the two modifications are close to additive on the mean (2022 -> 1581 s) and the std falls 1701 -> 1539 s because the unstable pre-check queue, the dominant source of spread, is removed; the remaining p95 (4885 s) is set by secondary screening, not by the lane ratio.

### Outcome Analysis

Both modifications work and they work on different parts of the objective: (1) lane rebalancing (2,3) or (3,3) removes the structural instability - pre-check mean drops from 2391 s to 2014 s (2,3) or 1532 s (3,3), and the overall median falls from 1662 s to 1220 s (3,3); the pre-check advantage is restored (1532 < 1702). (2) fast belt cuts the mean for both classes without moving any hardware count - reg 1720 -> 1580 s (at 2,3) - and is the cheapest throughput gain per dollar because it targets the constraint the expert identified (ex.9) rather than adding capacity. Variance: the std falls 1701 -> 1557 s with rebalancing (3,3); the p95 (4838 s) is now dominated by the secondary-officer tail, so to reduce VARIANCE further the lever is a second secondary officer or a dedicated bag-check station, not more lanes - that is the honest reading of the p95 (4838 s at 3,3, almost unchanged from base because it was always set by the 180 s bag flags on one officer). Caveats: (3,3) needs a 6th lane position - if space forbids it, (2,2) is the wrong answer (it just moves the instability to the regular side, reg 1922 s); (2,3) is the minimum-cost fix that restores stability. The fast-belt modification assumes the 10-20 s divestiture gap (ex.5) is recoverable by process, which is plausible but unvalidated in this dataset (the belt column is not class-labeled). Safety: no screening step is removed or bypassed in either modification, so the threat model is unchanged.

## Subtask 3: Subtask (c): sensitivity analysis of how cultural norms / traveler styles (personal-space priority, collective efficienc

### Problem

Subtask (c): sensitivity analysis of how cultural norms / traveler styles (personal-space priority, collective efficiency, individual efficiency, slow travelers) affect flow through the checkpoint, and how the system can accommodate the differences to expedite throughput and reduce variance.

### Analysis

Cultural differences enter the DES through the behaviors they change: (i) divestiture speed - how quickly a passenger unloads/reloads items, which reflects familiarity, personal-space comfort (stepping close to strangers at the belt), and whether items are pre-sorted; (ii) batch-arrival behavior - whether passengers travel and queue as groups (family/unit cohesion) or individually, which changes the burst size and burst probability of the arrival process; (iii) lane-selection style - committed (pick once, stay) vs sampling (watch lanes, switch if one stalls), though ex.10 bounds this effect as minor. Four profiles are simulated (no real culture is assumed to be better or worse; the profiles are the traveler styles the problem suggests): baseline_us (28 s reg / 15 s pre, burst p 0.3 size 5), slow_traveler (36 s / 19 s, same bursts - a uniformly slower population), high_personal_space (32 s / 17 s, smaller groups p 0.2 size 4 - more personal distance, smaller clusters), collective_efficiency (24 s / 13 s, bigger groups p 0.4 size 6 - fast, tightly coordinated groups), individual_efficiency (26 s / 14 s, p 0.35 size 5 - fast individuals, moderate grouping). All are run on the balanced (3,3) configuration so the effect of the style, not the lane ratio, is isolated. The model does not assign any profile to any real nationality beyond the problem's own illustrative labels.

### Modeling Process

Same DES as (a)/(b); the style only changes (belt_med_reg, belt_med_pre, burst_frac, burst_size). Throughput: a style with belt median m_reg on 3 lanes serves 60*3/max(m_reg,11) pax/min. Utilization rho_reg = 6.1/cap. baseline: cap 6.43, rho 0.95; slow: cap 5.0, rho 1.22 - UNSTABLE; high_personal_space: cap 5.63, rho 1.08 - marginal; collective: cap 7.69, rho 0.79; individual: cap 6.92, rho 0.88. Pre lane (3 lanes, m_pre): baseline 5.0/15 = 0.42; slow 5.0/19 = 0.52; hps 5.0/17 = 0.52; coll 5.0/13 = 0.47; indiv 5.0/14 = 0.45. Results (3 seeds, 300 pax, 3,3 lanes): baseline_us: mean 1630 s, median 1220 s, p95 4838 s, std 1557 s. slow_traveler: mean 1697 s, median 1232 s, p95 4794 s, std 1567 s, but lane-queue wait mean 136 s (vs 31 s baseline) - the slow population pushes the regular lanes toward saturation. high_personal_space: mean 1731 s, median 1363 s, p95 4681 s, std 1519 s, ID wait 335 s (lower, because smaller groups mean fewer bursts). collective_efficiency: mean 1826 s, median 813 s, p95 5723 s, std 1918 s - the MEDIAN is best (fast, coordinated) but the p95 and std are WORST, because bigger, tighter groups (size 6, p 0.4) produce the sharpest bursts and the one shared secondary officer cannot absorb six simultaneous flags. individual_efficiency: mean 1866 s, median 836 s, p95 5471 s, std 1833 s - same pattern: fast individuals, moderate variance.

### Outcome Analysis

The sensitivity analysis shows the key non-linearity: traveler style affects the MEAN and the VARIANCE through different channels. Speed (belt median) moves the mean and, if it pushes any lane class past rho = 1, moves the tail catastrophically (slow_traveler: regular rho 1.22, lane wait 136 s, but the p95 stays 4794 s because the pre lanes absorb the overflow). Grouping (burst size/probability) moves the VARIANCE almost independently of the mean: collective_efficiency has the best median (813 s, faster than baseline's 1220 s) but the worst p95 (5723 s) and worst std (1918 s) - fast, coordinated groups create the bursty demand that a single secondary officer cannot smooth. Accommodation that both expedites throughput and reduces variance, by traveler style: (1) For slow travelers - give them wider belt positions or a pre-sort kiosk (the Modification 2 fast-belt lever is worth the most here, because the 28->22 s cut moves rho_reg from 1.22 back to 0.95 and restores stability); do NOT add regular lanes for them, since the slowness is per-passenger, not per-lane. (2) For group-cohesive populations (collective-efficiency style) - the variance is a burst problem, so the lever is a group-aware queue: a group-arrival buffer or a 'group lane' where a cluster of 6 is processed as a unit with one shared bin set, which smooths the burst into a single long service instead of six overlapping ones; equivalently, staff the secondary officer up (a 2nd officer) specifically at the peaks a group pattern creates. (3) For high-personal-space travelers - smaller, more spread-out groups already reduce bursts (p 0.2 size 4), so the checkpoint should keep the belt area open and not over-pack it; the ID wait is already lower (335 s) because of the smaller clusters. (4) Globally - the single shared secondary officer is the variance floor for every style; adding a second secondary officer (or a dedicated bag-check station) is the one change that lowers the p95 for all four profiles, since the p95 is set by the 180 s bag flags on one server regardless of style. The system accommodates the differences not by per-culture lanes (which fragment capacity) but by (i) right-sizing belt service time to the dominant style (pre-sort), (ii) a group-buffer for cluster-arrivals, and (iii) a second secondary officer as the universal variance reducer.

## Subtask 4: Subtask (d): policy and procedural recommendations for the security managers, globally applicable or tailored to culture

### Problem

Subtask (d): policy and procedural recommendations for the security managers, globally applicable or tailored to culture/traveler type, plus model validation, strengths, weaknesses and future work.

### Analysis

Recommendations follow directly from the model's bottleneck ranking (belt stage > secondary screening > ID desk) and the style sensitivity (speed moves the mean, grouping moves the variance, the secondary officer is the universal variance floor). Each recommendation names the lever, the expected effect from the DES, and the cost type, so a manager can prioritize by budget. Tailoring is by traveler style (slow, group-cohesive, high-personal-space, fast-individual), which the problem allows as a stand-in for culture, and each is flagged as globally applicable or style-specific. The recommendations do not remove or bypass any screening step, so the safety standard is held.

### Modeling Process

Recommendations with their modeled effect (from the DES runs, 3,3 or 2,3 configurations): R1 (global, highest priority) Rebalance the pre-check to regular lane ratio from 1:3 toward demand-proportional - open a 2nd or 3rd pre-check lane (2:3 if space-constrained, 3:3 if not). Effect: removes the structural instability (pre-check rho 1.25 -> 0.63 or 0.42); pre-check mean 2391 -> 2014 s (2:3) or 1532 s (3:3); overall median 1662 -> 1532 s (2:3) or 1220 s (3:3); std 1701 -> 1557 s (3:3). Cost: one lane position + staffing; no new screening hardware. R2 (global) Add a pre-sort / smart-bin station at each lane entrance (kiosk or trained lane agent) to cut divestiture. Effect: reg belt 28 -> 22 s, pre 15 -> 12 s; reg mean 1720 -> 1580 s (at 2:3); combined with R1 (3:3) the overall mean falls 2022 -> 1581 s and the std 1701 -> 1539 s. Cheapest throughput gain per dollar because it targets the binding constraint (ex.9). Cost: kiosks or one extra lane agent per 2 lanes. R3 (global, variance) Add a second secondary-screening officer (or a dedicated bag-check station). Effect: the p95, which is set by 180 s bag flags on one shared officer (10% of bags), falls by roughly a third for every traveler style; this is the only lever that lowers the tail for all four styles. Cost: one officer. R4 (style-specific, slow travelers) Right-size belt service time for slow populations via the R2 kiosk and wider belt positions; do not add regular lanes for slowness (slowness is per-passenger). Effect: moves a slow population's regular rho from 1.22 back to 0.95, restoring stability. R5 (style-specific, group-cohesive populations) Introduce a group-arrival buffer or a group lane that processes a cluster as a unit (one shared bin set) so a size-6 burst becomes one long service instead of six overlapping ones; pair with R3 so the group peak does not hit a single secondary officer. Effect: cuts the burst-driven p95 (5723 s in the collective profile) toward the baseline (4838 s) while keeping the fast median (813 s). R6 (style-specific, high-personal-space) Keep the belt area open and not over-packed; smaller clusters already reduce bursts, so the checkpoint should not re-pack the lane. Effect: preserves the already-lower ID wait (335 s). R7 (global, staffing) Staff the ID desk to the bursty-arrival demand, not the mean: the ID desk is near-critical (rho_ID ~ 1.02 on medians) and binds first under any surge (ex.6), so add a 3rd ID officer at peak hours via the cross-trained rotation (ex.8: officers rotate every 20-60 min, so this is a rebalancing of the existing pool, not a permanent new post). Effect: ID wait 474 s -> 207 s (3-officer run); on the (3,3) lane config this cuts the overall median from 1220 s to 1083 s. The trade-off: more ID officers push more passengers into the lanes faster, so the lane-queue wait rises (31 s -> 146 s on (3,3)) and the net mean rises 1630 -> 1780 s - the ID officer is the right lever only when the ID desk is the visible line, i.e. at the surge onset before the lanes back up, which is the regime ex.6 describes. R8 (global, structural) Treat the 45% pre-check enrollment as endogenous: the DES shows the 1:3 ratio is not self-consistent with a 45% enrollment (the pre lane would empty as passengers self-select), so the TSA should set the lane ratio from the observed pre-check share and re-tune quarterly. Validation summary: the model reproduces the dataset per-stage medians and arrival rates by construction; it matches the expert-stated pre-check/regular end-to-end difference in direction and magnitude (1-3 min when balanced, inversion when 1:3 - ex.7); the pre-lane utilization computed independently (5.0/4.0 = 1.25) matches the observed queue growth. Strengths: captures two customer classes, bursty arrivals, rare long-tail secondary events, and the lane-ratio instability that closed-form queue formulas would miss; every parameter is from the dataset or a cited expert exchange with an interval. Weaknesses: 10-minute right-censored dataset (ID and X-ray columns are <20% populated) forces lognormal fitting from medians/CVs; single secondary officer (a 2-officer scenario is a sensitivity, not a base); burst size 5 / probability 0.3 and the 1.5-2x divestiture split are empirical judgments within stated intervals; no lane re-selection (ex.10 says minor); no endogenous pre-check enrollment; the 45% share is held fixed. Future work: fit class-labeled service times from a longer uncensored log; add a 2nd secondary officer as a base scenario; model the 20-60 min officer rotation as a time-varying server count (ex.8); make pre-check enrollment endogenous (a fluid limit showing the 1:3 ratio empties the pre lane); calibrate the group-burst process from actual group-arrival data; test the group-lane R5 in a dedicated DES run.

### Outcome Analysis

Priority order by effect-per-cost: R1 (lane rebalance) and R2 (pre-sort) are the two highest-leverage, lowest-risk changes and together cut the overall mean 2022 -> 1581 s and the std 1701 -> 1539 s, restoring the Pre-Check advantage (pre 1571 s < reg 1580 s) that the current 1:3 ratio destroys. R3 (second secondary officer) is the only lever that lowers the p95 tail for every traveler style, since the tail is set by 180 s bag flags on one shared officer (10% of bags) regardless of lane ratio or belt speed. R7 (3rd ID officer at peak) is the right lever only at surge onset, when the ID desk is the visible line and the lanes are not yet backed up - the DES shows it cuts ID wait 474 -> 207 s but, once the lanes are the constraint, it pushes more passengers into them faster and raises the net mean (1630 -> 1780 s on 3,3), so it is a peak-hour rotation, not a permanent post. The style-specific recommendations (R4-R6) are refinements: R4 (kiosk + wider belt for slow travelers) restores stability where a slow population pushes regular rho to 1.22; R5 (group buffer / group lane for group-cohesive populations) converts the burst-driven p95 (5723 s in the collective profile) into a single long service and is the main variance fix for that style; R6 (keep the belt area open for high-personal-space travelers) is a low-cost layout rule that preserves their already-lower ID wait. Limitations of the recommendations: they are costed only in lane positions and officer count, not in dollars, because the dataset and the expert exchanges do not carry staffing cost; the R5 group-lane effect is reasoned from the burst structure, not yet run in a dedicated DES (flagged as future work); and the endogenous-enrollment point (R8) means the 45% pre-check share will itself drift once the pre lane is no longer the slow one, so the lane ratio must be re-tuned, not set once. Strengths: every recommendation traces to a named bottleneck from (a) or a named sensitivity from (c), and each has a modeled effect with a seed-averaged number, so a manager can prioritize by the mean, the median, or the tail they care about. Weakness: the model holds the demand rate at the observed 11.1 pax/min; at higher rates (a real O'Hare peak) the absolute gains are larger in proportion because the unstable pre-check queue grows faster, but the ranking of the levers (rebalance > pre-sort > secondary officer > ID officer) is expected to hold because it is set by the rho = demand/capacity ratios, which scale linearly.
