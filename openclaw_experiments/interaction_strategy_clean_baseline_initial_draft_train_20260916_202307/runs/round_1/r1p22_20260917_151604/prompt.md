**2016 ICM Problem D: Measuring the Evolution and Influence in Society’s Information Networks**

**Objective:**  
Analyze the relationship between the speed/flow of information and its inherent value across different historical periods to understand the evolution of society's information networks.

**Historical Periods for Analysis:**  
1. **1870s:** Newspapers delivered by trains, stories passed by telegraph.
2. **1920s:** Radios became common household items.
3. **1970s:** Televisions were in most homes.
4. **1990s:** Households began connecting to the early internet.
5. **2010s:** Mobile phones provided global connectivity.

**Tasks:**

(a) **Model Development:**  
Create models to explore the flow of information and determine what qualifies as news.

(b) **Model Validation:**  
Use historical data to validate the model's reliability and predict current information communication scenarios, comparing them with today's reality.

(c) **Future Prediction:**  
Use the model to predict communication networks' relationships and capacities around 2050.

(d) **Public Influence Modeling:**  
Model how public interest and opinion can be influenced through information networks in today's connected world.

(e) **Information Spread Analysis:**  
Determine how information value, initial opinions, message form, source, and network topology can be used to spread information and influence public opinion.

**Data Sources:**  
- Circulation data and media availability reports.
- Historical perspectives on news and media.
- Consideration of significant world events and their hypothetical spread in different eras.

**Useful Links for Data and Historical Context:**
