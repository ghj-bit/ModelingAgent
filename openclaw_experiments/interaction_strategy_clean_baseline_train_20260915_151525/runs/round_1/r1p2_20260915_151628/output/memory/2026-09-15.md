## Task: HiMCM 2001 "Forest Service" wildfire unit allocation
- Solved the full problem end-to-end. Key model chain:
  1. Anisotropic Huygens/elliptical **arrival-time field** (directed Finsler graph,
     SciPy `csgraph.dijkstra`) on a 1 km lattice; head ROS = 10 % of wind
     (Cruz & Alexander 2019). Firebreaks = partial barrier (γ=0.6) + the only roads.
  2. Response: equipment convoy on the 5 km grid at 30 km/h + 0.40 h; helicopter lifts
     crews only.
  3. Containment probability = logistic of decayed unit contributions (τ=1.5 h),
     calibrated to NWCG/San Dimas fireline-production rates (Tech Tip 1151-1805P, 2011).
  4. Damage = p_c·A(t_attack) + (1−p_c)·A_escape(12 h). Objective = E[damage] over 400
     ignitions. Optimised with SA + exact symmetric brute force.
- **Result:** camps at **(30,30) (30,50) (45,30) (45,50)** km → E[burned] ≈ 37.8 km²/fire
  (corners = 101 km², centred block = 38.7 km²). Wind biases placement slightly west.
- **Forecast:** ≈ 38 km²/fire; 173 km² if it escapes; seasonal N×37.8 km² (≈ 6 % of area
  for ~10 fires). Escalate when a fire passes ~6 km² / 2 h or its first downwind firebreak.

## Lessons
- Dijkstra for anisotropic fire spread is fast and exact — the directed (asymmetric)
  graph is essential because R(θ) ≠ R(θ+π).
- With few, slow units in a windy landscape, **response speed dominates force size**;
  the sensitivity profile (τ, v_e) is as valuable as the optimum itself.
